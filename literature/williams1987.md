# Williams 1987: leaf area of Thompson Seedless against degree-days, three seasons

Whole-vine leaf area and dry weights of irrigated raisin vines at Kearney, near Fresno,
sampled through 1983-1985. Read for the shape and timing of leaf area development.

## What it holds

- **Site** (l. 22-38): 15-year-old own-rooted Thompson Seedless, head-trained and cane-pruned
  on a single wire at 1.3 m; vines 2.44 m, rows 3.66 m (8.9 m² a vine); flood-irrigated
  about every three weeks to a month before harvest, never visibly stressed (l. 174); shoots
  trimmed near the ground before harvest for raisin drying. 50 % budbreak on 12 and 8 March
  (1983, 1984; l. 106).
- **Clock** (abstract, l. 11-14): degree-days above 10 °C from 50 % budbreak.
- **Leaf area** (l. 70-75; Figure 3, read off the page image, approximate): total vine leaf
  area rose linearly from about 150 to about 1,000 °Cd, to maxima of about 24 (1983), 28
  (1984) and 27 m² (1985) a vine, then fell with trimming and leaf fall; all leaves were
  down by about 2,600 °Cd (l. 98). Half the maximum is reached near 550 °Cd (my reading of
  the figure).
- **Shoots** (Table 1, p. 326, from data before trimming): 52, 69 and 90 shoots a vine;
  4,336, 2,918 and 2,783 cm² of leaf a shoot; 22.2, 25.3 and 20.7 nodes a shoot; mean primary
  leaf 138, 118 and 110 cm². So 22.5, 20.1 and 25.0 m² a vine (my calculation).
- **Laterals** (l. 76-84): 27, 22 and 12 % of vine leaf area at 1,000 °Cd; lateral shoots had
  80 % of the primaries' nodes but smaller leaves.
- **Leaf mass per area** (abstract): from about 40 g/m² early to over 65 g/m² late.

## Dependence

- None: the author's own destructive measurements. The linear form is an observation, not
  a fitted model.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- **Out of region, for the shape only:** a large irrigated head-trained table/raisin vine
  (about 2.5 to 3 LAI over 8.9 m², my calculation) grows its leaf area almost linearly in
  thermal time, not sigmoidally, and reaches its maximum near 1,000 °Cd. Kin by form: a
  leaf-area curve on degree-days would be growth on thermal time (the engine's clock), so
  Agrarium's day-based logistic should take only its shape and timing, not its clock.
