# Kim et al. 2010: a short review of interpolation and satellite weather for site-specific disease prediction

A New Zealand review of how interpolation and remote sensing could give site-specific weather where there is no station; it presents no new data and no experiment. No record.

**Read this first.** A review: every number and finding in it is second-hand, from papers it cites, and none was checked here. The DOI is not printed. The authors' judgment that the interpolated and satellite data scales suit daily disease management has no test behind it in the text (l. 149).

## What it holds

- **Station representativeness** (l. 20): a single weather station represents only a small area around it. Interpolated estimates for sites without a station are called "simulated observations" (l. 44, cited from Magarey et al. 2001).
- **Interpolation** (l. 49, 58, 63): inverse distance weighting gives no measure of the precision or accuracy of its estimates; ordinary kriging gives the variance of the estimation error for each estimate (Meyers 1994, cited). Elevation, aspect and distance to ocean have been used as co-variables (Jarvis & Stuart 2001, cited).
- **Satellite data** (l. 75, 92, 95, 101): daily MODIS land surface temperature was reported similar to station measurements (Wan et al. 2004, cited), and MODIS data are freely available. Passive microwave data were used to detect dew on crops (Kabela et al. 2009, cited), but Coriolis data cover only short periods of the day, so a model is needed to predict leaf wetness.
- **Gridded sets** (l. 79, 93): DAYMET interpolates daily temperature and precipitation over complex terrain (Thornton et al. 1997); CRU climate surfaces have 10 arc-minute resolution from 30-year station records, 1961-1990 (New et al. 2002).
- **Leaf wetness** (l. 87): rarely measured at automated stations, though it matters for many fungal diseases (Gleason et al. 1997).
- **Prototype** (l. 148, 151, 153): where no weather data exist at a site, spatial interpolation generates site-specific data; a natural-neighbour method is implemented in the prototype data layer, which could draw on the national forecast system EcoConnect. No validation is shown.

## Bearing (2026-10-21)

For Agrarium's D48 study: vineyard variability, and what one station or a gridded or virtual source misses. Background only.

- **Takeable:** the statement that one station represents a small area, and a list of interpolation and satellite sources (kriging with a variance; MODIS temperature; gridded sets; microwave dew) with their gaps, in particular that leaf wetness is rarely measured and satellite dew data cover short periods of the day. Kriging's error variance is the property that a variance-based placement score would use; inverse distance weighting has none (l. 49, 58).
- **Not shown:** any sensor placement method, any held-out test, any spread or size of the loss from using a distant station, any number from its own data. The kind of result is a narrative review with no new data.
