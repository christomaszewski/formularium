# Riou, Pieri & Le Clech 1994: net radiation of vine rows and soil, and transpiration as a fraction of Penman ETP

The 1989 geometric light-interception model is extended to long-wave and net radiation of two exchange surfaces (vine rows, inter-row soil); sap flow then shows that a well watered vine transpires its share of Penman ETP.

**Read this first.** One vineyard, one summer for the energy balance (43 days, 1992) and 13 days of another summer (1993) for the net radiation check. The net radiation polynomial's coefficients Ca, Cs, Cf, Ci are named but not printed, so the model cannot be rebuilt from this paper; it needs Riou et al. 1989 (held as riou1989) and the geometry.

## What it holds

- **Site and vine:** Graves soil near Bordeaux, sandy with many coarse elements; foliage 0.35 m wide, 1.2 m high, total height 1.6 m, rows 2 m apart, vines 1 m apart (2 m2 per vine), rows 27 degrees off north-south (l. 130-138).
- **Net radiation model:** two surface temperatures (Ts soil, Tf foliage), emissivity 0.96 for both, inner faces of the box at air temperature (l. 105-112). Result is a polynomial Rn = Ca Ra + Cs sigma Ts^4 + Cf sigma Tf^4 + Ci sigma Ta^4 (l. 114). Atmospheric longwave Ra by Idso & Jackson 1969 with Arnfield's 1979 hourly correction (l. 190-191).
- **Radiation sensors:** pyranometers and albedometer on a 6 m mast; five net radiometers at 27 cm on a 12 m rail moving along the row, 11, 52, 98, 152 and 184 cm from the east row (l. 153-178). Foliage and soil temperatures by hand-held infrared radiometer (l. 145-147); air temperature by a ventilated thermocouple at 1 m; humidity and wind at 4.75 m.
- **Validation (Fig. 1, page image):** half-hour values, 13 days of summer 1993, n = 551, calculated against measured. Soil Rns: y = 1.02 x - 23, r2 0.98, and through the origin y = 0.96 x, r2 0.96. Vine rows Rnv: y = 0.99 x - 27, r2 0.96, and y = 0.87 x, r2 0.93. Whole canopy Rn: y = 1.04 x - 45, r2 0.998, and y = 0.96 x, r2 0.979. The text calls the fit satisfactory, with more scatter for the vine.
- **Partition table (daily, simulated, l. 84-92):** N-S rows, foliage 0.3 m wide and 0.9 m high, lateral porosity 0.33, clear day 20 August at Bordeaux. Row spacing 0.9 / 1.2 / 1.5 / 1.8 / 2.1 / 2.4 / 3.0 m: vine absorbs 0.62 / 0.54 / 0.47 / 0.42 / 0.37 / 0.33 / 0.29 of global radiation, soil 0.21 / 0.30 / 0.38 / 0.43 / 0.48 / 0.52 / 0.56, reflected 0.17 to 0.15. At 1.8 m spacing the vine share moves only 0.40 to 0.42 from June to September for N-S rows, but 0.31 to 0.46 for E-W rows (l. 97-99).
- **Fraction of net radiation vs of global:** Rnv/Rn and Rgv/[(1-a)Rg] stay close while D/Hf is at least 1 (Fig. 2; vine absorbs no more than 66 % of the total, l. 225). Bordeaux D/Hf is about 2 (l. 220).
- **Energy balance:** eddy covariance (1-D sonic, fine thermocouple, krypton hygrometer, at 4.68 m, l. 237-240); sap flow by heat balance on 6 vines, divided by 2 m2 (l. 241-250); soil heat flux from temperature profiles at 1, 4, 9, 16, 25 and 36 cm on 6 verticals (l. 251-266); soil specific heat from texture and density. 43 days from 16 July to 27 August 1992, with a Rain-ETP bucket check (200 mm store) that the soil was wet (l. 267-273).
- **Result:** Rn = H + G + LE closes acceptably; H is small; LE stays near Rn - G; total LE is about twice vine transpiration, whose slope on Rn - G is about 1/2 (l. 358, l. 386). Daily vine sap flow DS is proportional to Rnv and to Rgv (slopes 1 and 2/3 as guides in Fig. 7).
- **Formulation (l. 427-438, 410):** DS = (Rnv/Rn) ETP = (Rgv/[(1-a)Rg]) ETP, and the simpler DS = (Rgv/(0.8 Rg)) ETP, where 0.8 is the absorption of a grass-like continuous canopy; the vineyard albedo is about 0.2 so the two agree. Penman ETP computed with albedo 0.20 (l. 393); the wind function is garbled in the text (written 0.0026 (1 + 0.45? u) mm per 24 h per Pa) and is not used here. Sap flow runs near the estimate a few days after each of three trimmings (days 195, 212, 226).
- **Scope:** limited to wet soil and a developed canopy (cases a and b of l. 75-92); the dry-soil case and stomatal regulation are not treated (l. 97-98, l. 514-519).

## Dependence

- Builds on riou1989 (interception model, held), Valancogne & Nasr 1993 (sap flow), Idso & Jackson 1969, Arnfield 1979, Penman 1948 and Pieri's 1989 thesis on airflow in row crops.
- Shares authors with riou1989, pieri2003 and pieri1996 (Pieri, Riou). No dependence on the engine's wetness models.

## Bearing (2026-10-30)

- **Vine radiation share:** the table gives the fraction of daily global radiation a hedgerow vine absorbs by row spacing and orientation, a check for any simulated light model: at 2 m spacing and 1.2 m foliage the vine share is near 0.4 (interpolating the 1.8 and 2.1 m rows, arithmetic, not printed).
- **Transpiration:** the vine's transpiration is Penman ETP times its absorbed fraction of solar radiation; this is a candidate daily potential-transpiration formulation for the vine water balance (not for stress).
- **Not shown:** the coefficients of the polynomial, a leaf-level or in-canopy temperature, leaf wetness or humidity.
