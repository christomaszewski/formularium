# Bramley et al. 2011: spray programmes compared across whole Tasmanian blocks

Two whole-of-block experiments with commercial sprayers in Tasmania: reduced sulphur
programmes against powdery mildew in a 4.5 ha Pinot Noir block, and one fungicide at 80 %
capfall or at pre-bunch closure against Botrytis in a 2.4 ha Chardonnay block, with
disease on geo-referenced target vines mapped across each block.

## What it holds

- Powdery mildew: over 230 target vines (l. 126); near veraison programme S2 gave 1.5 %
  mean severity against 3.1 % for S1 (l. 141); incidence, severity and the treatment
  response were strongly spatially structured and followed the topography (l. 61, 145).
- Botrytis: 150 target vines per treatment (l. 130); on 24 March the pre-bunch-closure
  spray gave 1.6 % severity against 3.6 % at 80 % capfall (l. 150); in about half the block
  the timing made no difference (l. 158); severity also followed vine vigour (l. 61).
- The authors read the spatial pattern over four pre-harvest dates as a sign that
  secondary spread added little to Botrytis severity (l. 61).

## Dependence

- Its own field data; no model timed the sprays. Methods of spatial analysis follow
  Bishop & Lark 2006. No engine author.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- For Agrarium (real sites, terrain): published evidence that both disease and the
  effect of a spray programme vary with topography within a block of a few hectares, so
  a whole-block treatment effect is an average over very different responses; and for
  Botrytis, little within-season secondary spread. Not read in full.
