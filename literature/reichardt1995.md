# Reichardt et al.: daily rain at ten gauges within 1,000 ha in Piracicaba, Brazil, 1993-94

A one-year study of daily rainfall variability among ten observation points on about 1,000 ha, with coefficients of variation of daily data.

**Read this first.**
- The text prints no year and no journal on its first page; year, journal, volume and pages are from Crossref (DOI 10.1590/s0103-90161995000100008), which agrees with the printed submission and acceptance dates (1994-12-14, 1995-02-10).
- Tropical, 22.7 S and 580 m; the daily CV includes days with zeros at some gauges, so very light rain gives very high CVs.

## What it holds

- **Daily CV (l. 17):** among the ten gauges it varied from 2.2 to 169.3 % (1993-94), and the variability did not depend on rain type (convective, frontal or other).
- **Rainy days (l. 84):** of 87 rainy days, 15 had CV below 10 % and 8 had CV above 100 %. The high ones were very light rain with several zero gauges.
- **Distance (l. 18):** the data were not related to the separation distance (1,000 to 2,500 m between points). One point does not represent areas 1,000 to 2,500 m away for daily, monthly or even quarterly averages.
- **Annual totals (abstract):** the CV of yearly totals over the ten points was 3.06 %, so each point can replace another for annual totals.

## Dependence

- None recorded. Own gauge observations, October 1993 to October 1994.

## Bearing (2026-11-04)

- **weather.site_rain_cv and weather.site_rain_miss (Agrarium):** daily rain differs among gauges within 1,000 ha with a CV from 2.2 to 169.3 %, unrelated to distance at 1 to 2.5 km. Evidence: a one-year gauge comparison.
- **Limits:** subtropical Brazil with convective rain; not Penedès. Annual totals agree well, so only daily and short-period use is affected.
