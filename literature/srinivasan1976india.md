# Srinivasan & Jeyarajan 1976: grape downy mildew in India, foliar and fruit infection

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- Downy mildew on Anab-e-Shahi grapevine in Tamil Nadu, scored by leaf colour class; red and green-with-orange leaves had no symptoms (l. 113).
- Jade-green leaves were the youngest to show symptoms, at 24 days old (l. 114); agathia-green reached grade 3 soonest, 11 days after symptoms appeared (l. 120).
- Incubation: 18 days in red leaves, about 10 days in older leaves (l. 177, 375).
- Stomata per mm2 rose from 143 to 227 with leaf age (l. 215); inoculated flowers and mustard-sized berries dropped, pea-sized berries lost 53.1 per cent (l. 303).
- Table 1 disease percentages reproduce only with the diseased-leaf denominator (l. 59-63).

## Bearing (2026-10-25)

Re-read for leaf age, the one source found with leaf age in days (colour classes tracked every
4 days on 25 shoots of Anab-e-Shahi, Tamil Nadu). All five inoculated leaves of the youngest
classes (red, at least 14 d; green-with-orange, 18 d) were infected, with incubation 18 and 14
days against 10 on older leaves; the stomatal pore was wider than the zoospore germ tube at
every age. In the field the two youngest classes showed no symptoms (confounded with their longer incubation); Table 1 prints 49.9-63.5 % diseased for the classes of 24-65 days, a column whose denominator the reading agent could not reconcile. Flowers 82.9 %,
2 mm berries 79.6 %, 6.5 mm berries 49.8 % and 12.5 mm berries 0 % infected after spray
inoculation. A warm climate and a table grape.
