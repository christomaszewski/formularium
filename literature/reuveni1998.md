# Reuveni 1998: older grape leaves are less susceptible to downy mildew, and have more enzyme

Susceptibility of leaf discs and attached leaves by position on the shoot, with peroxidase and
beta-1,3-glucanase activity. Single author, Golan Research Institute, University of Haifa.

## What it holds

- **Material** (l. 148-158): leaves with midveins over 5 cm from healthy untreated field plants;
  discs of 12 mm, ten per leaf, from leaves counted from the shoot tip: top (3 + 4, "young"), middle
  (15 + 16), bottom (29 + 30, "old"). The authors take shoot position as leaf age (l. 156). In the
  second enzyme experiment the positions were 14 + 15 and 28 + 29 (l. 270-271).
- **Inoculation** (l. 177-211): 5 ul of 2e4 sporangia per ml on a disc, 19 °C for 24 h dark then
  24 °C with a 16 h day; severity scale 0-4 (4: over 50 % of the disc infected and sporulating),
  sporangia counted per cm2. Attached greenhouse leaves sprayed with 2 ml of the same suspension,
  19 °C for 20 h dark, rated at 10 days.
- **Table 1** (p. 528; page image), top / middle / bottom: severity at 4 days 0.4 / 0.1 / 0.03; at 5
  days 1.2 / 1.0 / 0.3; at 7 days 2.4 / 2.3 / 1.0; sporangia per cm2 of disc 13,874 / 8,547 /
  4,870. Significance letters as printed: top differs from the others at day 4; at days 5 and 7 the
  top and middle do not differ and the bottom does; in sporangia the top differs from the middle
  and bottom, which do not differ from each other; the test is not checked.
- **Field** (Figs 1-2, l. 281-299, 397-401): 1993, 19 July, two plots, Cabernet Sauvignon,
  Chardonnay and Sauvignon Blanc; 1995, 30 leaves a shoot top to bottom (Cabernet Sauvignon).
  No symptoms on leaves from number 12 down in 1993 and from number 27 in 1995; disease was less
  severe in 1993 than in 1995. Values are only in the plots. The Fig. 1 caption (three vineyards,
  20 shoots) conflicts with the methods.
- **Greenhouse** (Fig. 3, l. 388): all 14-17 leaves on six plants, twice; severity near 3.5-4 for
  leaves 1-6 falling to near 0 by leaf 18 (read from the figure, approximate).
- **Enzymes** (Tables 2-3): peroxidase 10 to 30 times (soluble) and two to seven times (ionically
  bound) higher in old than young leaves; beta-1,3-glucanase 87 % (soluble) and 65 % (bound)
  higher (32.5 to 60.9 and 7.5 to 12.4). Table 3's unit header (umol glucose per g per 10 min)
  and footnote (milliunits per g) disagree. The authors propose both enzymes as markers.
- **Cited:** powdery mildew colonies fewer and smaller on older grape leaves (Doster & Schnathorst
  1985); the same leaf-age pattern in other hosts.
- **Not printed:** infection percentage, an age in days, weather, correlation coefficients, the
  unpublished data on per-protein activity.

## Dependence

- No model; the disc method is adapted from Reuveni et al. 1986 (tobacco blue mould) and the
  laminarin assay from Abeles et al. 1971. Cited by `taylor2018`.

## Bearing (2026-10-19)

- **Age resistance, by position.** Bottom against top: severity 0.42 at 7 days and sporangia 0.35
  (1.0/2.4 and 4,870/13,874; own arithmetic). The middle leaves, 15-16 positions from the tip, were
  as susceptible as the top (severity 0.96 of the top at 7 days) with sporangia 0.62 of the top.
  Severity thus falls mainly between the middle and the bottom leaves, sporangia already at the
  middle. It is a position, one cultivar, one lab method, not days. `schumacher2024` gives growth-stage curves
  for clusters and leaf discs.
- **Field cut-off:** symptoms stop at leaf 12 in a mild year and leaf 27 in a severe one, so
  the age at which leaves escape infection moves with epidemic pressure (two years, sites not named).
- **Autumn.** Nothing on inoculum. The oldest, most resistant leaves are those that carry oospore
  mosaic symptoms in `taylor2018` (old leaves where infection is limited by veins), a connection
  that neither paper tests.
