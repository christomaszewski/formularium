# Bleyer, Bleyer & Schumacher 2023: the last spray before harvest on the upper canopy only

Held since 2026-10-07 (its reference list gave the catalogue the UC Davis index's authors);
this is its note. Field trials, no model.

## What it holds

- **Question:** whether the last treatment before harvest (LTBH, at BBCH 77-79) against
  powdery and downy mildew can be sprayed on the upper half of the canopy only, leaving
  the fruit zone, once the berries' ontogenetic resistance has set in (l. 22-29, 63).
- **Trials:** 19 field trials 2009-2017 (l. 205): powdery mildew on Cabernet Dorsa at LVWO
  Weinsberg in 2009, 2010, 2012, 2013, 2014 and 2016, inoculated from potted plants (l.
  91-104); downy mildew on Riesling at Weinsberg in the same years, inoculated at 25,000
  sporangia/ml (l. 107-108, 120-123, 155-156); a copper trial on Chasselas at WBI Freiburg
  2015-2017 (l. 175-179, 188-197); practical trials on Pinot gris at an Ihringen winery
  2014-2017 (l. 145-146). Four randomised repetitions, EPPO standards, 4 × 100 leaves or
  clusters rated at BBCH 81-85 (l. 135-143); ANOVA and Student-Newman-Keuls at p = 0.05
  (l. 146-148).
- **Spray timing:** powdery mildew by VitiMeteo-OiDiag (Kast & Bleyer 2014; l. 118-119),
  downy mildew by VitiMeteo-Plasmopara (Bleyer et al. 2008; l. 126-127); the models' rules
  are not printed, and the risk index used to plan intervals cites Dubuis et al. 2019
  (l. 101-104). Application rates scaled from 1× at BBCH 16 to 3.5× at BBCH 73 (l. 116).
- **Results:** powdery mildew leaf severity in 2016 was 19.9 ± 14.0 % after the upper-canopy
  LTBH against 4.6 ± 0.8 % for the whole canopy, with 86.7 % in the untreated control
  (Table 1, l. 184-187, 213-215); fruit incidence differed only in 2012 (50 % against
  95.8 %, l. 189-190). Downy mildew: the untreated control had 93.5 % leaf incidence in
  2009 (l. 238); fruit severity in 2016 was 13.3 % (whole) against 16.2 % (upper), not
  significant (l. 252-253); in the practical trials only 2017 differed, by 1.8 and 1.0 %
  of fruit severity, the whole-canopy treatment the worse (l. 265-268). Over all, one of
  eight years showed more fruit disease (abstract, l. 36), though the discussion says
  fruit severity rose "in three out of nine years (data not shown)" when the last two
  sprays were restricted (l. 296): a printed inconsistency. Copper alone at the LTBH beat
  penconazole against powdery mildew in two of three years (l. 288).
- **Cited biology:** clusters are most susceptible to powdery mildew about three weeks
  around bloom (Ficke et al. 2002) and almost resistant by BBCH 77 (Gadoury et al. 2003;
  Kast & Stark-Urnau 2000); to downy mildew from BBCH 53, falling a few weeks after bloom
  (Kennelly et al. 2005) (l. 74-82); the first full leaf under the apex reached 90 %
  severity after inoculation, the next two about 80 and 50 % (Steinmetz et al. 2012, l.
  88-90). Savings: 24,000 kg of fungicide and 365,000 EUR a year over about 25,000 ha in
  south-west Germany (l. 400-404).

## Dependence

- **Its own data,** timed by two VitiMeteo models (l. 118-119, 126-127): the schedules
  before the LTBH are model output, the disease scores are observations. Nothing fitted.
- **The catalogue's Gubler record:** the reference list prints Gubler, W. D., Rademacher,

## Bearing (2026-10-12)

- Nothing for the engine's models. For a truth: untreated-control severities at Weinsberg
  (powdery 86.7 % leaves in 2016; downy 93.5 % leaf incidence in 2009) and the finding that
  the fruit zone needs no late spray in most years, which bears on how a spray policy
  values late treatments.
