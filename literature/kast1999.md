# Kast & Stark-Urnau 1999: sporangia on potted vines stay infective for 9 days

Open access (Vitis, JKI's OJS), fetched 2026-10-12. The research note behind VitiMeteo's
longer sporangia survival (`viret2005` l. 145-146, 181-182; `bleyer2008` l. 156, "adjusted
to Kast 1999"), and the second paper, after Kast & Walter 1993 (`kast1993`), to say that
infective sporangia outlive the Bläser-based survival rules.

## What it holds

- **Why:** spore-trap work (Kast & Walter 1994, Kast 1997, Kast & Borowka 1998) often
  matched survival rates computed from Bläser 1978, but sometimes caught viable sporangia
  after a couple of dry days when the models said none should be left (l. 47-57).
  Most risk predictions assume that 24 h at 40 % RH and 20 °C kill all sporangia (l. 44-46).
- **Method:** 20 potted Trollinger cuttings inoculated at 40,000 sporangia/ml, 24 h wet in
  the dark at 22 °C, 7 d incubation at 20 °C and 30 % RH, sporulation induced at saturation
  for 24 h; then the sporulating plants kept at 22 °C and 26-34 % RH (a second set at
  15-20 °C and 60-80 % RH) (l. 62-66, 4-9). Every 24 h sporangia were brushed off 2-3
  leaves, suspended, and used to inoculate detached leaves (drops kept wet 4 or 24 h) and
  10 leaf discs; severity read 7 d later; zoospore release watched every 20 min in
  microtitre wells (l. 10-29).
- **Zoospore release:** fresh sporangia released zoospores 30 min after suspension; 3-7 d
  old sporangia not before 180 min (l. 20-24, 31-35). Zoospores per ml from 40,000
  sporangia fell from 50,000 (fresh) to 1,000 at 7 d (l. 107-113).
- **Infectivity (Table, p. 186):** sporangia kept at 22 °C and 26-34 % RH infected 100 % of
  leaf discs through day 6 (severity 25-30 %), 20 % on day 7, 45 % on day 8, 10 % on day
  9 and 0 on day 10; at 15-20 °C and 60-80 % RH, 95-100 % through day 6 (severity 14-47
  %), then 20, 25, 80 and 0 % on days 7-10 (l. 93-102). So sporangia "were able to infect
  leaves for at least 9 d after outbreak" (l. 25-26, 114-117), with declining viability.
- **Wet 4 h against 24 h:** no difference in infection within 6 d of outbreak; at 7 d,
  100 % of leaves wet for 24 h were infected against 1 % of those wet 4 h (l. 65-71, 105).
- **Against Bläser 1978:** Bläser's 24-72 h at 20 °C and 30 or 70 % RH came from detached
  leaves in Petri dishes over salt solutions or acid; potted vines give the sporangia a
  moister regime through stomatal transpiration (l. 117-128). Zachos 1959 found 4-8 d in
  Greek vineyards for sporangia on shaded leaves below 22 °C, and only 2 d at 22-25 °C
  (l. 128-135, 105-107). "Models calculating P. viticola infections should take into
  account that sporangia might be viable for 8-9 d" (l. 109-111); survival at other
  temperatures is left for the future (l. 111-113).

## Dependence

- **Its own data:** one cultivar (Trollinger), one greenhouse, two humidity regimes, no
  dates; nothing computed or dated by a model.
- **Contradicts** the data behind the engine's survival pieces: Bläser 1978's detached-leaf
  survival, which `blaeser1979.survival` (Rossi et al. 2008 eq. 6) and
  `brischetto2020.survival` rest on. It computes no engine equation and fits nothing.
- **Calibration data for VitiMeteo,** by the VitiMeteo papers' account: the model's sporangia
  death rate was adjusted to it (`bleyer2008` l. 156; `viret2005` l. 181-182). VitiMeteo is
  not an engine model, so this links nothing in the catalogue.
- **Authors:** Kast wrote `kast1993`, `kast2006`, `kast2010` and `kast2010window`; a flag only.

## Bearing (2026-10-12)

- An independent survival observation a truth may use: sporangia infective for 9 d near
  20 °C at 26-34 % RH, declining from day 7, against the engine's Bläser-based lifetimes
  of hours to about three days. With Zachos 1959's 4-8 d (`zachos1959`) it is the second
  non-Bläser survival record; the two disagree on warm days (Zachos: 2 d at 22-25 °C; here
  22 °C throughout).
- The trail in `bleyer2008` ("adjusted to Kast 1999") is now read at its source: the
  adjustment is to this table, not to a fitted curve, since the note prints no equation.
