# Marie-Jeanne et al. 2020: spatial genetic structure of 'Ca. P. prunorum'

Genotypes of the stone-fruit phytoplasma from 6,342 samples in three French regions. Similar genotypes cluster within 50 km, and the authors infer spread by psyllid migration from wild Prunus and shelter plants.

## What it holds

- **Samples:** 6,342 from three regions (l. 110).
- **Prevalence (Table 1):** 43.3% in wild bushes, 13.0% in orchard plants and 4.8% in psyllids (l. 119-122).
- **Clustering:** similar genotypes cluster within 50 km, not between nearby wild and cultivated Prunus (abstract l. 21-22).
- **Transfer:** infected plants move between production areas (abstract l. 23).
- **Sampling years:** mostly autumn and winter 2010 and 2011, some from 2007 to 2009 (l. 443-445).

## Dependence

- Join counts and permutation tests for spatial genetic structure (l. 46-48).
- Thébaud et al. (ref. 9) on psyllid migration and orchard contamination (l. 88-90).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- **Strands t6 (regional spread) and t7 (psyllid vectors):** the 50 km clustering gives a spread scale. It is a genetic result with no rate a tool could take, and it is stone fruit, not vine.
