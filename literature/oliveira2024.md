# Oliveira et al. 2024: aerial spraying of grapevine downy mildew at 22 to 88 L/ha

A remotely piloted aircraft sprays 22 to 88 L/ha on espalier grapevines in Brazil. The 44 L/ha volume gives the best coverage in the upper and middle canopy.

## What it holds

- Two experiments on cv BRS Magna at Pato Branco, Paraná. Experiment 1 used aircraft volumes of 22, 44, 66 and 88 L/ha. Experiment 2 used 44, 66 and 88 L/ha by aircraft and a backpack at 800 L/ha (l. 29, 103, 163-164).
- 44 L/ha gave the greatest coverage and droplet density in the upper and middle strata (l. 32). The backpack gave a better droplet distribution than the aircraft (l. 33).
- Volume median diameter rose with spray volume, within an average spectrum of 266 to 339 µm (l. 215-216).
- At 45 days after the first symptoms, 44 and 66 L/ha had less downy mildew spread than 22 L/ha, which did not differ from the control (l. 237-241; Table 2).

## Dependence

- Uses the Eichhorn and Lorenz (1984) growth scale and a Box-Cox transform with linear regression.
- Cites Coimbra and Catalano (1999) on aerial drift.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t1 (fungicide protection by aircraft spray). A growers' engine could take the volume and coverage trade-off, with 44 L/ha as the trial's best and a weak lower canopy. A simulator would need disease effects by volume, and this trial covers one site and two seasons.
