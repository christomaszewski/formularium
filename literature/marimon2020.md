# Marimon et al. 2020: degree-day trigger for peach powdery mildew sprays

A 220 degree-day trigger for the first spray held peach fruit incidence close to a calendar programme and used 33% fewer sprays.

## What it holds

- Fruit incidence in commercial peach and nectarine orchards in Lleida, Catalonia, over several years (l. 274-278).
- Disease onset at 242 ± 13 accumulated degree-days (ADD) (l. 90). Symptoms stopped developing after 484 ± 42 ADD (l. 92-93).
- Operating trigger at 220 ADD, where fruit incidence was about 0.05 (l. 94-95).
- Validation: incidence 0.244 untreated (l. 711), 0.049 with the calendar programme (l. 715), and 0.073 with the 220 ADD trigger (l. 717). The trigger used 33% fewer fungicide applications (l. 106).

## Dependence

- None recorded.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Off the vine: the data are peach fruit and Podosphaera pannosa. By disease it sits with t3, but no grapevine parameter can be taken from it.
- The method is a transferable template: a degree-day trigger, then a beta regression of incidence on degree-days, wetness and vapour pressure deficit (l. 82-85). Not read in full.
