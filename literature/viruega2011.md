# Viruega et al. 2011: olive scab infection by temperature, wetness and leaf age

From the Universidad de Córdoba. Olive, not grape; read for its generalized Analytis form.

## What it holds

- Olive scab (Fusicladium oleagineum) on olive cv. Picual, in growth chambers and a shadehouse at Córdoba, Spain (l. 42, 72).
- Inoculum: 1 × 10^5 conidia/ml from naturally infected leaves (l. 15, 65).
- Temperature x wetness data: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25°C and 6 to 96 h wetness, severity scored at 70 days; 2 plants per cell, 3 runs (l. 107-117).
- Fitted model: S = 2.263e-3 × T^1.2018 × (27 – T)^0.8944 × (W – 11.9)^0.4714, severity 0-8 (l. 241).
- Tmin 0 and Tmax 27°C are fixed from conidia germination limits, not fitted (l. 236). R2 0.957 (l. 244).
- Optimum 15.5°C and minimum wetness 11.9 h (l. 248, 252). At 15.5°C, 50% of maximum severity needs 30.2 h and 100% needs 91.4 h (l. 249-251).
- With continuous wetness the optimum is 12.4°C (temperature-only fit, l. 211, 219).
- Severity falls with leaf age: S = 5.0 – 0.72 N; incubation IP = 25.0 + 5.1 N days (l. 287).
- Dose: S = 0.7836 × log(I), base not stated (l. 190).
- Form from Hau & Kranz (1990); Magarey's model was tested and not chosen (l. 163-164). No model dated the data. Development use only: one cultivar, no field test.

## Dependence

- Its own chamber and shadehouse data; no model dated them. The form is the generalized Analytis (Hau & Kranz 1990, l. 164); Magarey's and Duthie's models were tried and not chosen (l. 163). Tmin 0 and Tmax 27 °C come from conidial germination work, not this fit (l. 236).
- No author of an engine model.
