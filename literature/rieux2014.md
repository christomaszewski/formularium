# Rieux et al. 2014: dispersal kernels of wind-borne ascospores and splash-borne conidia, measured to 1 km

Trap plants out to 1,000 m on eight transects around a source of a genetically marked strain
of Mycosphaerella fijiensis (ascospores), and to 25 m around a conidia source, fitted with
four kernel families with wind anisotropy.

## What it holds

- Two experiments in a rubber plantation in southwest Cameroon (l. 104); banana traps (cv
  Grande Naine) planted June 2010 (l. 107); resistant inoculum sources (l. 153).
- Ascospore site: 27 distances, 4 to 1000 m, eight transects (l. 112); one plot every 8 m to
  104 m, then every 50 m (l. 116, 118); 511 plants in 192 plots (l. 125).
- Conidia site: 3 km SW of the ascospore site (l. 132); one plant every 2.5 m to 25 m (l.
  135, 149).
- Source: 29 of 80 plants died at D0 on the conidia site (l. 167). First lesion 17 days
  after inoculation (l. 177); counts at days 26 to 30 (l. 180).
- Ascospores: lesions in 168 of 192 plots (l. 296); DL up to 5182 (printed unit, l. 298);
  resistant lesions to 1000 m, DRL 1.8 (W1000) and 5.6 (S1000) (l. 253-254); mean resistance
  75% (l. 252).
- Conidia: lesions on 16 plants, 31% (l. 238); none beyond 12.5 m (l. 240); mean resistance
  71% (l. 258).
- Source resistance 98% (ascospores) and 96% (conidia) (l. 244).
- Four kernels fitted (l. 194); exponential power best at both sites (l. 284, 321-322; AIC
  1164.45 and 134.07, Table 1 l. 359, 374).
- Ascospores: b = 0.064, CI 0.033 to 0.077 (l. 285), read as fat-tailed; raw mean 104 m (N)
  to 613 m (S) (l. 283); D1000 = 213.83 m (l. 300); s = 200.961 m per generation^(1/2) (l.
  304); distance anisotropy towards SW, DAIC = 11.52 (l. 293); density not significant, DAIC
  = 1.03 (l. 291).
- Conidia: b = 1.86, CI 1.36 to 2.38 (l. 322), thin-tailed; raw mean 0 to 7.6 m (l. 320);
  D25 = 3.15 m (l. 331); both anisotropies significant (l. 324).
- Wind: conidia density and distance correlate with wind frequency, R = 0.91 and 0.95 (l.
  322-324); none significant for ascospores (l. 321).
- Indirect estimate of 1.2 km per generation^(1/2) from Rieux et al. 2013 (ref 67) is more
  than 6-fold the direct value (l. 591-593). A repeat run was not feasible (l. 576-578).

## Dependence

- Its own field experiment; no model produced the data.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- For Agrarium's dispersal (the truth's anisotropic exponential-power kernel) and spread
  between parcels (t6): the same kernel family fitted best for both spore types, with a fat
  tail for airborne ascospores (shape b 0.064; mean distance 104-613 m by direction) and a
  thin one for splashed conidia (b 1.86; none beyond 12.5 m), and wind direction shaping the
  conidial kernel. Banana, not grapevine: forms and magnitudes, not parameters for P.
  viticola.
