# Moyer et al. 2010b: cold spells slow grapevine powdery mildew early in the season

Controlled-environment work on Erysiphe necator and V. vinifera leaves exposed to short cold
events (above freezing, below 8 °C), with historical weather and leaf radiative cooling
used to judge how often such events occur in vineyards.

## What it holds

- Leaves held at 2 to 8 °C for 2 to 8 h before inoculation had lower infection efficiency
  and slower colony expansion (l. 30); young colonies given the same cold showed hyphal death,
  slower expansion and longer latent periods (l. 19-21, right column).
- Such early-season cold events, helped by radiative cooling of leaves at night, are common
  in most wine regions; the authors offer them as a reason for slow powdery mildew in the
  month after budbreak and the jump once nights warm (abstract).
- Cited in the introduction: Delp found germination at 17 °C only 29 % and infection 50 %
  below the 26 °C optimum (l. 36-37), while some advisory systems rate risk low below 15-19 °C.

- The same work in short is literature/moyer2010 (the 2010 Bordeaux workshop's proceedings).

## Dependence

- Its own experiments. Magarey, Gadoury, Wilcox and Seem are authors of engine models
  (Magarey 2005, Kennelly 2005 and 2007): flags; no formulation here.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- For a powdery mildew truth (Calonnec's leaf curve, Moyer's release curve): a
  temperature response that only uses the day's mean misses a cold-night penalty on
  infection and colony growth; the effect sizes are in the results, not read here. Later
  work by the same group on cold-stress-induced resistance (Moyer et al., Eur. J. Plant
  Pathol., doi 10.1007/s10658-015-0745-1) was title-screened as context and not fetched.
