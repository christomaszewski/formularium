# Hilha et al. 2026: berry skin structure, polyphenols and ripe rot in 13 genotypes

Inoculation bioassays on 13 genotypes relate berry skin structure and polyphenol content to ripe-rot severity. The results are genotype-level associations, not an infection model.

## What it holds

- Inoculation of 13 grapevine genotypes, eight of them PIWI, with a Colletotrichum acutatum-complex isolate at the EPAGRI Videira station, Santa Catarina (l. 62, 130). The methods give four seasons, 2018/2019 to 2021/2022 (l. 201-202). The abstract says 2019 to 2022 (l. 63).
- Thicker cuticles and intact epidermis in PI4407 and Bronner (l. 66). The lowest AUDPC values were in PI4407, Concord, Poloskei Muskotaly, Regent, Prior and Merlot (l. 287-288).
- Polyphenols vary by season. Sauvignon Blanc had the highest total in 2019, at 333.32 µg/g (l. 336).
- Data are available on request from the corresponding author (l. 537).

## Dependence

- Builds on the Hsieh et al. (2023) review of ripe-rot species, the Dias et al. (2022) PIWI anthracnose work, and the Hilha et al. (2024) production-cost study. All are cited.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t4 (other grape diseases: ripe rot). A growers' engine could take only the genotype-level result: some varieties with thicker cuticles or lower polyphenols have lower AUDPC. The paper has no infection model or fitted rate, so it informs breeding more than a simulator.
