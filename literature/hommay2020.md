# Hommay et al. 2020: soft scale and leafroll virus spread in one Riesling plot, 2003 to 2015

A 13-year survey of one organic Riesling plot in Alsace. Infected vines rose from 54% to 78%
for GLRaV-1 and from 14% to 26% for GVA. New infections lay next to earlier ones, mostly
along the row, and were unrelated to how many soft scales a vine carried.

## What it holds

- Plot: Riesling, Nothalten, Bas-Rhin, under organic management for over 20 years (l. 20).
  704 initial vines, tested by ELISA or RT-PCR in two periods, 2003 to 2008 and 2009 to 2014
  (l. 53).
- Infection (abstract, l. 9): GLRaV-1 from 54% to 78% of vines; GVA from 14% to 26%.
  Over 12 years the rise is 24 and 12 points (l. 78).
- Spread: vines next to an infected vine in the same row were most often infected, then
  opposite vines and second neighbours in the row (l. 56; Table 2, l. 57 to 58). Spread
  across rows came later (abstract, l. 9).
- Vector: P. corni females up to about 200 per vine (l. 46). Virus presence did not follow
  the scale density (l. 77). New infections more than 4 m from an infected vine were few
  (l. 79); wind carrying nymphs is the suggested cause.

## Dependence

- Spread model: earlier leafroll and mealybug studies (refs. 6, 8, 10, 12) for the distance
  and within-row assumption (l. 29). Spatial procedures from Peyrard et al. (ref. 49).
  No model or dataset from another group is recorded.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strands t6 (regional spread) and t7 (insect vectors). Within-vineyard spread of a
  vector-borne virus is slow and follows the row. A simulator could use a row-first
  spread kernel, with the vector density as a weak term only.
- The spread numbers are for leafroll and GVA in one plot in one region, read from
  counts, not from a fitted model. Not read in full.
