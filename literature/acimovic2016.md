# Acimovic et al. 2016: how many basal leaves removed at flowering change fruitset and yield

Pinot Noir in Michigan, 2011-2012, with 0, 4, 6, 8 or 10 basal leaves removed at full
flowering: fruitset, bunch compactness, yield and composition.

## What it holds

- Michigan Pinot Noir (clone 777 on C3309), six-year-old vineyard at Benton Harbor, 2011 and
  2012 (l. 85-89).
- Leaves removed at full flowering (EL-20) from none, four, six, eight or ten basal nodes;
  six blocks (l. 20, 127).
- Eight basal nodes is the threshold: significantly lower fruitset (l. 21-22). Fruitset at
  EL-38 in 2011: 14.0% (LR-8) vs 27.5% (control) (Table 3, l. 302).
- Eight or ten leaves reduced bunch compactness; in 2011 LR-8 and LR-10 were 37% and 47%
  lower (l. 22, 261).
- Bunch mass in LR-10 fell by 65% (2011) and 62% (2012) (l. 243). Yield per vine in LR-10
  was 4.05 kg vs 9.12 kg (2011) (Table 5, l. 424).
- Rot severity was not altered (l. 23).
- Berries per bunch at EL-38 fell 51% (2011) and 35% (2012) in LR-8 (l. 227-228).
- Total sugar (TSS) rose in LR-10 in 2011 (24.0 vs 20.8 Brix) but not in 2012 (l. 424).
- Two years of repeated defoliation: no adverse effect the following year (l. 26-27).
- Recommended: six to eight basal nodes at full flowering (l. 25).

## Dependence

- Its own field trial; no model.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- For a damage function or a Botrytis truth: losing eight or more leaves at flowering halves
  fruitset and loosens bunches (37-47 % less compact), and ten cut yield from 9.12 to 4.05
  kg a vine in 2011, without changing rot; fewer leaves had no effect. A bound on how early
  leaf loss (from disease or management) turns into yield.
