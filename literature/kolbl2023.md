# Kölbl et al. 2023: laser-induced fluorescence detects downy mildew on vines

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- Potted Riesling vines: 10 inoculated at 10^5 sporangia per ml, 10 sprayed with water, bagged overnight in wet polyethylene (l. 163-193).
- A 17-day campaign with laser-induced fluorescence and daily visual and photographic ratings; 5,760 spectra and 1,080 images (l. 256-283).
- The ChlF ratio of inoculated leaves rises from 5 dpi and separates clearly from 9 dpi (l. 402).
- The BFRR UV index first differs at 8 dpi (l. 416).
- Inoculated and healthy vines can be told apart 5 to 7 days after inoculation, against about 7 days for visible symptoms (l. 537-538).
- The authors say no early remote detection is yet possible; the system is a monitoring tool (l. 542).

## Dependence

- cited only: Kennelly et al. 2007 is cited for oil-spot symptoms at the end of the incubation period (l. 269-270); no primary-infection model, incubation table or trigger is computed, borrowed or fitted here, so this is a citation, not a dependence.

## Bearing (2026-10-10)

- A small greenhouse dataset (one cultivar, one 17-day campaign) that gives observed detection timing for downy mildew, 5 to 7 days after inoculation, which a truth could use only as a check on detectability, not on incubation or infection dates.
