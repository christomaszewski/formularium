# Molot & Gaimon 2004: copper left on detached vine leaves after 0 to 60 mm of simulated rain

A French rain-simulator study of how much copper stays on vine leaves after rain, for two copper products, with tests of rain intensity, split rain, spray-to-rain delay and dose, and two fitted curves.

**Read this first.**
- It is a conference paper of a technical institute (authors of ITV France, Nîmes, and a student of ENSAM, as printed), listed in the repository as not peer-reviewed. It is the only wash-off curve for vine leaves in this strand, and it is copper only.
- The text was recovered from a Word 97 file by scanning for printable runs, not with a Word reader; accents are intact. The reading agent did not read Figures 1 to 9. The measured points below come from the document's embedded chart workbooks, read by the main session.
- The printed "H-0.24" and "H-0.35" are H to the power -0.24 and -0.35 (superscripts flattened); the workbook labels the exponents "m=-0.24" and "m=-0.35". The unit of H is not printed (mm from context). The power law diverges below about 1 mm.
- The fits overstate what is left at 2 mm (0.83 and 0.74 against measured 0.78 and 0.62). The text says "25 to 40 %" lost at 2 mm (l. 40); the measured points give 22 % and 38 % (arithmetic, not printed).
- Detached leaves, upper face only, one deposit level; no whole-vine or canopy test. The year's field trial gave no result (no downy mildew in 2003).

## What it holds

- **Design (l. 13, 18, 23, 35):** simulated rain on detached vine leaves, varying rain depth, rain intensity, the delay between spraying and rain, and the product: Héliocuivre against Bordeaux mixture BB RSR Disperss (the copper salt of Héliocuivre is not named). Spray and rain reach the upper leaf face only; the authors cite ITV Montpellier (Vernet) for at most 15 % of the product on the lower face (l. 18, cited). Simulator after Asseline (ORSTOM type): one nozzle on an oscillating arm at 3.5 m and 0.55 bar, drop energy said to match natural rain, intensity set by cycle time and not by drop size (l. 23). Deposit aimed at 2.6 µg Cu/cm² (26 mg/m², arithmetic, not printed), taken to equal 1500 g Cu/ha, with rain depths 0 to 60 mm (l. 35).
- **Measured points (the document's embedded chart workbooks, read by the main session):** measured share of copper left after 2, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 mm: Héliocuivre 0.78, 0.66, 0.58, 0.53, 0.50, 0.48, 0.43; Bordeaux mixture (BB RSR Disperss) 0.62, 0.51, 0.49, 0.45, 0.41, 0.39, 0.36. The workbook labels the fitted exponents "m=-0.24" and "m=-0.35", so the printed "H-0.24" is H to the power -0.24; the fits overstate what is left at 2 mm (0.83 for Héliocuivre and 0.74 for Bordeaux mixture).
- **Fitted curves, form R(H) = a H^-m + b:** Héliocuivre R = 0.8502 H^-0.24 + 0.1063, correlation 0.9957 (l. 91); BB RSR Disperss R = 0.7229 H^-0.35 + 0.1693, correlation 0.9743 (l. 92). The form is taken from "Weatley" (sic; Wheatley 1973), an initial fast loss then a gradual one (l. 85, cited); the authors call the loss hyperbolic (l. 120).
- **Shape in words:** the first millimetres cause the most loss, 25 to 40 % already at 2 mm (l. 40); from 5 mm the residue falls much more slowly and levels off near 40 % of the initial dose (l. 40); the first 5 mm give most of the loss (l. 118); about 40 % remains after 50 mm (l. 119).
- **What did not matter:** rain intensity of 15, 35, 60 and 90 mm/h for 5 mm, on BB RSR at 1500 g Cu/ha and Héliocuivre at 1200 and 600 g Cu/ha (l. 55, 61; a mainly depth-only test, as drop size is fixed); 30 mm at once against 3 x 10 mm with drying between, at 35 mm/h (l. 66); a 5 mm rain 2 h or 7 d after spraying (l. 79, 81; garden sprayer to near run-off, 30 leaves per cell); the initial dose, up to 5 and 10 times the label dose (l. 47). The Bordeaux mixture lost statistically more than Héliocuivre in the intensity test (l. 61).
- **Sampling and field check:** field leaves of the same age sprayed with a backpack pneumatic sprayer varied so much that 470 leaves per treatment were needed (l. 17); with a Potter tower 10 leaves give a relative precision under 10 % (l. 17). After one natural rain of 4 mm at 5 mm/h the loss on field sensors was close enough to the simulator's to allow extrapolation, but the CV between sensors was over 50 % (l. 32), so it was not tested.
- **Renewal threshold, modelled:** 15 to 20 mm for Héliocuivre at 1.5 L/ha (about 600 g Cu) (l. 122), from dissolved copper against an EC50 of 0.1 to 0.3 mg/L; "from 15 mm" in theory (l. 106); about 25 mm at full dose (l. 106), said to agree with usual advice. Not validated, as there was no downy mildew in 2003 (l. 122).

## Dependence

- **Own experiments** (2003), following ITAB-commissioned work begun in 2001. Rain simulator after Asseline (ORSTOM type); the R = a H^-m + b form from Wheatley 1973; the 15 % lower-face share from Vernet (ITV Montpellier), cited. No engine model is computed here.

## Bearing (2026-10-25)

- **Wash-off per mm of rain (E6; M4):** a measurement on detached vine leaves, upper face, two copper products. It supports a saturating loss with cumulative depth, not a constant fraction per mm: the share left falls from 0.78 and 0.62 at 2 mm to 0.43 and 0.36 at 50 mm (Héliocuivre and Bordeaux mixture). Intensity, splitting, delay and dose had no effect here, so one state (millimetres since the spray) is enough for copper. Not shown: depths below 2 mm (the fit diverges), rain above 60 mm, any product but these two, a lower face or a canopy.
- **Dose-efficacy:** nothing measured. The 15 to 20 mm and 25 mm renewal thresholds are a model on dissolved copper against an EC50, not a trial.
- **Decay with time and growth dilution:** not addressed. The delay test (2 h against 7 d) shows only that drying time did not change wash-off.
- **Deposit variability:** CV over 50 % between field sensors after backpack pneumatic spraying, and 470 leaves needed, blamed on the sprayer; not a VSP airblast canopy. Kind of result: laboratory measurement with the authors' fits, an institute paper not peer-reviewed.
