# Yuen & Hughes 2002: Bayesian analysis of plant disease prediction

From the Swedish University of Agricultural Sciences and the University of Edinburgh. A letter to the editor.

## What it holds

- A short methods letter, no new data. It shows how sensitivity and specificity turn a forecast into a change in the odds of disease.
- Likelihood ratios: LR+ = sensitivity / (1 - specificity), LR- = (1 - sensitivity) / specificity (l. 114-125). Posterior odds = prior odds x LR (l. 133).
- Eyespot of wheat (Jones 1994, 58 sites, England, 1980s): table 28/13/10/7 gives sensitivity 0.68 and specificity 0.41 (l. 73, 95-96, 106). LR+ 1.15, LR- 0.78 (l. 118, 126).
- Prior 0.16 (Cook et al. 1991, 633 crops, 1985-89): odds 0.19 become 0.23 after a positive and 0.15 after a negative forecast (l. 191-195).
- Sclerotinia stem rot of oilseed rape, Sweden: risk-points thresholds 35, 40, 50 give sensitivity/specificity 0.90/0.77, 0.77/0.84, 0.35/0.95 (l. 178-180). The ratios come from Twengström et al. 1998.
- Priors are a 20-year mean (1979-98) and a worst year, from a personal communication (l. 214-217): 0.16 and 0.61 for Uppland.
- The authors say the predictor's development data overlap the priors' data (l. 301).
- A predictor with 0.9 sensitivity and specificity lifts a prior of 0.1 only to 0.5 (l. 270-273).
- For the evaluator, not the truth; Hughes is also author of the scoring work.

## Dependence

- Worked examples on published data (Jones 1994; Twengström et al. 1998); likelihood ratios and predictive values. A reference-role method.

## Bearing (2026-10-08)

- Clear and a method: a forecast that cannot move the probability enough does not change a decision, the yardstick for Agrarium's evaluation of warnings.
