# Fenu and Malloci 2021: review of 46 machine-learning disease-forecasting works, 2010 to 2020 (mixed crops, review)

Reviews 46 works that forecast plant disease at a pre-symptomatic or early stage with AI, machine learning or deep learning, by data source, pre-processing and technique. No record: a review. Grape is a handful of rows.

**Read this first.** A review of other studies' scores, which the authors say cannot be compared. The paper prints both "29 papers, 63%" and other counts and shares for the weather and image groups that do not match each other (l. 29); the shares are garbled and not used here. The grape rows give inputs and no metric in the parts read.

## What it holds

- **Scope** (l. 421): "Forty-six research works were identified and reviewed", 2010 to 2020. Weather data were used in 29 papers (l. 29); the rest used images or a mix.
- **Comparability** (l. 407): high heterogeneity of approaches, datasets, parameters and metrics makes a systematic comparison of performance "unreliable".
- **Validation** (l. 419): "a restricted number of studies tested their solutions in different datasets", and there is "a shortage of model validation in real-world scenarios". The authors tie both to the lack of open data (l. 421).
- **Techniques** (l. 419): support vector machines, then neural networks, then random forests were the most used.
- **Sources** (l. 410): "the exclusive use of a single data source is not sufficient" to capture a disease's variability in the field. An example cited from a work the review summarises: adding remote sensing to weather raised overall accuracy from 69% to 78% (l. 38).
- **Grape rows** (l. 98-101, Table): a downy mildew, powdery mildew and anthracnose study with inputs that include pressure, visibility and wind as well as temperature, humidity and rain (metric N/A in the table); the other grape rows are a hidden Markov model study and, per the reading agent, reference numbers 77 and 71, which it matched to Chen et al. 2020 and Lu et al. 2020 (held here as chen2020ml and lu2020; not matched against the reference list for this note).

## Bearing (2026-10-27)

- **D58 (variable search):** gives no skill ceiling for hourly temperature, humidity and rain; its use is as a caution that scores in this literature are not comparable and that few models were tried on a second data set.
- **D60 / synthetic data:** no heading for simulated or augmented training sets in the parts read; the review does not show that any of the 46 works trained on synthetic data. A gap that supports the strand's finding that no paper trains on simulated epidemics and scores on real outbreaks.
- **Not shown:** per-study validation design, labels or scores.
