# Pereyra et al. 2023: why a high-vigour zone of a Tannat vineyard grows more

An 8-year Uruguayan study of two NDVI-defined vigour zones of one Tannat vineyard, relating soil and climate differences to vine growth, yield and berry composition.

**Read this first.**
- The results are in two-column text and the high-vigour (HV) and low-vigour (LV) numbers interleave at l. 456 to 460. The ranges below are as the reading agent separated them.
- It gives no share of variance explained by soil, and no spatial statistics.

## What it holds

- **Design (l. 30):** an 8-year experiment in two zones of a vineyard of about 1 ha (1.1 ha, Tannat, planted 1998, years 2014-2021, l. 127), chosen by NDVI as high vigour (HV) or low vigour (LV).
- **Soil (l. 35):** the HV zone had deeper soils, higher soil water and nitrogen, higher CEC and montmorillonite/illite ratio than the LV zone.
- **Plant (l. 35):** more extended roots, higher vegetative growth and higher yield in HV.
- **Pruning weight (l. 456):** HV 0.4 to 0.7 kg/vine; LV 0.1 to 0.4 kg/vine; the ratio of the means is about 2 (arithmetic, not printed).
- **Variability within the plot (l. 307):** a CV of 15 % for the plot in a soil water quantity (a figure in mm; the line before it is in the other column, so the variable is not certain).
- **Climate (abstract):** drier and warmer vintages increased the difference in growth and yield between the zones.

## Dependence

- Own field data; no model computed. None recorded otherwise.

## Bearing (2026-11-05)

- **fields.vigour_hollow_weight (Agrarium):** qualitative only. The high-vigour zone sits on deeper soil with more water and nitrogen (l. 35), which supports vigour tied to soil depth. No share of variance is printed, so no weight can be set from this paper.
- **fields.vigour_sd (Agrarium):** the pruning weight contrast of about two between zones (l. 456) is a between-zone ratio, not a within-zone SD. Field measurement, one vineyard.
