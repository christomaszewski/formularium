# Zanzotti & Giovannini 2022: how much copper per square metre of leaf protects vine leaf discs, and what a short wetting does

A research foundation's technical article on a trade portal: leaf-disc tests of copper efficacy against deposit per leaf area (1 to 10 mg Cu/m²) and the effect of wetting between spray and infection.

**Read this first.**
- It is a technical article on a wine-trade portal by a research foundation, not a journal paper. The year is not printed (the text cites 2022 works); the id year is an inference. It is not manufacturer material.
- The efficacy figures it prints are in prose; the charts (Figures 4 to 7) were not read. The tests are on leaf discs of potted Pinot grigio sprayed on the lower face with a Potter tower, under controlled conditions. There is no rain in them: "wetting" is deionised water put on a dry deposit.
- The per-hectare conversions (5 and 10 mg Cu/m² after 200 and 400 g Cu/ha), the poorly exposed foliage note and the season accumulation are cited from other works, not measured here.

## What it holds

- **Method (l. 63):** copper sprayed on the lower (abaxial) face of 19 mm leaf discs of potted Pinot grigio with a Potter tower; 30 min of drying before wetting or inoculation.
- **Dose against efficacy (own; l. 109, 110, 111, 120):** deposits above 2.5 mg Cu/m² of leaf gave a strong protection against the pathogen, with efficacy above 91.5 ± 1.2 % for Bordeaux mixture, copper oxychloride and copper hydroxide (l. 109, 110). Beyond 7.5 mg/m² more copper did not raise efficacy significantly (l. 111). At 1 mg/m² efficacy was 67.7 %, judged insufficient (l. 120; Bordeaux mixture and oxychloride).
- **Wetting, not rain (own; l. 143, 148):** wetting for under 24 h between treatment and infection did not impair protection at the high doses of 7.5 and 10 mg/m² (Bordeaux mixture) (l. 143); 2 mL of deionised water on the dried deposit. At 2.5 mg/m² efficacy fell by up to 17 % after wetting of 0.5 to 2 h, with no loss from 3 h (l. 148).
- **Field deposit per leaf area (cited):** about 5 and 10 mg Cu/m² mean leaf cover after 200 and 400 g Cu/ha with an ordinary airblast sprayer (l. 37, 38; Cabus et al. 2017), for a leaf wall area of about 12,000 m²/ha (l. 76). On a VSP trellis the deposit inside the canopy differs by position (l. 36). Values under 2.5 mg/m² occur on poorly exposed foliage even after 500 g Cu/ha (l. 210, 211; Mescalchin et al. 2011). Copper accumulates over a season above 10 mg/m² (l. 219; Mian et al. 2021).
- **Rain, no measurement (l. 31):** the authors list wash-off by 20 to 30 mm of rain as a known disturbance that reduces low-dose copper, without data.

## Dependence

- **Own** leaf-disc experiments (Fondazione Edmund Mach, San Michele; Potter tower). The mg/m² to g/ha conversion and the 5 to 10 mg/m² range are from Cabus et al. 2017 (full text not obtained); canopy variability from Mescalchin et al. 2011; accumulation from Mian et al. 2021. None recorded of the engine's models.

## Bearing (2026-10-25)

- **Dose-efficacy (M4):** the clearest copper dose curve on vine leaf tissue in this strand: 67.7 % at 1 mg/m², at least 91.5 % at 2.5 mg/m², no gain beyond 7.5 mg/m². It is per square metre of leaf, so a spray model needs the deposit per leaf area (the cited 12,000 m²/ha and 5 to 10 mg/m² after 200 to 400 g/ha give the link). Only two points lie below the plateau, so the rise between 1 and 2.5 mg/m² is not shown. Controlled leaf-disc measurement, potted Pinot grigio, lower face, unweathered.
- **Wash-off, decay, growth dilution:** no rain measurement; short wetting can cost up to 17 % at 2.5 mg/m² and nothing at 7.5 mg/m² and above, a hint that thin deposits are the fragile ones.
- **Deposit variability:** only statements (positions differ; under 2.5 mg/m² on poorly exposed foliage after 500 g Cu/ha), cited, with no CV. Kind of result: own leaf-disc measurement plus cited field levels.
