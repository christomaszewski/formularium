# Amir 2016: leaf wetness by an adaptive neuro-fuzzy system (MSc thesis)

An MSc thesis at Auckland University of Technology, on Plant & Food Research's station data.

## What it holds

- MCIS thesis (Auckland University of Technology, 2016): seven hourly wetness models scored against Campbell sensor data, New Zealand vineyards.
- Data: Plant & Food Research stations, 2012. Dataset A: five stations, January to February, N = 7128 h (l. 2138). Dataset B: three stations, four months, N = 8679 h (l. 2167).
- Wind came from NIWA stations 0.8 to 29.1 km away (l. 1833). Wet = CS reading >50 (l. 1866).
- Overall hourly accuracy, A / B: ANFIS 86.04 / 90.06, ANN 82.22 / 86.31, CART 84.26 / 82.84, SWEB 81.51 / 84.08, P-M 80.79 / 83.53, FLS 71.76 / 79.37, RH >= 90% 75.82 / 70.80 percent (l. 2845, l. 2973).
- ANFIS mean error -0.06 and -0.05 h/day, MAE 0.14 and 0.10 h/day (l. 2788, l. 2817, l. 2994).
- Data called imbalanced (l. 2831); no dry share printed (Table 17: 3 to 4 wet h/day, l. 2920).
- ANFIS and ANN were fitted to the station data (ten-fold cross validation, l. 3152); FLS starts from Kim's membership functions (l. 3015) but the author also speaks of training it (l. 2805); CART keeps Gleason's Ames, Iowa fit (l. 2871); SWEB uses defaults from one US vineyard (l. 2773).
- Claim of ANFIS best at four of five stations (l. 3153) does not match Table 15 (two).
- Sensor test, 36 days (l. 1992): Decagon dried 7.5 min after real leaves (l. 1919).
- Dependence: reference is sensor output; no model dated the data.

## Dependence

- It compares ANFIS and an ANN, fitted to the same station data they are scored on (ten-fold cross-validation), with RH ≥ 90%, CART, fuzzy logic, Penman-Monteith and SWEB at their published parameters. "Wet" is a Campbell 237 reading above 50.
- No author of an engine model.

## Bearing (2026-10-08)

- Clear; a comparison, not a formulation to run. It ranks the RH ≥ 90% rule, the engine's stand-in, lowest of the seven; the thesis's own tables do not all agree with its text.
