# Biglia et al. 2022: UAV spraying in a vineyard, flight mode and nozzle

A field trial of a UAV sprayer in a Barbera vineyard. Band flight put more spray on the canopy and less on the ground than broadcast flight.

## What it holds

- **Design:** 12 settings of band or broadcast flight, conventional or air-inclusion nozzles, and cruise speeds of 1 and 3 m/s. Linear mixed models were used (l. 103-108).
- **Band against broadcast:** canopy deposit rose from 0.052 to 0.161 µL/cm² (+309%). Ground loss fell from 0.544 to 0.246 µL/cm² (−54%) (l. 109-110).
- **Airblast comparison:** a conventional airblast sprayer at a low rate gave more canopy coverage and less ground loss than the best UAV set-up (l. 110-112).
- **Figures:** deposit by nozzle type (Fig. 4, l. 534-535), canopy deposit by nozzle (Fig. 5, l. 575-578) and over-spray by flight mode (Fig. 7, l. 606-608).

## Dependence

- Grella et al. 2020a sets the sampling layout (l. 324-325). Ground losses from earlier work are cited in the introduction (l. 143-150).
- Linear mixed models for the statistics (l. 107-108).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- **Strand t1 (spray deposition and loss):** the band-to-broadcast ratio and the ground-loss fraction are inputs a simulator could take for a UAV spray term. They come from one vineyard, and the trial year is not printed in the parts read.
