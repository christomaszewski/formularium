# Fonseca et al. 2016: copper left on citrus seedling leaves after 20 mm of simulated rain

An analogue crop: how much copper stays on citrus seedling leaves after one simulated rain, by copper formulation.

**Read this first.** **Analogue: citrus seedlings, not vines**, at Jaboticabal in São Paulo. One rain only (20 mm in 10 min, applied 90 min after spraying), so there is no curve against depth. The copper is measured in leaf washings (mg/L). The percentages retained for each product are in Table 3, which was not read; only the abstract's "more than 80 %" for suspension-concentrate formulations is quoted.

## What it holds

- **Design (l. 89):** simulated rain of 20 mm in 10 minutes, applied 90 minutes after spraying copper fungicides on citrus seedlings. That is 120 mm/h (arithmetic, not printed).
- **Retention (l. 20):** suspension-concentrate (SC) copper formulations kept more than 80 % of the copper on the leaves after the simulated rain.
- **Copper retained (l. 274):** after the 20 mm, from zero (no copper treatment) up to 14.5 mg/L in leaf washings for copper oxychloride SC; the other formulations were lower (Table 3 not read).
- **Authors' remark (l. 271):** rain reduces the content of deposited solution already in the first millimetres of rain.

## Dependence

- **Own** experiment (UNESP Jaboticabal). It cites Cabras et al. 2001 and Hunsche et al. 2007 as background. None recorded of the engine's models.

## Bearing (2026-10-25)

- **Wash-off per mm (E6; M4):** an analogue only. One 20 mm rain at 120 mm/h, 90 min after spraying, left more than 80 % of the copper of SC formulations on citrus seedlings. That is far more retention than the vine-leaf results for Héliocuivre and Bordeaux mixture (`molot2004`), so formulation, leaf surface and rain differ; it supports the point that formulation matters but gives no vine value.
- **Decay, dilution, dose-efficacy, deposit variability:** not addressed. Kind of result: measurement on citrus seedlings (analogue crop).
