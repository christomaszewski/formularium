# Read et al. 2019: an LSTM pretrained on an uncalibrated lake model, tested on sparse and out-of-range real profiles (lakes)

The process-guided deep learning (PGDL) paper: an LSTM with an energy-conservation penalty, pretrained on the output of the General Lake Model (GLM) and then trained on real lake temperature profiles, against a real-only LSTM (DL) and a calibrated GLM (PB). Four experiments: sample size, prediction outside the training range, 68 lakes, and reduced pretraining data. No record: a method with measured results.

**Read this first.** Lakes, not plants. The real check is on the variable the simulator also outputs. PGDL joins three things (LSTM, energy loss, pretraining) and the authors did not test them apart in this paper; they judge pretraining responsible for most of the gain (l. 779). Several RMSEs by profile count are only in Figure 2 and not in the text. The `jia2021` paper isolates the pretraining effect with the loss held fixed.

## What it holds

- **Simulator** (l. 353, 357, 406): the General Lake Model, uncalibrated, never adjusted to the observations; every daily output at every depth, test periods excluded. It is called PB0 ("0" dates used for calibration).
- **Real data** (l. 436, 33): Lake Mendota and Sparkling Lake, Wisconsin, 2009-2017, 35,242 observations from an automated buoy (the earlier design had 13,158 observations, 1980-2014); then a set of 68 lakes. Counts of synthetic samples are not printed in the lines read.
- **Validation design** (l. 424, 437, 531, 585): Experiment 1, random test dates (540 test dates at Lake Mendota) with 980, 500, 100, 50, 10 or 2 training dates (46 to 22,776 observations); Experiment 2, out-of-range tests (the warmest years, or summer, withheld); Experiment 3, 68 lakes (l. 466); Experiment 4, reduced pretraining data (l. 644). Not a held-out site in Experiments 1 and 2.
- **Lake Mendota, test RMSE in degC** (l. 571, 576-579): with 980 training dates PGDL 0.92, DL 1.07, calibrated PB 1.56. The real-only DL beats PB at 500 profiles or more (1.28 against 1.49) and loses at 100 or fewer (1.73 against 1.64). With 2 profiles PGDL scores 1.77 against 3.03 for PB. PGDL was better than DL and PB at every training size (l. 540); DL degraded most as data shrank (l. 562).
- **Out-of-range tests** (l. 585, 587, 596-602, 652, 654, 680): PGDL usually beat DL and PB outside the training bounds, with exceptions. The calibrated PB beat PGDL at Sparkling Lake when the three warmest years were withheld (1.37 against 1.52). For a summer test trained on colder seasons, pretraining with summers in it scored 1.19 and without 1.98, against 1.91 for the calibrated PB. For a warm-year test, extended against limited pretraining data scored 0.99 against 1.16.
- **68 lakes** (l. 34, 627, 629, 635, 641): median test RMSE PGDL 1.65, DL 1.78, calibrated PB 2.03 degC; ranges across lakes PGDL 0.91-2.66, DL 0.97-2.95, PB 1.46-3.63. The uncalibrated pretrainer scored a median 2.78, so PGDL beat its own teacher by a median 1.1 degC (arithmetic, not printed): 11 lakes by more than 2 degC, 55 by less, 2 not at all. With 10 training dates per lake PGDL matched or beat 50 of the 68 calibrated PB models and beat all DL models.
- **What the pretraining data must contain** (Experiment 4, l. 646, 649, 651, 685, 773): wider pretraining data helped most when real data were sparse or the regime shifted: 1.77 against 2.67 at 2 profiles (extended against limited). With plentiful real data, with training and test periods alike, the extent of pretraining mattered little; the benefit shrinks as real data grow.
- **Real-only baseline**: DL, in every experiment above. The energy loss and the pretraining are not separated (l. 779).
- **Authors' claims** (l. 717, 734, 754, 784): the greatest gains came when observations were scarce; pretraining acts as a "surrogate for real observations"; imperfections of the source model can be reduced by final training on observations (claimed, not tested as bias).

## Dependence

- Pretrained on output of the General Lake Model, configured with observable lake parameters. The architecture and the energy-conservation loss come from the same group's earlier work (Jia et al. 2019, cited, not read). Authors in common with `jia2021`, `jia2020` and `willard2022` (a flag).

## Bearing (2026-10-27)

- **The most informative failure case in the strand for D45 and D60:** a pretrainer that lacks the test regime loses to a calibrated process model (summer test, 1.98 against 1.91, l. 654; warm years at Sparkling Lake, 1.52 against 1.37, l. 587). A student fitted on a simulator is kin to it (D45) and inherits its coverage as well as its bias, so synthetic outcomes can test robustness but cannot establish a feature (D60).
- **Sample-size curves with a real-only arm in the same experiment** are what a placement or pretraining pilot would need to report: PGDL, DL and PB at 2 to 980 dates. The cross-over of DL and PB at about 100 to 500 profiles (l. 576-577) is a number to expect in a plant study too, but this is lakes.
- **Not shown:** any plant or disease, a hidden simulator variable judged by real data, or the separate effect of the energy loss.
