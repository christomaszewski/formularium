# Cappelletti et al. 2016: protein inducer and grapevine phyllosphere

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- Nutrient broth (NB), a protein derivative, reduced downy mildew symptoms and induced defence genes in in vitro and greenhouse grapevines (l. 35).
- In vitro, disease severity was 1.2 +/- 0.9% with NB against 19.1 +/- 5.6% with water (l. 299, 301).
- In two greenhouse experiments, NB gave a 60.0 +/- 1.3% reduction against 34.6 +/- 3.5% for a laminarin product (l. 325, 329).
- Disease was scored visually at 7 days after inoculation, as % leaf area sporulating, following EPPO 2001 (l. 197).
- Inoculum was 4 x 10^4 sporangia/mL in vitro and 2 x 10^4 in the greenhouse (l. 191, 247).
- NB changed phyllosphere bacterial and fungal communities and raised culturable bacteria (l. 39-41).
- No temperature, humidity or wetness is reported for the greenhouse runs (l. 253).

## Dependence

- none. The paper runs no epidemiological model: disease is a visual % sporulating area at 7 days (l. 197), and it has no incubation, infection, sporulation-temperature, wetness or phenology model. The authors cite Perazzolli et al. 2008 and Nesler et al. 2015 only for the induction time (l. 183-185). Shared authors: none with the engine models.

## Bearing (2026-10-10)

- Little for the truth or the engine: a controlled lab and greenhouse efficacy result for a protein inducer, with no weather or epidemic data, useful only as a citable efficacy value for a resistance-inducer parameter.
