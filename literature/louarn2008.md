# Louarn et al. 2008: a statistical 3D reconstruction of the vine canopy

The paper prints 2008 (Annals of Botany 101(8):1167-1184); the search's shortlist called it
2007. A static model: shoots are drawn from fitted distributions of their shape and the
leaves are placed on them, to give the canopy structure and the light it intercepts. There is
no heat sum and no growth in time.

## What it holds

- **Site and systems** (l. 124-143): Grenache N and Syrah on 110R, planted 1989 at
  Châteauneuf-de-Gadagne, Côtes du Rhône (the print gives 43.55 N, 4.56 E), rows 90° from
  north; four spur-pruned systems for each cultivar (GOB, BFC, VSP-1W, VSP-2W), so eight
  pairs; four rows of 12 vines a plot, 24 plants measured; shoots thinned at E-L 12 and
  bunches at E-L 27 to one a shoot.
- **Digitising** (l. 345-346): 200 shoots per pair (20-24 vines) in the winters of 2003 and
  2004; leaf orientation on one full vine a pair in July 2004 (E-L 33), 600-1000 leaves
  (l. 368-370); 15 shoots a pair for mean attributes (l. 428).
- **Allometries** (Eqs 3-4, Table 3, l. 534-545; units not printed, minus signs lost in the
  text layer and restored from the image): internode length Lin = (Ls/n)(a + b n). Primary
  axis: Grenache a 65, b -243.8 (r² 0.96); Syrah a 88.4, b -394 (r² 0.95). Branch side
  Lb = a' + b' nII: Grenache a' 43.2, b' -72.9 (r² 0.90); Syrah a' 38.6, b' -52.4 (r² 0.89).
  Fitted to weekly counts from budburst to setting on 60 shoots a genotype (l. 387).
- **Shoot shape** (Table 2): a multivariate normal of elevation, bending angle, curvature
  point and length for each pair. Syrah primary axes are longer (l. 584).
- **Leaf elevation** (Table 4, l. 596): mean (SD) 33.3 (19.7) to 50.2 (19.4)°; Grenache 41.7,
  40.6, 36.5 and 33.3 and Syrah 50.2, 49.9, 44.4 and 41.6 for GOB, BFC, VSP-1W and VSP-2W.
  Leaves are more erect on trellised systems.
- **Canopy** (l. 566, 631): the maximal vertical leaf area density runs from 4.2 m²/m³ (BFC
  Syrah) to 6.7 m²/m³ (VSP-2W Syrah); light interception efficiency 20-67 % at E-L 35;
  inter-run confidence interval of the density below 0.3 m²/m³ (l. 642).
- **Check against the vineyard** (l. 611-617): plot RMSE of light interception below 5.0 %
  (bias below 2.9 %) for the partially fixed runs; below 4.6 % for the fixed ones; 11.4 and
  10.8 % when one digitised vine is copied 48 times (l. 632).
- **Stages of the measurements:** hemispherical photographs about every two weeks at E-L 23,
  31 and 35 in 2004 (l. 423); secondary axes at E-L 36 (l. 389).
- **Not printed:** leaf area per vine or per metre of row, canopy height and width, porosity
  as numbers, any growth in time.

## Dependence

- **Own:** no equation is taken from another paper. It cites Louarn et al. 2007 and Lebon et
  al. 2004 for shoot development (l. 98, 734) and Louarn et al. 2005 for the light integral
  (l. 379). The structure is of the envelope and leaf-cloud family (Johnson and Lakso 1991;
  Sinoquet et al. 1992); the render follows Casella and Sinoquet 2003.
- **Same data:** the partially fixed runs take shoot parameters from the winter 2004
  digitising and are checked against the 2004 photographs of the same vineyard, so the check
  is not independent of the fit (l. 408-411, 611-614).
- **Shared authors** (Louarn, Lecoeur, Lebon): a flag only.

## Flags

- The text says "3-year follow-up" (l. 784); the study ran 2003-2004.
- Minus signs are lost in Tables 2-3 of the text layer (b' prints 272.9 and 252.4).
- l. 585-587 says secondary axes do not differ by genotype (F 1.48), yet Table 3 gives each
  cultivar its own a' and b'.

## Bearing (2026-10-14)

- **No heat sum and no growth in time:** a static reconstruction of canopy structure. The
  usable numbers are the leaf area density and the light interception by training system.
- **No record.**
