# Gubler, Rademacher & Vasquez 1999: the UC Davis Powdery Mildew Risk Index

An APS web feature that sets out a 0 to 100 daily index built on hours at 21 to 30 °C, with its use to lengthen or shorten spray intervals.

**Read this first.**
- **Author list differs from the record.** The printed author line is Gubler, Rademacher and Vasquez only (l. 264). The Affiliations field (l. 266-267) then names "C. S. Thomas, Western Farm Service, Santa Maria" with an affiliation of his own, as a fourth author would be printed; the web page's layout may have split him from the author line. Leavitt, an author a search summary added to the record, appears nowhere on the page. Crossref gives three authors.
- **The 35 °C rule is stated two ways.** The bullet table says the index decreases 10 points "on any day with a minimum temperature above 35°C" (l. 321). The prose says it loses 10 points on days when the 6 h requirement was not met "or if at any time during the day, the temperature rose to 35°C for at least 15 min" (l. 324). A day with a minimum above 35 °C is much rarer than a day reaching 35 °C for 15 min. The page does not say which is meant.
- A web feature, not peer-reviewed; no data, sites or years are printed.

## What it holds

- **Trigger (l. 318):** needs 3 consecutive days with at least 6 h between 21 and 30 °C to start the index.
- **Increase (l. 319):** +20 points each day with at least 6 h in 21 to 30 °C.
- **Decrease (l. 320):** -10 points each day with less than 6 h in 21 to 30 °C.
- **35 °C rule, table (l. 321):** -10 points on any day with a minimum temperature above 35 °C. **Prose (l. 324):** -10 points if the temperature rose to 35 °C for at least 15 min. The prose also says the index reverts to zero if the three-day condition is not met before the first trigger (reading agent).
- **Range (l. 317):** 0 to 100.
- **Reading the index (l. 322, 323, 324):** 60 to 100, the pathogen reproduces every 5 days; 0 to 30, minimal, every 15 days or not at all; 40 to 50 is normal, every 8 to 11 days.
- **Use (l. 342):** above 60 shortens the spray interval, below 30 lengthens it. The page reports 2 to 3 fewer applications with equal or better control, with no numbers, sites or years (reading agent).
- **Ascospore part (l. 312, 313):** the Mills conidial table at 2/3 of its leaf-wetness hours; at least 12 to 15 h of continuous wetness at 10 to 15 °C.
- **Heat kill (l. 301):** spores and colonies can be killed at extended durations above 33 °C; 35 °C for 12 h or more with UV (reading agent).
- **Data (l. 306):** only the sentence that the model "was built and validated in all California grape production areas".
- **Reference (l. 347):** Ypema & Gubler 1997, Plant Disease 81:1187-1192, is the only epidemiological source named for the temperature response.

## Dependence

- The Mills table for the infection phase. The temperature-reproduction relation is probably Ypema & Gubler 1997, as the reference list suggests; the page does not say it is borrowed. Validation by comparing spray programmes; no fit is described.
