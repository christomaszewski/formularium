# Quesada et al. 2018: a low-cost rotating-arm trap with qPCR

A motor, rod and two petroleum-jelly slides, with species-specific qPCR for Fusarium
  circinatum; the argument is cost, not catch efficiency.

## What it holds

- **Design:** six traps on three Florida pine sites; slides replaced weekly; qPCR with species-
  specific primers (l. 23-29).
- **Result:** low levels, 1.7 to 77.1 pg of spore DNA (SE 0.3 to 39.7), with high
  reproducibility (l. 30).
- **Cost:** the commercial samplers cost thousands of dollars (l. 19); the low cost allows
  several traps per site (l. 32-34).

## Dependence

- Species-specific qPCR primers and the rotating-arm principle (record).

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- s6 (methods): cheap replicated traps with qPCR as a way to get many spatial points. A
