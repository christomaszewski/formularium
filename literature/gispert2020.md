# Gispert et al. 2020: pruning-wound fungicide in a desert table-grape block

A six-year field trial in a new table-grape vineyard in the California desert. One block had
thiophanate-methyl on pruning wounds, the other none. A 25-year net-return model built on the
trial gives the economic benefit (l. 20-28).

## What it holds

- **Trial design** (l. 20-24, 361): two blocks, one treated for six consecutive years. 15 rows per
  treatment, 3,840 vines, about 55 % of the vineyard (l. 361).
- **Disease and replants** (l. 620-625): about twice as many vines were replanted each year in the
  untreated block (p < 0.01). Trunk disease incidence was about twice as high (l. 625). Fungi
  from the wood included Lasiodiplodia, Phaeoacremonium and Eutypella (l. 628-631).
- **Yield** (l. 643-645): total fruit was 9 kg per vine untreated against 12 kg treated, and
  marketable fruit 5 kg against 10 kg. The sentence reads "reduced from 12 kg ... to 9 kg", so
  the direction is taken from the context.
- **Economics** (l. 656-661): replanting cost avoided about $2,150 per hectare over 12 years.
  Net-return gains over 25 years have a present value of $313,100 per hectare at 75 % disease
  control and $210,919 at 50 %, or $12,523 and $8,437 a year on average. The abstract gives
  $8,500-12,500 a year (l. 26-27).

## Dependence

- The simulation is adapted from Kaplan et al. and Baumgartner et al. (l. 564).
- The yield-age profile is calibrated to a relative loss from Baumgartner et al. (l. 591-595).
- The treatment cost of $158 per hectare is from Baumgartner et al. (l. 614). The price of $2.03 per
  kg and the replant cost of $7.75 per vine are from cost studies (l. 576, 612).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t5 (damage and economics) and t4 (other grape diseases). A decision engine could value a
  trunk-disease step from replant and yield losses per vine, but the economics rest on one block
  over six years and on simulated prices.
