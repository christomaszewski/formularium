# Kenney & Hall 2021: sour rot sprays timed after symptoms

Two Missouri vineyards in 2018 and 2019 compared weekly sprays after symptoms with two or three sprays timed by Brix. The reduced schedules gave about the same control.

## What it holds

- **Weekly sprays:** four in total in each year (l. 120-121).
- **2018, Vineyard 1 (Hermann):** weekly sprays from 15 Brix gave lower incidence than the 16 and 20 Brix timing and the 16, 19 and 22 Brix timing (p = 0.001; Fig. 3, l. 133-137).
- **2019, Vineyard 1:** no treatment effect on incidence (p = 0.4062) or severity (p = 0.2680) (Fig. 5, l. 143-150).
- **2018, Vineyard 2 (Ste. Genevieve):** no treatment effect on incidence (p = 0.584) or severity (p = 0.987) (l. 136-138).
- **Conclusion:** more post-symptom sprays did not give better control (l. 147-148).

## Dependence

- Bisiach et al. 1986 on insecticide sprays against Drosophila, and Barata et al. 2012 on wounded clusters and nets (l. 110-120).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- **Strands t1 (spray timing and number) and t4 (sour rot):** a Brix-triggered rule with fewer sprays could be tested in a grower's engine. The trial is two vineyards in two years, and the threshold results are not a model.
