# Mucalo et al. 2021: early defoliation and cluster thinning on Trnjak, one season

One vineyard, one season (2019), four treatments and an untreated control on cv. Trnjak
in the Dalmatian hinterland. Veraison defoliation cut yield most. Treated vines had less
Botrytis at harvest than the control, and the wine effects follow the timing of the
treatment.

## What it holds

- Design: Trnjak on 1103 Paulsen, planted 2007, Vrgorac Valley, 2019. Defoliation before
  flowering (T1), after berry set (T2) and at veraison (T3), and cluster thinning at
  veraison (T4), against a control (C). Treatments from 11 June, third on 6 August (l. 133,
  145).
- Yield per vine: 1.83 kg in the control, 1.15 kg at T3 (37% less), 1.24 kg at T4, 1.54 kg
  at T1 (l. 27). Cluster number fell to 7.2 at T4 against 10.67 in the control (l. 26).
- Botrytis cinerea: "no ... incidence ... in any treatment except control" (l. 118). The
  incidence values are not given in the text layer.
- Wine: alcohol 13.9% at T3 against 12.1% in the control; reducing sugars 2.53 against 1.87
  g/L (l. 46 to 52). Total anthocyanins 82.69 at T3 and 162.20 at T2, against 137.29 in the
  control (l. 89; units as in Table 4).

## Dependence

- Builds on earlier leaf-removal work (refs. 2, 3, 9, 15) for the timing effects. No model or
  dataset from another group is recorded.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t5 (damage and economics) and t4 (other grape diseases, Botrytis only as an
  incidence). The timing of leaf removal moves both yield and bunch rot. A simulator could
  take the yield loss at veraison (37%) as a check on a canopy term, if it tracks leaf area.
- One vintage, one vineyard, no replicate years, and Botrytis read as present or absent.
  Not a response curve. Not read in full.
