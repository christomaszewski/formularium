# Sarejanni 1951: problems of downy mildew epidemiology in Greece

An essay from the Benaki Phytopathological Institute (Kifissia), drawn from "our personal
observations, over a period of 20 years". No experiments. Its value is a Mediterranean
record of which seasons bring epidemics, and the author's reasons.

## What it holds

**Extent.**
- First found in Messenia in 1881 (Gennadios); in Argolis, Elis, Achaea, Athens and Zante
  by 1890.
- In 1900, just before a fine harvest, it destroyed two thirds of the country's crop in a
  few days.
- **Every year** in the rainiest regions (Macedonia, around Thessaloniki and Drama), in
  irrigated southern vineyards (around Chalkis, Euboea), and on coasts with night dews near
  torrent mouths (northern Peloponnese).
- **Only in "mildew years"** over most of viticultural Greece. None in the dry war years,
  when copper was nearly unobtainable.
- **Some places were long free of it:** Megara first saw it in 1936, near Attica and
  Corinth, which had suffered in 1910 and 1900.

**Dispersal and conidia (his reasoning, citing Ravaz).**
- **Long-distance transport is harder** in southern Greece's bright, dry late spring.
- **Conidia's lifetime:** Ravaz found germination keeps 5 days at most in the best
  humidity, and only hours in full sun.
- **In 1926 at Coropi (Mesoghia),** an Attic year at the limit for mildew, only heavy soils
  were attacked. Untreated vineyards on light soils beside them were spared.
- **A dry climate can turn an infecting rain into new sporulation only,** unless rain keeps
  the air saturated "pendant au moins 8 heures, dont quelques unes nocturnes".
- **Oil spots can keep forming conidiophores** for at least 40 days (Istvánffi & Pálinkás
  1913, cited).

**Winter conidia.**
- **They form all winter** south of the 38th parallel. At Kifissia, in mild winters,
  successive generations formed on green leaves left unpruned, until budburst in late March
  or early April.
- **They don't make mildew years:** 1946 and 1948 had mild winters and abundant spring
  rain, and were not mildew years.
- **Early pruning removes them,** though abandoned, unpruned vineyards may keep them.

**Mildew years in the currant regions, 1885-1939** (production in Venetian litres,
477.05 g; Organisation Autonome des Raisins Secs):
- **The four marked years and their crops:** 1900 (97 million), 1916 (185 million), 1920
  (180 million), 1931 (141 million). The 20 other years listed produced 230-393 million.
- **1916 and 1931** were also rust years.
- **Capus's rule** (south-west France, via Arnaud & Arnaud 1931; Tunisia, Chabrolin 1931):

  | Nov-Apr rain | May-Jun | Invasions |
  |---|---|---|
  | abundant | rain | severe |
  | abundant | drought | none |
  | weak | rain | weak |
  | weak | drought | none |

  - It holds "grosso modo" for Greece in his long observation.
  - He used it to tell the authorities each January in 1941-1944, dry wartime winters, that
    no invasions were to be feared, although copper was almost unobtainable.
  - **Its local exceptions:**
    - vineyards flooded in winter, by irrigation or by floods;
    - coasts and valleys where spring and early-summer dews act as infecting rains;
    - Macedonia and the rainier north.
  - **So mildew years** depend first on conditions for the formation, ripening and early
    germination of oospores.

**Autumn and the "preparatory year."**
- **Late rains alone are not enough:** in Attica in 1947-1948, rains began only in early
  January and were abundant all winter and spring, and there was no trace of mildew.
- **Early autumn rains set the number of oospores.** Early, regularly spaced autumn rains
  with a late onset of cold give many oospores; a late rainy season or early cold gives
  almost none.
- **The preparatory year:** in dry climates such as Attica a mildew year needs a
  preparatory year before it, one that multiplies oospores in autumn. A mildew year is
  itself one.
- **He doubts Zillig's sequence** (3-4 successive invasions before a mass invasion, in the
  Rhineland). Greece soon turns dry, with maxima past 30 °C, "maximum pour le développement
  du mildiou".
- **Frequency of severe epidemics:**
  - Mesoghia plain: two in 40 years, 1910 and 1940. 1940 destroyed 85-90 % of the harvest.
  - Attica: about every 20 years since 1900.
  - Chalkis, before the war: one year in 2 or 3, under winter flooding and marine dews,
    with rain hardly more frequent than in Attica.
- **His proposed warning:**
  - inspect chosen vineyards in mid-October and mid-November for autumn lesions ("pointes
    de tapisserie");
  - potato late blight appearing in autumn is a practical sign in Attica.
- **1940, Mesoghia:** the early invasions are the dangerous ones. Light soils, untreated,
  lost all; heavy soils with one or two pre-flowering sprays were saved. Their owners had
  lost everything in 1936.

**Treatments.**
- No Greek spray trials existed.
- Practice ran from none (Attica, far from a mildew year) to an average of 15 a year, with
  24 and 35 in some years, between Corinth and Patras.
- Dews for secondary infection are poorly recorded, and local climates differ between
  neighbouring districts.

## Dependence

- **No formulation.** Capus's rule is cited, qualitative, and has no thresholds. Nothing
  here computes or fits an engine model.
- **No author shared with the engine,** apart from co-authors of the Institute's annual
  reports: Zachos, whose incubation the engine now runs, is not an author of this essay.

## Bearing (2026-10-09)

- **A Mediterranean season-level pattern for Agrarium's history matching:**
  - severity set by winter (Nov-Apr) and spring (May-Jun) rain together;
  - severe epidemics rare in dry regions (every 20 years or so in Attica) and frequent with
    winter flooding or dews (every 2-3 years at Chalkis).
  - Penedès is Mediterranean too, though wetter than Attica.
- **A structural gap in Rossi-type primary models,** and so in F1:
  - Sarejanni ties a season's inoculum to the previous autumn: early rains, late cold.
  - The 1952 report ties it to late-winter drought ([sarejanni1950reports](sarejanni1950reports.md)).
  - A truth whose oospore pool ignores both cannot show "preparatory years".
- **Statements, not data:** 8 saturated hours to infect, 30 °C as the maximum for
  development, 40 days of conidiophore formation (cited).
