# Diputación Foral de Álava 2025: the viticulture service's report on the exceptional 2025 downy mildew year in Rioja Alavesa

A three-page regional extension report, dated 8 July 2025, on a very wet spring that made spraying impossible on many plots and led to many passes in June.

**Read this first.** Grey literature: a regional extension report written by the viticulture service from technicians' observation, not a survey or a measurement. It describes an exceptional year (the title says so), so its spray counts are for a high-pressure, wet spring, not a typical season. Its counts are qualitative ("in some cases", "in many cases"). The report prints no duration for the spraying block: "2 to 3 weeks" is the strand agent's reading of the dates (mid-May foci, early June resumption), not a printed figure. The mixed conventional and organic population is not split, except for one sentence on organic growers.

## What it holds

- **Trafficability, mid-May 2025:** in some plots treatments were not possible because the heavy rain made it difficult or impossible to enter with the machinery (l. 56).
- **Preventive spraying blocked:** preventive treatments across the board were "almost impossible"; only light or well-drained soils could be treated (l. 57). This is a limit that depends on soil type.
- **Resumption:** not until early June did the weather allow spraying to become general again, after the mid-May infection foci (l. 101).
- **Spray counts:** by early June some growers had made 4 sprays (l. 102), and by the end of June 8 passes had been applied in many cases (l. 103). Passes alternated systemic, penetrating and contact products (l. 66-67).
- **Organic growers:** with preventive-only tools, they increased the number of treatments to limit the damage (l. 98).
- **Supply:** shortages of some active substances occurred because mildew hit several denominations (Rueda, Montilla-Moriles, Jerez) at once (l. 71).
- **Damage:** the share of affected bunches ranged from about 10 % in the least affected plots to 70 to 80 % in others, with 40 to 60 % not rare (l. 88-90); damage was lower in higher, better-aired and lighter-soil vineyards (l. 79-80).

## Dependence

- None recorded. Observation by technicians. The second report of the series, dated 23 June 2025, is held as `dfa2025situacion`; the two do not agree on the count (see that note).

## Bearing (2026-10-25)

- **Delay limits (grower.delay_limits):** a documented block on spraying after heavy rain on heavy or waterlogged soils, lasting from the mid-May foci to early June, with light or well-drained soils sprayed first (l. 56, 57, 101). Supports a multi-day to multi-week block in a farm-manager approval model after heavy rain, by soil type. Kind: observation by an extension service, one region, one year. No soil-type thresholds or rainfall amounts are printed.
- **Sprays per season (spray.per_season):** 4 sprays by early June and 8 passes by the end of June, in many cases, in a very wet year (l. 102-103); that implies 4 passes within June, about weekly (arithmetic, not printed). The 23 June report gives only "up to 4 and 5" passes by that date, so the weekly cadence is uncertain; treat 8 as the upper end of a wet-year range.
- **Response to pressure:** organic growers raised the number of treatments in a bad year (l. 98); this supports a rule in which the count rises with pressure.
- **Not shown:** wind, labour or weekend limits, sprayer capacity, any warning-to-spray delay, Catalonia.
