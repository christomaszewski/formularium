# Ouyang et al. 2021: UAV point clouds for canopy decline in a Shiraz block

A 2018-19 Shiraz block in Eden Valley, South Australia. A drone point cloud at E-L stage 15
mapped Eutypa dieback-like canopy decline, with 87.4 % accuracy against a ground assessment.

## What it holds

- **Site and flights:** 1.2 ha Shiraz, 2018-19 season (l. 137-140). Phantom 4 Pro, nadir RGB
  images, point clouds in PIX4Dmapper (l. 122-140).
- **Result:** the point cloud found 12.5 % of canopy length with symptoms; ground assessment
  found 11.4 % (l. 32-33). In metres, 299.5 m against 272.9 m of 2398.1 m (l. 379-387).
- **Agreement:** accuracy 87.4 % and kappa 0.43 against the ground assessment (l. 33-34). The
  kappa is modest.
- **Timing:** E-L stage 15 suited the method (l. 36-37), as it did the ground check (l. 170).

## Dependence

Builds on the ground visual assessment for Eutypa dieback at E-L 12 to 17 (Bertsch et al. 2013)
and on plant area index from canopy images (De Bei et al. 2016). No model or dataset is
recorded as borrowed. The clustering is k-means, as the paper runs it.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Serves t2 (observation): a UAV scan maps decline with coordinates, faster than a walk. A
  growers' engine could use the map as a layer for remedial planning. The disease itself is a
  trunk disease, for t4.
- One block, one season, kappa 0.43. Thin for a model. Not read in full.
