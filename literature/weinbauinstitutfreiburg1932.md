# Freiburg wine institute report for 1931, p. 8: winter spores formed in 14 days at 5.5-6.4 °C

One sentence in an annual report: at 5.5-6.4 °C no outbreaks in a 14-day test, "but in this
time winter spores formed". It is the source of "Gessner 1932" in Grünzel's 1961 survey of
oospore formation.

## What it holds

- **Passage** (l. 374-378, the institute's own experiment; no method, number of plants or
  material given): "Of this only the interesting phenomenon may be reported, that at 5.5 to
  6.4 °C outbreaks ('Ausbrüche') were not observed during a test duration of 14 days, but
  that winter spores ('Wintersporen') formed in this time." It follows a statement that tests on
  outbreak at such low temperatures will go on next year (l. 371-374).
- **Authorship:** the section III a is not signed; the next block (III b, test of
  control agents) ends "Geßner." (l. 465), and the section after that is signed Leibbrandt
  (l. 507). Gessner is the report's plant-protection botanist (listed at l. 2301, 2375).
  The attribution of III a to him is inferred from this layout, not printed at the passage.
- **The same section, low-temperature work** (l. 357-374): germination tests of Peronospora
  conidia at low temperatures were repeated to refine the times; germination after 10.5 h at
  2.3-3 °C, but the released swarmers stayed motionless; first slow movement after 12 h,
  only a few; so germination at 2.3-3 °C seems possible after 10.5 to 11 hours; differences up
  to 2 hours between the 12 preparations. New infection tests at 7-8 °C on one Ruländer vine:
  4 of 47 infections on 12 leaves succeeded; the authors think they know why the yield was so
  low and will continue.
- **The 1931 season at the institute's Freiburg plots** (l. 400-412): Peronospora first seen
  3 June, stronger outbreaks 12 July, and "owing to the persistent rainy weather" the fungus
  spread extraordinarily in the course of the season. No link is made to the weather of
  autumn 1930 or winter 1930/31.
- **Not stated:** how the winter spores were identified, their number, how the temperature
  was held, whether they were counted per area, or anything on overwintering.

## Dependence

- No citation for the passage. Grünzel 1961 quotes it as "no sporangium formation at
  5.5-6.4 °C, but oospores after 14 days" (as read in the excerpt of that paper), which
  reads "Ausbrüche" as sporangium formation; the report says only "outbreaks".
- No formulation computed or tested.

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- **Data, one unreported test:** winter spores formed within 14 days at 5.5-6.4 °C, a cool
  temperature at which infection did not break out. So oospore formation is not tied to the
  weeks after autumn leaf fall nor to warm weather; it says nothing of how many form.
- Nothing on whether an autumn sets next spring's primary infections.
