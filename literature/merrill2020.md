# Merrill et al. 2020: flowering under 20 to 37 °C in growth chambers

Fifty varieties forced in a greenhouse after chilling, their budburst and leafout
matched against the Davis vineyard, then ten varieties' flowering plants in five chambers
from 20 to 37 °C: the time to flowering did not change with temperature, flower abortion
did. A test of the slowing that phenology models assume at high temperature.

## What it holds

- **Design:** cuttings chilled 21 d at 4 °C, dormancy induced at 6/4 °C with 8 h days,
  351 pots to a greenhouse on 15 August 2016 (l. 194-201); vineyard stages at UC Davis
  from 6 March 2015 every 3-4 days (l. 126); chambers at night/day 17/23 to 34/40 °C,
  means 20, 26, 30, 34, 37 °C (l. 226-231). 28 of 50 varieties never flowered; 26 plants
  of 10 varieties went in (l. 36, 241).
- **Budburst:** greenhouse budbreak 17 August to 6 September, lab against field
  correlated for budburst and leafout (F 14.6 and 18.5, p < 0.001; l. 229-234).
- **Flowering:** 34-51 d from forcing to 10 % flowering, mean 42.6; no chamber effect on
  the time to 10 % (p 0.52) or 50 % (p 0.49) (l. 272-294); buds aborted per plant 4.5,
  2.8, 5.8, 27.6, 57.3 from 20 to 37 °C (p 0.01; l. 278-279).
- **Review:** ten earlier chamber studies covering ten varieties (Table 1); Greer &
  Weedon's variety-specific optimum temperatures cited (l. 215, 291, 376).

## Bearing (2026-10-13)

- The upper temperature response that Wang-Engel and capped degree-day forms build in
  did not show in flowering here; the cost of heat was abortion, not delay.
