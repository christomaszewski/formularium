# Chen 2019: downy mildew risk in Bordeaux from regional data

Directed by David Makowski (INRA, UMR Agronomie), co-supervised by François Brun (ACTA),
with Marc Raynal (IFV) on the jury. A statistical thesis: it builds no mechanistic model
of any downy mildew process.

## What it holds

**Papers it contains.**
- Ch. 4: Chen, Brun, Raynal & Makowski, *Phytopathology* 109:787-795,
  doi 10.1094/PHYTO-12-17-0412-R (time to first symptoms).
- Ch. 5: Chen, Brun, Raynal, Debord & Makowski 2019, *Crop Protection* 126:104926 (expert
  elicitation of onset).
- Ch. 6: delaying the first fungicide application (submitted, *Scientific Reports*).
- Ch. 7: forecasting severe attacks by machine learning (submitted, *PLoS ONE*; probably
  Chen et al. 2020, *PLoS ONE* e0230254, from memory).

**The data: IFV's untreated plots** (TNT network, §3.2.1, pp. 56-61).
- 2010-2017 on the Atlantic coast: 543 plots, 1,282 site-years. In Bordeaux: 122 plots,
  258 site-years, 23-53 a year. "7 055 observations ... sur 274 sites-années entre 2010
  et 2018". The chapters use subsets (266 site-years in ch. 4; 151 plots in ch. 7).
- **Layout:** one untreated row at the plot's upwind edge inside a sprayed plot, with 2-3
  untreated guard rows; about 53 vines a row (3-165).
- **Measures:** weekly from week 12 to week 33: incidence on vines, leaves and bunches;
  severity (% area) on leaves and bunches. Onset: the first week with at least 1 % of vines
  (or bunches) symptomatic.

**Onset distributions** (ch. 4, the thesis's own results).
266 site-years, 2010-2017, weekly from week 12 to week 33 (§4.3.1, §4.4.1).
- Vines, by Turnbull's estimator: no onset before week 19; 90 % of plots symptomless at
  week 21, 50 % at week 24; 29.3 % still symptomless at week 32 (a plateau after week 29).
  The log-logistic fit: 90 % at week 19.1, 50 % at 25.3, 10 % at 33.6.
- Bunches: none before week 21; 90 % symptomless at week 23, 50 % at week 27; 42.1 % from
  week 29 on. Log-logistic: 90 % at 22.6, 50 % at 27.8, 10 % at 34.4.
- Between years (Table 4.1, Cox, vines): hazard ratios against 2010 from 1.05 (2011) to
  6.02 (2015), significant for 2012-2015 and 2017. In 2015, 50 % of plots had symptoms by
  week 21; in 2011 not until week 33 (observed).
- Weather (§4.4, after Table 4.2): March-June rainfall had a significant effect on onset on vines
  (p < 0.01) and bunches (p < 0.001); spring temperature, and autumn rain and temperature,
  did not (p > 0.05). Dry 2011 (1.51 mm/day) against wet 2013 (5.45 mm/day): 50 % of plots
  symptomless at week 27 against week 24 (log-normal, vines). The text says symptoms
  "appeared later in wet conditions"; its own numbers, and the sentence before, say the
  opposite: a misprint. No coefficient for rainfall is printed.

**Season severity** (ch. 7): median leaf incidence near 0 % in 2011 and 90.1 % in 2018.
Classifiers (GLM, LASSO, random forest, gradient boosting) predict a high end-of-season
value from onset date and monthly March-June weather; best AUC 0.87.

**Regional first-symptom dates 2010-2018** (Table 8.1, from the BSV bulletins), leaves:
11/05, 03/05, 15/05, 22/05, 29/04, 11/05, 09/05, 30/05, 07/05.

**Public data** (github.com/MathildeChen/PhD-Supplementary-Data, commit 8f43079, no
licence stated; opened 2026-10-08):
- `Supp_Data_Chap_7.xlsx`: 156 plot-years, 2010-2018 (coded 1-9), one row each: onset week
  (`Date_App`), mean daily rain and temperature for each month March-June, and four
  end-of-season measures (leaf and bunch incidence, `MFF_fin`, `MGF_fin`; leaf and bunch
  severity, `MFI_fin`, `MGI_fin`) coded **1 above the median, 0 at or below**: no values.
  - The onset weeks run 19 to 33. 29 are fractional, so imputed. Ch. 7 says 97 onsets were
    censored (40.2 % right, 38.1 % left, 21.7 % interval) and imputed with a survival model
    whose covariate is March-June rainfall. A rain-onset relation measured on this file is
    partly built in.
  - Its rainfall columns run 0.57-3.68 when averaged over March-June, against ch. 4's 1.51
    and 5.45 mm/day for the 2011 and 2013 springs: a different unit or aggregation, not
    resolved.
  - Earlier onset ranks a plot above the median: AUC 0.82 for leaf incidence and severity,
    0.80 bunch incidence, 0.79 bunch severity (computed 2026-10-08 by Agrarium's
    `scripts/diagnostics/chen_patterns.py`; ch. 7's best model, with weather, reached
    0.87).
  - Plots above the median for leaf incidence: 2 of 28 in 2011, 11 of 12 in 2018.
- `Supp_Data_Chap_5.xlsx`: the elicited experts' onset distributions by plot and date.
  `Supp_Data_Chap_6.xlsx`: fungicide exposure inputs. Neither is a season's data.

## Dependence

- Its models are statistical, fitted to IFV data with SAFRAN weather. No engine model is
  used, and the observation window is the IFV protocol, not a model.
- Caveats: the experts of ch. 5 saw unnamed model outputs; 97 of ch. 7's 151 onsets were
  imputed with ch. 4's Cox model.
- Incubation figures it quotes (4-9 or 4-19 days; 7-10 days) are cited from others.

## Bearing (2026-10-08)

- **This is the Bordeaux pattern TRUTH-METHOD part 2 names** ("Chen et al. 2020"). It
  gives four patterns for history matching:
  - onset quantiles on vines and bunches, with the share never symptomatic (ch. 4);
  - the spread between years (ch. 4, Table 4.1);
  - spring rain, not spring temperature, sets onset (ch. 4);
  - earlier onset goes with a worse end of season (the public file).
  The first two are Bordeaux's weather and need a Bordeaux climate. The last two are
  relations, which should transfer to other regions.
- Observed epidemics are three untreated rows inside treated vineyards, with the central
  row scouted weekly: a simulated check must sample the same way, and needs treatments
  around the rows to be like for like.
