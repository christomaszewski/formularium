# Blaeser & Weltzien 1978: sporulation, survival and dispersal of sporangia

Seven pages from Bonn, in German with an English summary: the laboratory data behind the
1979 paper's survival curves.

## What it holds

- **Plants:** Müller-Thurgau in a greenhouse at 20-25 °C and 70-90 % RH, 16 h of light in
  winter; sporangial suspension sprayed on the lower leaf surface, bagged 24 h (p. 156).
- **Sporulation (pp. 157-158):** oil-spot plants in saturated air in dark chambers at
  9-29 °C, discs counted after 16 h. None at 9 and 11 °C, weak at 13 °C without a lawn;
  density rises from 15 °C and is roughly level at 17-27 °C (Abb. 1, mean of 10 counts,
  confidence range 29,000-189,000 sporangia/cm² at P = 0.01 around 109,000); none above
  29 °C. RH: sporulation at 100, 99 and 98 %, none at 97 and 94 % (Tab. 1, 20 °C, dark).
  Light stops it entirely; at 18 °C and 100 % RH at least 4 h of darkness are needed.
- **Germination by the RH sporangia formed at (Tab. 2):** 100 %: 99 % germinate, start
  after 50 min, zoospores swim 26 h; 99 %: 53 %, 70 min, 14 h; 98 %: 37 %, 70 min, 13 h.
- **Survival (pp. 157-159, Abb. 2-3):** sporangia on the leaf, and detached onto aluminium
  foil, held at 10-30 °C and 30-100 % RH; germination counted after 23 h at room
  temperature; each test three times. The endpoint is the time germinability reaches zero.
  Both high temperature and low RH shorten life; RH matters most at 10-15 °C, less when
  warmer, and not at 30 °C. At 100 % RH sporangia at 10 and 15 °C still germinated after 10
  days, and at 20 °C after 4 days, when tests were stopped. Detached sporangia are more
  sensitive to low RH; at 30 °C below 60 % RH they did not last even 8 h. Values by
  condition are bars in days, read only from the figures.
- **Summary:** viability falls with rising temperature and falling RH; at 30 °C it is at most
  6 h ("bei 30° C liegt sie bei maximal 6 Std").
- **Dispersal (pp. 159-160):** no sporangia caught by a Rotorod trap or on Vaseline slides
  around infected plants unless hit by splashing rain; in a wind tunnel sporangia came off
  only above 540 m/min (force 5-6); a rain-water trap under foliage caught sporangia with
  the first outbreak.
- **Discussion (p. 160):** summer temperatures over 25 °C at about 60 % RH kill sporangia
  quickly; the 13 °C minimum often limits sporulation; first infections in 1977: Ahr (Bad
  Neuenahr) 11 July, Pfalz (Deidesheim) 5 August, Siebengebirge 12 August.

## Dependence

- Their own experiments. No model.

## Bearing (2026-10-09)

- The data the 1979 survival curves were fitted to (inferred). Brischetto et al. 2020's
  "<24 h at 20 °C and 30 % RH" is a reading of Abb. 3's bars, not a printed number.
- Sporulation needs at least 4 h of darkness at 98 % RH or more: the conditions behind
  later "moist dark hours" rules, though the engine's Caffi 2013 rule was fitted to its own
  data.
