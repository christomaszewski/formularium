# Chrelashvili 1993: the Müller curve a month early, 16 years in east Georgia

## What it holds

- **Table 1** (Kvarely, Kakheti, 1971-1986, p. 126): the first date of mildew by the
  Müller curve (Müller & Rabanus 1923's incubation calendar, from climate) and the date it
  was seen. The observed date was later every year, by 20-48 days (mean 33.5, computed by
  the reading agent). Observed first appearance ran from 17 May (1981) to 5 July (1983).
- **Timing:** first appearance coincided with flower-bud opening, not leaf budbreak.
- **Table 2** (July 1976): 1-12 newly infected leaves on each of 14 days without rain or
  dew; one dew point is printed above the air temperature.
- **Overwintering:** mycelium in 7 of 50 shoot sections (Table 3); the text says "many".
  Overwintering in buds is cited to Yachevski 1909 and Istvánffi & Pálinkás 1913, not
  shown.

## Dependence

- None: the Müller curve is not an engine model (the engine's incubation is Goidanich's
  and Zachos's). No equation is printed.

## Bearing (2026-10-10)

- Sixteen seasons of observed first appearance in a continental vineyard, with a classical
  incubation calendar a month early every year: a season-timing pattern, and a warning that
  incubation calendars alone place onset too early where primary infection waits on
  something else.
