# Nanni 2016: curative activity of four carboxylic acid amide fungicides on sensitive and resistant P. viticola

A PhD thesis whose detached-leaf test applies four CAA fungicides 1 day before or 1 day after inoculation, on CAA-sensitive and CAA-resistant strains.

**Read this first.**
- Only 1 day after inoculation was tested, so this gives no curative window in hours or days, only that a CAA still works at 1 day on sensitive strains.
- A thesis, not peer-reviewed as such. Only the test chapter and the introduction were read; the rest (molecular work on PvCesA3) at abstract level.
- The test conditions are an artificial rapid-development setting (see l. 1955); the authors say they may shorten the curative window.

## What it holds

- **Design (l. 1903):** preventive trials applied the fungicide 1 day before inoculation; curative trials 1 day after. Detached leaves, 18 to 20 h wet at 18 C (conditions as summarised by the reading agent).
- **Inoculum (l. 1905):** a suspension of 2 x 10^5 sporangia per mL.
- **Rates (l. 1897):** maximum registered field rates: 150 g/ha mandipropamid, 220 g/ha iprovalicarb, and 35 g/ha of the active named after it (cut at the line end; dimethomorph's rate is on the line before).
- **Sensitive strain (l. 1941):** the tests confirmed that all CAAs (dimethomorph, mandipropamid, iprovalicarb, benthiavalicarb) have curative activity against the CAA-sensitive strain.
- **Resistant strain (l. 1943):** this curative activity is lost on the CAA-resistant strain.
- **Mechanism (l. 1949, cited, not the thesis's own):** CAAs act most when cell walls are being actively synthesised during zoospore encystment, which is why curative activity is weaker than preventive.
- **Test conditions (l. 1955):** the authors say the activity window is lost in the curative test especially under the test conditions, which were set up for rapid development; a field window may be longer.
- **Residual (l. 1965):** some residual curative activity remains on resistant strains, especially when application is close to inoculation.

## Dependence

- Own tests with CAA-sensitive and CAA-resistant isolates; no model. It leads to Pirondi et al. 2014 (Proc. 7th Int. Workshop on Grapevine Downy and Powdery Mildew, pp. 108-109), whose abstract says iprovalicarb, benthiavalicarb and cyazofamid strongly prevented sporulation when applied 24 to 36 h after inoculation and controlled oil spots poorly. That abstract was not fetched in full and is not quoted here. Toffolatti et al. 2011 (J. Phytopathol. 159:201, doi 10.1111/j.1439-0434.2010.01748.x) is a second lead, not fetched.

## Bearing (2026-11-03)

- **Treatment protection (E6, Cooptera):** an own measurement that all four CAAs act when applied 1 day after inoculation on sensitive strains, and not on resistant ones. A lower bound on the curative window for CAAs, not the window.
- **Resistance (E6):** curative activity depends on strain sensitivity, so a window per class holds only where the CAA-sensitive population dominates.
- **Kind of evidence:** detached leaves, one curative delay, one temperature. Not field, no hours scale.
