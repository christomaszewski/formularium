# Wu et al. 2019: warming the bunch zone by 1.5 C in the field for two seasons, and what it did to berry metabolites

A passive polycarbonate open-top heater around the bunch zone of Cabernet-Sauvignon and Sauvignon blanc rows, fruit-set to harvest, 2015 and 2016.

**Read this first.** The biochemistry is outside Agrarium's scope and was read only in the abstract and conclusion. What matters here is the heating method, the size of the temperature offset it gave, and the measured side effects. Five replicates of five vines per block, one vineyard, two years, no disease measured.

## What it holds

- **Site:** VitAdapt experimental vineyard, Bordeaux (44 46 46 N, 0 34 01 W); flat; vines 8 years old, spur pruned, 1.6 m between rows and 1 m between plants, vertically trellised (l. 190-205).
- **Heater (l. 215-235):** modular rectangular polycarbonate panels 100 cm high and 300 cm wide, two per side of the row at about 45 degrees per 5 adjacent vines, top and sides open, enclosing the soil surface and most bunches (at the bottom of the canopy) but not most leaves. After Sadras & Soar 2009 (not held), modified for a denser trellis. Panels transmit more than 90 % of visible light, so radiation on bunches is not reduced significantly.
- **Design:** two blocks of about 30 vines (5 plants by 6 rows) per variety, one control, one heated, one buffer row between; 5 or 6 replicates of 5 vines, central three vines sampled; same vines in both years.
- **Sensors:** soil, bunch-zone air and berry temperature and bunch-zone relative humidity every 20 minutes: Vaisala HMP155 air temperature and humidity probes, copper-constantan thermocouples on berries and in soil, Campbell CR1000 logger.
- **Offset (Fig. 1, page image; text l. 300-310):** mean bunch-zone air temperature +1.5 C (H against C) over the whole period; larger by day and on clear-sky days; berry temperature rise in the same range, up to +5 C at times. Fig. 1b means: 2015 dTmin 1.0, dTmax 2.8, mean 1.2 C; 2016 0.8, 1.1, 0.8 C (approximate, page image). In 2015 dTmax reached about 5 C early in July and ran about 2 to 4 C in August.
- **Side effects (text, data not shown):** the daily cycle was kept; relative humidity slightly higher and VPD higher inside; soil temperature, soil water content and predawn and midday water potential not noticeably changed; berry weight and sugar unchanged in both seasons (Table 1).
- **Biochemistry (abstract only):** primary metabolites unchanged; anthocyanins reduced, tannins up before veraison then both down; alanine, serine and phenylalanine lower; IBMP lower in Cabernet-Sauvignon; thiol precursors much lower (up to -70 % at maturity in 2015 for Sauvignon blanc).
- **Context cited:** mean temperature in France projected to rise 0.6 to 1.3 C by 2021-2050 (Ouzeau 2014), with ripening-period warming far larger as ripening moves earlier.

## Dependence

- Method from Sadras & Soar 2009 and Sadras & Moran 2012; shares authors (Pieri, Hilbert) with pieri2016, whose berry-temperature figures are modelled; this paper's temperatures are measured at the berry.

## Bearing (2026-10-30)

- **Measured bunch-zone offsets:** a field experiment where the bunch-zone air and berry temperatures were measured under a warming treatment, with daytime offsets up to +5 C and a mean of +1.5 C: a scale for a simulated warming scenario at the bunch, and a data case for in-canopy humidity and VPD (the paper says RH and VPD rose but prints no values).
- **Not shown:** the RH and VPD values, wind, leaf wetness, or any disease outcome.
