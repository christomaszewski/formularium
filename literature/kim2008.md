# Kim, Beresford & Henshall 2008: a cost-loss analysis of weather estimates feeding a botrytis risk model

A New Zealand paper that values weather estimates (spatial interpolation against the nearest station) by the spray decisions they give a grape botrytis risk model, using Richardson's cost-loss form.

**Read this first.**
- The disease is botrytis bunch rot, not downy mildew. Only the decision form carries over.
- The text layer garbles the byline. The authors above were read in the page image by the main session (The Horticulture and Food Research Institute of New Zealand, Mt Albert Research Centre, Auckland). The symbols of equations 1 to 6 are lost (the equations are images).
- No money values for spraying or for crop loss are printed in the lines read.

## What it holds

- **Method (l. 12):** a cost-loss analysis of spray decisions from a disease risk model run on estimated against measured weather; the form is Richardson 2000's.
- **Loss and cost (l. 61, 62):** with no action, an event gives loss L. Action costs an expense X whatever happens (for example a spray) and prevents a part G of the loss (the potential gain).
- **Decision matrix (l. 84):** a two-by-two table of correct and incorrect predictions gives the hit rate H and the false alarm rate F.
- **Error source (l. 33):** a false alarm is the non-occurrence of a predicted event; a miss is an unpredicted event. Both come from error in the weather estimate.
- **Spray rule (l. 144):** action (a fungicide spray) when the accumulated Bacchus botrytis risk value exceeded 4 in a week.
- **Frequency (l. 165):** over both sites the risk index exceeded 4 in a week 29 times.
- **Result (l. 206):** spatial interpolation was worth more than the nearest-neighbour estimate when the return ratio (G/X) was above 2.6.
- **Shape (l. 208):** the relative value of interpolation rose logarithmically with the return ratio.
- **Season (l. 135):** crop value varies from season to season, so the cost-loss ratio is variable, not fixed.

## Dependence

- Applies Richardson 2000's cost-loss model; uses the Bacchus botrytis index (Rengasamy & Edwards, as cited by the reading agent); New Zealand weather of 2003-05. Not downy mildew. Nothing here is borrowed from a model Cooptera runs.

## Bearing (2026-11-03)

- **Value per decision (E7, Cooptera):** a worked form for a value per decision: cost of acting X, loss L, gain G, hit rate H, false alarm rate F, and the return ratio G/X that decides which method is worth having. Kind of evidence: a method paper with one site pair and one disease.
- **Not downy mildew:** the Bacchus rule and the 2.6 threshold are botrytis, New Zealand and a weather-source question. No euro or dollar figure for a missed or spurious downy mildew spray is given.
- **Season variation (l. 135):** supports a cost-loss ratio per season, not one constant.
