# Alimad et al. 2016: chasmothecia in Sweida vineyards form late, mostly empty, and release nothing at budbreak

A two-season survey of eleven vineyards at five sites in Sweida, southern Syria: where
chasmothecia form, how many a leaf carries, how they mature through the autumn, how few are
viable by spring, and that spore traps caught no ascospores at budbreak.

## What it holds

- **Occurrence** (l. 52-62, 135): chasmothecia in 45.5 % of the vineyards and on 38.7 % of
  infected leaves (12.5 % upper side, 18.4 % under, 7.5 % both); 0.4-0.6 per leaf on average;
  vineyard 1 carried 390 per 100 leaves in October 2014 and about 260 in 2015 (l. 331).
- **Maturation** (l. 299-334, Table 2): 61.1 % immature on 20 July 2015; peaks of 60 % on 30
  July and 61.9 % on 25 September; the text's "20 August" for an upper-surface share is
  October in the table.
- **Viability** (l. 65-67, 351): 65.6 % empty; 1.2 % viable in March and 0.2 % in April
  (0.17 % in the results).
- **Spring** (l. 205, 226-228): no chasmothecia on bark and no ascospores on 192 trap slides
  at budbreak in March-April; no counts printed.
- **Cited for comparison** (l. 515-543): 1-10 per leaf (Halleen & Holz 2000); 6.6 % of
  ascospores before budbreak (Holb & Füzi 2016); bud thresholds of -22 and -26 °C from apple
  (Spotts & Chen 1984).
- **Conclusion** (l. 552): chasmothecia may not be the primary inoculum at the season's
  start there.

## Dependence

- Observed; one-way ANOVA and Tukey (SPSS 15). The bark sampling follows Cortesi et al.
  1997 (l. 138). No model, nothing fitted.

## Bearing (2026-10-26)

- For Cooptera's ascospore records (the 10-20 search): a warm-climate case where the
  overwintering fraction is near zero by spring and no ascospores fly at budbreak, the
  opposite of the Pacific Northwest and New York data behind `thiessen2018` and
  `moyer2014`. No record.
