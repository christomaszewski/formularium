# Fuller et al. 2017: what neighbouring vineyards gain by controlling a disease together

A bioeconomic model of two adjacent Napa Valley blocks with Pierce's disease and its
sharpshooter vector moving between them, comparing each owner treating alone with joint
control over 50 years.

## What it holds

- Model of two adjacent Napa Valley blocks with Pierce's disease (PD), spread by blue-green
  sharpshooters (BGSS) with dispersal between blocks, and a bearing and nonbearing vine
  stock (l. 16, 148-160, 177-186). Dollar values are US dollars.
- Equations: BGSS dynamics (eq. 1), vine stocks (eqs 2-3), land constraint (eq. 4), joint
  objective (eq. 5), profit per acre (A1) (l. 181-247, 837). Parameters in Table A1 (l.
  813-829): control cost $10.50 per unit, vine investment $13.50 per vine, damage 0.020
  (nonbearing) and 0.018 (bearing), kill 0.10, discount 0.05, maturation 0.20, death 0.025,
  prices $3,000 and $6,000 per ton, dispersal 0.0875.
- Sources: investment costs loosely from Klonsky and Livingston (2009); USDA NASS crush
  data; UC cost studies; biological parameters are educated guesses (l. 831-858).
- Gains from cooperation (Table 2, % of NPV over 50 years): no treatment in either block
  3.77 %; Block 1 treats alone 3.09 %; Block 2 treats alone 1.86 % (p. 137). Overall roughly
  1 % to 4 % (l. 661-662).
- Base case starts at 75 % of carrying capacity (l. 440); without treatment BGSS reach 100 %
  in twenty years (l. 488-489).
- Cited: PD welfare loss $92 million per year (Alston et al. 2013); transmission up to 90 %
  per feeding (Purcell 1979); flight range about 800 feet (Hill 2010).
- Napa crush value about $720 million in 2014 (l. 68); mean price $3,600 per ton (2010 to
  2014, footnote 8).

## Dependence

- A model with parameters from cost studies and crush data; its biological parameters are
  'educated guesses' (l. 831-858). No field data fitted.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- For Agrarium at cooperative scale (M7) and Cooptera's value questions: coordinated control
  between neighbouring blocks adds about 1-4 % of 50-year net present value over acting
  alone (Table 2), small because one block's spillover is modest at these dispersal rates; a
  published frame for the value of a cooperative's joint decisions. A vector disease, not a
  mildew.
