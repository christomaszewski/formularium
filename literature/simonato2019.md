# Simonato et al. 2019: noble rot and the aroma of Garganega passito wine

Experimental vinifications of withered Garganega grapes with 0 to 50% naturally noble-rotten
berries. Noble rot raised several volatile compounds and sherry lactones, and PLS regression
tied them to honey, apricot and caramel notes. It is a winemaking study, not a vineyard one.

## What it holds

- Withering for about four months, to 40 to 45% weight loss (l. 13). Mixes of 20, 40 and
  50% noble-rotten must with sound must, in triplicate (l. 14 to 15). Two groups, A (12 L) and
  B (40 L).
- Infection: B. cinerea by PCR, absent in sound grapes and present at low level in
  noble-rotten ones (l. 30).
- Volatiles: some compounds rose by over 100% in noble-rotten wines (l. 34). 1-octen-3-ol
  reached its odour threshold only in A-N40 (OAV 0.9; threshold 25 µg/L) (l. 36). Isoamyl
  acetate OAV fell from 29 to 10 (l. 36).
- Sensory and PLS: R² 0.944 for honey, 0.749 for figs (l. 43). Sherry lactones,
  N-(3-methylbutyl)acetamide and vanillin tied to honey, apricot and caramel (l. 43).

## Dependence

- Methods follow earlier papers by the same group (refs. 3 to 6, 13, 14) for the grape and
  wine analysis. No model or dataset from outside the group is recorded.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t5 (damage and economics), at the margin. Noble rot on withered grapes changes the
  aroma and so the value of a passito wine, a post-harvest effect.
- Off the point for a vineyard simulator or a growers' engine: it is wine chemistry after
  harvest, with no vineyard data, no disease rate in the field and no yield loss. Not read in
  full.
