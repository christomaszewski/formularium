# Kudinha 2014: leaf wetness in a citrus canopy (PhD thesis)

A PhD thesis at the University of the Free State (promoter Sue Walker).

## What it holds

- PhD dissertation (University of the Free State, 2014). Citrus (Satsuma mandarin) orchard at Stellenbosch, South Africa, 2012-07-27 to 2012-11-30; 62 days with visual wetness scoring (l. 434).
- Reference was visual: 10% of a leaf's upper surface wet counts as wet, every 15 min (l. 1378). Six sensors (3 Campbell 237 unpainted, 3 Decagon); thresholds set from the visual data (l. 2507).
- Dew-day mean LWD: upper 12.2 h, lower 10.3 and 10.7 h (l. 2179). The abstract's 15.5, 14.3, 14.1 h are rain-day means (l. 448, l. 2180).
- Sensor MAE on dew days 57 to 70 min (upper), 2 and 2.65 h (lower) (l. 2817, l. 2820).
- Dew began when leaf T fell below the dew point on all nights but one (l. 3375); visual onset came 46.30 min after that point, sensors 15.65 min after it (l. 3391, l. 3394).
- Models, upper canopy, all hours: SWEB Fc 0.78, CSI 0.60, FAR 0.30, Bs 1.15; RH model 0.84, 0.68, 0.17, 0.95 (l. 5048, l. 5049).
- The RH threshold is 75%, the lowest RH seen in the same dew data (l. 4979). SWEB only had LAI 3.67 and height 2.14 m set (l. 4973).
- Leaf T by Newton-Raphson energy balance: R2 0.89 and 0.71 after zero wind was set to 0.0005 m/s (l. 4708, l. 4750).
- Dependence: SWEB is Magarey et al.; RH and sensor thresholds are fitted to the scored data.

## Dependence

- Its own measurements in a Satsuma orchard (2012). Its RH threshold (75%) and sensor thresholds were set on the same observations they are compared with: circular. SWEB was run with LAI and height only.
- No author of an engine model.

## Bearing (2026-10-08)

- Clear. Upper and lower canopy wetness differ by 1 to 2 hours a day in citrus, against none in Sentelhas's grape canopy: a range for Agrarium's within-canopy wetness.
