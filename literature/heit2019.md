# Heit et al. 2019: Lobesia botrana flights and risk in Argentina

Degree-day models for the four male flights of Lobesia botrana in Mendoza, from trap catches over three campaigns. Habitat models then project the risk to other Argentine wine regions.

## What it holds

- Weibull regressions of cumulative male catches on degree-days, from 506 pheromone traps (l. 108–110, 122).
- Fifty per cent emergence of males at 248.79, 860.18, 1,671.34 and 2,335.64 degree-days, for the four flights (l. 39–41).
- Fit of the four flight models: R² from 0.91 to 0.95 (l. 201–204).
- Uncolonized Cuyo areas have a suitability similar to the quarantined area. The upper Río Negro and Neuquén are similar too (l. 42–43).
- The abstract gives degree-days from 1 July (l. 37), but the methods count from 1 September (l. 103). Check before use.

## Dependence

- Uses the 7 °C development threshold of Gallardo et al. 2009 (l. 105–107).
- The pseudo-absence mask follows Heit et al. 2013. Trap pre-selection follows Damos and Savopoulou-Soultani 2012. The consensus model follows Narouei-Khandan et al. 2016.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Serves t7 (insect pests) and t6 (spread of an invasive vine pest into new areas). Degree-day thresholds for each flight could seed a Lobesia module in a vineyard engine, for Argentine sites only.
