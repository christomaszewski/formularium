# Maggi et al. 2017: where new flavescence dorée cases appear in a Barbera vineyard

Every symptomatic vine mapped each summer 2011-2015 in a 1.75 ha Barbera vineyard (8,749
vines), with point-pattern statistics and recovery rates.

## What it holds

- Site: 17,500 m2 vineyard, Piemonte, Italy; 76 rows; 8749 Barbera vines; rows 2.5 m apart,
  vines 0.8 m apart; about 5000 per ha (l. 22, 132-135, 165).
- Survey: symptoms mapped each July to mid-August, 2011 to 2015; at least 10 symptomatic and
  10 healthy vines per year tested by PCR (l. 145, 150).
- Incidence: about 25% symptomatic in 3 to 4 years (l. 319-320); dead below 0.05% (l. 335);
  about 7% recovered by 2015 and about 30% of symptomatic plants recovered the next year (l.
  341-342). Figure 3 is a plot only; values read from it are approximate.
- Recovery rate r = -[P_t(t) - P_t(t - 1 year)] / [Delta tau P_t(t - 1 year)] (rendered p.
  6): 1.37e-5 per day (2013) to 1.73e-3 (2012); average 5.73e-4 per day, 0.21 per year (l.
  290-292). Formula after Maggi 2014a,b; matches the mechanistic estimate of Maggi 2014b (l.
  293).
- Recovery: about 4 to 5 years on average; 25% to 35% chance within 5 years (l. 298-300).
- Spatial: quadrat D about 1 at small scales, above 1 at larger scales; CSR rejected
  overall; quadrats 8 to 1152 m2 (l. 197, 235).
- Nearest neighbour: CSR not rejected in 2011 and 2012; aggregated in 2013 and 2015 (l.
  331-333).
- New infections: at least 50% within 3 m of a symptomatic plant from 2012; 90% in later
  years (l. 396, 406).
- Vector: 2 to 4 vines (1.5 to 3 m) per stay (l. 518).

## Dependence

- Its own survey with PCR checks; the recovery-rate formula follows Maggi 2014a,b.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- For within-vineyard spread (t6): from 2012 at least half, later 90 %, of new cases
  appeared within 3 m of a symptomatic vine, a short-range clustering a scout's sampling
  plan should expect; incidence reached about 25 % in 3-4 years. A vector disease, not a
  mildew.
