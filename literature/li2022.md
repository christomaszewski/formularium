# Li et al. 2022: logistic-regression weights on the inputs of a k-nearest-neighbour model for wheat Fusarium head blight (plant disease, field data)

Predicts field incidence of wheat Fusarium head blight (FHB) in four Anhui counties from satellite indices and interpolated weather, with a k-nearest-neighbour (KNN) classifier whose inputs are weighted by the coefficients of a logistic regression that the authors call the "disease mechanism". Compared with the same KNN without the weights. No record: a method, on field survey data.

**Read this first.** "Mechanism" is a label for a logistic regression fitted to the same field survey, not a process model or a simulator (l. 439): there are no synthetic data, no pretraining and no epidemic model, so this is not a simulation-trained or hybrid model in the sense of this strand. It is included because it is billed as a hybrid for a plant disease. The test is a random 7:3 split of 154 survey points from two dates in one season, so the scores say nothing about new years or new sites. The body text layer is overprinted; numbers from Tables 4 and 5 were read from interleaved lines.

## What it holds

- **Data** (l. 26, 221-231, 267-268, 295, 659): two field investigations of FHB incidence on 29 April and 10 May 2021 in Changfeng, Dingyuan, Fengyuan and Feidong counties, Anhui Province; 154 homogeneous wheat areas selected as sample points on the two dates (l. 231, 659); Sentinel-2, MODIS and Sentinel-3 reflectance; meteorological factors kriged from 47 stations in and around the study area. Synthetic samples: none.
- **Validation design** (l. 518, 659-660, 710-711): the 154 points were randomly divided into training and test sets at 7:3; the generalisability check repeats 10 random splits and averages the ROC curves (AUC above 0.5, rising as the date nears the onset). Same season, same two dates; no held-out year or site. Fivefold cross-validation on the training set chose k (l. 673-678; k = 3 gave the highest mean accuracy, 0.865 on 29 April, Table 3, l. 684-690).
- **Scores, logistic-KNN against plain KNN** (abstract l. 30-32; Tables 4 and 5, l. 754-762 and 777-790; the 0.79 and 0.80 are at l. 790): accuracy 0.88 and F1 0.86 on 29 April and accuracy 0.92 and F1 0.94 on 10 May for the weighted model; plain KNN 0.75 and 0.68 on 29 April, 0.79 and 0.80 on 10 May. The authors report an accuracy gain of "approximately 13 %" (l. 30-31), which equals the difference in the tables (arithmetic, not printed).
- **Baseline**: plain KNN without the logistic weights is the only comparator; no process model of the disease and no weather-threshold model is scored.
- **Positive-class definition** (l. 703-706): disease incidence greater than 0 counts as diseased.

## Dependence

- Logistic regression (Campbell and Madden 1990, cited) on the same training split; Sentinel-2, MODIS and Sentinel-3 products and station weather. No disease process model is used or computed.

## Bearing (2026-10-27)

- **The nearest real-data plant-disease "hybrid" this strand found is not a simulator hybrid.** The word "mechanism" here means fitted coefficients, so it gives no evidence for D45, D58 or D60 on training with simulated epidemics; keep it out of the count of simulation-trained disease models.
- **A caution on validation:** random 7:3 splits of points from two dates in one season (l. 659-660) allow neighbouring points to sit on both sides; TRUTH-METHOD's calibrated fraternal-twin OSSE would be judged on new seasons, which this paper cannot support. Treat 0.88 to 0.92 as a development figure.
- **Not shown:** any process model of FHB, any synthetic samples, or any held-out year or site.
