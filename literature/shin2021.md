# Shin, Park & Kim 2021: emulators of a Penman-Monteith leaf wetness model

The authors train four machine-learning emulators (ELM, random forest, ν-SVM, DNN) to
reproduce the wet or dry output of a Penman-Monteith (PM) leaf wetness model on fewer inputs,
then run them on eight Gyeonggi farms. No record: the emulators have no closed equation and
the PM rule is Sentelhas et al.'s (2006).

## What it holds

- **PM model** (l. 133-199): leaf wet when the latent heat flux LE > 0, with
  LE = -(s Rn + 1200 (es - ea)/(ra + rb)) / (s + γ), γ = 0.64 kPa/K, rb = 307 (D/WS)^0.5,
  ra from a log profile with d = 0.5 Zc and z0 = 0.13 Zc. Eight inputs: RH, wind, T, shortwave,
  sensor height, leaf size, albedo and a relative-shortwave term. The text layer garbles Eqs
  2-4; constants (the 100 in Eq 3, 4.901e-7, the 237.3 offset) look like FAO-56 daily constants
  and need checking against FAO-56 before any use.
- **Emulators** (l. 300-485): input sets M1 to M8 drop inputs; 100,000 PM-generated, balanced
  rows to train; ELM 400 nodes, RF 500 trees, DNN 100-1000-100 nodes. DNN depth conflicts
  (four hidden layers, l. 318; three, l. 479).
- **Simulation accuracy** (Table 3): with all inputs SVM 0.994, DNN 0.993; without shortwave,
  albedo and the relative term about 0.84; temperature only about 0.60. RH is the most
  critical input for the emulators.
- **Case study** (l. 784-911): eight farms (rice, grape, apple, pear), hourly, 2009 to 2017
  except 2013; T and RH at 1.5 m, wind and wetness at 2 m; wetness by two Campbell 237 flat
  plates at 45° north; observed about 25 % wet. Mean wetness accuracy: DNN with RH and T 0.847,
  PM 0.752. Hours of error (Fig. 6, medians by eye): PM RMSE about 7.5 h per day, MBE about +4 h;
  DNN with RH and T RMSE about 5 h, MBE about -1.5 h.
- **Data:** nongupepi.gg.go.kr (download route printed; licence not).

## Flags

- **Test-set contamination:** the test set is all input combinations, including the 100,000
  training rows (l. 447), so simulation accuracies are in part in-sample.
- **Tuned on the test set:** hyper-parameters chosen on that test accuracy (l. 467).
- **The count of combinations** (54,311,468, l. 439) does not match Table 2's value sets
  (408,934,656).
- **No RH-threshold run:** the paper cites Park et al. 2019 and Gao et al. 2020 as finding
  RH-threshold models best, but runs none itself.
- **Related:** `shin2020` (same group; whether the farm data are shared is not stated).

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

For strand 3 and what Agrarium's truth can take:

- **The PM rule (wet when LE > 0) is a physical wetness model**, unlike the engine's RH ≥ 90 %
  stand-in. It needs shortwave, wind, sensor height and leaf size; with RH, T, height and leaf
  size alone (the best emulator) accuracy against the PM model is about 0.71, and against
  observed wetness about 0.85, with PM itself at 0.75 on those farms. So a physical rule did
  not beat a humidity-and-temperature emulator against sensors.
- **Not usable as a truth:** emulators are trained on PM output, not on a vineyard; the grape
  farm is one of eight; no weights printed.
- **Can it run:** PM in principle (Eqs 1-10, with checks of constants); the emulators no.
