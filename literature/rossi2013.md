# Rossi, Caffi & Gobbin 2013: what genotyping changed in downy mildew models

A review with no new data. Its models are the engine's (Rossi et al. 2008; Caffi et al.
2012-2013) or built on them (Rossi, Giosuè & Caffi 2009). Its value is the field patterns
it summarises from Gobbin et al. 2005 (*Plant Pathol.* 54:522-534) and 2006 (*Mol. Plant
Pathol.* 7:519-531).

## What it holds (cited from Gobbin)

- **The survey:** 39 vineyards in 2000, 2001, 2002 and 2004 (Germany 4, France 4, Italy
  12, Greece 10, Switzerland 9); 155 samplings, about 10,000 oil spots, 4 microsatellites.
- **Genotypes:** 85 % (mean) found only once or twice; in most vineyards only 1-2 genotypes
  reproduced asexually to any extent; dominant genotypes made 4-95 % of severity.
- **Timing:** clonal (secondary) infections stayed low until mid-July and rose in late
  summer; new genotypes, from oospores, appeared all season.
- **Spread:** clones aggregated by leaf, shoot and vine; generally less than 20 m per
  asexual cycle, 130 m once (Freiburg).
- **A second pattern:** few primary infections and one or a few clones, in low-severity
  central-European epidemics, Greek islands and coastal vineyards (dominant genotypes up to
  about 95 %).
- **Freiburg, June 2004:** incidence from 0 to 99 % of vines and 0 to 45 % of leaves in a
  week, about 220,000 lesions/ha.
- **Greece:** two peaks, the epidemic stopping in midsummer heat and restarting in autumn
  from oospores.
- Populations more than 5 km apart are mostly distinct.

## Dependence

- The models it presents are not independent of the engine.
- Gobbin's genotype observations are field data no engine model produced: independent
  targets. Gobbin co-wrote this review with Rossi and Caffi (a flag).

## Bearing (2026-10-08)

- **History-matching patterns for a truth's epidemic structure:** oospore infections all
  season, a few clones carrying the secondary spread, spread mostly under 20 m a cycle,
  two-peaked Greek seasons. TRUTH-METHOD part 2 already names Gobbin 2005; these are its
  numbers.
