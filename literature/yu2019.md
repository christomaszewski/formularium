# Yu et al. 2019: the identical twin overstated the gain from satellite fields and understated the gain from profiles

The first direct comparison of an identical twin and a nonidentical twin for the same ocean assimilation system (an ensemble Kalman filter, the Gulf of Mexico, April to September 2010). It is the measured source of the sentence that TRUTH-METHOD gives without a name. No record: a method with a measured comparison on synthetic twins.

**Read this first.** One regional system, one filter, one season, so a single case and not a general measure. The comparison is identical against **nonidentical** twin (a different model type: a HYCOM truth and a ROMS forecast), not Halliwell's fraternal twin (same model type, different configuration); the authors name the fraternal case as the intermediate one. No real-data experiment for the same configuration is reported here.

## What it holds

- **What it is** (l. 30): the "first direct comparison" of nonidentical and identical twins in an ocean data-assimilation application.
- **The result, in the abstract** (l. 36-38, 40): the identical twin "produces a biased skill assessment, overestimating" the improvement from assimilating sea surface height and temperature (dense satellite fields) "while underestimating" the value of temperature and salinity profiles; such biases can lead to undervaluing observing systems such as profilers (l. 40).
- **Design** (l. 141, 195): in the nonidentical twin the truth is interpolated daily output of a 1/12 degree data-assimilative global HYCOM, the forecast model is ROMS, and synthetic observations (SSH error 2 cm, temperature 0.3 degrees C, salinity 0.01) are sampled from the truth; in the identical twin the truth is a ROMS run that differs from the free run only in initial, boundary and wind forcing.
- **Table 1, change in mean absolute difference (MAD) from truth, relative to the free run** (l. 348-356): nonidentical, SSH and SST only (N1): SSH -51 %, temperature -29 %, salinity -11 %, velocity -25 %; with profiles added (N3): temperature -33 %, salinity -23 %, velocity -26 %. Identical, SSH and SST only (I1): SSH -59 %, temperature -45 %, salinity -21 %, velocity -46 %; with profiles (I3): temperature -50 %, salinity -27 %, velocity -46 %. So profiles add 12 points of salinity improvement in the nonidentical twin against 6 in the identical, and 1 point of velocity against 0 (arithmetic, not printed).
- **Subsurface circulation** (l. 368): at 400 m, assimilating SSH and SST cuts MAD by about 67 % in the identical twin against about 45 % in the nonidentical.
- **Mechanism, as the authors see it** (l. 439-444): the lack of an intrinsic difference between truth and forecast model in parameterisations and resolution makes it easier to correct subsurface fields from SSH and SST alone, so the information in profiles is "much" smaller.
- **Error growth matched, bias remained** (l. 219-222, 529): the SSH error growth was 0.048 cm per day (nonidentical) and 0.040 (identical) against 0.042 in real observations; both twins matched reality, yet the identical twin was biased.
- **Conclusion** (l. 504-509, 456-457): matching the error-growth rate in SSH alone was an insufficient criterion; error growth in one property is insufficient and additional criteria are needed.
- **Recommendation** (l. 496-498): skill assessments and OSSEs from identical twin experiments "should be avoided or, at least, regarded with caution".
