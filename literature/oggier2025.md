# Oggier et al. 2025: coppicing hazel to cut the alternative Flavescence dorée vector

Eight sites in Ticino, four coppiced in March 2022 and three left as controls. Coppicing
cut the populations of Orientus ishidae in the hazel and in the vineyards near them over
three years. A hatching test in 2023 showed that wild hazel hosts this leafhopper.

## What it holds

- Design: 8 vineyards with forest edge, Ticino, chosen by 2021 trap counts. Four HM sites
  and three controls (l. 12, 17; 21 for the 2021 trap counts). Hazel shoots within 3 m of the hotspot traps coppiced
  in March 2022 (l. 36).
- Hatching (Arzo, 2023): nymphs hatched from hazel wood at all sampling heights and
  diameters but one. Hatching began at the end of May at 687 degree-days (l. 43 to 44).
  There was no preference by wood size (l. 110).
- Population change after coppicing (yellow sticky traps): the first year (2022) fell
  significantly in HM sites, including untreated traps in the same sites (l. 79). Three
  years on, treated positions fell 75% to 86% (l. 104). Vineyard traps in HM sites fell
  19% to 87% (l. 105).
- Controls: the population rose by 55% (Biasca), 23% (Genestrerio) and 3% (Maggia) in the
  same span (l. 104). In the vineyards it stayed stable or fell 73% at Maggia (l. 105).

## Dependence

- Builds on earlier finds of infected hazel and O. ishidae in Ticino (Casati et al. 2017;
  Rizzoli et al. 2021; l. 7). No model or dataset from another group is recorded.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t6 (regional spread) and t7 (insect vectors). A host-side control, coppicing hazel
  near vineyards, cuts a vector population for three years after one treatment. A
  simulator could add a vector-density term that falls when wild hosts are removed.
- Trap counts are not infection rates. The paper gives no FDp infection in the insects, so
  it does not show less spread. Not read in full.
