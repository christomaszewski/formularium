# Mastin et al. 2020: where to put survey sites to catch an invading pathogen early

Simulated huanglongbing epidemics on Florida's citrus landscape are coupled to a
stochastic optimisation of which survey sites maximise the probability of detecting the
invasion before a set prevalence; the result is compared with risk-based site choices.

## What it holds

- The detection model (Table 1): a sampled infected tree is found by visual inspection with
  probability 0.5, 'obtained from a comparison of PCR and visual inspection' in Florida
  groves (l. 444-448, its ref. 59); detectability after infection rises logistically from
  0.006 at a rate of 1.00 a year, fitted to Devil's Garden data (l. 419-426).
- 50 samples per site each round (1 km²), 1 to 150 sites, daily or weekly rounds (l. 444
  ff.). Its questions include how site number, survey frequency and diagnostic sensitivity
  change the best arrangement (l. 162-166).

## Dependence

- Epidemic simulations from its authors' earlier HLB model; no grapevine data.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- For Agrarium's scout operator and Cooptera's survey planning (t2, with combes2025 and
  hauser2022): a visual-inspection sensitivity of 0.5 per infected host with detectability
  growing over months after infection, the form a latent period gives oil spots; and a
  method for placing scarce surveys by risk. Results figures were not read.
