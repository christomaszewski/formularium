# Torregrosa et al. 2017: grapevine response to temperature, from whole-plant chambers (microvine) to heated bunches, as a breeding programme

**Read this first.** A programme review from the ClimWine 2016 conference. The temperature work is in controlled chambers with day/night set points, and the bunch work reuses the Pillet fruiting-cutting rig. No vineyard microclimate is measured.

## What it holds

- **Chambers (l. 163-175):** microvine experiments Exp. 1-7; control air 25/15 C day/night, treatments from 22/12 to 30/25 C for 1 to 2 months, plus 2-day couples from 20/15 to 35/30 C; 14 h photoperiod, daily PAR about 19 mol m-2 d-1; mean VPD about 1 kPa (Exp. 1-5) and about 2 kPa (Exp. 6).
- **Whole-plant gas exchange (l. 272):** net photosynthesis optimum between 25 and 30 C and falling beyond 30 C (Figure 3a); night respiration over 15 to 30 C (Figure 3b). Numbers are in figures not read.
- **Bunch heating (l. 225-230):** fruiting cuttings of Cabernet Sauvignon, fan heaters on clusters, other organs shielded; control daytime fruit temperature set to 26 C against 34 C hot, "a mean T difference of about 8 C", said to be close to the range between sun-exposed and shaded berries (cited as Pieri and Fermaud 2004).
- **Reference (l. 545):** the text and list give "Pieri P, Fermaud M (2004) Effects of defoliation on temperature and wetness of grapevine berries. Acta Horticulturae 689: 109-116"; ouakedlecourieux2017 gives 2005 for the same item.

## Dependence

- Reviews the group's own work; the bunch-heating results are those of pillet2012, ouakedlecourieux2017 and lecourieux2020. Microvine (Chaib et al. 2010) not held.

## Bearing (2026-10-30)

- **Photosynthesis optimum:** 25 to 30 C for a whole microvine plant under 19 mol PAR a day is a possible range check for a carbon module, with one genotype in a chamber. No disease and no field temperature here.
