# Wang, Fisher & Wolff 2008: rain rates from a tipping-bucket gauge

How well a tipping-bucket gauge gives rain rate when the tip times are turned into 1-min rates by a cubic spline, tested against a Joss-Waldvogel disdrometer. No record: an error study.

## What it holds

- **Reference and period** (l. 122-150, 212-231): impact disdrometer (drops 0.3 to 5.5 mm, 50 cm2, 1-min records; records under 20 drops per minute and 0.2 mm/h treated as noise) at Roi Namur, Kwajalein, 9 April to 22 November 2005, only when both instruments worked. Totals: disdrometer 901.19 mm, gauge 735.58 mm, so the gauge is 81.62 % of the disdrometer (l. 216-217). For 30 events of at least 5 mm: gauge 382.78 mm, 7.26 % below the disdrometer 412.74 mm; event-total MAE 1.07 mm, sd of differences 0.91 mm (l. 213, 231). A correlation of 0.996 is printed without a clear reference (period or event level; l. 219).
- **Gauge:** 0.254 mm (0.01 in) per tip (l. 110); buckets larger than that add uncertainty (cited, Habib et al. 2001; l. 530).
- **Method:** each tip is a cumulative step; a piecewise cubic (continuous first and second derivatives) gives 1-min rates; linear interpolation is the comparison. This is the operational TRMM 2A-56 method.
- **Error by rate and averaging** (abstract; l. 16-21): median relative absolute error of 1-min rates about 22 % above 3 mm/h and 32 % below; at 7-min averaging about 5 % and 14 %. Errors drop sharply once 1-min rates are averaged over 4 to 7 min or more (l. 18).
- **Table 1** (l. 647), median relative absolute error (%), 1-min (7-min), by event gap 10 / 15 / 20 / 30 / 60 min: above 3 mm/h 22.00 (5.20), 22.12 (5.07), 22.62 (5.33), 22.89 (6.28), 24.68 (8.53); at or below 3 mm/h 32.10 (12.87), 31.87 (13.87), 32.11 (15.62), 33.33 (17.67), 37.69 (27.03); all rates 23.90 (6.31), 24.58 (7.33), 25.29 (8.10), 26.67 (9.43), 30.24 (14.27). Longer gaps add low estimated rates and raise errors; the authors prefer 15 min (l. 627, 679).
- **Clock error** (l. 713-739): a 1-min logger shift raises MAE by 0.88 mm/h (1-min rates) and 0.28 mm/h (7-min); a 10-min shift raises MAE from 1.76 to 5.25 mm/h (1-min) and from 0.52 to 2.81 mm/h (7-min), correlation from 0.95 to 0.26 and 0.99 to 0.45. Logger drift about 1 min per month at 20 C (MadgeTech) and about 1 min per week (Hobo) (l. 720).
- **Data:** TRMM 2A-56 and TSVO products are NASA ground-validation products; no access route or licence in the text read.

## Contradiction carried, not resolved

Only the correlation reference above (0.996, period or event level), which the text does not make clear. Figures 5 and 8 were not rendered; text values only.

## Dependence

- The spline method is the authors' group's operational TRMM 2A-56 method, not borrowed. 7-min averages feed a Ze-R relation (TSVO). The disdrometer is the reference, not a model. No shared calibration printed.

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

Strand 5 (sensor error):

- **Bias and noise for a rain gauge:** a tipping bucket reads low, 18 % over a season and 7 % for large events, against a disdrometer in a tropical setting; its 1-min rate error is 22 to 32 % and falls to 5 to 14 % at 7-min averaging. A sensor-error model for rain in the sweep can use a tip-size quantisation (0.254 mm here; 0.1 mm for the Hungarian gauges in lakatos2026) and a rate error that depends on rate class and averaging interval.
- **Timing:** logger clock drift of 1 min per week to 1 min per month and shifts of a few minutes matter for 1-min series; they are small for hourly data. A leaf-wetness or wetness-onset time from a logger needs the clock checked.
- **Limits:** tropical rain at one site; one gauge type and tip size; says nothing about wetness sensors.
