# English et al. 1989: leaf removal raised cluster-zone wind three to four times, with no consistent change in leaf wetness duration

A two-season field comparison of grapevine canopies with and without leaf removal around the clusters, in three California vineyards.

**Read this first.** The wetness sensors were rarely wet (values mostly below 1 on a 0 to 10 scale), so the finding of no consistent wetness difference is weak evidence about wind and drying. Wind was sampled for 5 minutes every 2 hours at cluster height only; there is no above-canopy wind. Figs. 1 and 2 (hourly wind) are plots with no numbers in the text. The companion `thomas1988` says wind was recorded hourly; this paper says every 2 hours.

## What it holds

- **Sites:** three vineyards, Zinfandel in Lake County and Chenin blanc in Napa and Monterey counties; vines 2.4 m apart in rows 3.6 m apart. Rows in Lake and Napa were perpendicular to the prevailing winds, rows in Monterey parallel to them (l. 107).
- **Treatment:** leaves and laterals around the clusters removed about two weeks after bloom. Two replicate sensors of each type stood at cluster height in the canopy halves with and without removal (l. 172).
- **Instruments:** wind by dual resistance-temperature-detector anemometers (Sierra Instruments series 690), 5-minute records once every 2 hours (l. 165). Leaf wetness by Campbell 237 grid sensors on a 0 to 10 scale; complete wetness is a reading of 5.0 or more (l. 159, 180).
- **Wind:** of all the variables, wind speed was affected most; leaf removal raised the average wind speed up to three or four times the speed in unaltered canopies (l. 41).
- **Wetness:** values in both canopy types were mostly below 1 and peaked in the early morning, except during and after irrigation, when they were near 10 (l. 315).
- **Result:** despite 3-4 times more wind at cluster height, differences between canopy types were slight and there was no consistent difference in the duration of leaf wetness (l. 320).
