# Park et al. 2019: random forest, deep network and extreme learning machine for daily leaf wetness at nine Korean farms

Three machine-learning wetness models, fitted on hourly station data, are compared with the CART, an RH > 90 % model (NHRH) and Penman-Monteith (PM) against flat plate sensors.

**Read this first.** Eight of the nine sites are rice, apple or pear, one is a grape orchard (l. 139); no result is given for the grape site alone in the text read. The ML models use latitude, longitude and hour of day as inputs, so they are fitted to these nine sites. The text gives the RF correlation as 0.811 (l. 669) and Table 3 as 0.807 (l. 728); the table is used. Eq. 2 of the CART is printed with a garbled term.

## What it holds

- **Data:** observed LWD and weather from nine farms in South Korea (l. 19); hourly (l. 127); LWD is the sum of hourly wetness from 0 to 23 h, so the errors below are hours per day (l. 129). Data 2009-2017, 2013 dropped (l. 130).
- **Reference sensor:** two adjacent flat plate sensors (Campbell Scientific Model 237) at 2.0 m, facing north at 45 degrees (l. 122-123). Air temperature and RH sensors at 1.5 m (l. 120); radiation and wind at 2.0 m (l. 121). Sensors were not at canopy height.
- **Crops:** one grape orchard (site 4, 37.217 N, 126.702 E, 6 m), two apple, one pear and five rice sites (Table 1, l. 136-144).
- **CART printed:** thresholds Tdew 3.7 C, wind 2.5 m/s, RH 87.8 % (l. 260). Eq. 1: 1.6064 sqrt(Tair) + 0.0036 Tair^2 + 0.1531 RH - 0.4599 WS x D - 0.0035 Tair x RH > 14.4674, with D = Tair - Tdew (l. 267-276). Eq. 2 has the threshold 37.0 (l. 272); its Tair x WS and WS x D terms are garbled here (Wang 2019 and Gillespie 2021 print +1.0613 WS x D).
- **NHRH:** wet when RH stays above 90 % for more than a threshold number of hours (l. 282). The hours threshold is not printed in this text.
- **PM:** net radiation was not measured and was estimated by the method of Walter et al. (l. 328); the paper says the PM model is limited by the precision of net radiation (l. 297).
- **ML models:** inputs Tair, RH, short wave radiation, WS, latitude, longitude and hour (l. 332). Trained on 2009-2016 (2013 dropped), tested on all sites in 2017, 3285 points (l. 338, l. 1078).
- **Errors, all data, 2017 (Table 3):** RMSE (h per day) ELM 4.484, RF 3.681, DNN 4.020, CART 5.429, NHRH 4.606, PM 5.180 (l. 731); MAE ELM 2.851, RF 2.314, DNN 2.502, CART 3.479, NHRH 2.999, PM 3.526 (l. 737). Text: ML RMSE under 4.4 h, existing models over 4.6 h (l. 679-680). So the RH > 90 % model is 0.69 h per day worse than RF in MAE (arithmetic, not printed), and the best of the three existing models.
- **Importance in RF:** RH, short wave radiation, Tair, hour, latitude, longitude, WS, in that order (l. 1215). About 70 % of records have zero LWD (l. 659).
- **Release:** observed data can be downloaded from the Gyeonggi-do Agricultural Research and Extension Services (l. 128); article is CC BY 4.0 (l. 1570). No code is released.

## Dependence

- CART is Gleason et al. 1994 as extended by Kim et al. 2002 (kim2002, gleason1994); NHRH follows Sentelhas et al. 2008 (sentelhas2008); PM follows Sentelhas et al. 2006. The same CART coefficients appear in wang2019 and gillespie2021.
- Shares the RH-threshold form with sentelhas2008.wetness and cooptera.rh90_wetness (structure tag rh-threshold-wetness). The ML models are own fits to Korean data and share no equation with cooptera.logistic_wetness.
- Authors: K.R. Kim is a flag only (a Kim of the Korean group; not shown to be the author of kim2002, whose first author is K.S. Kim).

## Bearing (2026-10-27)

- **RH > 90 % error:** on daily hours, the existing models (CART, the RH > 90 % rule, PM) had MAE above 3.0 h per day and RMSE above 4.6 h (l. 680-681) against flat plate sensors at 2 m on Korean apple, pear, rice and one grape farm. A station-only RF model had MAE about 2.3 h (l. 692-693). Kind of result: a re-fit by the authors, tested on one held-out year (2017), not on held-out sites.
- **Station-only inputs:** all inputs are standard station variables plus hour; short wave radiation is the second most important (l. 1215). Useful as an upper bound on what station data can give, not as a portable model, since latitude and longitude are inputs.
- **Sensor placement:** the reference sensor is 2.0 m up and faces north, not in a vine canopy. Not shown: results for the grape site alone, or any canopy-height sensor.
