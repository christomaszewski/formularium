# Úrbez-Torres et al. 2010: Botryosphaeriaceae spore trapping in California vineyards

From UC Davis and county extension advisers.

## What it holds

- Not downy mildew: Botryosphaeriaceae spores (bot canker) in California vineyards (l. 1). Useful only as an example of spore-trap data against rain.
- Slide traps at seven vineyards, 10 per vineyard, changed weekly, Sept 2006-May 2009 (l. 74, l. 78, l. 104); Burkard traps at Monterey and Sonoma, 2008-2009, 10 L/min (l. 81, l. 91).
- Weekly slide counts vs precipitation: r2 = 0.7, n = 847 (l. 118). Daily Burkard counts vs rain: Sonoma r2 0.58, Monterey r2 0.76 (l. 199); release after at least 2 mm of rain (l. 212).
- Seasonal share: abstract says over 60% in Dec-Feb, 22% in fall and early spring, 3% in late spring and summer (l. 29, l. 31, l. 32); Results say 68.9%, 30.1%, 1% (l. 149, l. 153, l. 155). The two do not agree.
- Authors advise delaying pruning (l. 318).
- Flag: W. D. Gubler is last author (l. 10). No equation, no model-dated data.

## Dependence

- Its own slide and Burkard traps against rain. Abstract and results disagree (over 60%, 22% and 3% against 68.9%, 30.1% and 1%).
- Gubler is the last author: a flag through the engine's powdery mildew index.

## Bearing (2026-10-08)

- Clear, and of indirect use: rain-driven spore release for a trunk disease, an example of trap data against weather.
