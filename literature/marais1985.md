# Marais & Hattingh 1985: Phytophthora cinnamomi through vineyard soil

Field work in South African vineyards on how Phytophthora cinnamomi moves through soil. It spreads mainly with water, and faster down a slope in a soil with a perched water table.

## What it holds

- Isolated from dead or diseased vines in vineyards in 14 districts (l. 13). Found in 69 of 114 vineyards surveyed, but in soil around infected vines in only 11 (l. 70–71, 110).
- In the perched-water soil (Estcourt), spread downhill was 6 m per 6 months (l. 137). After reaching the freer-draining Clovelly soil, it advanced only 6 m in 18 months (l. 139–140).
- Lateral spread was about 1 m in the first year and hardly any after (l. 71–72).
- The pathogen was found to a mean depth of 320 mm in Clovelly soil and 240 mm in Estcourt soil (l. 122–123).

## Dependence

- Extends an earlier survey by Marais (1979a) (l. 47). Uses lupin baiting (Chee and Newhook 1965) and the disease potential index of Tsao (1960).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Serves t6 (regional spread), for a soil-borne root pathogen, not an airborne one. A soil-pathogen spread model could take the slope and soil-drainage dependence. The data are from one eight-year-old vineyard in the 1980s.
