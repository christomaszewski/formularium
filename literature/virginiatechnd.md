# Virginia Tech guide and VitiScribe blog (n.d.): post-infection days for downy mildew fungicides, unsourced

An online extension guide ("Online Guide To Grapevine Diseases", chapter "Downy mildew of grape") and a commercial blog, each giving a number of days after infection in which a fungicide still works.

**Read this first.** Both are grey and unsourced. Neither cites a measurement for its days, and the blog contradicts papers read elsewhere. Do not use these days as values; use them only to see what growers are told.

## What it holds

- **Metalaxyl (guide, l. 116):** Ridomil formulations are locally systemic and have "good curative activity if used within 4 days of infection". No source is given.
- **Phosphorous acid (guide, l. 120):** products give good control of downy mildew; the line also gives a zero-day pre-harvest interval and a 4 h re-entry interval.
- **Blog, post-infection days (l. 35 to 38 of its text; not among the script-checked quotes):** Zampro (ametoctradin + dimethomorph) up to 4 days; Revus Top (mandipropamid + difenoconazole) up to 3 days; Ridomil Gold 1 to 2 days; Forum (dimethomorph) kickback within 24 to 48 hours. No sources.
- **Contradiction:** the blog's Ridomil 1 to 2 days is shorter than the 3 to 5 days the reading agent took from Wong & Wilcox 2001 for metalaxyl.
- **Maryland extension (reading agent's note, not in the script-checked quotes):** phosphites give good post-infection and anti-sporulant activity, especially on oil spots not yet sporulating.

## Dependence

- None recorded. Neither source says what its days rest on.

## Bearing (2026-11-03)

- **Treatment protection (E6, Cooptera):** shows the rule-of-thumb windows a US grower hears (metalaxyl 4 days, CAAs 1 to 4 days). Kind of evidence: unsourced extension text and a vendor blog, low reliability.
- **Use:** a check that measured windows are not wildly different, never a parameter source.
