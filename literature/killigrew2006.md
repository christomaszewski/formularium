# Killigrew 2006: oospores in 1 of 19 Western Australian vineyards, and a first symptom that is one large lesion

A funder's final report (supervisors K. Sivasithamparam and E. S. Scott) on a 2001 to 2005 survey of downy mildew in Western Australia, with oospore searches, germination tests and bud assays.

**Read this first.**
- It is a research-funder report, not peer-reviewed.
- Bud perennation as the source of first infection is the author's inference. The report itself says the findings "require confirmation via further field studies" (l. 135, 137). Do not use it as a measured primary-inoculum source.
- The grower and advisor reports of very early epidemics are verbal, not measured (l. 176, 177).

## What it holds

- **Survey (l. 241, 242):** 19 vineyards in 13 regions of Western Australia, where downy mildew had been reported, surveyed between 2001 and 2005.
- **Oospores (l. 92, 93):** found in only a single vineyard of the 19. Even there, very few could be induced to germinate in the laboratory.
- **The one site (l. 481, 485):** oospores were found in colonised leaf tissue from one vineyard only (Manjimup, relocated vines, per the reading agent), after the 2001/2 epidemic.
- **First symptom (l. 103):** the first symptom seen early in the season was often a single large lesion across distorted tissues (leaf, petiole, shoot, tendril), not the sparse oil spots that the report says typify primary infection elsewhere.
- **Verbal reports (l. 176, 177):** advisors and growers said epidemics started very early and went further than oospore primary infection could explain. Not measured.
- **Winter bud burst (l. 270):** in the Swan Valley a small number of buds burst in late July after a warm period (three or more days over 20 °C maximum, per the reading agent); some shoots were systemically colonised and sporulating.
- **PCR (l. 671):** P. viticola DNA in all 12 dormant buds excised from colonised nodes (growing season); 8 of 13 winter buds follow, per the reading agent.
- **Inference (l. 135, 137):** the first infection may come from within the vine, not from soil oospores. Stated as unconfirmed.
- **Why no oospores (l. 541):** the author speculates that oospores do not form in years when the disease is sporadic (single mating type).

## Dependence

- Computes no model. The oospore survey and bud assays are its own. The heterothallism argument is borrowed from Michelmore 1981 and Wong et al. 2001, as cited.

## Bearing (2026-11-03)

- **Primary inoculum timing (Agrarium D67; Formularium request rossi-climate-d67):** Agrarium's truth follows Rossi 2008's oospore lineage and puts first symptoms weeks early in Germany and New Zealand. This survey finds oospores in 1 of 19 Western Australian vineyards, and a first symptom that is often one large lesion on distorted shoot tissue. It is a counter-case to an oospore-only first infection. Kind: survey and field observation, a report.
- **Contrast:** taylor2018 (held) found oospores in all ten sampled vineyards in 2015. The two disagree, 17 years apart in the same state; a flag, not a resolution.
- **Bud perennation:** the author's inference, unconfirmed. No parameter can be taken from it.
