# Oggier et al. 2024: gone-wild grapevines and the Flavescence dorée reservoir in Ticino

Thirteen forest plots in Ticino, sampled for the phytoplasma in gone-wild grapevines (GWGV)
and in the two leafhopper vectors. Infected GWGV and vectors carry the genotype that
matches cultivated vineyard outbreaks. The forest reservoir is near vineyards and is a
likely source for spread.

## What it holds

- Plots: 13 forest plots. Scaphoideus titanus nymphs in all of them; Orientus ishidae nymphs
  in 9 of 13 (69%) (l. 208). Totals: 244 and 27 nymphs (l. 208 to 216); 1,088 S. titanus and
  324 O. ishidae adults (l. 216 to 221).
- GWGV infection: 120 leaf samples, 36 from symptomatic vines and 84 from asymptomatic.
  FDp in 91.7% of symptomatic and 7.1% of asymptomatic samples (l. 223 to 225).
- Vector infection: S. titanus infection rate of 14.9% to 49.6% in the southern area; none in
  the north (l. 233 to 234). Four O. ishidae carried FDp: three M54, one M50 (l. 252 to 253).
- Genotype: the GWGV and vector sequences match M54 and M50, the genotypes of cultivated
  outbreaks (l. 255 to 257).
- Distance: 70% of GWGV plots lie within 26 m of cultivated vineyard (l. 256 to 257).

## Dependence

- Builds on the 2023 survey of the same region (Oggier et al. 2023; l. 98) and on the vector-dispersal
  study of Lessio et al. 2014 (up to 30 m; rare beyond 330 m; l. 254 to 256). No model or
  dataset from another group is recorded.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strands t6 (regional spread) and t7 (vectors). A forest reservoir close to vineyards, with
  vector infection rates near 15% to 50% in one area, could set an outside-source term for a
  spread model.
- Plot counts, not a transmission rate. The forest sites are not vineyards, and the
  infection rates come from sampled insects. Not read in full.
