# Vömel et al. 2007: solar dry bias of the Vaisala RS92 humidity sensor

Simultaneous soundings of the RS92 radiosonde and a cryogenic frostpoint hygrometer at Alajuela
  show a daytime dry bias that a simple pressure- and temperature-dependent correction reduces.

## What it holds

- **Design:** simultaneous RS92 and CFH launches at Alajuela, Costa Rica, July 2005 (l. 23-25).
- **Result:** at solar zenith angles of 10 to 30 degrees the mean dry bias is about 9 % at the
  surface and 50 % at 15 km (l. 26-27).
- **Correction:** a pressure- and temperature-dependent correction brings the error under 7 %
  up to 15.2 km, independent of RH (l. 27-30); night-time calibration factors 0.98 at 0 C to
  1.13 at -70 C (Table 1, l. 291-299).

## Dependence

- Reference is the CFH; builds on the time-lag work of Miloshevich et al. 2004 and the AWEX
  comparison (l. 41-52); not read.

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- s5 (sensor error): a sunlight-driven dry bias of a capacitive humidity sensor, 9 % at the
  surface. A simulator of vineyard RH error could take the sign (dry in sun) and the order of
  size for an unshielded sensor; a radiosonde at surface pressure is not a shielded logger. Not
  read in full.
