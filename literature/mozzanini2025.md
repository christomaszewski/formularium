# Mozzanini et al. 2025: drone band spray against airblast, canopy coverage

A drone band spray at two rates and two growth stages in a Ruché vineyard, compared with an airblast sprayer, measured as canopy spray coverage.

## What it holds

- **Rates and stages**: 53 and 133 L/ha, with ISO 015 and ISO 04 nozzles (l. 131), at BBCH 55 and BBCH 81 (l. 115–116).
- **Averages**: the higher rate raised canopy coverage by 41.7 % on average (abstract, l. 23). The airblast gave about 70 % more coverage than the drone (l. 24).
- **Drone coverage**: at 133 L/ha, 3.79 ± 0.44 % early and 4.07 ± 0.50 % late. At 53 L/ha, 2.75 ± 0.51 % early and 1.80 ± 0.34 % late (l. 188–191).
- **Airblast coverage**: 25.68 ± 0.44 % early and 20.19 ± 2.9 % late (l. 212–213).

## Dependence

- Canopy measured by the inclined point quadrat method, as in Biglia et al. 2023 (l. 116–117).
- The vineyard and variety (cv. Ruché, 5,000 vines/ha, l. 106) appear to match mozzanini2026b. This is a different and earlier analysis, with two rates against the five in that article, so it is not a copy.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Serves t1 (spray coverage): the drone gave under 5 % canopy coverage against 20 to 26 % for the airblast. A growers' engine should not treat band spray as equal to airblast coverage. A simulator would need the deposit fractions.
