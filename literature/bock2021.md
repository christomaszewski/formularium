# Bock et al. 2021: a century of visual severity estimation, reviewed

A review of how accurately people estimate plant disease severity by eye, what makes them
err and what improves them (scales, diagrams, training).

## What it holds

- Review of visual severity estimation (PREPRINT; no journal, year or DOI printed) (l. 4).
  No equations, no new data.
- Most raters overestimate, especially at low severity; a few underestimate (l. 1395, 1397).
- Many small lesions are overestimated more; the tendency is greatest below 20% severity (l.
  1422, 1432).
- Raters favour 5 and 10% values, mostly above 20% severity (l. 1402).
- Lin's ρc: about 0.90 or more is accurate (l. 1306); training or SADs usually give 0.85 to
  0.95 (l. 1322); consistent ρc above 0.95 is rare (l. 1320); no-experience raters 0.60 or
  less (l. 1335).
- Altman (1991) takes 0.90 as accurate; McBride (2005) takes 0.95 (l. 1337, 1339).
- Time: no number printed; faster raters less precise (Parker et al. 1995b) (l. 1446).
- Inter-rater reliability: definitions only, no values (l. 685).
- Franceschi et al. (2020): new SADs gave significantly more accurate estimates (l. 1028);
  least accurate raters gain most from SADs (l. 1032).
- Unaided SDs greatest over 18 to 62% (l. 625) or about 18 to 52% (l. 810): the review
  conflicts with itself.
