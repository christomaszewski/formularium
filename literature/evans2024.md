# Evans & Pirie 2024: weather variables for within-vineyard Botrytis risk

Botrytis bunch rot severity in Riesling is explained by the daily Bacchus index and afternoon
  vapour pressure deficit, and a mapped block shows the variation within a vineyard.

## What it holds

- **Data:** eight site-years, 2009 to 2014, in two Tasmanian regions, plots of about 0.5 ha (l.
  33-34).
- **Result:** two Bacchus-index variables, E-L 19 to E-L 31 or 34, explained over 80 % of the
  variance of final mean severity; the BBR-I risk index with median 15:00 vapour pressure
  deficit explains up to 99.5 % (l. 34-37). Late-season interval length or median 15:00 RH
  explain 86.4 or 83.3 % (l. 37-38).
- **Spatial:** severity mapped in 4.8 ha of Sauvignon Blanc, 2018-19, confirming the need for
  risk indicators at a fine scale (l. 38-39).
- **Code:** formulae with sample data in Excel accompany the paper (l. 46); not checked.

## Dependence

- Builds on the Bacchus index (the Bacchus model) and vapour pressure deficit, as the record
  states.

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- s4 (measured vineyard microclimate): shows within-vineyard variation of a humidity-driven
  risk, at a scale of 0.5 ha plots to a 4.8 ha block, and which weather variable (15:00 VPD,
  RH) tracks it. A simulator could take the use of afternoon VPD as a site-level modifier.
  Botrytis, not downy mildew. Not read in full.
