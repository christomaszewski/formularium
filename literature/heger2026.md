# Heger et al. 2026: air samplers and multiplexed detection of three grape pathogens in Michigan

From Michigan State University and USDA-ARS (Corvallis). A proof without volume or pages.

## What it holds

- A multiplex qPCR for P. viticola, Botrytis cinerea and Erysiphe necator in air samples, made from three published assays with primers and probes unchanged (l. 111). The P. viticola primers are Si Ammour et al. 2020 (l. 184), the same sequences as Valsesia et al. 2005.
- Detection limits in multiplex: 1 fg P. viticola, 10 fg each for the others (l. 294); the abstract says 10 fg for each (l. 32).
- Site: small Vignoles vineyard (~500 m2), Clarksville, Michigan (l. 167), 26 July-30 Sept 2021 and 28 July-29 Sept 2022 (l. 165), post-bloom to veraison (l. 203).
- Rotating-arm samplers at 0.6, 1.4 and 1.8 m, three each, plus end-row at 1.4 m (l. 219). For P. viticola no height or position effect in 2021 or 2022 (l. 308, l. 276); B. cinerea differed.
- Table 2: P. viticola predicted mean 8.98 x 10^-5 ng/m3 at 0.6 m in 2022 (l. 373); 2.73 x 10^-5 in 2021 (l. 361).
- Burkard (10 L/min) vs rotating-arm P. viticola rho 0.48 (l. 299); abstract says 0.53 (l. 38). SporeCam vs rotating-arm rho -0.48 (l. 343).
- Only secondary inoculum was sampled (l. 394). Observation data; no equation.

## Dependence

- Three published assays unchanged; its P. viticola primers equal Valsesia 2005's Giop set (the reading agent compared the sequences). Its abstract and results disagree on sensitivity (10 fg against 1 fg) and on one correlation (0.53 against 0.48).
- Mahaffee and Neill are authors of the engine's Thiessen 2018 rule: flags.
