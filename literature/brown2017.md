# Brown et al. 2017: citizen-science reports in a regulatory oak survey, England and Wales

Self-reported acute oak decline sightings are used to design a 2014 systematic survey of England and Wales. The affected area is then mapped from both sources.

## What it holds

- **Survey design:** a 2013 pilot of 116 woodlands found 17 more symptomatic sites (l. 251-256). The 2014 survey counted 160 hectads. The 38 hectads already holding the disease were not visited, which raised the share of hectads that could be sampled from 13.7 % to 17 % (l. 252-255).
- **Detection:** no detection probability is estimated. The authors call the symptoms "highly noticeable" (l. 164). The 2014 survey made 22 new detections. Three of 33 later self-reports lay in surveyed hectads where the survey found nothing (l. 359-365).
- **Effort:** simulated surveys visited 4 to 24 % of locations (l. 328-329). The best maps came from surveys covering over 20 % of the host area (l. 497). Adding 29 or 145 infected locations (1 % or 5 % of infected squares) improved the maps (l. 347-349).
- **Method comparison:** the interpolation gave significantly better maps than kriging (l. 409). Stratifying the sample did not beat random selection (l. 416). Converting prevalence to presence-absence did not hurt the maps and improved the predictions (l. 424-425).
- **Result:** the final map has 246 positive and 137 negative sites. It puts about 38 % of England and Wales (56,725 km2) as likely to hold affected woodland (l. 392-401).

## Dependence

- The interpolation builds on an earlier method for spread between individual trees, by Parnell et al. (ref 31) and Luo et al. (ref 32) (l. 113-115).
- The survey design uses risk-based sampling principles from Parnell et al. 2014 (ref 33) (l. 165-167).
- Host abundance comes from the Forestry Commission's National Forest Inventory. Landowner reports come from the Tree Health Diagnostic and Advisory Service (l. 217, 240).
- The method was tested on citrus outbreak census data (l. 307-315).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Serves t2 (observation) and, through its map, t6 (regional spread). It shows two observation sources: systematic visits, and presence-only self-reports with uneven effort. The paper gives no per-visit detection rate. For acute oak decline surveyor sensitivity, see literature/combes2025.md, which is another paper.
- Its coverage result (over 20 % of host area) is a survey-design input for a surveillance module. The numbers are for oak in Great Britain, not vines.
