# Gil et al. 2021: spray volume per leaf wall area for three sprayers

Three sprayers at three growth stages in trellised vines, compared by deposit and efficiency per unit of leaf wall area (LWA). The text read is a revised manuscript. It prints no year or volume.

## What it holds

- **Sprayers**: a tractor-mounted air-assisted sprayer (TMS), a hydraulic backpack (HBS) and a pneumatic backpack mistblower (PBS) (l. 562–589).
- **Rates**: TMS at 200 L/ha LWA for the first stage and 370 for the others. The backpacks went up to 800 L/ha LWA (mistblower) and 1250 L/ha LWA (hydraulic) (l. 244–253).
- **Efficiency factor F** compares the actual deposit with the deposit expected from the adjusted LWA rate (l. 276–277).
- **Results**: at BBCH 55 the hydraulic backpack was the most efficient at every volume. The mistblower at 1000 and 1250 L/ha was the least efficient (l. 1403–1408).
- **Conclusion**: LWA helps set spray volumes, but canopy density and the sprayer both matter (l. 275–277).

## Dependence

- The LWA volume rates build on earlier vineyard work (refs 25 and 42, l. 688). The tracer method follows Gil et al. (ref 42, l. 893).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Serves t1 (spray deposit and coverage): a device-by-volume efficiency table could set a spray-volume input for a growers' engine, if the LWA units are kept. A simulator would need the deposit fractions per device.
- One site, three growth stages. Not read in full.
