# Chen, Ji & Saibaba 2026: D-optimal placement by column subset selection, scaled to tens of thousands of candidates

Chooses k of n candidate sensors for a Gaussian process by column subset selection on the candidate covariance, with randomised and Cholesky variants that avoid the full SVD, and compares D-optimality with random placements and an efficient greedy. No record: a method (preprint).

**Read this first.** It is an arXiv v1 preprint. No sensor cost enters the criterion: the "costs" in the paper are flop counts and wall-clock times (Table 1, l. 706-709; Table 3, l. 1583), not prices per site. There is no held-out placement test: the thin-film hyperparameters are tuned on the same t = 140 snapshot that the placement uses (l. 769-779), and the surrogate's hyperparameters are tuned on a different pair of bumps from the truth (l. 1738-1739). The surrogate problem is 4D in the main text (l. 1439) and 10D in Appendix A.3 (l. 1733); the inconsistency is unresolved. Eqs. 1, 9, 14 and 15 are partly lost in the text layer and are recorded as unchecked.

## What it holds

- **Objective** (Eq. 8, p. 7, l. 368): D-optimality, phi_D(S) = logdet(eta^-2 S^T K S + I_k), equal to the mutual information between the data and the unobserved function (l. 309). It is monotone submodular, so greedy is within (1 - 1/e) of the best k-subset (Eq. 13, l. 688).
- **Method** (Algorithms 1 to 3, l. 403-411, 515-552, 609-641): the conceptual GKS takes the SVD of K and pivoted QR on the top-k right singular vectors, at O(n^3 + n k^2) flops (l. 399). NysGKS uses a randomised Nystrom approximation (O(n^2 k) for the sketch, l. 555; O(n k^2) with H2-matrix products, l. 566-568); CholeskyGKS uses greedy or random pivoted Cholesky at O(n k^2) (l. 636). The efficient greedy benchmark drops from O(n k^4) to O(n k^2) with Cholesky updates (l. 679). Theorem 1 (p. 8, l. 414) bounds phi_D below for any S with rank(V_k^T S) = k; Eq. 9 is garbled.
- **Thin film, n = 6001, k = 30, t = 140** (l. 763, 781-785, 793, 805): GKS D-opt 221.39 and relative error 0.0025; RPCholesky 221.36; greedy pivoted Cholesky 221.28 and NysGKS 221.24 (l. 805); efficient greedy 218.58, the lowest of the methods. Baseline: 10,000 random placements (l. 789).
- **Sea surface temperature, k = 250** (l. 835-837, 882-883, 915-917, 935-941, 975-976): 44,219 sea points of 64,000 as candidates; weekly NOAA OI SST v2, test week 4 February 2018 (l. 854, 860). NysGKS D-opt 1074.8 and relative error 0.0781; greedy pivoted Cholesky 1076.6 (highest); POD-DEIM, trained on five years, D-opt 754.6 and error 0.0680, rising to about 15 % over time. Baseline: 10,000 random placements (l. 912-917).
- **Surrogate (Zhou function), n = 10,000, k = 3,000** (l. 1743-1749): D-opt 6157.94 (RPCholesky + GKS) and 6266.76 (greedy pivoted Cholesky + GKS), relative errors 8.84 % and 10.12 %, both much higher than random.
- **Timing** (Table 3, l. 1583): NysGKS (H2Pack) took 15.53, 23.18, 30.04, 32.13 and 38.76 s for n = 2048, 4096, 8192, 16384 and 32768 at k = 250, roughly linear in n (l. 1580).

## Dependence

- Builds on the Golub-Klema-Stewart column subset framework, efficient greedy MAP for determinantal point processes (Chen, Zhang & Zhou), randomised Nystrom (Frangella, Tropp & Udell; Halko, Martinsson & Tropp), RPCholesky, strong RRQR (Gu & Eisenstat), and Ko et al. and Krause et al. for greedy MI placement. Data: NOAA OI SST v2. No crop or disease model.

## Bearing (2026-10-21)

For Agrarium's placement-methods study (PLAN 15, D48): greedy MI alternatives and scale.

- **Scale:** the only one of these eight papers that reaches the order of Agrarium's vines: n up to 32,768 candidates (l. 1583), against about 25,000 vines. The 15.53 to 38.76 s timings use H2Pack on a smooth kernel with coordinates (l. 566-568, 1583); an empirical covariance of simulated seasons would need the naive O(n^2 k) sketch (l. 555), which is the inference to check, not a result of the paper.
- **Versus greedy:** D-optimality is close to greedy in the numbers above (221.39 against 218.58 for the film, l. 784, 805), and the paper itself reports efficient greedy at O(n k^2), the same order (Table 1, l. 709). So the gain is small and the scale argument is for the Cholesky-update greedy as much as for GKS.
- **Not shown:** held-out seasons, sensor cost or noise per site in the criterion, a re-plan, field data.
