# Gouache, Roche, Pieri and Bancal 2011: climate change and three fungal pathosystems (English chapter)

The health chapter of the CLIMATOR green book: a wetness variable, vine Botrytis (merlot) and two wheat diseases, each run on 12 French sites under A1B to 2100.

**Read this first.** There is no downy mildew here: the words "mildew" and "mildiou" do not occur in this text or in the other CLIMATOR files read (grep count 0). The vine part is Botrytis only, on merlot, from the model of Fermaud, Pieri and Mimiague 2003. The French twin is in brisson2010; gouache2010 is the talk summary.

## What it holds

- **Pathosystems (Table 1, l. 45, 65-178):** HUMEC wetness, vine Botrytis (cv merlot), wheat septoria, wheat brown rust; degrees of host-pathogen coupling and decision rules for sprays.
- **Wetness (l. 53-54, 236, 243-251):** HUMEC comes from a micrometeorological model assuming a wheat-type leaf. Wetness duration is about 1.5 times higher in the north than the south of France and falls in spring and summer with warming; wet degree-days (Figure 2) rise slightly. Figures give spring and summer means by site; the p. 47 text of gouache2010 prints 8.19 and 8.27 h falling to 7.84 and 7.32 h, and 2.92 and 4.58 wet degree-days rising to 3.52 and 4.75.
- **Vine Botrytis (l. 333-364, 537):** merlot only; sites Avignon, Bordeaux, Toulouse, Dijon plus prospective Lusignan, Versailles, Colmar. Risk days fall at traditional sites and are high and variable at newly feasible ones. Risk days against rain from veraison to maturity have a slope of about 0.2 risk days per mm (l. 360). Simulated sprays are near current practice, 0 to 2 (l. 537).
- **Limits stated (l. 707-716):** overwintering inoculum is not modelled for Botrytis and brown rust; no CO2 effect on pathogens; no pathogen adaptation; one pathogen at a time.

## Dependence

- The Botrytis model and HUMEC are not printed; only their references (Fermaud et al. 2003; Lhomme and Jimenez 1992). Nothing here shares an equation with the downy mildew models in the catalogue.

## Bearing (2026-10-30)

- **Use:** a statement, with numbers, that wetness-hours fall while wetness-degree-days rise under warming, and that a rain total (about 0.2 risk days per mm, one cultivar) tracks a fungal risk on vines. It is a check on the direction of a wetness input, not a downy mildew input.
- **Not for:** the truth or the engine's disease models; the vine disease is Botrytis and the wetness model is for wheat.
