# Alonso-Villaverde et al. 2011: stomata number on one leaf of eight Albariño clones, and a loose link to field downy mildew severity

A two-page research note that counts stomata on one fixed leaf at bloom for three years and sets the counts beside each clone's field severity; it has no leaf-age series and no infection test.

**Read this first.**
- **One leaf position, one stage.** Every count is on the 8th leaf from the base of a fertile shoot, taken when most shoots had 12 to 14 internodes, around bloom (l. 57-60). It cannot show how stomata or susceptibility change with leaf age or shoot stage.
- **The link to susceptibility is called a hint.** The authors write that the correlation "was not always given" and that stomata number is not the only factor (l. 94-96). No correlation coefficient is printed. Field severity is one three-year mean per clone, and the date of scoring is not given.
- **Text and table disagree on CSIC-9.** The text says CSIC-9 "always" had the largest number of stomata and was the most susceptible (l. 62-64). In the table CSIC-6 has more stomata in 2005 and 2007 (36 and 38 against 33 and 37; l. 80, 82), and CSIC-11 has the higher severity (0.058 against 0.050; l. 84, 82), though the two share a letter group. The table is used below.

## What it holds

- **Material:** eight clones of Albariño (CSIC-1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11) from the Misión Biológica de Galicia collection, Pontevedra (l. 53-56).
- **Sampling:** the 8th leaf from the base of a fertile green shoot, when most green shoots had 12 to 14 internodes (around bloom, June); five plants a clone; 2005, 2006 and 2007 (l. 57-60). Stomata were counted on a nail-polish print of the underside, per 0.196 mm2 (l. 4).
- **Stomata number:** the clone x year interaction was significant (P < 0.01; l. 26-27). The mean was 32 per 0.196 mm2 in 2005, 33 in 2006 and 35 in 2007, 2007 being significantly higher (l. 30-31). That is about 163 to 179 per mm2 (arithmetic, not printed). By clone and year the table runs from 27 (CSIC-3, 2005) to 38 (CSIC-6, 2007) (l. 79, 80). The authors tie the 2007 rise to cooler, wetter June and July, citing other authors on leaf development (l. 32-41); they did not test it.
- **Stomata size:** length did not differ among clones; width did, from 18.17 to 21.59 um across clones and years (l. 51-52, 77, 81).
- **Field severity:** a disease severity index, the summed area of all lesions over total leaf area, from three years of field scoring (l. 16-19). Clone means: CSIC-11 0.058, CSIC-9 0.050, CSIC-3 0.022; LSD (0.05) 0.011 (l. 84, 82, 79, 85). The scoring date and leaf stage are not stated.
- **The link:** CSIC-6 and CSIC-9 always had the largest numbers of stomata (l. 48-49), and CSIC-9 was the second most susceptible clone by the table (l. 82, 84); the authors say the results "hint" at a link to stomata number rather than size (l. 91-94). CSIC-6 had the highest count in 2005 and 2007 (36 and 38) but a severity of only 0.028 (l. 80), so the link is loose across the eight clones.

## Dependence

- **Own data.** The stomata method is after D'Ambrogio de Argüeso 1986 and the severity index after Boso et al. 2004 (l. 4, 19). It cites Lu et al. 2010, Jürges et al. 2009 and Allègre et al. 2006 on stomata and susceptibility.
- **Authors:** Boso, Alonso-Villaverde, Gago, Santiago and Martínez also wrote the held `boso2011` (44 varieties, Pontevedra). A shared group is a flag only. Alonso-Villaverde is also an author of Gindro et al. 2012 (stomata of clusters by electron microscopy), which was not obtained.

## Bearing (2026-10-25)

- **Decision 19 (susceptible from budburst, or from 10 cm or four unfolded leaves):** nothing. It has no shoot-stage or leaf-age series and no infection test. Its only stage is bloom, on the 8th leaf.
- **Leaf ontogenic resistance (the truth has no leaf cohorts):** not shown. It gives one density for a nearly full-grown leaf (about 163 to 179 stomata per mm2, Albariño, Pontevedra; arithmetic, not printed), so it neither supports nor contradicts a change with leaf age. Kind of result: a measurement of stomata, with a field severity index; not an infection experiment.
- **host.inflorescence_after_budburst_days and host.rachis_only_factor:** nothing. It is about leaves only. The one point it adds is that clones of one cultivar differ in field severity by about 2.6 times (0.022 against 0.058, arithmetic, not printed), a clone spread the truth does not model.
