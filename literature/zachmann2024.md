# Zachmann et al. 2024: expected adoption of fungus-resistant grapevines in Switzerland

A survey of 436 Swiss grapevine growers on the land share they expect to plant with fungus-resistant varieties in ten years. It also tests which farm factors go with that expectation.

## What it holds

- **Survey (l. 19-21):** 436 growers. About one third of new plantings in the next decade are expected to be resistant (l. 21, 130).
- **Land share (l. 22-24, 131-133):** 27.4 % expected in ten years, against 10.2 % in 2022, a rise of 169 %.
- **Scale (l. 683-684):** the authors reckon at least about 32,000 fewer fungicide treatments a year at that share. The stated assumptions are in footnote 16.

## Dependence

The expected value is taken from a triangular distribution around the elicited share, after Hardaker et al. (2015) (l. 125-127). The 80 % fungicide cut from resistant varieties is cited, not measured, to Poni et al. (2017) and Viret et al. (2019) (l. 68-70).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- **Strand t1 (fungicide protection), by adoption.** A farm-level scenario for how much resistant land could cut sprays in Switzerland. The input is a land share over time, not a spray dose.
- **Not read in full.** The 32,000 treatments figure rests on the stated stock and replanting assumptions (footnote 16). Check those before a simulator uses it.
