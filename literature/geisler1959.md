# Geisler 1959: zoospore release and germination against the osmotic value

Open access (Vitis), fetched 2026-10-12. A laboratory note from Geilweilerhof on how the
osmotic value of the medium changes sporangium emptying and germ-tube formation; no
weather, no field.

## What it holds

- Sporangia from fresh sporangiophores in hanging drops of cane-sugar solutions up to 36
  atm at 25-27 °C, emptied sporangia counted about every 20 min (l. 40-60); conidia 1-2
  days old already release fewer zoospores (l. 47-48).
- Table 1 (0, 1.1, 2.7, 4.0, 11.2 atm): 74 % emptied at 3 h 20 in water, 95 % at 4 h 44
  at 1.1 atm, 0 % at 2 h 40 at 11.2 atm (l. 67-85); the optimum for zoospore formation is
  1.1 atm, release barely slowed to 3 atm, strongly slowed between 3 and 10, almost
  stopped at 10-12 atm, yet single sporangia still empty after days at 36 atm (l.
  100-146).
- Germ tubes form best at 8-10 atm (about 9), rarely in distilled water where zoospores
  die first, rarely up to 36 atm (l. 148-170); leaf osmotic values of 7-15 atm overlap
  that optimum, which the author says cannot explain species resistance (l. 204-212).
- Cited: Wüthrich 1892, Arens 1929, Maier 1941 (direct conidial germination), Millardet.
