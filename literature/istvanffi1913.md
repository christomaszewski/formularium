# Istvánffi 1913: the latent period by time of season, before Müller and Goidanich

## What it holds

- **Definition:** the latent period runs from zoospore penetration to the full oil spot.
- **Methods:** natural infections after rain, dated from the institute vineyard's weather
  station; and artificial infections in the open, the more reliable. Results of 1911 and
  1912 combined.
- **Latent period on leaves, by half-month:**
  - early and mid-May, "when the weather is still relatively cool": 15-18 days;
  - late May: 12-15;
  - early June: 11-13;
  - mid-June: 9-11;
  - late June: 6-7;
  - July and August: 5-6.

  "It shortens gradually as the weather warms."
- **Humidity:** humid air or wet vines shorten it by 1-2 days, and continuous warm wet
  weather to 4-5 days. Dry air lengthens it by 1-2 days or more.
- **Bunches:**
  - 12-14 days in early June, 9-11 in mid-June, 10-12 at the end of June and 12-14 in
    early July;
  - longer through the berry stalk (+1-2 days) and rachis (+4-5), up to 17-18 days.
- **Temperature, qualitatively:** sporulation after 5-6 days at 10 °C; 1-2 days above 30 °C
  kill the mycelium; optimum 18-22 °C at 95-100 % RH.
- **Spraying:** after a heavy rain of 10-12 May, expect oil spots about 25-28 May (15-18
  days). Probes of 40-50 leaves kept 3-4 days in a warm place show spots early.
- **Not printed:** temperatures by period, rain amounts, counts of infections, the
  vineyard's location, values by year.

## Dependence

- The author's own 1911-1912 records. It predates Müller & Sleumer 1934 and Goidanich et al.
  1957, and cites neither. Whether they used it is not stated here.
- Zachos 1959 cites Istvánffi & Pálinkás 1913 on oospores ([zachos1959](zachos1959.md)), a
  different paper.

## Bearing (2026-10-09)

- An incubation calendar independent of the engine's Goidanich and Zachos data, but by date,
  not by temperature. Without the station's temperatures it can be matched as a pattern,
  not fitted as a response.
