# Choudhury et al. 2017: airborne Peronospora effusa near spinach fields in Salinas

Rotating-arm impaction traps with a species-specific qPCR follow airborne P. effusa DNA by a susceptible field in two winters (l. 22-28). The pathogen is Peronospora effusa on spinach, not Plasmopara on grapevine.

## What it holds

- **Seasons:** winters 2013-14 and 2014-15 (l. 26-27); DNA low from December to February, rising in January with disease onset (l. 29-32).
- **Spatial:** disease incidence dependent within an average range of 5.6 m (l. 24-25); spore distribution during an epidemic fit a power law best, an exponential also (l. 26-28).
- **Long-distance dispersal:** two traps by a commercial field in Soledad (to 3 April 2013, l. 104); Salinas trap data 30 January to 11 February 2014, with five other sites 15 to 20 km apart (l. 107-111).
- **Weather:** NARR reanalysis at 32 km and 3 h (l. 141-142), nested WRF to 4 km, MtnRT at 1.5 km giving hourly data (l. 146-148); WRF 12 km for early 2015 (l. 153-155).

## Dependence

- Spore-trap qPCR assay from Klosterman et al. 2014 (l. 59, 62); AIC for model choice.

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- **s1 autumn inoculum / s6 methods:** a way to see off-season airborne inoculum and its dispersal shape (power law). Host and climate differ from grapevine. Not read in full.
