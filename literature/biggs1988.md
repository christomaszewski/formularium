# Biggs & Northover 1988: brown rot infection of peach and cherry by temperature and wetness

From Agriculture Canada, Vineland Station, Ontario. Stone fruit; read for its forms.

## What it holds

- Brown rot (Monilinia fructicola) on fruit: sweet cherry (Vista, Bing) and peach (Loring), Jordan Station, Ontario, 1985 (l. 70, 83).
- Droplet inoculation, 15 to 30 C in 2.5 C steps; wetness 6-18 h (cherry) and 3-15 h (peach); 25 cherries or 12 peaches per cell, two experiments (l. 16, 74, 90).
- Cherry infection is logistic: logit = b0 + b1 W + b2 T + b3 WT + b4 T^3, combined b0 -11.715, b1 0.529, b2 0.466, b3 -0.0073, b4 -3.1e-4 (l. 252-258). R2 0.929. Optimum ~19.6 C (l. 362).
- Peach infection is a polynomial: Y = b0 + b1 T + b2 WT + b3 WT^3 = -0.546 + 0.0255 T + 0.00462 WT - 3.7e-6 WT^3 (l. 489-514); optimum ~23.9 C (l. 405).
- Cherry incubation period uses the Analytis Beta form: ln IP = 5.369 - 0.173 ln t - 0.0891 ln(1-t) - 0.288 ln W; Tmin 3, Tmax 33 C from ref 8 (l. 159, 307-313).
- Analytis form on incidence fits worse (l. 421).
- Form cited to Bulger et al. 1987 (l. 155). Own data. No model dated them. Wetness is droplet time on fruit (l. 126). Tables are OCR-garbled; exponents read from the PDF image.
