# Borghezan et al. 2012: shoot growth of Merlot and Cabernet Sauvignon at 1,293 m

Two seasons of weekly shoot length at a high-altitude Santa Catarina vineyard, fitted
with cubic polynomials on days after budburst and plotted against photoperiod. A
growth paper; the photoperiod relation Chris asked about is a scatter without an
equation.

## What it holds

- **Site:** São Joaquim, 1,293 m, planted 2002 on Paulsen 1103 (l. 74-78); station 2,800 m
  away, with photoperiod of unstated source (l. 89-94); 2006/07 median 16.1 °C, 1,112
  mm between budburst and harvest; 2008/09 mean 13.9 °C, 1,474 mm (l. 126-133, 240-243).
- **Stages** (Table 1): Cabernet Sauvignon budburst 25 September, veraison 31 January,
  harvest 17 April (2006/07) and 27 September, 25 February, 18 April (2008/09); Merlot
  18 September to 3 April; harvest above 21 °Brix (l. 166-174, 196-221).
- **Growth:** about 4 cm a day at the peak around day 30 (36 mm a day in the text), means
  14-16 cm a week, under 10 mm a day at veraison, cessation 143 d after budburst in
  2008/09 (l. 25, 203-236); cubic fits y = −0.000002x³ + 0.0004x² + 0.008x − 0.055 (R²
  0.91, Cabernet) and similar for Merlot, in Figure 1 only, with y unstated and the
  curve not matching the plotted points by eye (l. 148). Figure 3 plots growth against
  photoperiod (13-15 h) with no equation (l. 253).
- Leeuwen et al. 2004 cited for shoots stopping under 5 mm a day and about 170 d after
  budburst (l. 217, 248).
