# Taylor 2018: where Western Australia's downy mildew survives the winter

A thesis on a clonal population: does the disease overwinter as oospores or as mycelium in buds, and
are there oospores at all. Chapter 6 is an economic spread model.

## What it holds

- **Oospores are present** (Ch. 5, l. 328, 3897, 4034-4046, 4277-4298): leaves with mosaic symptoms
  (infection limited by veins; Emmett et al. 1992b; Vercesi et al. 1999, 2000) were taken from ten
  vineyards in the Swan Valley, Pemberton and Margaret River in April 2015, after the spring 2014
  infections "of various levels of severity" (about 50 leaves each); oospores in all ten, and in an
  unmanaged vineyard from November 2015. Density from 4 to 10 oospores in some leaf strips to
  several hundred in others; diameter 30.6 um (SD 3.87, n = 20), double-walled.
- **Against Killigrew et al. 2005** (l. 938, 4348-4354): that survey (2001-2003, 3-5 years after
  the disease reached WA) found oospores in one of 16 vineyards, and is cited under the title
  "Absence of oospores of downy mildew of grape caused by Plasmopara viticola as the source of
  primary inoculum in most..." (l. 6929). The thesis puts the difference down to the 17 years
  since detection, and also notes the difficulty of seeing oospores in leaf tissue. Killigrew
  et al. 2005 is not held here; its "absence of oospores" is known only as this thesis cites it.
- **Mating** (Ch. 5, l. 3903, 4199-4331): 11 isolates, 726 disc crosses at 2.5e4 sporangia per ml;
  oospores in three, two of them crosses (AT326 x AT101, AT326 x AT312) and one a self-cross
  (AT324). No mating-type ratio; the thesis questions whether both types occur in WA.
- **Population** (Ch. 3, l. 275, 289, 2492): 413 samples worldwide; WA populations are clonal (27
  clone-corrected isolates, 26 genotypes; lowest diversity of all); US and French ones sexual.
- **Not overwintering in buds** (Ch. 4, l. 313, 2850, 3419-3489, 3856): no flag shoots in WA
  vineyards. Of 575 shoots tagged in late December 2014, 154 gave healthy new-season shoots, 87 buds
  were killed and none produced systemically infected shoots. Cuttings with an infected node: 47 %
  budburst against 94 % in controls; cuttings inoculated on 21 December and 21 January (2017) had 25 and 45 % of nodes
  infected and 17.5 and 35 % dead buds at budburst (Table 4.2). DNA was amplified from 33 % of healthy-looking buds and 75-83 % of dead or infected ones.
  Conclusion: flag shoots are not a source of primary infection in WA, and oospores are
  (l. 5530).
- **Autumn and the next season** (Ch. 7, l. 5392-5404): higher autumn incidence has been
  associated with higher severity the next season (Carisse 2016, cited, not tested here); a longer
  period from harvest to leaf senescence leaves more unprotected leaves.
- **Oospore literature cited** (l. 3953-3978, 4432): mature over winter on the floor but may not
  germinate (Rossi & Caffi 2007; Vercesi et al. 1999); survive several years in soil (Caffi et al.
  2011; Jermini et al. 2003); germinate at 10 to 28 °C, optimum 20-22 °C (Burruano et al. 2006).
- **Chapter 6** (l. 4605-4990): a spread-and-cost model after Cook et al. (2015): logistic density,
  satellite sites, a weather variable w. Weather rule for oospore infection: shoots 10 cm or more, 10
  mm of rain, at least 10 °C over 24-48 h (Rossi & Caffi 2012); for secondary infection 98 %
  humidity, 4 h of darkness, 13 °C minimum (Gessler et al. 2011). Favourable weather one year in
  three (text) or 30 % a year (Table 6.1): a conflict. Table 6.1 PERT ranges: new foci 1-5e-2 per m2,
  growth rate 1-2 per year, K 100-1000 per m2, diffusion 2-4e5 m2 per year, yield reduction despite
  control 2-5 %.
- **Not printed:** oospore viability or germination in WA, oospore decay in days, how long
  mycelium lives in buds beyond the season, the mating-type ratio, any dataset or licence for the
  survey.

## Dependence

- Chapter 6 adapts Cook et al. 2015's spread model and takes the weather rules from Rossi & Caffi 2012 and
  Gessler et al. 2011: a borrowed form, not a model the tools run. The thesis cites `vercesi1999`
  and `reuveni1998` for oospore and leaf-age points. No record here.

## Bearing (2026-10-19)

For the autumn before a season:

- **Leaves from a diseased spring carry oospores in the autumn**, in every vineyard sampled in a
  clonal population, at densities from a few to several hundred per leaf strip; no relation
  between the severity of the spring and the oospore density is given.
- **Oospores, not buds, start the season in WA**: no flag shoots, and infected buds die. So for
  a truth that starts a season from the autumn's oospore pool, this thesis supports that route
  for Western Australia and none through surviving mycelium (the inference of one region and
  one clonal lineage; the survey did not follow oospores to spring infections).
- **Bearing on Killigrew et al. 2005's "absence of oospores"** (not held): a WA survey 17 years
  after detection found oospores in 10 of 10 vineyards, so the earlier absence reflects time since
  introduction or detection method, not a feature of WA climate; the thesis proposes the former.
- **Autumn incidence and next season:** only cited (Carisse 2016), not measured.
