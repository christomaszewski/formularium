# Bois et al. 2018: regression-kriged daily Tmin and Tmax for Bordeaux, and a maturity-date zoning from the GFV model

Daily Tmin and Tmax from 32 to 68 stations, 2001-2005, interpolated at 50 m by regression kriging, checked by leave-one-out, and used to run the GFV thermal-sum phenology model for Merlot.

**Read this first.** A zoning paper, with the same stations and period as bois2008c; its value for Agrarium is the error sizes of an interpolated temperature field and the GFV parameters for Merlot. Extraction of the text columns is scrambled in places, so equations and Figures 2-4 are not used, only text numbers and Tables 1-2. Nothing here is in-canopy.

## What it holds

- **Data (l. 185-195):** daily Tmin and Tmax from DEMETER and Meteo-France Cimel stations, 2001-2005 (1826 days); stations 32 in 2001 rising to 68 from 2003; 221 series (4565 values, 2.3 %) discarded as outliers (database errors, one-day lag, drift, sudden gaps).
- **Method:** four interpolators benchmarked (nearest neighbour, ordinary kriging, multiple linear regression, regression kriging); regression kriging clearly best and retained. Covariates at 50 m: DEM descriptors (Joly et al. 2012) and a Landsat 7 NDVI (l. 316-323). Leave-one-out cross validation; R2, modelling efficiency EF and RMSE.
- **GFV model (l. 289-309, Table 1):** sum of daily mean temperature above 0 C from day of year 60; stages when the sum reaches F*. Merlot: mid-flowering 1266 (RMSE 4.24 d), mid-veraison 2627 (1.75 d), 200 g per L sugar 3165 (7.89 d; l. 281-283 cites 7.98 d), after Parker et al. 2011 and 2012. Harvest comes 2 to 3 weeks after the 200 g per L date.
- **Indices checked by cross validation (l. 361-380):** mean Tmin, April-September, R2 0.54 to 0.69; Tavg 0.41 to 0.61; Tmax 0.48 to 0.81; cool night index 0.59 to 0.62; spring frost day counts (Tmin at or below -1 C, 1 March to 30 June) poor in years with fewer than 2 frost days; high heat days (Tmax at or above 35 C, 1 June to 30 September) fair in warm years. RK underestimates spatial variability (regression slopes below 1). Interpolating daily temperature then computing the index beat the reverse, mostly, and clearly so for frost and heat day counts.
- **Phenology check (l. 703-715):** 112 Merlot plots with mid-flowering dates (320 observations) and 32 with mid-veraison (68 observations), observation uncertainty up to 6 days (3.5 average). Predicted mid-flowering explains at most 36 % of the variance within a vintage; mid-veraison up to 77 % (2003). The model underestimates spatial variability.
- **Zoning (l. 731-745, Table 2):** five equal-interval classes of the day of 200 g per L sugar over 912 418 pixels; class means differ by 2 to 4 days, extremes up to 20 days (2001: very early 242 to very late 255, average difference 14 d, maximum 20 d; 2003 228 to 236, 8 d and 11 d). Class standard deviations 0.6 to 1.6 d, about the cross-validation RMSE of 1.6 to 2.9 d. 69.1 % of the AOC area is intermediate; early areas along the Garonne and Dordogne valleys; late in the northwest Medoc and the northeast.

## Dependence

- GFV from Parker et al. 2011 and Parker 2012 (not held); RK from Odeh et al. 1994; shares stations, period and approach with bois2008c; independent of the engine's phenology pieces unless Cooptera runs a GFV or Ramos/Molitor sum (check the D22/D44 kinship before use).

## Bearing (2026-10-30)

- **Weather and station error:** the temperature interpolation errors quoted (R2 0.4 to 0.8 for seasonal means, poor skill for rare frost days) give a realistic range for a simulated station that does not sit at the site, and show that frost and high-heat day counts are the most error-sensitive indices.
- **GFV parameters for Merlot** (thresholds above 0 C from day 60) are a ready phenology kernel if Agrarium wants a thermal-sum phenology check; its kinship with the engine's phenology pieces is for the D22/D44 rule to settle.
- **Not shown:** in-canopy or humidity values, a station-versus-site correction, hourly temperature.
