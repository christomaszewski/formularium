# Gadoury et al. 2003: ontogenic resistance of grape berries to powdery mildew

From Cornell (NYSAES Geneva).

## What it holds

- Inoculation experiments in a 1-ha V. vinifera vineyard (planted 1974; Chardonnay, Riesling, Gewurztraminer, Pinot Noir) in New York State, 1996-1998 (l. 44, 46).
- Mildew-free bagged clusters were inoculated at 5-6 stages from 10% bloom to 9-12% brix; 5 clusters per vine on 3 vines (l. 74, 82, 85).
- Only fruit inoculated within 2 weeks of bloom developed severe disease (l. 26-27). Fruit were nearly immune 3 to 4 weeks after bloom (l. 148).
- Brix reached 8% only at 55 to 70 days postbloom (l. 187-189) and 15% near 100 days (l. 191). Resistance came weeks before both thresholds.
- One equation: Y = 5.962 - 3.557X, Y = log10 relative severity (%), X = log10 days after bloom, valid 13 to 56 days, R2 97.9% (l. 194, 197).
- Relative susceptibility is 100% before day 13 (l. 114). Predicted: 99.9% at 13 d, 21.6% at 20 d, 5.1% at 30 d (l. 171-172).
- Fitted to the authors' own pooled inoculation severities, each cultivar-year scaled to 100% at its maximum (l. 94-97). No model dated or shaped the data.
- Rachis inoculated at 26 days postbloom got only trace mildew (l. 175-176); rachis inoculated at bloom kept worsening (l. 177-179).
- Two sprays 10 days apart from bloom controlled fruit mildew as well as eight (l. 225-231).
- The authors suggest degree-days could replace days after bloom elsewhere (l. 245); not done here.

## Dependence

- Its own inoculations; the one equation, relative susceptibility on days after bloom (Y = 5.962 - 3.557X, the dash read as a minus by recomputation), is fitted to them. No model dated the data.
