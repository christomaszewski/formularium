# Calvo-Garrido et al. 2019: biocontrol of Botrytis bunch rot in field trials

Ten field trials in six vineyards over three seasons compared one new Bacillus strain (S38) and nine commercial biocontrol products against Botrytis bunch rot in south-west France.

## What it holds

- Ten field experiments in six vineyards over three seasons, 2015 to 2017 (l. 34-35). Sprays went on at five or six key stages, or late in the season at a Disease Risk Index (DRI) trigger (l. 37-39).
- Strain S38 cut average severity by 35 to 60% (l. 50).
- Commercial products cut severity by 21 to 58%, not in every trial (l. 53). Bacillus amyloliquefaciens reached 58% (l. 56).
- Incidence and severity (% symptomatic berries per bunch) were scored at harvest (l. 38-39).

## Dependence

- Uses the DRI, which the paper says was developed earlier at INRA Bordeaux-Aquitaine (l. 225-226).
- The S38 strain was chosen on the authors' earlier lab and vineyard work (Introduction).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t4 (Botrytis bunch rot), with t1 for the late-season spray timing. The DRI is a degree-day index, WI = 4 × 10⁻⁷ X² + 0.0003 X + 0.0988, where X is the sum of daily mean temperatures from a station near the plot (l. 242). Late sprays began only after first symptoms passed 1% incidence (l. 250-253). A growers' engine could copy that trigger rule, not the efficacy figures.
- Efficacy varied by trial and season (l. 53-54). A simulator should carry that spread, not one efficacy value. Not read in full.
