# Binks et al. 2020: canopy wetness in an Amazon rainforest

A study of leaf wetness sensors and a physical model in a tropical forest canopy at the Caxiuana reserve; only the discussion of dew was read.

**Read this first.** The site is a rainforest, not a crop canopy. Only one passage was read, so the rest (model, sensors, rain drying) is not covered here.

## What it holds

- **Dew drying (l. 586):** dew typically dries out by about 08:00 to 08:30 in the wet season (sunrise 06:23) and an hour earlier in the dry season (sunrise 05:52), so the canopy is commonly drying for the first 1 to 1.5 h every morning.
- **Dew share (grep, l. 586):** dew events that occur independently of rain account for 36 % of the total wetness in the dry season (Analysis B, Tables 3 and 4).
- **Rain (grep, l. 586 area):** rain events are the dominant driver of leaf wetness through the year (Table 4, Analysis B).
