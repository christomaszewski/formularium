# Wang & Cheng 2025: sentinel selection on networks by emergence probability and topology

A simulation study of choosing surveillance sentinel sites on modular networks, with a genetic algorithm and a random-forest surrogate (l. 15-24 of the abstract). The printed title is not the record's title ('Network topology outweighs emergence probability ...'). A preprint, not peer reviewed. No record.

## What it holds

- **Method:** outbreaks simulated on synthetic modular networks; detection scored as the reduction in outbreak size; a genetic algorithm finds sentinel sets, which train a random-forest surrogate (RFSM) (abstract l. 15-20).
- **Result:** strategies using emergence probability beat topology-only ones, though modestly (l. 25-26); the surrogate matches the genetic algorithm with about 1/24,000 of its run time and 1/100 of the greedy run time at 200 nodes (l. 26-28).
- **Sentinels:** six sentinels reach about 90 % of the performance of full-network surveillance (l. 33-34); overlap among sentinels is the most important feature (l. 28-29).

## Dependence

- Builds on synthetic network models, genetic algorithm and greedy baselines, and a random-forest surrogate.

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- **Strand s6 (methods):** a surrogate that ranks sensor placements cheaply once an expensive search has been run, plus the finding that overlap among sensors counts. A simulator could take the overlap penalty for trap and sensor placement. Synthetic networks, no field data.
