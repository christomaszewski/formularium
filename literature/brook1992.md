# Brook 1992: eleven Auckland seasons read with Galet's rules

Eleven seasons (1971/72 to 1981/82) of weekly leaf records on tagged canes of Black
Hamburgh at the Mt Albert Research Centre, with the station's rain and temperature 450 m
away, read against borrowed thresholds; the author's verdict is that primary infection in
Auckland is too rare for forecasting models to pay. From DSIR Plant Protection.

## What it holds

- **Site:** 42 vines planted 1965 (l. 112-114); sulphur and carbaryl every two weeks, no
  fungicide in 1971/72 (l. 128-131). One bud tagged on each of six vines, every leaf's
  status recorded about weekly (l. 136-138).
- **Rules used, all borrowed (p. 38):** primary infection on two consecutive wet days
  with oospores continuously wet (Galet 1977), rain exceeding the month's mean
  evaporation, 2.5 mm/d in September to 5.3 in January (Hessell 1988; l. 191-197);
  secondary infection at 9 h of leaf wetness at 6 °C falling to 2 h at 25 °C (Blaeser &
  Weltzien 1977, l. 199-200); incubation 18 and 13 d on old leaves, 12 and 7-8 d on
  young, at 14 and 20 °C (Rafaila et al. 1968; Galet 1977; l. 206-210). For
  anthracnose, 7-10 h of wetness at 12 °C and 3-4 h at 21 °C, incubation 7-12, 5-7 and
  3-4 d at 12, 16.5 and 21 °C (Brook 1973, l. 187-203).
- **Own incubation test** (February 1978, potted Palomino outdoors at a mean 20.6 °C):
  sporulation on the fourth night, chlorosis on young leaves on day 7, on the oldest on
  day 13 (l. 216-219).
- **Table 7 (p. 44; `brook1992.auckland`):** first downy mildew lesions from 19 November
  (1973/74) to 2 February (1977/78), none in 1972/73, with the wet period the author
  postulates as the primary infection, its net rain (4.1 to 123.2 mm) and mean air
  temperature (15.8-20.7 °C). Primary infection never before a mean daily temperature of
  about 15 °C (l. 2584; Roussel 1971's germination threshold 11 °C, l. 2588), mainly in
  November-December (l. 2591), always one or two lesions (p. 47).
- **Table 10 (p. 47):** 50 days after the first lesion, 74.2, 42.4 and 55.3 % of leaves
  mildewed in 1973/74, 1978/79 and 1980/81 on 48, 58 and 36 mm of rain, against 0.8 and
  2.5 % in 1975/76 and 1981/82 on 234 and 99 mm (l. 2261-2262): the wettest seasons had
  the least mildew, against Galet's rain rule (l. 2736), and the author proposes an
  optimum beyond which heavy rain hinders (l. 2741). Under the "rhythm of rains", less
  than 1 mm sufficed: the rain of 3 January 1974 infected 90 leaves (l. 2240, 2636).
- **Verdict:** EPI (Strizyk 1983), Tran Manh Sung et al. 1990 and Muckensturm et al. 1990
  are cited and not used (l. 2774-2775); where primary infections give so few lesions,
  prediction models are "probably inappropriate" and monitoring (Seem et al. 1985) is
  the alternative (l. 2777-2782).

## Dependence

- **Computes** no model: the infection periods in Table 7 are the author's reading of
  the rain record by Galet 1977's two-wet-days rule, so they are a rule's dates, not
  observed ones; the first-lesion dates are observed. The thresholds are Blaeser &
  Weltzien 1977's (not held; `blaeser1978` and `blaeser1979` are the pair the catalogue
  holds) and Rafaila et al. 1968's (`rafaila1968.incubation`).
- **Authors:** none shared with engine models.

## Bearing (2026-10-13)

- An eleven-season observed record from a wet maritime climate whose two wettest seasons
  had the least mildew: a counter-case for any rain-driven season-severity rule, and a
  warning that Galet's two-wet-days and 10 mm rules are not universal.
- Evidence that under 1 mm of rain can infect once lesions are present, the point
  `barrios2004` makes for 3-5 mm springs.

## Bearing (2026-10-25)

Re-read for primary infection counts. In an isolated Auckland vineyard (42 vines, about
300 m²), primary infection was "invariably one or two lesions" a season, about 0.005 per m²
(arithmetic, not printed), and there was no disease at the end of December in 8 of 11
seasons. The lowest count found by Agrarium's 2026-10-25 pass: a low-inoculum case, against
0.07-3.5 per m² in untreated European and Chinese plots (`gobbin2005`, `yu2022`).
