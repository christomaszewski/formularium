# Gallardo et al. 2009: forecasting European grapevine moth flights in Extremadura

Degree-day regressions on twelve years of pheromone-trap catches, to forecast the second and third flights of Lobesia botrana in Ribera del Guadiana.

## What it holds

- Twelve pheromone delta traps in six vineyard districts, 1990 to 2002. The year 1996 was left out (l. 59–60, 72, 92).
- The first flight is minor and was not forecast. Its degree-days were used for the later flights (abstract, l. 19–25).
- For the second and third flights, log-probit linear equations give pooled r = 0.836 and 0.873 (l. 323; Table 2, l. 378). Per-year second-flight fits had r above 0.95 in 90 % of cases (l. 314–316).
- Errors were largest for the second flight (l. 310–311).

## Dependence

- Degree-days from 1 March, with 7 and 30 °C thresholds, by the sine method of González-Andújar and Hernández Vinuesa (1990) (l. 96–99, 122–124).
- Compared with an earlier Andalusian study by Del et al. (2001) (l. 102).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Serves t7 (insect pests): day-degrees from 1 March, and the day when 50 % of the previous flight is caught, set the timing of the next flight (l. 96–104). A vineyard simulator could take that rule, but the fits are for one region and one trap network.
