# Aylor and Ferrandino 1985: the share of bean-rust spores that leave a closed bean canopy

Spore concentration was profiled above a severely diseased bean field, and the upward flux was set against the deposition on sticky slides inside the canopy to estimate how much inoculum escapes and how much stays.

**Read this first.** An analogue. Bean rust (Uromyces phaseoli) is a rust, not a downy mildew, and the crop is a bush bean 0.5 m tall in a 46 x 52 m field (about 0.25 ha), not a vine. The "10 to 18 % escaped" rests on an assumption: that deposition on foliage and ground equals deposition on the sticky slides (l. 446-448). It does not separate leaf from ground. The text is OCR of a scan ("fora" for "for a"); no number was lost.

## What it holds

- **Site and canopy:** a bush bean field (Phaseolus vulgaris, 'Bush Blue Lake 47') at Hamden, Connecticut, planted 22 May, 46 x 52 m, rows 0.75 m apart (l. 61-63); the Discussion gives 17.5 plants per m of row and rows 0.76 m apart (l. 452). Crop about 0.5 m tall; leaf area index about 3.4 on 19 July, 3.5 on 20 July and 3.6 on 23 July (l. 203-205). Severely diseased (l. 18).
- **Measured:** the upward flux (escape) of single urediniospores 0.5 m above the canopy was 6 to 31 spores per m2 per s (l. 23); singlets escaped about 2 to 7 times more than doublets and about 5 to 30 times more than triplets (l. 27-28), and the shares escaping matched the shares released, as caught on sticky slides in the crop (abstract).
- **Escape against deposition:** the calculated upward flux was 50 to 100 % of the coincident deposition flux on the sticky slides inside the canopy (l. 444-445).
- **Share that escapes:** assuming deposition on foliage and ground equalled that on the slides, between 10 and 18 % of the total inoculum escaped the canopy (l. 446-448), so 82 to 90 % was deposited on foliage and ground inside the field (arithmetic, not printed). This agrees with the authors' transport model (l. 448-449).
- **Source strength:** with about 2,000 lesions per plant (l. 451) and 17.5 plants per m of row (l. 452), 6 to 30 singlets were released per lesion per hour (l. 453-454). The authors say counted lesions include non-sporulating ones, so this is probably low (l. 454-456).
- **Conditions:** a fully closed canopy, H = 0.5 m, LAI = 3.5 and friction velocity about 0.25 m/s (l. 457-458); the authors suggest the values may serve elsewhere.
- **Background (cited, not measured):** in the canopy, deposition on plants and on the ground makes the aerial concentration fall rapidly with distance from a source (l. 36-37, with the paper's ref 1); only spores that escape into faster air spread between fields (l. 38-40).

## Dependence

- The spore transport model of Aylor (the paper's refs 2 and 3). The estimate of deposition on foliage and ground borrows the slide deposition; there is no separate leaf-capture efficiency. The same first author wrote `aylor1983`: a flag only.

## Bearing (2026-10-25)

- **dm.capture_fraction 0.3** (share of sporangia landing in a vine's cell that land on its leaves): the closest measured share of any paper in this strand, and still not it. In a closed bean canopy 82 to 90 % of the spores released stayed inside, on foliage and ground together (l. 446-448). A leaf-only share cannot be read from it, so it neither supports nor contradicts 0.3. Bush bean, one field, one week in July; a vine canopy is more open and has trunks and wires, so the numbers do not carry over.
- **dm.wind_scale_m 3 m and dm.kernel_radius_m 40 m:** nothing quantitative. The paper only states that in-canopy deposition makes concentration fall quickly with distance (l. 36-37, cited).
- **Kind of result:** measured flux and slide deposition, with the retained share inferred under an assumption. An analogue.
