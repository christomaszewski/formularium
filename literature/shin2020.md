# Shin et al. 2020: leaf wetness from a geostationary satellite and machine learning

From the Korean National Institute of Meteorological Sciences. Two copies were dropped, extracted differently; the numbers agree.

## What it holds

- Remote Sensing 12(18):3076 (2020). Hourly leaf wetness predicted from GEO-KOMPSAT-2A imager radiances (16 channels, 2 min scans, 0.5 to 2 km; scan at minute 00 used) with six ML classifiers, scored against Penman-Monteith (PM) fed with station weather.
- Data: Korean RDA stations, Campbell 237 pairs, November 2019 to April 2020 only (l. 120, l. 740). 211 stations, 21 kept (l. 173, l. 183); kept only if the RH >= 90% rule correlated r > 0.6 with the sensor (l. 182).
- Split by station: 2 test stations (8409 h, l. 246), 19 training stations (79,749 h, l. 250); hyperparameters tuned on 20,000 validation points.
- Test hours about 80% dry (l. 541), so always-dry scores 0.8.
- Accuracy: RF 0.8226, PM 0.7878, DNN 0.7094, LR 0.6493 (Table 2, l. 528, l. 524). F: PM 0.5157, RF 0.4563; RF recall 0.3360 (l. 524, l. 528).
- Daily LWD RMSE (Table 3): PM 5.15 h, DNN 5.19, RF 5.50 (l. 592, l. 596, l. 598); Fig. 6 gives RF 6.791 h without zero days (l. 581).
- PM constants: gamma 0.64 kPa/K, D 0.07 m, d 0.5 Zc (l. 213, l. 221, l. 226); net radiation from ASCE method (l. 232).
- Authors say results are indicative only (l. 747); PM judged best overall (l. 696).
- Dependence: PM follows Sentelhas et al. (2006); stations pre-filtered by the NHRH rule; ML fitted to same-network data. No model dated the wetness data.

## Dependence

- **Its training data were selected by an engine model:** stations were kept only where the RH-hours method of Sentelhas et al. 2008 (its ref. 36, the engine's `sentelhas2008.wetness`) predicted the sensors' wetness with r > 0.6 (l. 176-182). Its machine-learning models are thereby calibrated with that model: kin under D27 as amended.
- Its Penman-Monteith model follows Sentelhas's (ref. 13) and Rao et al.

## Bearing (2026-10-08)

- **Kin by calibration** (Agrarium's candidates). As an observation operator, satellite wetness's error is the useful number: accuracy 0.82 for random forest against about 0.80 for always guessing dry, in a test set about 80% dry.
