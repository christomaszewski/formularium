# Hasanaliyeva et al. 2024: cover crops and dispersal from the ground

Small-scale trials and two organic Italian vineyards show that cover crops reduce airborne and
  splash-borne inoculum leaving the ground and delay downy and powdery mildew epidemics.

## What it holds

- **Small trials:** horseradish cover cut airborne Botrytis conidia escaping the canopy by more
  than 85 % (l. 43-46); raindrops hitting the soil down 46 % to 74 %, splash droplets escaping
  the ground down 75 % to 95 % (l. 47-51).
- **Vineyards:** two organic vineyards, two years (2019, 2020; Table 1, l. 183 on, Table 2 l.
  355); fall- or spring-sown covers delayed epidemics by 14 to 30 days and reduced them by up
  to 90 % (l. 52-58), more in untreated plots.
- **Weather:** automatic weather stations in the vineyards (l. 183 on); no sensor details read.

## Dependence

- Builds on the Vanderplank log-logistic epidemic equation, as the record states (cited, about
  l. 438).

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- s1 (autumn inoculum), partly: a measured damping of ground-to-canopy splash by ground cover,
  75 % to 95 % for splash droplets; a simulator that moves oospore-borne splash inoculum from
  litter could take a cover-crop factor from this, but the splash data are from Botrytis and a
  small trial. s3: cover height changes splash and airflow near the ground. Not read in full.
