# Request: Rossi et al. 2008's primary infection model outside the climates it was built in (Agrarium D67)

From Agrarium's session on `agents` (ckt), 2026-10-29, Chris: "Go ahead". For an `ingest`
session: papers stay with you (D40).

## Why

Agrarium's truth runs Rossi's lineage for primary infection: rossi2008.oospores, rossi2008.primary
and rossi2008.incubation, with fedele2025.dose. Agrarium ran it on the weather of 136 real downy
mildew seasons in 14 countries (Formularium #73's among them). Read as an observer would, at 3 % of
the vines with lesions, its first symptoms come within about two weeks of the observed in most
regions. Two regions stay weeks early whatever the reading, and no `assumed` value within its range
moves the first lesion (Agrarium's docs/TRUTH-CALIBRATION-REPORT.md, CB2):
- Auckland, New Zealand, 1971-1982 (Brook 1992): 40-114 days early;
- the German sites (1977, 2000, 2020-2021): 63-102 days early.

The first lesion's date is set by the oospores' ripening, the rain that triggers infection and the
incubation, on that weather. Agrarium needs to know whether Rossi's lineage is known to start too
early in such climates, or whether the cause is elsewhere: the reanalysis's weather, or how the
dates were observed.

## What to find

1. **Where Rossi et al. 2008's oospore and primary infection model was built and validated:** the
   sites, years and climates, from the paper and its validation papers (Rossi, Caffi, Giosuè and
   colleagues). Do any lie in mild-wintered oceanic climates, as Auckland's, or in Germany's?
2. **Any test of the model,** or of its oospore ripening ("hydro-thermal time" from 1 January),
   outside northern Italy: in particular in New Zealand, Australia, South Africa, Brazil, Germany,
   France or Switzerland. What it found about timing: early, late, by how much.
3. **What the authors say about the starting day** of the oospore count (1 January in the north)
   and its use in mild winters, where little cold breaks the oospores' dormancy.
4. **Anything published on oospore dormancy and maturation** depending on winter cold, which would
   make a count from 1 January run early where winters are mild.

Search the old and local names too: *Plasmopara viticola*, *Peronospora*, Rebenperonospora, mildiou.
