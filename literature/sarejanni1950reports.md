# The Benaki Institute's annual disease reports, 1949-1953: the downy mildew lines

Short yearly reports of what the Institute identified. Their downy mildew lines record
Greek seasons as seen by the people behind [sarejanni1951](sarejanni1951.md).

## What it holds

- **1949** (in 4(1-2)): downy and powdery mildew on vines "comme tous les ans sans causer
  cependant de dégâts notables".
- **1950** (in 5(1)): no grapevine downy mildew noted. The mildew in it is potato late
  blight in Crete.
- **1951** (in 6(1)):
  - a particularly dry year; vine downy mildew appeared only in a few rare places, around
    Patras for example, with insignificant damage;
  - right after harvest, a sudden fall in temperature and frequent rains in late August and
    early September favoured it on leaves, where it formed many oospores almost everywhere,
    "même en Attique".
- **1952** (in 7(1)):
  - "une année sans mildiou aux vignes";
  - in autumn 1951 oospores were numerous on leaves across the country, but "ces spores ne
    germèrent pas au printemps, même quand on les exposait expérimentalement à des
    conditions optima au laboratoire";
  - probably the long drought of late winter devitalised them, or delayed their ripening
    beyond June.
- **1953** (in 8(2)): no grapevine downy mildew noted. Powdery mildew in its "flag" form and
  root rots are.

## Dependence

- Observations; no formulation.

## Bearing (2026-10-09)

- **1951-1952 is a test case against Sarejanni's "preparatory year" taken alone:**
  abundant autumn oospores, then a late-winter drought, and no mildew. Capus's rule, with
  winter rain, predicts it. A truth's season pattern should reproduce both: autumn
  oospores matter, and late-winter drought can cancel them.
