# van den Hurk 1996: sparse-canopy land-surface schemes tested on a La Mancha vineyard

A thesis that compares land-surface (SVAT) schemes for sparse canopies against flux measurements from two campaigns in a sparse vineyard in La Mancha, builds a combined scheme and couples it to a one-dimensional boundary-layer model (l. 71-95). No record.

## What it holds

- **Data:** two campaigns in a sparsely vegetated vineyard in La Mancha, with sensible, soil and latent heat flux, radiative fluxes, aerodynamic properties, soil and vegetation data (l. 71-75).
- **Analyses:** aerodynamic transport in a sparse canopy compared with a Lagrangian diffusion alternative; spatial and temporal variability of sparse-canopy albedo; a photosynthesis-based stomatal conductance model scaled to the canopy (l. 76-82).
- **Result:** none of the compared SVAT models described the observed fluxes best; a combined model was built from the best parts (l. 83-88). In the boundary-layer study, large effects came from a one-layer 'big leaf' scheme, a resistance soil heat scheme and too low aerodynamic resistance (l. 90-95). No flux error figure was located in the front matter.
- **Form:** about 13,950 lines of text; only the front matter and abstract were checked.

## Dependence

- Compares existing sparse-canopy SVAT schemes (Dickinson 1986 and others named in the record); builds on the EFEDA field data.

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- **Strand s4 (measured vineyard microclimate) and s3 (terrain and canopy physics):** measured energy-balance fluxes over a sparse vineyard, and a statement that the aerodynamic treatment and the soil heat scheme drive the answer. A simulator could take the sparse-canopy two-layer view and the albedo variability. The text is a thesis, so equations and tables are inside the chapters, not read.
