# Brink et al. 2016: spray deposition and Botrytis on table grapes

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- Table grape cv. Waltham Cross, Paarl, South Africa; bunches 2004-05, leaves 2005-06; six spray volumes with fenhexamid and fluorescent pigment (l. 90-106).
- Dry B. cinerea conidia applied after spraying; incubation 24 h at 22 °C, RH 93 % or more (l. 202-210).
- Natural infection on bunch parts under 1 % (l. 325).
- Deposit quantity and quality rise with volume and correlate with infection; infection falls as volume rises (l. 311, 425).
- FPC75 benchmarks: about 8.7 % (quantity) and 23.9 (quality) on upper leaf surfaces at about 4 mL (l. 410-413).
- Quality gains stop past a point; the authors recommend FPC75 per growth stage (l. 466-468, 527-531).

## Dependence

- none. The paper uses a fluorescence-based deposit assay and sigmoidal and Hoerl fits of its own data. The RH of 93 % in the incubation chambers (l. 208) is a lab setting, not a leaf-wetness or RH-as-wetness stand-in for infection; no listed engine model is computed or fitted to the same data.

## Bearing (2026-10-10)

- A detached-part dataset linking spray deposit to Botrytis control on grapevine, with deposition benchmarks, that can inform a truth's spray-to-infection observation; it has no model the engine runs.
