# Taibi et al. 2023: resistance inducers alter P. viticola epidemiology on leaves

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- Six PRIs were tested at Castell'Arquato (Piacenza) on Barbera, 2020-2022, against leaf inoculations 1, 3, 6, 12 and 19 days after treatment (l. 668, 669).
- Laminarin, potassium phosphonate and fosetyl-Al cut disease severity and lesion DNA most at 1 and 3 days after treatment (l. 18).
- PHO, FOS and cerevisane cut sporangia production by over 75%; LAM and PHO by 78.1% and 98.1% (l. 23, 497).
- Untreated latent periods averaged 10.0, 18.6 and 15.6 days (l. 140); three table rows do not match their dates (l. 160, 171).
- Hourly weather came from an on-site station (l. 678).
- The discussion proposes preventive PRI use 1-3 days before infection, citing two infection models that are not run here (l. 643, 654).
- Inoculum is printed as 5 x 10^4 and as 1 x 10^4 sporangia/mL (l. 698, 721).

## Dependence

- none computed or fitted. The paper's discussion (l. 654) cites two process-based infection models (Rossi et al. 2008 [90]; Brischetto et al. 2021 [91]) as the basis for risk-timed PRI use, but it does not run, borrow or describe them. Flag only: Vittorio Rossi is an author, and is a co-author of refs 90 and 91 (shared authors are flags, not dependence).

## Bearing (2026-10-10)

- Field data on the monocycle after PRI treatment, with observed latency and lesion counts for 2020-2022 at one site, that could calibrate or test the truth's latent period under resistance, once its table errors are resolved; it runs no model itself.
