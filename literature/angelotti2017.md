# Angelotti et al. 2017: heat lengthens the latent period, and a favourability map for Brazil

Open access (SciELO), fetched 2026-10-12. A growth-room test of warming on infection and
latency, and monthly favourability maps of Brazil under IPCC AR4 scenarios, in the line
of `hamada2008` and `hamada2012`.

## What it holds

- **Growth room** (Embrapa Semiárido, Petrolina): seedlings of Crimson Seedless, Thompson
  Seedless and Alicante Bouschet with four to six leaves, sprayed with 10⁵ sporangia/ml
  (the exponent is lost in the text layer), 24 h in a humid chamber, then held at 26,
  28, 29.1, 30.4, 31.8 and 33 °C, the additions 2.0, 3.1, 4.4 and 5.8 °C being the
  scenario warmings over the valley's 26 °C mean (l. 19-21, 65-69, 107-113); severity by
  Buffara et al. 2014's scale (`buffara2014`), latent period as days to sporulation (l.
  73-75). Latent period 5 d at 26 °C for all three cultivars, 9 d at 31.8 °C, no symptoms
  at 33 °C; infection highest at 26 and lowest at 31.8 °C (l. 159-163, 180); the values
  at the middle temperatures are only in Fig. 1.
- **Favourability rule:** a month is favourable with leaf wetness above 2 h a day and a
  mean temperature of 16-28 °C, less favourable above 28 °C, unfavourable below 2 h or
  below 16 °C (l. 137-144), bounds set "from the information obtained experimentally" and
  adjusted until Embrapa and IAC staff judged them right against known occurrence (l.
  135-150); leaf wetness from RH by Hamada et al. 2006's method, not printed (l. 95-96);
  RH for the future from AR4 specific humidity and pressure by Tetens' formula (l. 88).
- **Maps:** the 1961-1990 normal against 2011-2040, 2041-2070 and 2071-2100 under B1 and
  A2, with 7 models for humidity and pressure and 13 or 15 for temperature, interpolated
  to 0.5° (l. 24, 122-131): now about 90 % of Brazil favourable in the year, 1 % less
  favourable, 5 % unfavourable (l. 213-215); under B1 by 2071-2100, 43 %, 48 % and 8 %
  (l. 216-219); under A2 the favourable area falls by 20 % and the less favourable rises by
  70 % (l. 219-222). Risk months: January-July in the Northeast, October-May in the South
  and Southeast (l. 210-211); eastern São Paulo most exposed in October-December (l.
  251-253).
- **Cited:** Lalancette et al. 1988 for infection at 12-28 °C with free water (l. 184),
  Caffi et al. 2016 for a 20-25 °C optimum (l. 85-87), Kennelly et al. 2007 for reduced
  sporulation on hot days to 42.8 °C (l. 192-193), Rossi et al. 2008 on latency (l. 167).

## Dependence

- **Its own growth-room data,** and a favourability rule tuned by expert judgement, not
  fitted; the wetness comes from Hamada et al. 2006's RH method (an engine-like RH
  stand-in of unprinted form, cf. `hamada2008`'s use of Lalancette's equation).
- **Engine pieces:** none computed; Lalancette 1988 is cited for its range only.
- **Authors:** Hamada, Ghini and Pedro Júnior: `hamada2008`, `hamada2012`, `lulu2008*`
  flags.

## Bearing (2026-10-13)

- Two observed points a truth's temperature response could be checked against: latency 5
  d at 26 °C and 9 d at 31.8 °C, nothing at 33 °C, for warm-climate table cultivars. The
  maps are a rule's output, not data.
