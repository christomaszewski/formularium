# Hauser et al. 2022: how fast searchers find a target plant, by plant, background and experience

A detection experiment: 29 observers searched 20 × 20 m plots alone for planted invasive
hawkweeds (flowering or not, two species) against backgrounds of different vegetation and
yellow-flower cover; times to detection were fitted with an exponential (random-encounter)
survival model with observer, plot and target effects.

## What it holds

- 1,111 detection opportunities on 72 targets; 53 % detected overall, 25 % of
  nonflowering against 81 % of flowering targets; 67 % in grass, 45 % in mixed and 50 % in
  heath plots (l. 93).
- 31 false positives, from 10 of 29 observers, 13 of them from one observer (l. 93).
- Detection improved over the first 4 h after 09:00 and declined after; against a median
  observer with 20 h of experience that summer, detection took 37 % longer with no
  experience and 26 % less with 600 h (l. 99).
- Detection is modelled as a rate per unit of search effort (exponential), the form the
  hawkweed programme uses to allocate search effort (l. 85).

## Dependence

- Its own experiment; no disease model.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- For Agrarium's scout operator (with combes2025): a published form for detection as a
  rate per minute of search, raised by conspicuous targets and experience and lowered by
  cluttered backgrounds, with between-observer variation as a random effect. Plants, not
  lesions: forms and magnitudes for an operator, not parameters for oil spots.
