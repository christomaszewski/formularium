# Riou, Valancogne & Pieri 1989: a box with porous walls for a Bordeaux vine row

The first printed row-geometry model of the solar radiation a vine row intercepts, and the model that Pieri & Gaudillère 2003 (`pieri2003`) fit their closed forms to. French text; the equations are images in the text layer and were read in the page images. No record yet.

## What it holds

- **Geometry** (l. 79-84, 105; Eq. before Fig. 1, p. 442): the foliage is a box, two solid horizontal faces of width L and two vertical walls of height H and porosity p, p being the fraction of wall area that gives no interception and independent of ray direction. Measured on digitised photographs and set to 1/3 (l. 105). Leaf area per metre of row if the walls were covered with leaves: Sf = 2L + 2(1 - p) H, 1.8 m² for L 0.3, H 0.9, p 1/3 (p. 442), equal to the ground between two rows, so 10 000 m²/ha (Carbonneau & Huglin 1980 measured 10 500).
- **Direct beam** (Eqs 1-10, p. 443): with sun elevation h, azimuth a from south and a' the azimuth of the row's normal, lambda = Iv/Ih = cos h cos(a - a') / sin h (Eqs 1-2). Shadow of a neighbouring row: lit height X = (D - L) tg h / cos(a - a') = (D - L)/lambda (Eq. 8); fully lit when lambda <= (D - L)/H (Eq. 9).
- **Diffuse** (Eqs 11-21, pp. 443-444): uniform sky radiance; the two walls take nH = (D - L)/D (1 - tg(i0/2)), i0 = arctg((D - L)/H) (Eq. 16); the lower face takes nL,i (Eqs 17-20); total nd = (1 - p) nH + L/D + p nL,i (Eq. 21).
- **Interception by case of lambda** (Eqs 22-27, p. 445): five ranges of lambda from L/H up to (D + L)/H and beyond, to three rows, "the vine then absorbs 97 % of direct radiation" (l. 266). Ri = (1/D)[...] Ih + nd Rd.
- **Absorbed and reflected** (Eqs 28-29, p. 445): leaf albedo af, soil albedo as; reflection toward the neighbouring row neglected because leaves lie 60-70 degrees from horizontal (l. 268); light reflected by the soil is shared half up, half down.
- **Test vineyard** (l. 304, 311-315): Latresne, rows N-S, spacing D 1.8 m, total height 1.5 m, foliage height H 0.9 m, width L 0.3 m, vines 1.1 m apart. No latitude printed.
- **Measurements** (l. 340-341, 352, 356): 25-30 September, four pyranometers at the soil between two rows, 15-min means; the text says 1986, the Table I caption 1987. af 0.20 (5 % visible, 40 % near infrared), dry soil 0.18.
- **Check** (Table I, p. 446, page image): six days, mean absorbed 29, 153, 156, 168, 172, 180 W/m² measured against 27, 158, 159, 170, 177, 180 calculated; reflected 8, 59, 59, 61, 65, 65 against 10, 59, 58, 61, 63, 64. "Ecart moyen" (printed as the sum of squared 15-min differences over n; units W/m² as printed) 3-22 absorbed, 2-5 reflected. Mean absorption coefficient 41.0 % (39.3-42.7) and mean plot reflection 15.3 % on days under 30 % diffuse (l. 373-375). Model underestimates reflected light near noon by under 10 % (l. 387). Dispersion larger at high values (Fig. 4, p. 447).
- **Orientation** (Table II, p. 448; simulated clear days): Ra/Rg N-S against E-W 40.1 against 31.5 % on 29 June (+27 %, l. 397), 41.6 against 36.4 % on 20 August (+15 %), 42.1 against 45.9 % on 28 September (-8 %, l. 398). N-S coefficients vary little over the year, E-W much more (l. 402).

## Dependence

- Own model. Cites Allen 1974 (direct beam only), Fuchs & Stanhill 1980, Jackson & Palmer 1972 and Charles-Edwards & Thorpe 1976 as models it differs from; the sun-plane formulae are Varlet-Grancher 1975 and Sellers 1965.
- Calibration: porosity from the authors' own photographs; af from a spectral mean; validated on the authors' own six days (not independent, but no parameter was fitted to them as far as the text says).
- Same first author as Riou 1994 (the RIOU phenology sum in `cortazar2009`): a flag only.

## Flags

- Eqs 17-20 are not mutually consistent as printed (Eq. 17 has no 1/pi, so an unobstructed lower face gives pi L/D, not L/D; Eq. 20 is "shown from Eq. 13" without steps): arithmetic, not printed. Code Eq. 17 numerically and test that nL,i tends to L/D when D is large against H.
- Eq. 26 and 27 do not join (at lambda H = 2D - L, Eq. 26 gives D - p²(D - 2L), Eq. 27 gives D - p³(D - L)); the range of Eq. 27 is not printed. Cases 1-4 (Eqs 22-25) join continuously (arithmetic, not printed).
- Needs I_h and R_d separately; the paper takes Ih = Rg - Rd measured.

## Bearing (2026-10-28)

- For D61 (light in a vine row): complete enough to code for N-S and E-W rows with a pyranometer-grade check; the whole-row interception and the lit fraction of each wall follow from lambda. Two walls only: no sunlit leaf area by depth, no leaf angles (the porosity p stands in), no oblique rows beyond a' (the paper treats a' generally in Eq. 1 but tests only N-S).

## From the Pieri collection (2026-10-30)

Lines to add to the note riou1989:

- **Used later:** riou1994 (Vitis 33:109-115) extends the interception model of this paper to net radiation of vine rows and inter-row soil (emissivity 0.96, polynomial Rn = Ca Ra + Cs sigma Ts^4 + Cf sigma Tf^4 + Ci sigma Ta^4, coefficients not printed) and checks it over 13 days of 1993 (n = 551 half-hours): vine rows Rnv y = 0.99 x - 27 (r2 0.96), soil Rns y = 1.02 x - 23 (r2 0.98), whole canopy Rn y = 1.04 x - 45 (r2 0.998).
- **Transpiration share:** riou1994 and riou1995 use the absorbed shortwave fraction k = Rg,v / (0.8 Rg) as the vine's share of Penman ETP (about 0.5 for the Bordeaux trellis; ETv = k ETP). The same model supplies the absorbed radiation in gowdy2022b (sap-flow conductance) and the Lebon et al. 2003 water balance used in deresseguier2023 and gowdy2018.

## Later use (Pieri 2010, Ecological Modelling 221:791-811; read 2026-10-30)

- **Neither paper restates or corrects Riou et al. 1989's Eq. 17 or Eq. 27.** Paper I (pieri2010, doi 10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2009.11.019) takes its shortwave partition from Riou 1989 by citation (p. 792) and prints no shortwave equation. Paper II (pieri2010b, doi 10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2009.07.028) drops Riou's porous-wall interception for a shadow function delta(x) (Eqs 8-13) and a point view factor for diffuse light (Eqs 15-19); neither contains a lower-face diffuse term or Riou's lambda ranges.
- **Same enclosure algebra, a different factor.** Pieri 2010 Eq. 2, F_EW = tan(1/2 arctan(Hf/(D - w))), is the wall-to-wall factor (also `pieri2003` Eq. 1). Riou's Eq. 16, nH = (D - L)/D (1 - tan(i0/2)) with i0 = arctan((D - L)/H), uses the top-to-bottom factor tan(1/2 arctan((D - L)/H)) of the same rectangle (arithmetic, not printed). Both follow Ozisik's crossed-string result F13 = tan(alpha/2).
- **Normalisation check for Eq. 17:** Pieri 2010 Eq. 15, F = 1/2 (sin Phi1 - sin Phi2) for an infinite strip, is 1 for a half-plane strip. It is normalised, as the module's version of Riou's Eq. 17 is; it is a different quantity and not a proof.
- **Validation of the family:** the shortwave partition was used in a second, independent plot (Pieri 2010, Bordeaux Graves, rows 2 m apart, 0.35 m wide, porosity 0.25, leaf albedo 0.25, soil albedo 0.18); the whole model with longwave reached a mean error of about 20 W m-2 on the soil's net radiation (Fig. 11: y = 1.01x + 9.9, r2 0.98, n 551). The shortwave part alone was not scored.
- **Different parameter values** in the 2010 plots: porosity 0.25 (Paper I; 0.2, 0.5, 0.7 by zone and 0.25 for diffuse, Paper II), against Riou's 1/3; leaf albedo 0.25 against 0.20; soil 0.18 in both.
