# Zaffaroni et al. 2022: pathogen sex and durability of resistance deployment

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- A stochastic, spatially explicit model of P. viticola on a landscape with two qualitative resistance genes (l. 86-91, 122-124).
- Four deployment strategies compared: rotation, pyramiding, mixture and mosaic (l. 122-123).
- Three sexual-reproduction mechanisms: recombination, dormancy and dispersal dimorphism (l. 96-98).
- One sexual cycle at season end, several asexual cycles in the season (l. 105-106).
- Sexual spores stay viable in soil for at least 65 months, citing Caffi et al. 2011 (l. 108).
- Sexual spores reach the canopy by rain-splash; asexual spores by air (l. 110-111).
- Of 43 pathogens in McDonald & Linde (2002), 17 are purely asexual (l. 34-36).

## Dependence

- none. The model is adapted from Rimbaud et al. 2018 (l. 86-87), a landscape model of its own group, and no engine model is computed, fitted or shared in form. Caffi et al. 2011 is cited for oospore survival (l. 108) as a fact, not a model.

## Bearing (2026-10-10)

- A model framework only: it gives the truth a structural note (sexual spores persist in soil and reach the canopy by rain-splash) and no data, and nothing the engine's listed models use.
