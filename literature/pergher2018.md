# Pergher & Zucchiatti 2018: how a tunnel sprayer's deposit on leaves varies through a VSP vineyard canopy over the season

Five field tests with a tracer dye from budbreak to berries touching: deposit per leaf area, its spread between leaves, and the difference between inner and outer canopy.

**Read this first.**
- A tracer-dye water deposit (µL per cm² of leaf), not a fungicide mass and not an efficacy. Both leaf sides are counted together (l. 333), so there is no upper/lower split.
- A **tunnel** (recycling, shielded) air-assisted sprayer, not an airblast. The CV is between individual sample leaves (Table 3). The table's SD column does not match it (arithmetic, not printed: 0.088/0.727 = 0.12 against the CV of 33.4 %); the basis of that SD was not read.
- The page range of the article is printed only as a page number of the issue; the end page was not read.

## What it holds

- **Design (l. 332, 467):** vertically shoot-positioned vineyards; Test 1 at Romans d'Isonzo (Gorizia), Pinot grigio, and Tests 2 to 5 at San Martino al Tagliamento (Pordenone), Merlot spur cordon. Stages BBCH 15, 57, 73, 77 and 77; hollow-cone turbulence nozzles in Tests 1 to 4 and flat-fan air-injection nozzles in Test 5. Two leaves from each of nine locations (3 heights x 3 positions across the canopy) per sample vine, 144 leaves per test (l. 332). Leaf area index 0.154, 0.748, 1.333, 1.594 and 1.603 in Tests 1 to 5 (l. 467).
- **Deposit on the target (l. 40):** total deposit on leaves and bunches rose from 14.8 % (BBCH 15) to 53.9 % (BBCH 77) of the volume applied as the leaf area index rose from 0.15 to 1.60.
- **Mean leaf deposit and CV between individual leaves (Table 3; l. 480 to 484, 497):**
  - Test 1: 0.727 µL/cm², CV 33.4 % (l. 480).
  - Test 2: 0.652 µL/cm², CV 47.2 % (l. 481).
  - Test 3: 0.697 µL/cm², CV 50.0 %, the highest (l. 482).
  - Test 4: 0.709 µL/cm², CV 42.5 % (l. 483).
  - Test 5: 0.703 µL/cm², CV 43.4 % (l. 484).
  - The CV ranges from 33.4 % to 50.0 % over the season (l. 497).
- **Inner against outer canopy (l. 508, 522):** in Test 3 (BBCH 73) the inside of the canopy carried 22 % less than the average of the two outer sides (l. 508); inside/outside differences were significant only in Tests 3 and 5. With air-injection nozzles (Test 5) there was no significant difference by height, so the remaining variability is between individual leaves (l. 522).
- **Underside (l. 115, cited):** from Pergher et al. 2013, a tunnel sprayer gave more variable deposits on leaf undersides at the end of flowering than a conventional sprayer; no number is printed here.

## Dependence

- **Own** field tests (University of Udine); tracer-dye method after Pergher & Petris 2009. None recorded of the engine's models.

## Bearing (2026-10-25)

- **Deposit variability through a VSP canopy (M4):** the best vine-canopy numbers in this strand for the spread between leaves: CV 33 to 50 % between leaves at nine canopy positions, with a mean deposit of 0.65 to 0.73 µL/cm² at every stage, and the inside of the canopy 22 % lower in one test (BBCH 73). A measurement with tracer dye on vine leaves, for a tunnel sprayer, both leaf sides together.
- **Not shown:** the abaxial/adaxial split, fungicide mass, an airblast sprayer, or application volumes per hectare (not read). The rise in deposit on target with leaf area (14.8 to 53.9 %) says that early-season sprays put much of the volume on the ground, which matters for dilution by growth only as a deposit per applied volume. Wash-off, decay and dose-efficacy: not addressed.
