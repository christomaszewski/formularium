# Cui et al. 2026: stochastic generators for minute-scale wind vectors

Time VQ-VAE generators of June wind vectors at Lamont, Oklahoma, reproduce diurnal wind
  volatility but not the extreme wind-speed distribution.

## What it holds

- **Data:** about 30 years of high-quality minute data at the ARM Lamont facility, restricted
  to June (l. 14-15, 63-66); station operating continuously since 1994 (l. 99-100).
- **Method:** quantized variational autoencoders, a day at a time or conditioned on the
  previous day, with and without a discrete weather state (l. 19-22); weather state counts in
  Table 1 (l. 134).
- **Result:** the best models mimic diurnal changes in wind volatility but struggle with
  extremes (l. 22-24); evaluated with a bidirectional LSTM discriminator and energy scores (l.
  73, 1169).

## Dependence

- Builds on the Time VQ-VAE (Lee et al. 2023) and ARM MET data (record).

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- s2: a machine-learning generator for wind only, at minute scale, in one month of one place;
  it shows a learned generator misses extremes. A sweep simulator would take nothing for
  humidity or wetness. Not read in full.
