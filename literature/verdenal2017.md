# Verdenal et al. 2017: pre-flowering defoliation in Pinot noir, Switzerland

Six years (2010 to 2015) of Pinot noir defoliation at three stages in Pully, Switzerland.
Pre-flowering removal cut berry set and yield, thickened the berry skin and changed the wine.
The Botrytis result comes from one season only.

## What it holds

- **Trial:** Pinot noir (clone FAW-1), Pully, 2010 to 2015 (l. 144-147). Control, and
  defoliation at BBCH 57, 67-69 and 77 (l. 132-138).
- **Yield:** the abstract gives -30 % (l. 24). Table 3 gives 0.9 kg/m2 for the control and
  0.8 for pre-flowering (l. 402), a smaller gap than the abstract's figure. The 2011 to 2015
  yield potential estimate gives a 35 % loss at pre-flowering (l. 363-364).
- **Berries and wine:** skin thickness "doubled" and polyphenols rose; free glutathione fell
  (l. 24-25). Pre-flowering skin 219 ± 17 µm, bunch-closure 149 ± 13 µm (l. 421-422).
- **Botrytis, 2012 only:** control 8.3 ± 3.0 % attack, against 0.2 to 1.2 % in the three
  defoliated treatments (l. 403; text l. 429-431).

## Dependence

None recorded as a model or dataset. The introduction frames the trial against earlier
pre-flowering work. The weather series is from a Geneva station (Table 1 source).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Serves t4 through the 2012 Botrytis result, one season against a control, and t5 through the
  yield cost of pre-flowering removal. The wine results are off the disease and pest strands.
- Not read in full. A canopy-work model could take the berry-set and yield loss as a cost term;
  the disease result is too thin for a grey mould rule.
