# Fermaud et al. 2001: light and cluster compactness change how far Botrytis spreads in ripening clusters (growth chamber, 3 trials)

**Read this first.** A three-page bioassay, not a field study. The title says "microclimate", but the only microclimate factor varied is incident light on the cluster; air temperature, RH and wetness were the same cycle for every cluster, and the text prints only the temperature cycle. Nothing was measured in a vineyard. The volume is the IOBC wprs Bulletin 24(7), 2001, "Integrated Control in Viticulture"; the paper is at pp. 7-9.

## What it holds

- **Plants and trials (l. 1209, 1239-1240):** three trials of about 20 days, 12 clusters each, Cabernet Sauvignon fruiting cuttings, clusters at the end of veraison, sorted by compactness (visual score and a volume-ratio index).
- **Chamber (l. 1241):** a Conviron E7 chamber. Daily cycles of air temperature 13 to 23.8 C and of RH and wetness duration, identical for all clusters (l. 1212). The RH and wetness values are not printed.
- **Inoculation (l. 1239):** two or three points per cluster; two monoconidial B. cinerea strains (234 and 266), one strain in trial III.
- **Light (l. 1255):** one lamp without UV; central clusters 150 to 250 W m-2, side clusters shaded at 30 to 40 W m-2. Light sensor type not stated.
- **Response (l. 1256):** rotted berries next to an inoculation point, counted 12 to 15 days after inoculation (per infection focus).
- **Table 1 (l. 1275-1276), rotted berries per focus, exposed vs not exposed:** trial I 3 vs 3.25; trial II 2.5 vs 3. The text calls the reduction significant (letters differ).
- **Table 2 (l. 1284), trial III:** average compactness 3.7 not exposed, 2.8 exposed; greater compactness 8.5 not exposed, 5.3 exposed. So compactness raised spread by a factor of 2.3 in the shade and 1.9 in the light (arithmetic, not printed).
- **Strain (l. 1264):** 3.25 rotted berries per focus for strain 234 against 2.5 for 266 (trial II).
- **Conclusion (l. 1291):** compactness is "assumed to be a factor strongly affecting" susceptibility after veraison and may account for cultivar differences.

## Dependence

- Own experiment. Builds on the fruiting-cutting model of Ollat et al. 1998 and the strain collection of Martinez et al. 2000 (neither held). Shares no equation with the engine's Botrytis or wetness pieces.
- Authors: Pieri (INRA Bioclimatologie) is the second microclimate author; Fermaud's other papers are held (ciliberti2015berries, calvogarrido2019, delbac2026); a flag only.

## Bearing (2026-10-30)

- **What Agrarium can take:** a direction and a size for how light on a bunch cuts Botrytis spread (3.25 to 3.0 and 3.0 to 2.5 rotted berries per focus, about 8 to 17 % fewer, arithmetic not printed) and how compactness raises it (about 2 times). Agrarium's disease is downy mildew; this is Botrytis on berries, so it is context for a later Botrytis module, not an input to the present one.
- **Not shown:** no leaf or bunch temperature or wetness was measured; no field data; the light effect is not separated from heating of the cluster by the lamp (no cluster temperature printed).
- **Lead:** Pieri and Fermaud 2005, "Effects of defoliation on temperature and wetness of grapevine berries" (Acta Hortic 689:109-116, doi 10.17660/ActaHortic.2005.689.9), is cited by three papers read here and would hold the field bunch temperature and wetness measurements.
