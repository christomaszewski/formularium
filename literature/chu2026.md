# Chu, Tran & Luong 2026: packet delivery above 90 % in a LoRaMESH farm network

A Vietnamese test bed (about 2.5 km by 1 km of farmland) of a LoRaMESH sensor network with a Wi-Fi HaLow backhaul; the part used here is packet delivery.

**Read this first.**
- Packet delivery is link loss per message, not lost days of data. Retries or buffering in a logger can hide it.
- Only the abstract and the related-work table were read. The second and third numbers below are other studies' results, cited in the table.

## What it holds

- **Own result (l. 9):** "packet delivery ratios above 90 %" across one to three hops, on a short test.
- **Cited, LoRaWAN farm (l. 93):** 3 ha farm, four nodes: delivery about 81 to 83 % for three nodes and 70 % for one greenhouse node, at spreading factor 7 (Rivera-Guzman et al., as the table cites it; a different study).
- **Cited, single-hop LoRa (l. 94):** 50 ha, up to 875 m: delivery 76 to 92 % (a different study).
- **Also in the abstract (not in the JSON facts, from the text file header):** end-to-end delay 10.6 to 13.3 s; Wi-Fi HaLow delivery 99.5 % at 50 m and 89.68 % at 200 m; sleep current 21.19 µA.

## Dependence

- The related-work rows cite other studies' delivery ratios. The test bed is the authors' own.

## Bearing (2026-11-04)

- **sense.logger_loss_per_day (Cooptera, Agrarium sensing):** packet delivery of 70 to 92 % in field LoRa networks, above 90 % in this one. This is link loss per packet, an upper bound on lost readings if the logger does not buffer. Kind of evidence: short test-bed runs and a literature table.
- Not shown: days lost over a season.
