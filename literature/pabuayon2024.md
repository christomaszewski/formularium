# Pabuayon et al. 2024: hail netting and spray droplets in grape canopies

Water sprayed on one day in a Texas vineyard shows how hail netting changes droplet size and coverage within the canopy.

## What it holds

- One spray day, 23 September 2023, at a Texas Tech research vineyard in Lubbock. Water was sprayed by a venturi airblast sprayer calibrated to 374 L/ha (l. 166-169).
- Water-sensitive papers at five canopy locations, four replicates, and two cultivars: Petite Sirah and Touriga Nacional (l. 38, 156, 162-163).
- Without netting, Touriga Nacional had higher DV_1, DV_5 and spray coverage than Petite Sirah at Location 2, the cluster zone. With netting, the two did not differ there (l. 217-224; Table 1 at l. 226).

## Dependence

- Builds on the DepositScan analysis of water-sensitive papers (Zhu et al. 2011).
- Uses a SAS 9.4 mixed model with netting, cultivar and location as fixed effects (l. 205-206).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t1 (fungicide protection: spray reaching the clusters). A growers' engine could take only the finding that netting evens out cluster-zone coverage. The data are water only, one day and one site, so this is context for canopy deposition, not a usable dataset.
