# Ficke et al. 2002: grape berries become nearly immune to powdery mildew within a month

A symposium paper summarising the Geneva group's work on berries' ontogenic resistance to
Uncinula (Erysiphe) necator, with new microscopy of where infection stops.

## What it holds

- Berries become 'nearly immune to infection within 4 weeks after fruit set' (l. 32); only
  berries inoculated within 3 weeks of bloom developed severe disease (l. 90-91), a window
  centred on bloom (l. 90).
- On berries about 3 weeks old and older, conidia still adhere, germinate and form
  appressoria, but few germlings form secondary hyphae (l. 37); resistant berries express a
  germin-like protein 16-48 h after inoculation (l. 194-195).
- Infection after resistance sets in gives diffuse, nonsporulating colonies hardly visible
  in the field (l. 195-196), which predispose berries to Botrytis bunch rot and spoilage and
  degrade wine quality (l. 29-32, 201).

## Dependence

- The susceptibility window is the group's earlier data (Gadoury et al. 2003, held as
  literature/gadoury2003, and refs 7, 12, 13; l. 88-91), restated here. Gadoury and Seem
  are authors of the engine's Kennelly pieces: flags.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- For a powdery mildew truth or Cooptera's spray timing: the berry window is about 3-4
  weeks from bloom or fruit set, the same organ-age form as the engine's downy mildew bunch
  window and Calonnec's leaf curve; and late, invisible berry infections matter for rot
  and wine quality (t5). Not read in full.
