# Tello & Ibáñez 2018: review of grapevine bunch compactness

A state-of-the-art review of bunch compactness. It covers the effects on Botrytis and fungicide reach, the ways to measure compactness, and the viticultural controls on it.

## What it holds

- **Risk:** compact bunches favour Botrytis and other rots. Fungicide spraying is less effective in compact bunches, and it reaches only the outer parts (l. 90-92).
- **Microclimate:** the inside of a compact bunch gets more water vapour and longer surface wetness, which raises the risk of microcracking and rot (l. 131-134).
- **Losses:** the cost of controlling Botrytis damage is about A$52 million a year in Australia, US$22.4 million a year in Chile and SA Rand 25 million a year in South Africa. These figures come from Dean et al. 2012 (l. 118-124).
- **Scope:** a review with no new formulation or field dataset. The measurement methods differ between studies, so results are hard to compare (abstract, l. 21-23).

## Dependence

- Hed et al. 2009 and 2011 on bunch structure and Botrytis severity (l. 98-103).
- Vail and Marois 1991 and Vail et al. 1998 on bunch microclimate and rot (l. 98-103).
- Molitor et al. 2012 and 2015 on compactness (l. 101-103).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t4 (Botrytis bunch-rot risk), with t5 (damage and economics). A simulator could take the rule that spray reach falls as bunches get tighter. The review gives this in words, not as fitted coefficients.
