# Itier et al. 2010: how much rain becomes aquifer recharge under different crops, and how that falls with climate change

A slide deck from the CLIMATOR project: STICS and Biljou simulations of recharge on 12 French sites for eight cropping systems (vine is one), for 1970-2000 and 2020-2050.

**Read this first.** A policy-scale deck with no vineyard-specific result in the text; vine is a legend entry in a recharge-against-rain plot, and Pieri is one of twelve authors. Read lightly. Nothing here bears on in-canopy microclimate.

## What it holds

- **Water balance (slides):** recharge = drainage + runoff - irrigation, a function of annual rain, ETo and cropping system; effective rain is R - Ro; ETa = R - recharge; capillary rise neglected.
- **Design:** CLIMATOR, 8 cropping systems, 12 French sites, 3 soils, at least two crop models per crop, three 30-year periods (observed 1970-2000, simulated 2020-2050 and 2070-2100 from Arpege, scenario A1B per the plot titles).
- **Result (abstract):** the fall in annual recharge is at least two thirds of the fall in rain, under each system; recharge differs between rainfed and irrigated, winter and spring crops, annual and perennial vegetation.
- **Vine:** plotted with wheat, grass, forest and sunflower for annual recharge against annual rain at the sites, no number in the text; the mapping of regression lines to crops is ambiguous in the extraction, so none is quoted.

## Dependence

- STICS (Brisson) and Biljou (Granier) crop and forest models; independent of the engine.

## Bearing (2026-10-30)

- **Not useful for Agrarium's microclimate or disease simulation.** At most a context point for the climate-change scenario scale (rain falls, recharge falls by at least two thirds of that fall). It holds no vine water use, canopy or weather-station data that Agrarium can use.
