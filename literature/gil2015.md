# Gil et al. 2015: the SWEB wetness model adapted to greenhouse tomato

Magarey et al. 2006's surface wetness energy balance model (SWEB), changed to run in a greenhouse with dew as the only water input, tested against dielectric wetness sensors in two Colombian greenhouses. No record: SWEB's equations are not printed in this paper, only cited to Magarey et al. 2006 (l. 97-105), so there is nothing to record or test from here.

## What it holds

- **Greenhouses** (l. 157, 109, 112-113): Santa Sofia (2427 m) and Sutamarchan (2112 m), Alto Ricaurte, Boyaca; cultivars Bachue and Ichiban; periods of 40 days (12 December 2012 to 21 January 2013) and 36 days (29 August to 4 October 2012). The dates are ambiguous in the text.
- **Model structure** (l. 99-105): four sub-modules (surface water distribution, canopy water budget, energy balance, transfer function); Tanner & Fuchs 1968's combination energy balance; Bird et al. 1960's transfer coefficient "previously calibrated" to surface wetness under controlled conditions (the study is not named, l. 104). Hourly time step. Precipitation interception removed (l. 140-144).
- **Parameters** (l. 124-138): LAI 3 m2 m-2; Wmax 0.5 (Magarey et al. 2006, l. 125); crop height 2 m (l. 126); C1 measured on 30 leaflets, mean 4.56 cm, range 1.3 to 9.6 cm (l. 138). The surface wetness threshold is not printed.
- **Wind in the greenhouse** (l. 166, page image): Uz = 0.127 + 0.036 Vout (Wang et al. 2000), units not given; borrowed, not refitted.
- **Sensors** (l. 146-151): dielectric sensors (two per height), 10-min logging; wet when at or below 271 mV (set in the laboratory). At Sutamarchan the voltage stays near 270 mV for most of the day, close to that threshold (Figure 2A, by eye).
- **Wetness hours, h per day** (l. 24-25, 351-353, 404, 417): sensors 9.6 (Santa Sofia) and 14.4 (Sutamarchan, two heights); model 9.9 and 12.1. No vertical gradient in the canopy (l. 366).
- **Error** (l. 28-29, 437): mean daily difference 0.8 h at Santa Sofia and about 4 h at Sutamarchan. The 4 h is not signed; the means give a signed gap of 2.3 h (12.1 against 14.4), so it is presumably a mean absolute difference, which the paper does not say. Wilcoxon V = 254.5, p = 0.04 and V = 146, p = 0.002 (l. 431-434).
- **Fraction of correct estimates, two figures for Sutamarchan:** the abstract and text give 92 % (Santa Sofia) and 72 % (Sutamarchan) (l. 31, 403); Table 2 gives 0.92 and 0.77 (l. 415-416). The paper does not reconcile them. Table 2 also: critical success index 0.87 and 0.59; false alarm ratio 0.06 and 0.33; bias score 0.97 and 1.24.
- **Cited about an RH model** (l. 61-70): in an earlier rose-greenhouse study (Gil, Bojacá & Schrevens 2011, Acta Hort. 893:797-804, not read) empirical RH and dew-point-depression thresholds were more accurate and precise than the physical model, which did best at 1.2 m. The paper runs no RH model itself.
- **Not printed:** SWEB's equations, its wetness threshold, the sensor calibration method, any data or code.

## Dependence

- SWEB structure and Wmax from Magarey et al. 2006; energy balance Tanner & Fuchs 1968; transfer coefficient Bird et al. 1960; wind regression Wang et al. 2000. The sensors are the validation, not a fitting set; C1 was measured here.

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

Strand 2 (weather) and strand 3 (canopy physics).

- **Against the engine's RH >= 90 % stand-in:** no comparison here. The only statement is a cited one: in a rose greenhouse thresholds on RH and dew-point depression beat the physical model (lead: Gil et al. 2011). So this paper is no support for replacing the stand-in with an energy balance.
- **What a physical wetness model costs:** LAI, crop height, leaf storage, net radiation, wind. Two sites, one season each, and an error of 0.8 to about 4 h per day on a daily mean of 10 to 14 h.
- **Greenhouse, tomato, dew only:** it does not carry to rain-wetted open vines.
