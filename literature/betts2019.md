# Betts et al. 2019: ERA5 near-surface biases on the Canadian Prairies

Hourly data from four Saskatchewan stations and a BSRN site are used to measure ERA5 biases in
  diurnal temperature, wind, precipitation and radiation, with fits of the temperature bias to
  effective cloud albedo.

## What it holds

- **Temperature:** ERA5 diurnal biases are much reduced against ERA-Interim and do not differ
  with snow cover; fits of the mean bias to ERA5 effective cloud albedo can correct the diurnal
  cycle (l. 16-21).
- **Wind:** a negative wind speed bias that grows about linearly with wind speed and is larger
  by day than by night (l. 21-23).
- **Precipitation:** warm-season ERA5 8 +- 9 % above the Mekis adjusted dataset; winter -22 +-
  7 % below it (l. 27-29).
- **Radiation:** BSRN site at Bratt's Lake, 25 km south of Regina; shortwave and longwave
  biases small (l. 30-33, 137).

## Dependence

- Builds on ERA5 (IFS Cy41r2), the Mekis and Vincent 2011 adjusted precipitation and an ERA-
  Interim comparison (record).

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- s2 (weather for the sweep): sizes of ERA5 bias, with a cloud-albedo fit for temperature and a
  wind bias growing with wind speed; no humidity or leaf wetness. A sweep generator driven by
  ERA5 could take the diurnal temperature correction and the wind-bias slope; the region is
  prairie, not vineyard. Not read in full.
