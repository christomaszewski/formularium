# Hughes & Burnett 2017: evaluating probabilistic disease forecasts with scoring rules

A method for evaluating probabilistic disease forecasts with predictive values, the Brier and divergence scoring rules and an index of separation, split into uncertainty, resolution and reliability (l. 18-25). A different paper from hughes2017 (Hughes, McRoberts & Burnett, 107:158). No record.

## What it holds

- **Method:** scoring rules and their decomposition, with the index of separation and expected mutual information (l. 18-25).
- **Scenarios:** four data scenarios, A, B, C1 and C2, in Table 1 (l. 129), from Sclerotinia stem rot in Sweden (l. 81) and Fusarium head blight in the United States (l. 83); numerical illustration only.
- **Context:** a disease forecast used in management cannot be scored simply because action changes the outcome, unlike a weather forecast (l. 28-40).

## Dependence

- Builds on Bayesian updating of disease prevalence (Madden 2006), the Brier score and the divergence score (Weijs et al. 2010).

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- **Strand s6 (methods):** a recipe for scoring a probabilistic risk output from a simulator against field outcomes, with the uncertainty, resolution and reliability split. A simulator could take it as the scoring step for a sensor-driven risk forecast. No new field data.
