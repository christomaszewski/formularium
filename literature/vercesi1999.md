# Vercesi et al. 1999: when oospores in overwintering leaves mature

An electron-microscopy study of oospores in leaves held outdoors over three winters near Verona.
Cited by `burruano2000` and `taylor2018`.

## What it holds

- **Material** (l. 59-94): leaves with mosaic symptoms from a naturally infected Corvina vineyard near
  Verona; 24 nylon bags a year (100 um mesh, read from context), each with ten oospore-rich leaf
  fragments, laid on the vineyard soil surface; one bag taken at random each month, October 1992
  to May 1995. No temperature, rain or moisture record is printed. Germination assay: 400 isolated
  oospores on water agar at 20 °C for 14 days.
- **Three types** (l. 20-23, 163-180): type I (thin inner wall 0.6 to 1 um, big lipid globules, two nuclei by DAPI) in
  October only and treated as immature; type II (inner wall to 3-3.5 um, one nucleus, fused) from
  November; type III (one to five very large lipid globules) a small minority. From November to
  April only types II and III were found, type II to type III 7-10 to 1 depending on the year; no
  counts printed.
- **Maturity:** ultrastructure does not change from November on; the authors infer mature oospores
  by mid-November (l. 352-353) and that maturation is not what limits germination (l. 354).
- **Germination:** a germ tube 29 to 56 um long with a 27 x 45 um sporangium. In May a few type II
  oospores showed the first signs of germination. Oospores collected in January or February
  germinated in 6 to 7 days in the assay, those from April in 3 to 4 days (l. 315-317).
- **Cited pattern** (l. 44-53; Arens 1929, Zachos 1959, Burruano & Ciofalo 1990, Vavassori 1994): no
  germination before November, peak rates February to April, year and area differences attributed to
  overwintering rain and temperature (Ronzon 1987; Serra & Borgo 1995). No rates printed.
- **Working hypothesis** (l. 361-366, untested): rain and temperature during overwintering act on
  processes leading to germination of already mature oospores.
- **Spring foci** (l. 346-350): in untreated vineyards no primary foci appear in spring even after
  germination weather; the authors attribute it to immature oospores. No data are printed.
- **Not printed:** germination percentages, any date-by-date maturity percentage, weather, and any
  link between one season's disease and the spring's inoculum. The pore size and the DAPI/TEM
  numbers were read from page images.

## Dependence

- No model, equation or data fitted. It cites the authors' own earlier work (Vercesi et al. 1991,
  1993) and the older germination studies. The germination timing it cites is a pattern, not a
  dependence of any formulation.

## Bearing (2026-10-19)

For the autumn before a season:

- **Oospores in October leaves are not yet mature.** At Verona type I (immature) oospores were the
  frequent type in October and only types II and III from November, so oospores in a leaf that
  fell in October count for the next spring only after about mid-November. That bears on a truth
  whose oospore pool is set by autumn disease: maturation takes a month or more after the leaves
  hold oospores, not simply the season's end (inference from one site, three winters).
- **Nothing links the previous season's disease to spring inoculum here.** The leaves were chosen
  rich in oospores; the paper counts no oospores per leaf area, so it gives no dose.
- **Germination timing:** none before November (cited), first signs in May for some type II oospores in
  these bags, 6-7 days to germinate in the assay in January-February; no weather record to tie it to.
