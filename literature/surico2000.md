# Surico et al. 2000: spatial spread of esca in Tuscan vineyards

Four Tuscan vineyards with esca, tested for spatial pattern with dispersion indices, runs tests and distance-class analysis. The patterns point to airborne spread from distant or internal sources, not to pruning tools.

## What it holds

- Cumulated incidence over 4 to 6 years: 49.09% at Gambassi Terme, and an average of 13% across three Castelnuovo Berardenga blocks (l. 26-28).
- Aggregation appeared only where incidence was higher (GTFI and CBSI-3), with infected vines aggregated along columns in CBSI-3 (l. 29-32).
- Conclusion: esca spread "by airborne spores from distant and/or internal sources" rather than by contaminated pruning tools (l. 36-37).
- Survey periods: GTFI 1993 to 1998, CBSI 1995 to 1998 (l. 94-95).

## Dependence

- Dispersion indices (Morisita, Lloyd, variance-to-mean) and the Madden et al. 1982 runs test (l. 110-111).
- 2DCLASS distance-class analysis (Gray et al. 1986 and others, l. 112).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t6 (regional spread), for a trunk disease. The down-column test is a descriptive check, not a dispersal rule.
- The paper gives no transmission rate or kernel a simulator could take. Not read in full.
