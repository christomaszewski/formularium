# Bell, Dance & Waller 2022: biases of built-in car air-temperature sensors against a 1.5 m model field

A Met Office proof-of-concept trial of vehicle air-temperature readings sent from smartphones, with quality control and the differences between the readings and a convection-permitting model.

**Read this first.**
- The 5 to 25 C engine-proximity warm bias is not measured here. It is a rough estimate cited as a personal communication (Mercelis et al., 2021) (l. 747). The paper's own numbers are differences from a model, not from a reference thermometer.
- The reading is of the arXiv v1 text; the journal version may differ. The reading agent did not read the data statement.

## What it holds

- **Engine warm bias, cited (l. 747):** a "rough estimate of the bias due to the vicinity of the engine is 5" to 25 C for sensors near the engine; personal communication, not measured.
- **Cited trial (l. 169):** in Mercelis et al. 2020, "sensors near the vehicle engine had to be discarded due to sensor biases"; sensors far from the engine agreed with road stations.
- **Degree of influence (l. 164, l. 744):** the engine's influence depends on sensor position; the external sensors "may exhibit a warm" bias (section 2.2).
- **Ventilation threshold (l. 419, l. 427):** the largest air temperatures (above 26 C) occurred for speeds below 25 km/h; the authors set a 25 km/h minimum speed. Low-speed data are flagged.
- **Quality control (l. 430):** the QC dataset holds 17 425 observations, 25.6 % of the original; 74.4 % were removed (arithmetic from the printed share), by speed and GPS checks.
- **Bias against the model (l. 809, table):** QC dataset, observation minus 1.5 m model: mean +0.67 C, standard deviation 1.24 C. Against the surface-air model: +0.12 C, standard deviation 1.59 C (the reading agent's claim; the quoted line is the second row only).
- **Sunny conditions (l. 812):** 1 878 sunny observations; mean bias against the 1.5 m model +1.05 C (reading agent, from the next line).
- **Per vehicle (l. 869, l. 837):** mean bias against the 1.5 m model for 12 vehicles, moving above 25 km/h, from -0.56 to +1.80 C (the row begins 0.43, 1.24, 0.89 and so on). The text notes vehicle-to-vehicle spread.
- **Precision (l. 742):** 1 C reporting precision; the root-mean-square quantisation error is 1/12 (printed, units as in the text).

## Dependence

- Warm-bias range cited from Mercelis et al. (personal communication, 2021) and the Mercelis et al. 2020 trial. Own data: the Met Office trial; comparison with the Met Office UKV model. Leads: Mercelis et al. 2020 (IEEE VTC2020-Spring); Mahoney & O'Sullivan 2013 (BAMS 94:1007).

## Bearing (2026-11-04)

- **platform.petrol.probe_warm_bias (Cooptera, vehicle-borne sensing):** the fleet means are small once slow data are removed: +0.67 C mean against the 1.5 m model, vehicles from -0.56 to +1.80 C (model-dated, not a thermometer reference). The 5 to 25 C figure for engine-near sensors is a citation of a personal communication and not a measurement.
- A speed filter at 25 km/h is the authors' remedy for low-ventilation warming. Whether it holds for a slow vehicle in a vineyard is not shown.
- Kind of evidence: crowd-sourced car sensors, UK roads, 1 C resolution; no vineyard.
