# Carisse et al. 2021: airborne inoculum of two P. viticola clades in Quebec

From Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada (Saint-Jean-sur-Richelieu), Phytodata and INRAE Bordeaux.

## What it holds

- Carisse et al. measured airborne P. viticola sporangia in Quebec, not disease onset (l. 26).
- Two experimental vineyards 2015-2018; 11, 14 and 15 commercial vineyards in 2016, 2017, 2018 (l. 258).
- A rotating-arm impaction sampler at 45 cm, run 50% of the time from 08:00 to 14:00, three times a week (l. 264, 265, 267). Sporangia are counted by clade-specific qPCR (limit one sporangium, l. 343).
- Chancellor vineyard: almost only clade riparia (l. 351). Lesion sporangia were identified by clade at least three times a season (l. 254).
- Cumulative inoculum as a proportion of the season's maximum (PASC) was fitted with PASC = Y0 + a e^(b t), t in days since 1 May (l. 301, 304), to 80 curves (l. 294).
- Time to 50% PASC: riparia 105.0, 100.9, 96.5 days (l. 359); aestivalis 125.6, 117.7, 110.3 days (l. 361); means 100.8 and 117.9 (l. 26), 17 days apart (l. 363).
- The authors infer riparia epidemics start over 2 weeks earlier (l. 461).
- No model dated or screened the data. Descriptive; a check on timing of airborne inoculum, not a formulation.

## Dependence

- Its own sampler and qPCR data; no model dated or screened them (l. 301). Clade sequences from Rouxel et al. (Delmotte's group).
- No author of an engine model.

## Bearing (2026-10-08)

- Clear. A timing pattern for airborne sporangia (50% of the season's inoculum 100.8 days after 1 May for clade riparia, 117.9 for aestivalis; l. 26) and a qPCR sampler with a one-sporangium limit (l. 343): data for M2's spore-trap operator.
