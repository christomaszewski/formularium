# Shah et al. 2014: boosted regression trees on the same 527 wheat Fusarium head blight observations (analogue crop)

Refits the 527-observation wheat set of shah2013 with boosted regression trees on 350 weather variables and compares test misclassification with the earlier logistic models. No record: a method with measured results.

**Read this first.** Wheat, not a vine, and the same random 70/30 split as shah2013, so the comparison is paired but no year or state is held out. The weather inputs are hourly temperature, relative humidity and rain only.

## What it holds

- **Real data** (l. 4): the same 527 observations from 15 states over 26 years (shah2013's set); labels are observed severity, major epidemic at 10% or more (l. 4).
- **Inputs** (l. 4): 350 weather variables from hourly temperature, relative humidity and rain in windows around anthesis, plus categorical variables for wheat type, corn residue and cultivar resistance.
- **Synthetic or augmented data:** none.
- **Validation** (l. 4, 9, 16): one fixed random split, 369 observations for training and 158 for testing, "by the same split used previously"; each imputed weather data set was split alike.
- **Score** (l. 9): boosted trees had test misclassification 0.23, "31% lower than the average misclassification rate over 15 logistic regression models we had presented earlier" (those averaged 0.34 on test in shah2013, l. 123 there).
- **Variables** (l. 9): the strongest predictors were mean daily relative humidity, mean daily temperature, and the number of hours with temperature between 9 and 30 degC and relative humidity of 90% or more at the same time. Predicted risk rose substantially when mean daily RH passed 70%, "a lower threshold than previously modeled for most plant pathosystems".
- **Authors' caution** (l. 11): "not all researchers are convinced by claims of their superior predictive performance over more traditional methods".

## Dependence

- Labels observed; no process model. Baseline: the authors' own logistic models of shah2013 and the national De Wolf models. Same data and authors as shah2013 and shah2023: a flag.

## Bearing (2026-10-27)

- **D58 (variable search):** a ceiling on the same hourly T, RH and rain: test misclassification 0.23 with boosted trees against 0.34 with logistic models, on one random split of 158 test observations. The variables that led (mean RH, mean temperature, a joint temperature-band and RH 90% hours count) are all from the hourly series, but none was tested on held-out years.
- **RH threshold:** 70% mean daily RH as a jump point is lower than the 80 to 90% used in earlier models; one split, one crop.
- **Not shown:** held-out years, synthetic training (D60), a vine.
