# Angeli et al. n.d.: Ampelomyces strains vs powdery mildew, Trentino

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- Greenhouse trials (2008) compared Ampelomyces quisqualis strain AQ10 with yeasts, bacteria and natural substances against grapevine powdery mildew (l. 55-70).
- Natural parasitism of powdery mildew cleistothecia by A. quisqualis rose from 0.17 per cent (2004) to 3.51 per cent (2007) across 27 vineyards (l. 232-234).
- Parasitised cleistothecia per vineyard rose from 3 to 30 over the same years (l. 187-189).
- About 200 strains found; three representative ones kept; 22 strains tested (l. 239-249, 275).
- On cucumber, four strains cut conidia by 77 to 88 per cent; AQ10 cut 22 to 63 per cent; ATCC grapevine strains 32 to 37 per cent (l. 285-291).
- Highly aggressive strains cut sporulation by more than 90 per cent; strains show no origin-linked differentiation (l. 367-376).

## Dependence

- none. This is a mycoparasite biocontrol study with greenhouse trials and a parasitism survey. It computes, borrows and fits no engine model, and it has no incubation, sporangia survival, wetness or infection rule. Its temperature and humidity remarks are qualitative (l. 37-41), with no equation.

## Bearing (2026-10-10)
