# Katurji & Zawar-Reza 2016: infrared camera on a vineyard canopy

A technical note on a FLIR camera recording canopy brightness temperature every 10 s at night,
  set against eddy-covariance data and clustered with self-organising maps.

## What it holds

- **Site and time:** Marlborough, South Island (l. 106); night of 17 to 18 May 2014 in quiet
  synoptic conditions (l. 388-389).
- **Instrument:** FLIR A644sc, 7.5 to 14 µm, 50 Hz (l. 263); frames at 10 s intervals (abstract
  l. 15).
- **Result:** the brightness-temperature signal follows turbulent heat flux surges and above-
  canopy wind, and the spatial pattern follows cloud cover and downwelling long-wave radiation
  (l. 16-22).

## Dependence

- Builds on self-organising maps and an eddy-covariance system, as the record states.

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- s4 and s3: spatial canopy surface temperature at night, which is where dew and wetness form;
  no wetness data. s5: a camera, not a point sensor. A simulator could take the point that
  canopy surface temperature varies in space during stable nights. Not read in full.
