# Viruega et al. 2013: olive scab survival, inoculum and splash dispersal

From the Universidad de Córdoba. Olive, not grape; read for its dispersal gradient.

## What it holds

- Study: 4 seasons (Nov 1993 to Sep 1997) in one 1.2-ha Picual olive orchard near Cordoba, Spain (l. 83).
- Lesion conidia counted every 2 weeks on 120 leaves (l. 94); peak 1 to 5 x 10^5 conidia cm-2, November to February in favourable years (l. 22).
- No conidia on soil-surface leaves; old conidia last up to 12 weeks (l. 245). No sexual spores (l. 49). Summer: no conidia (l. 211).
- Cumulative viable conidia per cm2 follows a monomolecular curve in t days from 22 September (l. 224) ; fits 16.702 - 0.034t, 41.371 - 0.1384t, 15.600 - 0.0281t (l. 234) (l. 235).
- Splash dispersal: slide traps at 0, 4, 10, 40 m, five one-week runs, 0 to 114.0 mm rain (l. 177) (l. 183) (l. 368).
- Under the canopy, 1,129.80 conidia/cm2 in the wettest week and 5.11 in the dry week (l. 368) (l. 372). Counts rise linearly with rain (Y = 9.440X at 0 m) (l. 373).
- Printed gradient Y = 396.52 exp(-0.4335 S), R2 0.9890 (l. 273). It gives 70 at 4 m; Table 2 mean there is 7.1. Refit: b about 1.0 m-1 at 0-4 m, falling with distance (checked by the reading agent).
- Burkard trap: 47 conidia in 6 months (l. 272); the authors conclude rain-splash dispersal (l. 413).

## Dependence

- Its own counts in one orchard, 1993-1997; generic forms only (monomolecular, exponential; l. 158, 434). No model dated or selected the data.
- No author of an engine model.

## Bearing (2026-10-08)

- Clear. Its printed gradient, Y = 396.52·e^(-0.4335·S) (l. 273), does not reproduce its own Table 2 (70 against 7.1 conidia at 4 m, the earlier session's check, confirmed): refit from Table 2 before use. Splash, not air, carried the conidia (a Burkard caught 47 in six months, l. 272), as Agrarium's dispersal assumes.
