# Rao et al. 2026: worker movements between properties and kiwifruit Psa-V spread

A metapopulation model of Psa-V in New Zealand kiwifruit, driven by 86,624 worker movements
between properties in 2022. It is calibrated to the 2010 to 2012 national outbreak counts.
Most spread is local, but some long links are needed to match the national curve. Kiwifruit,
not grapevine: the "vine" in the abstract is kiwifruit.

## What it holds

- Data: 86,624 worker movements between properties in 2022, from a company's biosecurity
  platform covering over 20,000 properties (l. 18, 29).
- Calibration: 14 monthly counts of new infected properties, national, not geolocated (l. 70).
  The seasonal network fits best by RMSE (l. 110). For the moving-average data the peak is
  5.5% to 16.6% lower than observed; raw peaks are underestimated (l. 109, 143).
- Local spread: over 90% of new infections lie within 10 km of their likely source (l. 114).
  Long-range links are needed for a nationwide outbreak (abstract, l. 9).
- Sensitivity: outbreak size depends on the month of importation. Autumn and winter
  importations give higher incidence at the peak (l. 132). Onset comes 2 to 4 months and
  peak 7 to 8 months after importation (l. 133).
- The cost of the 2010 outbreak is put at $310 to $410 million over five years (l. 19).

## Dependence

- Metapopulation model of susceptible and infected properties (ref. 48), and temporal network
  snapshots after Bajardi et al. (ref. 44) for the seasonal network. Calibrated to a 2012
  report from Kiwifruit Vine Health (ref. 52). The movement data come from one company (Onside).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t6 (regional spread). The method is a transferable way to count between-property
  human movement, by season, as a spread term. Long-range links and season set the outbreak.
- Not grapevine, and a bacterium on kiwifruit. A vineyard simulator could only borrow the
  structure: a seasonal worker-and-machinery movement term between blocks or farms. Not read
  in full.
