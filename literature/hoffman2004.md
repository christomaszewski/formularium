# Hoffman et al. 2004: black rot control timed to the berries' susceptible weeks

Five seasons (1995-1999) of myclobutanil programmes at 2-week intervals in two New York
vineyards, Dresden (moderate pressure) and Naples (extreme), against host phenology,
ascospore availability and the removal of mummies from the trellis.

## What it holds

- Sprays over the 4 weeks up to the immediate prebloom stage gave only 13-91 % control of
  diseased clusters at Dresden (l. 29-30), although 95 % of the season's ascospores were
  released while they protected (l. 18). Sprays just before bloom and 2 and 4 weeks later,
  covering the fruit's highly susceptible period, gave virtually complete control (l. 20-21).
- Mummies left in the trellis at Naples kept releasing ascospores and conidia for 6-8 weeks
  after those on the ground had stopped (l. 29-30).
- From the introduction (cited, refs 1, 10, 19): laboratory discharge released 90-100 % of
  New York mummies' ascospores by bloom to a month after; trapping ended about 3-4 weeks
  after bloom in Switzerland (l. 49).
- The companion paper on the berries' susceptible period is its ref. 15, Hoffman et al.
  2002, Phytopathology 92:1068-1076 (berry age, susceptibility and incubation;
  literature/hoffman2002).

## Dependence

- Its own field trials. The earlier reduced-spray programme it revisits used curative
  sprays after a putative infection period (ref. 33, Spotts 1977, printed with volume 76 for 67;
  literature/spotts1977), not this paper's design. Wilcox, Gadoury and Seem are authors of the
  engine's Kennelly pieces: flags.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)
