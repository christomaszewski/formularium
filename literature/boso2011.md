# Boso et al. 2011: field susceptibility of 44 varieties to downy mildew, Pontevedra 2009 and 2010

A two-year natural-infection screen of 44 varieties in one Galician collection plot, scored for incidence and severity on leaves and clusters.

**Read this first.** No Macabeo (Viura), Xarel·lo, Parellada or Chardonnay appears anywhere in the paper (Table 1, l. 81). The "Mean" columns of Tables 3 and 4 are not the arithmetic mean of the two year columns (Table 3: 44 of 44 leaf rows and 36 of 44 cluster rows differ by more than 0.05; Table 4: 17 leaf and 22 cluster rows); the paper does not say how they are formed, so use the 2009 and 2010 columns where the arithmetic matters. Severity is coarse: the nine classes of Table 2 (0, 1, 5, 10, 25, 50, 75, 90, 100 %; l. 164-187) give many ties and LSD values of 0 (l. 345). The text and the tables disagree in places: the "least susceptible" four (Silveiriña, Caíño Bravo, Follajeiro, Brancellao Blanco) are said to have severity under 5 % in both organs in both years, but Table 4 prints 25 % leaf severity in 2010 for all four (l. 355-358). Table 1 lists Ratiño where Tables 3 and 4 carry Blanca Cabanelas. A few Table 3 cells have fewer decimals ("3.97", "53.3"; l. 275-276).

## What it holds

- **Site and method:** 44 varieties of the MBG-CSIC collection, 10 plants each, one 1.4 ha plot at Pontevedra (42 25 12 N, 8 38 8 W; 35 m; sandy loam), grafted 11-13 April 1994, 2.5 x 2 m (2,000 plants per ha), Sylvoz trellis (l. 120-130, 66). Natural infection scored in 2009 and 2010 only, April to October, by the method of Boso et al. (2005) and OIV (2009): leaves 3 weeks after the start of flowering, clusters before veraison (l. 142-147). Incidence = affected / total x 100 per plant; severity = affected area share on the Table 2 scale (l. 148, 200). Mean annual temperature 14 degC, annual rain 1,687 mm (l. 135-136). No fungicide information and no inoculation.
- **The two years:** 2009 was judged optimal, with several infection cycles from June to August (l. 186-187, 222-223); in 2010 there was one serious infection at the end of June, and a hot, dry July and August stopped further infection (l. 188-190). Means over varieties (Table 3, l. 282): cluster incidence 69.50 % (2009) and 59.16 % (2010); leaf incidence 38.9 and 35.7 %. Variety, year and their interaction were all significant (P < 0.001; l. 172-175).
- **Spread among varieties** (Table 3 and 4 "Mean" columns, as printed): cluster incidence from 96.32 % (Chenin Blanc, l. 244) to 12.85 % (Caíño Bravo, l. 279), about 7.5-fold (derived); leaf incidence from 63.19 % (Albariño, l. 240) to 14.38 % (Verdejo Negro, l. 268); cluster severity from 100 % (Chenin Blanc, Petit Bouschet, Tempranillo; l. 300, 306, 317) to 5.0 % (eight varieties, including Chasselas Dorée; l. 318, 334, 336-341); leaf severity from 75.0 % (Albariño, l. 302) to 5.0 % (Albarín Francés, l. 343). Top five by cluster incidence: Chenin Blanc 96.32, Albarín Negro 96.10, Moscatel de Grano Menudo Rojo 94.51, Petit Bouschet 93.74, Tempranillo 92.90 (l. 244, 253, 245, 265, 239); bottom five: Caíño Bravo 12.85, Follajeiro 14.73, Couxo 15.29, Silveiriña 20.28, Treixadura 31.93 (l. 279, 278, 280, 281, 250).
- **Varieties others use** (cluster incidence / cluster severity, "Mean"): Chenin Blanc 96.32 / 100.0 (l. 244, 300); Albariño 80.18 / 84.7 (l. 240, 302); Savagnin Blanc 72.62 / 30.0 (l. 247, 313); Chasselas Dorée 73.07 / 5.0 (l. 270, 336); Palomino 56.44 / 57.5 (l. 261, 301); Cabernet Sauvignon 52.70 / 51.7 (l. 255, 303); Tempranillo 92.90 / 100.0 (l. 239, 317). Chasselas Dorée, the susceptible reference of `gindro2022`, is among the least severe here. The year effect can be large for one variety: Cabernet Sauvignon cluster incidence 78.51 % in 2009 and 14.01 % in 2010 (l. 255), Caíño Blanco 81.72 and 11.20 % (l. 246).
- **What did not explain the ranking:** incidence and severity were not correlated, nor leaves and clusters (l. 26); leaf against cluster r2 = 0.0775 for incidence and 0.4097 for severity (l. 372), so the leaf rank does not predict the cluster rank. No relation with berry colour, leaf hairs or cluster compactness (l. 366-400). The authors guess that regional varieties present for over 300 years show least disease, a hypothesis only (l. 433-437).
- **Data:** Tables 3 and 4 are the only data; no repository or licence stated.

## Dependence

- **Borrows** the scoring from Boso et al. (2005) and OIV (2009); the severity scale is adapted from Mohibullah (1991), a study of onion and other bulb vegetable diseases, not of grapevine (l. 195, 472). Phenology and ampelography from Martínez and Pérez (1999, 2000), Santiago et al. (2005a, b) and Gago et al. (2009).
- **Same group and collection** as Boso and Kassemeyer 2008 (held, `boso2008`): shared authors and collection, a flag only.

## Bearing (2026-10-22)

- **Penedès whites:** none appear (no Macabeo, Xarel·lo, Parellada or Chardonnay), so the paper gives no Penedès variety its place in the ranking. Of the varieties other sources use, Palomino and Cabernet Sauvignon rank 29th and 31st of 44 by cluster incidence mean and Chasselas Dorée 18th (counted from Table 3's means).
- **Spread:** between the most and least susceptible variety the cluster incidence mean differs about 7.5-fold (96.32 against 12.85 %), cluster severity 20-fold (100 against 5.0 %); but the same variety moves by up to several-fold between the two years, and severity is in coarse classes. A cultivar multiplier for downy mildew infection is therefore not derivable from rank alone, and leaf data cannot scale bunch infection (r2 0.0775 for incidence).
- **Kind of result:** a field observation in a single plot, two seasons, natural infection, ordinal severity; no weather by month in the text (Figure 1 is a graph) and no spray record.
