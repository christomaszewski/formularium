# Shah et al. 2023: random forests on 999 wheat Fusarium head blight observations with nested resampling (analogue crop)

Builds random forests for a major epidemic (index of 10% or more) from 328 weather summaries, with three variable-selection algorithms and nested resampling to measure the stability of the selected variables. No record: a method with measured results.

**Read this first.** Wheat, not a vine. The resampling is by observation: individual observations are "assumed to be spatially and temporally uncorrelated", so one season's weather can sit in both training and assessment folds. The held-out AUCs by model are in figures; only the nested-resampling mean is printed.

## What it holds

- **Real data** (l. 22): 999 observations of the FHB index over 32 years (1982 to 2015) from 17 states in five climate regions; the index is the mean percentage of spike surface with symptoms. Labels are observed.
- **Class balance** (l. 24): 73% of observations were non-epidemics, so 27% epidemics (arithmetic, not printed). No oversampling; the best cut-point averaged 0.29 and not 0.5 (l. 55).
- **Inputs** (l. 24): 328 weather variables summarising conditions in windows relative to anthesis (hourly temperature, humidity and rain), cut to 90 by removing zero-variance variables and any with correlation above 0.9; the 90 include categorical variables for resistance and for maize residue or spring wheat.
- **Synthetic data:** none.
- **Validation** (l. 24, 29): nested resampling, outer 10-fold cross-validation repeated 10 times, inner 25 bootstrap resamples with Boruta, varSelRF and VSURF selection; "not sufficiently large to split into independent training, validation, and test sets". Random by observation, so seasons are not held out.
- **Score** (l. 37): mean outer-fold ROC-AUC 0.898 (minimum 0.792, maximum 0.972, standard deviation 0.036) for forests of a median 79 variables. Post-selection filtering gave 58 competitive forests with no more than 14 predictors (abstract, l. 10).
- **Against baselines** (l. 55): the forests had higher sensitivity and specificity, ROC-AUC and PR-AUC and lower Brier scores than earlier logistic models (De Wolf et al. 2003; Shah et al. 2013, 2019, 2021) in the figures; those are the earlier models' reported values, so the splits differ.
- **Variables** (l. 10, 74): the most frequently selected predictor was a temperature-stability term in the 20 days before anthesis, then mean dew point and two VPD windows; "a departure from the prominence of relative humidity-based variables previously reported in LR models".

## Dependence

- Labels observed; predictors are summaries of hourly T, RH and rain (the 328 variables are cited from a 2021 paper). Baseline: the authors' earlier logistic models. Same authors and a successor set to shah2013 and shah2014: a flag.

## Bearing (2026-10-27)

- **D58 (variable search):** a ceiling on hourly T, RH and rain with 999 observations: ROC-AUC 0.90 on random folds. The leading variables depend on the model class (temperature variability, dew point and VPD here, relative humidity in shah2013), so a ranking from one class is not a result.
- **Design lesson:** shows how to quantify selection stability (25 bootstrap resamples per outer fold) when variables number in the hundreds and observations in the hundreds; the independence assumption flatters the score on seasons not seen.
- **Not shown:** held-out years or states, synthetic training (D60), a vine.
