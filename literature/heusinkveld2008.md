# Heusinkveld 2008: dew measurement and micrometeorology in the NW Negev

A thesis on dew formation and evaporation in a sandy arid coastal site, with two new sensors (an automated microlysimeter and a remote optical surface wetness sensor), flux measurements and a dew model (l. 119-130). No record.

## What it holds

- **Dew amount:** microlysimeter records give dew of almost 0.4 mm a day, depending strongly on soil type (l. 124-125).
- **Wetness sensor:** a remote optical sensor of surface wetness at the interface, non-destructive and usable in sunlight, which measured leaf water content and near-surface soil water (l. 126-132).
- **Energy balance:** a new near-surface soil heat flux approach gave very good closure of the surface energy balance (l. 138-140).
- **Dew model:** a detailed model for dew yield; 0.30 mm a day possible 40 km from the coast at the end of the dry season, 0.10 mm a day on the artificial flat surface tested (l. 163-166).

## Dependence

- Builds on the sensors developed in the thesis and on eddy-covariance and soil heat flux measurements; a Penman model with a soil vapour pressure reduction is used for dew (l. 143-145).

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- **Strand s5 (sensor error) and s4:** a wetness-sensor design and a statement that dew can foul open-path gas analysers unless heated. A simulator could take the idea that a wetness record depends on the sensor type; the site is a desert, not a vineyard, and no wetness-duration figure for a canopy was located.
