# Bailey and Stoll 2013: a row canopy behaves, on average, like a uniform canopy of the right effective leaf area index

A large-eddy simulation of sparse, row-oriented canopies against uniform canopies of equal effective leaf area; only the abstract was read.

**Read this first.** Abstract only, and model output, not measurement. The canopies are idealised rows, not vines, and the abstract does not say how the effective leaf area index is chosen.

## What it holds

- Heterogeneous row canopies with varying row vegetation density and spacing were simulated and compared with horizontally homogeneous canopies of equivalent effective leaf area index (page abstract).
- With a proper effective leaf area index, plane-averaged mean velocities and bulk scaling parameters had only small errors when the heterogeneity was ignored; many cases had significantly larger second- and third-order velocity moments with heterogeneity (l. 41).
- With large row spacing, coherent structures penetrated deeper into the canopy than in the equivalent homogeneous canopy (l. 41).

## Bearing (2026-10-25)

- **Canopy wind (SWEB Eq 15):** supports using a uniform-canopy wind formula for the row-averaged wind, provided the leaf area is entered as an effective value. It gives no ratio of mid-canopy to open wind, and its second-order statistics (gusts, mixing) do differ from a uniform canopy.
- Kind of result: model (large-eddy simulation) in an abstract; not vine-specific.
