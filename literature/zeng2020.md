# Zeng et al. 2020: an essay on how OSSEs are used in the United States, with recommendations and limits

A short essay based on a NOAA Science Advisory Board task force's white paper: what OSSEs have been used for, how they should be calibrated, and where their conclusions are weak. No record: a review by practitioners.

**Read this first.** Written by people who run OSSEs. Its claim that early OSSEs "determined correctly" the pre-launch potential of proposed systems has no numbers or table in the essay. Only parts were read.

## What it holds

- **A claim of success, not a measurement** (l. 120, 126): the OSSEs of the 1980s onward "determined correctly the quantitative potential" for several proposed satellite systems before launch, and the current methodology "has been accepted nationally and internationally" as the way to get credible results.
- **Calibrate with OSEs** (l. 297): a recommendation to use OSEs for current observation systems "for calibration of OSSEs".
- **Replace the nature run** (l. 292): use different approaches to replace the nature run, since any given nature run may have deficiencies. A continuous long nature run "is likely to drift away from a truthful representation" through model-physics, dynamics and boundary errors (l. 332).
- **Reliability depends on the assimilation system** (l. 312, 314-318): reliability depends critically on the data assimilation method and the forecast models, and relative impacts "may depend critically on the data assimilation system". Example from real operational systems, cited: NCEP's 2018 system showed no quantifiable impact from cloud- and rain-affected satellite radiances, while ECMWF's 2018 system ranked them as the most impactful source.
- **Assumed error characteristics** (l. 319, 322): reliability depends on the assumed error characteristics of a new observing system, unknown before launch; it can be addressed by running best-case and worst-case error scenarios.
- **Quick OSSEs** (l. 327): the authors "strongly caution against" using single extreme-event nature runs in decision-making for observing-system design.
- **Ocean** (l. 345): ocean OSE and OSSE conclusions about impacts need to be questioned because ocean models are deficient in capturing important modes and variability.
