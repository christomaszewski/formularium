# Czemmel et al. 2015: leaf genes and latent wood infection

A glasshouse study of the latent phase of Neofusicoccum parvum in Cabernet Sauvignon vines. Four leaf genes are proposed as candidate host markers of latent wood infection.

## What it holds

- Latent phase from 0 to 1.5 months after inoculation, with no spread beyond the inoculation site. Lesions tripled between 1.5 and 2 months (l. 37-41).
- Design: four sets of plants inoculated at two-week intervals for two months, in a glasshouse (l. 151-153).
- RNA-Seq and a screen of 21 grape microarray datasets gave 20 candidate genes. The four best were not affected by other foliar or wood pathogens or by abiotic stresses (l. 44-50).
- RNA-Seq data at GEO, accession GSE58653 (l. 49, 322).

## Dependence

- The screen of 21 public grape microarray datasets for candidate genes (l. 144). No other model or dataset is recorded.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strands t4 (other grape diseases: a trunk pathogen) and t2 (observation). A latent-phase marker would matter for a screening tool.
- The paper gives no detection accuracy, field lead time or test on other cultivars, so this is a lead and not a tool. Not read in full.
