# Nail & Howell 2004: powdery mildew and leaf carbon assimilation in potted Chardonnay

Potted Chardonnay vines were inoculated with grape powdery mildew. Leaf gas exchange was measured against CO2 and light, and chlorophyll fluorescence was measured as well, at Michigan State University in the 2002 season.

## What it holds

- **Severity (l. 123, 126-127):** infected leaves had 60 to 90 % of their area diseased. Vines were estimated at 50 % severity at measurement.
- **Gas exchange at 360 ppm (Table 1, l. 164-166; l. 168):** A360 4.1 against 10.8 µmol CO2 m⁻² s⁻¹. A360 and gs360 on infected vines were 38 % and 36 % of those on healthy vines.
- **Carboxylation and maximum uptake (l. 164-165, 170-171):** carboxylation efficiency k 0.037 against 0.101 (37 %). Amax at high CO2 10.4 against 21.9 (47 %).
- **Light use and fluorescence (l. 169; abstract l. 24):** quantum yield φ 52 % lower in infected leaves. Fv/Fm unchanged.
- **Stomatal limit (Table 1):** lg 52.2 % in infected leaves against 39.4 % in healthy ones.

## Dependence

None recorded. The response-curve and stomatal-limitation methods follow the cited work of Layne and Flore and of Jones (1985), as the paper cites them.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- **Strand t5 (damage).** A leaf-level damage term. At about 50 % severity, infected leaves kept about 38 % of their CO2 uptake at 360 ppm, and their light use fell by 52 %. In a canopy model this is a per-leaf photosynthesis cut that scales with severity.
- **Not read in full.** One cultivar, potted vines, one season. The paper gives no link from canopy severity to yield, so the step from leaf to vine needs a separate source.
