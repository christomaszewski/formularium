# Vogel et al. 2020: timing and extent of fruit zone leaf removal, Chardonnay, Georgia

Seven leaf-removal treatments on Chardonnay in a commercial vineyard in Dahlonega, Georgia,
over 2017 and 2018. Removing leaves after fruit set cut bunch rot, lowered acidity and kept
yield. The rot effect was not the same in the two seasons.

## What it holds

- Design: seven treatments, from no removal (NO) to prebloom four or six leaves (PB-4, PB-6),
  post-fruit-set four or six (PFS-4, PFS-6), and the two-stage PB-2/PFS-2 and PB-3/PFS-3
  (abstract, l. 42 to 45; Dahlonega, l. 134).
- Rot, 2017: botrytis severity was 48% to 77% higher in NO than in every treatment except PB-6
  (l. 230 to 231). No sour-rot effect in 2017 (l. 232 to 233).
- Rot, 2018: botrytis incidence fell by 50% (PFS-4, PB-2/PFS-2), 59% (PB-4), 71% (PB-6), 76%
  (PB-3/PFS-3) and 80% (PFS-6) (l. 235 to 237). Severity fell 94% only with PFS-6 (l. 238 to
  239). Sour rot fell 63% (PFS-6) and 49% (PB-3/PFS-3) (l. 241 to 242).
- Fruit-zone radiation (CEFA, "incident radiation reached the clusters"): PB-6 and PFS-6 were
  423.1% and 407.7% above NO (l. 240). Season 2017: 2,276 growing degree-days and 1,088 mm of rain, April to October
  (l. 240 to 241).
- Yield and acidity: fruit-zone removal lowered titratable acidity and raised soluble solids.
  PB-6 cut berry number and cluster weight, and yield, against PFS-4 (abstract, l. 49 to 53).
  The authors recommend post-fruit-set removal to zero leaf layers over no removal.

## Dependence

- Builds on earlier fruit-zone leaf-removal and bunch-rot work: Poni et al. 2006, Reynolds
  and Wolf 2008, Hed et al. 2015, Hickey and Wolf 2018 (introduction). No dataset or model from another group is recorded.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strands t4 (other grape diseases) and t5 (damage and economics). Bunch rot falls with
  leaf-layer removal near the fruit, and the two seasons disagree. A simulator's rot term
  should take leaf layers at the fruit zone, not leaf area alone.
- Two seasons on one commercial site, with a Georgia climate, not a general rule. Not read in
  full.
