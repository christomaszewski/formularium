# Pla & Nacci 2001(?): traditional and new vineyard land management in Alt Penedès

A proceedings paper describing traditional contour terraces and the new mechanised layout in two Catalan dry-land vineyard regions, Alt Penedès and Priorat, with erosion and soil effects.

**Read this first.**
- The year is not printed on the first page (title, authors, abstract only). It is kept as 2001(?) because it cites Nacci 2001. The id stays.
- The text layer is imperfect (the abstract has "mark" for marl). The "5-20 ha" figures are printed once for family properties and once for "every field"; the two uses differ (see below).
- Statements are the authors' description of the area, not measurements made for this paper.

## What it holds

- **Slope and altitude:** Alt Penedès cropped fields on 4-20 % slopes, at altitudes of 250-400 m (l. 122).
- **Property size:** Catalan dry-land vineyards are still in "relatively small (5-20 ha) family properties" (l. 81). This is property size, not parcel size.
- **Traditional layout:** planted along contour lines, in broad-based terraces, supported by stone walls on steeper slopes (cited to Ramos & Porta 1997), with "every field (5-20 ha)" surrounded by drainage ditches (l. 133, 134). The 5-20 ha is as printed; it reads as a property, not a single planted block.
- **New layout:** straight long rows separated by 2-3 m (the number is on l. 143, the sentence ends on l. 144), for mechanised work including harvest, with fields enlarged and slopes smoothed (l. 142-145). The sister paper (pla2001) prints "more than two meters".
- **Terraces in new vineyards:** ploughing displaces 20-40 cm of surface soil from the upper to the lower part of the inter-rows (l. 167), and every 10 to 15 rows, depending on slope, a narrow (2-3 m wide) bench terrace with reverse slope is built to stop runoff and sediment (l. 168). The soil movement implies rows run across the slope (implied, not stated as azimuth).
- No row azimuth is printed.
