# Litschmann & Středa 2019: how far plant tissue falls below screen air on cold nights

Night-time minima of plant surface temperature (grapevine budding leaves, apricot flowers and others) in South Moravia, set against four ways of measuring air temperature.

**Read this first.**
- The values for vine leaves are in the prose and a few lines of the discussion; the statistics sit in Figures 6 to 9, which the text layer does not carry. Fig. 7 values (average and range of minimum leaf minus minimum air temperature) are not available as text.
- The difference is leaf minimum against the minimum of air in the standard shelter. It is not given as a function of wind, and the vine-leaf runs cover only a few nights (22 April to 1 May 2018 per the reading agent).

## What it holds

- **Abstract (l. 37-40):** air temperature measured the standard way (Stevenson screen or radiation shield) was "almost always higher" than plant tissue in periods of negative energy balance; the difference between minima was about 0.5 °C (l. 39-40).
- **Extreme cases (l. 40):** screen air more than 1.5 °C higher than the plant tissue.
- **How air was measured (l. 158-160, 289-290):** air temperature in a radiation shield at a standard height of 2 m; sensors at the same height as the crop for the surface and unsheltered thermometers. The 2 m shelter value is also quoted for a nearby meteorological station (l. 290).
- **Vine leaves against the shelter (l. 289-292):** shelter air, in close proximity to the surface or at the station at 2 m, was higher than the tissue by 0.4-0.8 °C on average (l. 289-291). The largest deviation was in budding grapevine leaves, where it reached 1.5 °C (l. 292).
- **Unsheltered thermometer (l. 286):** the best match to the leaf; maximum deviation of the minimum for the budding vine leaf was -0.74 °C.
- **Wind selection (l. 202):** the example cases shown are ones with wind speed not exceeding 0.5 m/s. The text gives no dependence of the difference on wind speed.
- **Apricot flowers (l. 253):** on a radiative, windless night air and flower surface temperatures dropped sharply and the flower surface fell to its lowest value. No number in the line.
- **Cited, not measured here (l. 294-297):** Dětinský & Havlík 1990: 0.3 °C between cherry blossom and the Stevenson screen at 2 m during night advection, and 0.9-1.3 °C for cherry leaves in radiation fog.
- **Cited (l. 308):** on clear nights the surface of objects near the ground falls below ambient air through longwave loss (Curtis 1936).

## Dependence

- Own measurements at Moravský Žižkov, South Moravia, April to May 2018 for grapevine (reading agent). Cited: Dětinský & Havlík 1990 for the cherry values. None recorded otherwise.

## Bearing (2026-11-03)

- **micro.leaf_cooling_c (Agrarium, registry value 2.5 °C, no source):** on clear, near-windless spring nights the measured vine-leaf minimum was 0.4-0.8 °C below screen air on average and at most 1.5 °C (l. 289-292). The registry value is above the largest value printed here.
- **Kind of evidence:** field measurement on budding vine leaves in one season at one site, against a shelter at 2 m. Radiation fog cases of 0.9-1.3 °C are cherry, cited. A leaf minimum is not a leaf-wetness or infection temperature; no wind dependence is given.
