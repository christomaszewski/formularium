# Jin et al. 2026 (Sensors): a ventilated temperature sensor's radiation error, and a network that corrects it

A Pt100 in a copper shell with guide plates and aluminium shields, its geometry chosen by
CFD, and a multilayer perceptron trained on 784 CFD runs that takes radiation, wind, air
density and albedo and returns the radiation error: 0.0032 °C RMSE on CFD cases and 0.052
°C against an aspirated reference over three field days at Nanjing. The other Jin 2026
(`jin2026`) is this group's earlier shield.

## What it holds

- **Sensor and CFD** (l. 29-71): Pt100 in an 8 mm copper shell, guide plates and aluminium
  shields; ANSYS ICEM CFD 15 and FLUENT with k-ε and SIMPLE; 834,640 cells; baseline 300 K,
  1 m/s and 1000 W/m²; guide spacing 9-24 mm (24 chosen) and tilt 25-55° (45 chosen).
- **Error** (l. 88): radiation error 0.028-0.136 °C across the solar term P3 and 0.092-0.153
  °C across air density.
- **Correction** (l. 91-126): inputs P1, P2, P3, density, wind, albedo and a form factor,
  z-scored; a 7-16-12-1 ReLU network, Adam at 0.001, batch 8, 1000 epochs, patience 30, on
  784 CFD samples split 70/15/15; RMSE 0.0032 °C and r 0.9979 on the CFD test set, better
  than linear regression and SVR; the weights are not printed.
- **Field** (l. 130-157): Nanjing (32.12° N, 118.42° E, 22 m), a MET ONE 076B aspirated
  reference, three days; long-wave P2 computed by Eq. 10 with σ 5.67e-8 (l. 137, called
  measured at l. 143), density fixed at 1.225 kg/m³, albedo 0.2; RMSE falls from 0.127 to
  0.052 °C, MAE 0.042 °C, r 0.92; Table 5's MBE equals its MAE, so every error shares a
  sign.
- **Inconsistencies:** Eq. 6 applies ReLU to the output though l. 102 says hidden layers
  only; four references look mismatched to what they support.
- **Not printed:** the SVR kernel, the CFD equations, the field dates (data on request).

## Dependence

- The correction is fitted to CFD outputs, a model's data, and tested on field
  observations not used in training (l. 150). Nothing held computes it. The group's other
  shield papers (`jin2026`, `liu2023`) share authors: flags.
