# KC et al. 2022: red blotch progression in southern Oregon

A survey of eight vineyard blocks in the Rogue Valley for grapevine red blotch. It shows rising incidence where vines were not rogued, spatial aggregation, and the virus in wild grapes near the blocks.

## What it holds

- Incidence in older blocks ranged from 2.81% to 58.78%, and in younger blocks from 0.29% to 1.11% (l. 9-11).
- Where there was no roguing or replanting, incidence rose nearly 30-fold in three years (l. 13-14).
- Spatial pattern "highly aggregated" by SADIE (l. 14-15).
- Of 410 asymptomatic vines beside 41 symptomatic vines, two tested positive (l. 16-17). GRBV was in 10% to 70% of wild grape samples (l. 19-20).
- Survey years: the Table 2 columns are 2016, 2017, 2018 and 2020 (l. 206-210).

## Dependence

- AUDPC from Madden et al. 2007a (l. 102). SADIE indices from Perry 1995 (l. 171).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strands t6 (regional spread, roguing and wild grape sources) and t4 (red blotch, another grape disease). A regional model could test a roguing rule.
- It gives no yield or quality numbers, and wild-host testing covers two years. Not read in full.
