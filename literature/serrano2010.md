# Serrano et al. 2010: lower pesticide doses with softened water and additives

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- Five-year field trial at Duras and Mauzac B., AOP Gaillac: reference, half dose, half dose with softened water, half dose with additives, untreated (l. 34-37).
- Tap water was 10 to 23 times harder than the softened water (l. 63).
- Full dose 94 % efficacy; half dose about 83 % over five years (l. 72, l. 76).
- Softened water and additives added nothing (l. 83, l. 88).
- 2009 trial: folpel with phosphites, misted at 10-day intervals (l. 116, l. 145); folpel alone under 50 % (l. 154); fosetyl-Al gave 75 % (l. 159; Figure 2, page image p.211).
- Conclusion: a half dose holds efficacy; phosphites with a low folpel dose help (l. 188, l. 199).

## Dependence

- none. The paper tests water softening, two adjuvants and phosphites against downy mildew in vineyard trials. It computes no infection, incubation, sporulation, wetness or phenology model, and cites none of the engine-list models.

## Bearing (2026-10-10)

- Field efficacy data for a half-dose and adjuvant strategy against downy mildew in France, with a ratio-to-untreated measure; no infection model, so it is a validation reference for spray-efficacy assumptions only.
