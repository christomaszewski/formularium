# Cruz et al. 2019: grapevine yellows from leaf-clipping photos

A convolutional network sorts photos of Sangiovese leaf clippings into grapevine yellows and other classes. The test is in the lab, not in the field.

## What it holds

- Six CNN architectures are compared. ResNet-50 is named the best mix of accuracy and training cost (l. 48-49). Table 3 gives the per-architecture results (l. 533).
- Sensitivity 98.96% and specificity 99.40% on leaf clippings of red cv Sangiovese (l. 46). The predictive value is 35.97% and 9.88% better than a baseline without deep learning and than trained people (l. 46-47).
- Images come from field surveys in Tuscany, July to October 2017, with healthy controls from a greenhouse (l. 209-210).

## Dependence

- Uses six published CNN architectures and transfer learning.
- Builds on the authors' earlier olive quick decline detection work (Cruz et al. 2017), cited as prior work (l. 130-131).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t2 (observation), with t4 for the disease itself. A growers' decision engine could take only the screening idea: an image step that flags likely samples before a lab test. The numbers are lab numbers on leaf clippings, so they do not carry to the field.
