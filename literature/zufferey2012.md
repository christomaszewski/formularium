# Zufferey et al. 2012: Chasselas leaf area per vine at two canopy heights

Leaf-to-fruit ratio set by canopy height and cluster thinning, and its effect on stored
carbohydrate, at Pully, 1998-2002. Read for leaf area per vine over a season.

## What it holds

- **Site** (l. 83-94): Chasselas on 3309 C planted 1986, Pully (46°32' N); VSP, cane
  pruned, six shoots a vine; 4,900 vines/ha (2.4 × 0.85 m) or 9,800 (1.2 × 0.85 m); canopy
  0.75 or 1.25 m above a 0.6 m trunk, topped from DOY 182 every three weeks (l. 92, 111);
  rain 1,044 to 1,565 mm a year; well watered (l. 308).
- **Leaf area per vine** (l. 198-200; own vein-length allometry, n 200): 2.5 m² with the
  0.75 m canopy and 4.5 m² with 1.25 m, end of flowering to harvest, 2000.
- **Season** (Figure 3, read off the page image, approximate): both near 0.5 m² at DOY 130,
  rising to a plateau at DOY 210 (0.75 m) and 225 (1.25 m), falling to about 4.0 m² by
  DOY 290 in the tall canopy. Budburst of 2000 is not printed (Figure 4 puts it near DOY
  110-120, approximate).
- **Ratios** (l. 217, 233-235): sugar highest above 1.5 m² of exposed leaf per kg of fruit;
  reserves highest above 2.0 m²/kg, halved at 0.5 m²/kg.
- **Not printed:** LAI, budburst date, gap fraction and canopy thickness values.

## Dependence

- Exposed leaf area by Carbonneau's 1995 formula; BBCH after Lorenz et al. Fits are the
  authors' own. No model shaped the data.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- **Out-of-region comparator (cool, wet, topped):** 2.5 to 4.5 m² a vine with six shoots,
  0.4 to 0.75 m² a shoot, set by topping height. The plateau comes about 90 to 110 days
  after budburst (figure, approximate), later than Agrarium's curve puts 95 %.
