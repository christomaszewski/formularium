# Trought et al. 2008: soil-texture zones within one Marlborough vineyard differ in flowering and veraison date

A single-vineyard study (Sauvignon blanc, Wairau Plains) of how soil texture sets vine vigour, phenology and fruit composition.

**Read this first.**
- The spread is from soil, not from cold air. The abstract says soil temperature at 30 cm was higher by 1 to 2 °C where gravels reached the surface, while air temperature was unaffected. It is no evidence for a hollow delay.
- The reading agent skimmed the paper. The JSON said the author list was truncated; the byline prints seven authors, given in the citation.

## What it holds

- **Zones:** vines are grouped by trunk circumference (XS the smallest, XL the largest), which follows depth to gravel and soil conductivity (EM38); the zones are not randomly spread in the vineyard (abstract and results).
- **Flowering:** XS vines flowered earlier than XL vines by about 3 days (l. 212).
- **Veraison:** XS vines reached veraison earlier by 7 days (l. 176).
- **Cause named:** differences in phenology correlated with trunk circumference and in turn with spring soil temperature (l. 210, passage read, not quoted).
- **Scale:** the authors say phenology and fruit composition follow soil texture over quite short distances within vineyards (abstract).

## Dependence

- Own field data from one vineyard. The EM38 survey and phenology scoring are the authors' own.
