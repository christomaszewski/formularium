# Möth and Redl 2023 (more authors not printed): copper and potassium bicarbonate against powdery mildew chasmothecia

Late-season copper and potassium bicarbonate sprays cut chasmothecia on vine leaves in Austrian
commercial vineyards, and in an application trial (l. 9-11). Chasmothecia are the overwintering
inoculum of powdery mildew, so the cut matters for next season's risk.

## What it holds

- **Commercial vineyards, Burgenland** (l. 29-30, 55): 32 vineyards in 2019 and 31 in 2020. In
  2019, powdery mildew was on grapes in 22 of 32 vineyards. In 2020 it was on 7 of 31.
- **Spray sequences** (l. 97-98): four copper applications gave fewer chasmothecia than two in 2019
  (W = 33, P = 0.01). Five potassium bicarbonate applications gave fewer than none in 2020
  (W = 48.5, P = 0.026).
- **Application trial, Krems** (l. 33, 103, 116): control counts were 3,973 ± 2,062 chasmothecia
  per 100 cm2 in 2018 and 535 ± 332 in 2019. Copper and potassium bicarbonate cut counts in 2018.
  Two potassium bicarbonate sprays cut them in 2019 (P = 0.002, l. 11).
- **Berry incidence did not predict chasmothecia** (l. 113): the incidence at berry touching did
  not match the chasmothecia counts after harvest in the same vineyards.
