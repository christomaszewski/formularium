# Pertot et al. 2016: review of plant protection tools to cut vine pesticide use

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- A 2016 review of grapevine protection tools; fungicides are the largest share of treatments, 12-15 per year on average and up to 25-30 (l. 67-71).
- P. viticola, E. necator and B. cinerea are the most important diseases (l. 73-76).
- Oospores were assumed to cause only the first infection; later work shows oospores drive infections all season and sometimes outweigh secondary infections (l. 662-668).
- Rossi et al. 2008's primary model was tested in more than 100 vineyards and cut sprays by 50-66 % when used to schedule them (l. 675-683).
- Secondary-infection models start from first lesions or the 3-10 rule; many of them fail to predict real epidemics (l. 648-655).
- vite.net cut fungicide use by 24 % in 21 organic farms; PURE trials gave 31.5 % (IPM) and 43.7 % (organic) reductions (l. 799-809).

## Dependence

- Describes, does not compute. (1) Rossi et al. 2008's primary-infection model (engine list) is described as a mechanistic model linked to a life-cycle model (Rossi et al. 2009), evaluated in more than 100 vineyards and used in vite.net (l. 671-684). The review reports its use; it borrows nothing for its own tables. (2) The 3-10 rule is named as the basis of the first-infection rules in several secondary-infection models (l. 648-649). (3) Mildium (Naud et al. 2010), VitiMeteo (Bleyer et al. 2011) and EPICure (Raynal et al. 2010) are listed as DSSs in use (l. 806-808); no equation is given. Shared authors, flags only: Caffi and Rossi (authors of Rossi et al. 2008 and of several engine-list items) are co-authors of this review.

## Bearing (2026-10-10)

- A review that reports the engine-list primary model (Rossi et al. 2008) and the 3-10 rule as used by other DSSs, with summary savings and the oospore-season point; it offers no numbers the truth can use directly.
