# Cornelissen et al. 2023: bunch rot assessment and cost at South African wineries

A review of how producer wineries judge bunch rot at intake and what rot costs in juice, wine quality and value. Infrared spectroscopy is proposed as the sustainable option.

## What it holds

- 43 producer wineries process 75% of South Africa's wine grape crop, about 1 094 000 t (l. 39-40).
- Sour rot at 30% severity can cut recovered juice by up to 25% (l. 122-123, citing Barata et al. 2011b).
- Rot-affected grapes carry an estimated loss of up to 40% in rand value per tonne, against healthy fruit (l. 70-71).
- Healthy grapes give about 770 L of juice per tonne (l. 126-127, citing PwC 2013).
- Visual rot scoring is subjective and biased (l. 93). Infrared spectroscopy is proposed as the objective on-site option (l. 29).

## Dependence

- The 25% juice figure is Barata et al. 2011b's, and the 770 L figure is PwC 2013's, as the review cites them. No model is built.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t5 (damage and economics), with t2 for the visual-scoring error. The paper gives loss per tonne at a given rot severity, a loss function a winery or growers' engine could use.
- The review adds no field data; its figures are quoted from other sources. Not read in full.
