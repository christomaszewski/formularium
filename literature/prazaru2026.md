# Prazaru et al. 2026: heat and the Flavescence dorée vector, Scaphoideus titanus

A lab study of the leafhopper that spreads Flavescence dorée phytoplasma. Heat speeds up
development but raises mortality, so warming may limit the vector in the warmest vineyards
and let it reach cooler ones (l. 27-28). No record: the paper studies the insect, not the
disease, and it builds no vineyard model.

## What it holds

- **Nymphs under four fluctuating regimes** (l. 21-23): development got faster with heat, but
  survival, adult longevity and total lifespan fell. Under the warmest regime, +6 °C above
  baseline, total nymphal duration fell by up to 40 % and mortality rose significantly (l. 22-23).
  The coldest regime had the longest development (l. 658-659).
- **Adults, 4 h at 32, 36 and 40 °C** (l. 24, 659-660): male mortality after seven days was 22 %,
  69 % and 78 %. After one day it was 3 %, 6 % and 16 %. Female mortality after one day did not
  differ by temperature (l. 696-699).
- **Lethal temperatures, 1 h constant exposure** (l. 25-26): females had LT50 of 37.5-41 °C and
  LT99 of 45-50 °C, both above those of males.
- **Setting** (l. 139-152, 176): nymphs reared in climate chambers on Glera leaf discs. The
  regimes follow weather recorded in 2022 at San Pietro di Feletto.

## Dependence

None recorded. The regimes follow 2022 weather (l. 176). The paper cites its own earlier work
on temperature and insecticide efficacy (Prazaru et al. 2023a, b; l. 108).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t7 (insect pests). A stage-structured vector model could take the trade-off in the
  abstract: heat speeds nymphs up but raises their death rate, and females tolerate more heat
  than males (l. 21-26).
