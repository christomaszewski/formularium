# Lu et al. 2020: grape powdery mildew forecast by a Bayesian network

From Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada, the University of Victoria and partners; Frelighsburg, Quebec.

## What it holds

- Bayesian network forecasting leaf incidence (DI) of grape powdery mildew at Frelighsburg, Quebec, three hybrid cultivars (Chancellor, Geisenheim-318-57, Frontenac) (l. 261, 233-236).
- Data: hourly canopy weather and DI, twice weekly, 2 shoots on 8 vines per plot, 2000-2011 (l. 264-271). Trained on 2000-2010, tested on 2011 (l. 622). The authors call it in-sample validation (l. 1126).
- Process equations are borrowed. Eq. 4 PAR: alpha 1.95, beta 1.91 and Eq. 5 ADR: delta 0.969, lambda 0.0004 are Caffi et al. 2011 (l. 427, 440, 520-521); Eq. 5 came from Gadoury and Pearson's 1990 data (l. 440).
- Temperature effect theta(T): normalised beta, shapes 1.055/0.338 (infection) and 1.24/0.27 (latent), 5-33 C, citing Analytis 1977, 1980 (l. 391, 516-519). Latent period minimum 5 d, AAFC data (l. 522). Conidial infection maximum 0.53 from Calonnec 2008 (l. 523).
- Pmaxacc, a degree-day model from Carisse et al. 2009, uses data from the same farm; its 3 C base was picked by correlation with DI (l. 572, 576).
- Best start date is the observed first-disease date (l. 610, 1075-1076).
- Fungicide decay k = -0.0916 assumes 20% to 8% in 10 days (l. 743-744).
- Thresholds disagree in the print (rain 2 vs 2.5 mm; T bounds 5-33, 6-32, 4-30 C; l. 156, 406, 445, 517).

## Dependence

- It computes Caffi et al. 2011's ascospore equations (its eqs. 4-5, ref. 26; eq. 5 from Gadoury's data) and an Analytis beta for temperature; Magarey is not cited. Neither Caffi 2011 nor Gadoury's data is an engine model or its data.
- Carisse is an author; no author of an engine model. The validation is in-sample, the authors say.
