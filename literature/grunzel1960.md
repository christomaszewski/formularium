# Grünzel 1960 (excerpt): how often oospores are found, by author

The opening of a section on oospore formation in a study of the biological differentiation of
the fungus: a short survey of how often earlier authors found oospores. The result of the
author's own autumn 1952 examination is cut off in the excerpt (the 1961 paper,
[grunzel1961](grunzel1961.md), is held and records it).

## What it holds

All of this is cited from other authors; "a series of partly contradictory observations"
(l. 6-7). The survey is the same as in the introduction of the 1961 paper.
- **Many:** Prillieux (1881) found oospores abundantly in French plantings (l. 7-8); Müller &
  Sleumer (1934) say "the oospores are enormously frequent" (l. 19-20); Arens (1929 a) found
  them everywhere in the Franconian wine region in 1923-1926 (l. 20-21); after Sarejanni
  (1951) they occur in southern Greece in large numbers in certain weather (l. 21-23).
- **Few or none:** Farlow (1882) and Istvánffi & Pálinkás (1913) report seeing oospores only
  rarely (l. 8-10); Wortmann (1919) doubts that, given the great rarity of oospores, new
  outbreaks are merely a result of their germination (l. 10-12); Pioth (1957) "did not find
  them at all in field material from autumn 1952 to spring 1955, in greenhouse material only
  once in January 1954, in several leaves" (l. 12-14); Vivet (1940) found them seldom in
  Algeria and Venkatarayan (1956) none in India (l. 17-18); Morel (1944) saw none in tissue
  cultures of Vitis inoculated with the fungus (l. 23-24).
- **Own, cut off** (l. 27-34): in autumn 1952 more than 700 spontaneously infected leaves from
  the Naumburg institute's plantings were examined, "without" (the sentence ends; the rest
  falls on the next page).

## Dependence

- Every statement is attributed to a named author; none is checked here. No formulation.

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- **Cited data and opinion mixed:** frequency of oospores in the field differs widely among
  authors and regions, from none in several years (Pioth, Naumburg 1952-55) to abundant
  (Arens, Franconia 1923-26). That makes any assumption that every autumn leaves a large
  oospore pool a doubtful one; it is not a measurement of any spring's primary infections.
