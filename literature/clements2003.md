# Clements, Whiteman & Horel 2003: the Peter Sinks cold-air pool

A field study of a 1-km limestone sinkhole in Utah (floor about 2500 m, about 150 m of relief,
drainage area 2.25 km2, volume 0.25 km3), in the campaign of 8-12 September 1999 (three
experiments, EXP1 to EXP3). The minus sign prints as "2" and the degree sign as "8" in the
text layer; read as -56 °C for the record minimum and so on, unverified against figures.

## What it holds

- **Structure** (l. 360, 485, 723): a cold-pool surface layer to about 75 m and a capping
  inversion layer from 75 to 120 m above ground in EXP1 (base about 65 m); growth stops near
  the effective basin height of about 88 m, where cooled air leaves through low gaps. A 17 K
  potential-temperature jump across the capping layer; inversion 22 K in 100 m (abstract, as
  printed).
- **Timing** (l. 709): full depth 2 to 3 h after sunset; winds in the basin too weak to measure
  after about 2 h; slope flows die about 2 h after the inversion starts, which the authors say
  breaks the slope-flow convergence model of cold-pool growth.
- **Heat budget** (Eq 1, l. 417-440): heat loss 83 W m-2 at sunset falling below 10 W m-2
  after midnight; about 1 MJ m-2 by sunrise; net longwave loss near 90 W m-2 at the floor at
  sunset.
- **Slopes:** sidewalls 1 to 5 K warmer than the basin centre at the same altitude by sunrise;
  the cold layer at the ground is only 1 to 2 m deep; top-of-slope to floor gap 16 °C at
  sunrise (55 m apart in height).
- **Control:** winds above the basin set the timing; crest winds of 5 to 6 m/s delayed
  formation and on one night eroded the pool (l. 679).
- **Humidity:** sensors measured RH (±3 %), none reported; the nights were clear, dry and
  dusty with no fog or dew (l. 124-129).
- **Not printed:** data location, a DOI in the text file, cp numerically, event tables.

## Dependence

- **None.** Eq 1 is the authors' own; the paper builds on Geiger (1965), Whiteman et al.
  (1989, 1996) and Kondo et al. (1989) for theory.

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

For strand 3 and what Agrarium's truth can take:

- **Off the engine's point.** It describes an enclosed basin cold pool: depth, fill time
  (2 to 3 h), heat loss and what ends it (wind aloft). It is a physical anchor for how fast
  and how deep a hollow cools, not a vineyard.
- **No wetness or humidity:** no dew or fog fell, and humidity is not analysed, so it says
  nothing about the engine's RH ≥ 90 % stand-in.
- **Use:** a reference when judging whether a hollow's cooling in a sweep is plausible; it is
  not a model that can run.
