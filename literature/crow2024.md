# Crow et al. 2024: a land-surface twin matrix where state scores survive a wrong model and flux scores do not

Synthetic fraternal twin experiments with four Noah-MP land-surface configurations, each in turn the "nature" model and the assimilation model, over two US boxes (a humid south-east and a semi-arid southern Great Plains). Soil-moisture observations are made from the nature run and assimilated by an ensemble Kalman filter. No record: a method on synthetic data.

**Read this first.** Land surface and hydrology, not plant disease. Every result is on synthetic truth: nothing real judges it, and the injected errors are chosen from values "reported in the literature". Two-column layout; page numbers were not in the text read.

## What it holds

- **Design** (l. 22, 139, 244): SFTDA, "synthetic fraternal twin data assimilation": a nature run with model-error perturbations gives the synthetic truth (l. 139); degraded states become synthetic observations and are assimilated into a differently configured land model; the filter has 20 ensemble members (l. 244).
- **Headline** (l. 24-25): the accuracy of the water-flux analysis is "sharply reduced" by state-flux coupling bias, "even in cases where the precision of soil moisture state estimates" improves.
- **State precision has no diagonal peak** (l. 463, 466, 471): assimilation raised the soil-moisture correlation by 10 to 30 % relative to the open loop in every experiment. There is "no clear tendency" for the gain to peak where the assimilation model equals the nature model (the diagonal): state precision improved whether or not the assimilation model had the true coupling.
- **Flux accuracy does have one** (l. 464): runoff and evapotranspiration accuracies are "more sensitive" to coupling bias; the advantage of assimilation falls sharply off the diagonal, except evapotranspiration when the runoff coupling is varied. Runoff error off the diagonal is "sharply degraded" when observations are first rescaled to the assimilation model's climatology; without rescaling the runoff gain was uniform across the matrix (l. 538-545).
- **Rescaling can hurt** (l. 28): the standard rescaling of observations to the model is "not always a robust strategy" and can degrade flux accuracy.
- **The conclusion depends on the injected error** (l. 732-733, 740): the authors say twin results are "relatively sensitive to" the type and size of the systematic errors built in. Kumar et al. 2009 (cited) injected vertical-coupling errors in a similar design and found higher off-diagonal degradation of root-zone precision.
- **Design rule** (l. 721): twin experiments "must be based on systematic errors known to be" present in the models.

## What TRUTH-METHOD says of it

Not cited in TRUTH-METHOD. It is a measured case of the diagonal-versus-off-diagonal comparison that TRUTH-METHOD's part 4 sets up (truth family against engine, scored on a matrix): the diagonal flatters the flux score (l. 464, 545) but not the state score (l. 463-471). Verdict: **not in the paper's reference list; new evidence.** It also shows the matrix depends on which error is put in (l. 740).

## Bearing (2026-10-27)

- **TRUTH-METHOD, part 4:** a twin matrix can flatter one score and not another, so report each output the engine is judged on (state-like and flux-like) separately, family by family; do not infer from one that the other is safe.
- **D58, D60:** supports using the simulator to screen, and real data to judge: the same simulated errors can leave a state score intact and ruin a flux score. Injected errors should be ones known to exist in real models (l. 721).
