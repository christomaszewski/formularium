# Pañitrur-De la Fuente et al. 2020: early NDVI as a bunch rot risk indicator

Field trials in France and Chile, 2010 to 2019. Early ground NDVI, measured at berry pea size,
tracked Botrytis bunch rot at harvest. An NDVI of 0.5 to 0.6 or less kept severity near 5 %.

## What it holds

- **Field:** field experiments in France and Chile, 2010 to 2019 (l. 33-34). Indicators were
  ground NDVI at berry pea size and skin tannin at the herbaceous stage (l. 35-38).
- **Threshold:** an NDVI between 0.5 and 0.6 keeps bunch rot severity at or near 5 % at
  harvest, the tolerance for wine quality (l. 40-42).
- **Leaf removal test (France):** NDVI set at 0.45, 0.60 and 0.78 gave 2.4, 6.6 and 9.9 %
  severity (l. 43-45).
- **Tannin:** skin tannin fell from 47 to 22 mg/g skin as NDVI rose (l. 46-47).
- **Weather:** weather before harvest was the main factor in every regression (l. 47-48).

## Dependence

None recorded as a borrowed model or dataset. The discussion compares its NDVI thresholds with
earlier values (Yang et al. 2008; Zhang et al. 2017, l. 733-735). Earlier Botrytis weather
models (Broome et al. 1995; Ciliberti et al. 2015) appear as context.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Serves t4 (Botrytis bunch rot). It bears on t1 too, since the paper proposes NDVI as an input
  to spray decisions (abstract, l. 48-50).
- A decision engine could take early canopy vigour as a risk term. The 0.5 to 0.6 threshold is
  a field result from two countries, not a published fit.
