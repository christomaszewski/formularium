# Redl et al. 2021: how many overwintered powdery mildew ascospores are alive in spring

Chasmothecia on bark and in leaf litter in 13 Austrian vineyards, 2017-2018, their viability
at budbreak, laboratory survival at 7 and 17 °C, and infection from bark chasmothecia on
detached leaves.

## What it holds

- Bark chasmothecia were common in 13 Austrian vineyards in 2017 and 2018, with 0-3656 per
  100 g bark (l. 367-368).
- Viable chasmothecia on bark: up to 36.7% in 2018 and 26.7% in 2017, as a maximum across
  sites (l. 35, 360-361; Table 1). Viable ascospores per vine: 0-351 (2017) and 0-251 (2018)
  (l. 40, 362).
- Leaf litter: density up to 110,000 per 100 g (Krems, 2017), but only one sample had viable
  chasmothecia, at 5.3% (l. 420, 428-429; Table 2). Viable share 0-5% (abstract).
- Field viability was sampled once per spring, between bud swell and bud break (l. 136-138).
  There is no monthly series.
- Lab viability at 7 C: 8.23 viable ascospores per chasmothecium after 8 weeks, falling to
  0.48 after 30 weeks; at 17 C, 4.08 after 8 weeks and none after 30 weeks (l. 437-444; Fig.
  2).
- Weather differences between the 2016/17 and 2017/18 seasons are small: mean 7.1-8.2 C and
  7.3-8.4 C (l. 203-204).
- Detached-leaf inoculation with bark chasmothecia gave infection efficiency 2.4-6.6% (l.
  435-436; Table 3).
- The authors credit vineyard management (sulphur sprays at bud break; spray programmes)
  more than weather for the viability differences (abstract l. 27; l. 529-534, 596-597).
  That reading is theirs.
- Not printed: monthly viability, per-sample values, weather behind Fig. 1 beyond the
  figure, and the data licence.

## Dependence

- None: its own surveys and assays; viability methods follow Cortesi et al. 1995 and Vagi et
  al. 2016, the inoculation Grove 2004 (methods, not models).
- Steinkellner and Redl are authors of literature/redl2021 too; no engine author.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- For a powdery mildew truth's primary inoculum: bark chasmothecia, not litter, carry the
  viable ascospores (at most 36.7 % viable; litter at most 5 %), and warmth shortens
  survival (none viable after 30 weeks at 17 °C). The authors credit spray programmes more
  than weather for differences between vineyards.
