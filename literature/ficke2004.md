# Ficke et al. 2004: why older grape berries stop powdery mildew

Microscopy of Uncinula (Erysiphe) necator on berries of increasing age: what changes in
the berry as it goes from highly susceptible a week after bloom to nearly immune 2-3 weeks
later.

## What it holds

- Berries are highly susceptible 1 week after bloom and acquire ontogenic resistance 2 to 3
  weeks later (abstract); the earlier papers put near-immunity within 2 to 4 weeks after
  bloom (introduction, refs 8-10).
- Cuticle thickness increased with berry age but the pathogen stopped before a visible
  penetration pore; cell-wall thickness did not change over the first 4 weeks; phenolic
  autofluorescence and cell discoloration under appressoria were more frequent on
  susceptible berries (abstract).

## Dependence

- Its own microscopy; the window restates the group's earlier data (Gadoury et al. 2003,
  literature/gadoury2003; Ficke et al. 2002, literature/ficke2002). Gadoury and Seem are
  authors of the engine's Kennelly pieces: flags.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Mechanism, not a model: it supports the 2-4 week berry window for a powdery mildew truth
  (ficke2002) and adds no number a simulator would run. Not read in full.
