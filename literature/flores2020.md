# Flores et al. 2020: a nocturnal cold-air pool in an Atacama basin, with sensor cross-checks

A five-day campaign (14 to 19 August 2017) with ceilometer, temperature profiles, a grid of fixed ground stations and mobile sensors in a semiclosed basin (l. 18-21). No record.

## What it holds

- **Cold pool:** a strong nocturnal potential temperature inversion of 18 K in 150 m and a diurnal temperature range of 30 C (l. 22-24); the capping inversion holds the dust below it (l. 24-27).
- **Instruments:** a grid of eight HOBO UA-002 loggers at 1.5 m, with 10 min response (l. 148-149, 199-202); iMet-XQ sensors on a drone and on two pickup trucks, 2 s response (l. 277-286).
- **Sensor error:** iMet-XQ against HOBO: RMSE 0.71 C, MBE 0.28 C (l. 282); truck iMet-XQ within 50 m of a HOBO: RMSE 0.25 C, MBE 0.03 C (l. 315).

## Dependence

None recorded.

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- **Strand s4 (measured microclimate) and s5 (sensor error):** a measured cold pool in a closed basin and a small-error cross-validation of mobile against fixed temperature sensors, with the response-time lag explained. A simulator could take the 0.25 to 0.7 C sensor allowance and the idea of basin-floor cooling; the terrain is a desert basin, not a vineyard valley.
