# Wang et al. 2023: hand-held spectroradiometer detection of leafroll virus in two winegrape cultivars

A hand-held spectroradiometer reads vine canopies in an Adelaide Hills vineyard. PLS-DA models separate GLRaV-1 and GVA infected vines from healthy ones in Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, month by month.

## What it holds

- **Sample (l. 22, 55):** 173 Pinot Noir and 174 Chardonnay vines, with ELISA as the reference. 206 of 347 samples were positive for GLRaV-1 and GVA together, and none for GLRaV-3 or -4 (l. 55).
- **Chardonnay, best calibration model (Table 2, l. 85):** March, accuracy 0.76, MCC 0.47. In November the accuracy was 0.60 and MCC 0.19 (l. 77).
- **Pinot Noir (Table 3):** January, accuracy 0.89, MCC 0.78 (l. 98). April, accuracy 0.96 and MCC 0.92 in calibration; 0.95 and 0.89 in cross-validation (l. 104).
- **Season:** one vineyard, 2020 to 2021 (l. 21).

## Dependence

None recorded for shared data or models. The paper uses PLS-DA (PLS_Toolbox in MATLAB), Savitzky-Golay smoothing (l. 32) and standard normal variate scaling, with ELISA as the reference test.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- **Strand t2 (observation), with t4 (other grape diseases).** A canopy sensor that flags virus-infected vines. The Chardonnay model is weak (accuracy 0.76 at best). The Pinot Noir model works only from about January.
- **Not read in full.** A roguing or scouting term could take the monthly accuracy as an observation error. The paper is one site and one season, and the author list is not printed in the text.
