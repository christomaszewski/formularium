# Fedele et al. 2020: temperature and humidity in Botrytis biocontrol on berries

Six commercial biocontrol products, on Merlot berries held at four temperatures and four humidities for 1 to 13 days before Botrytis inoculation, gave one fitted equation for control (R² above 0.94).

## What it holds

- Six commercial BCAs: Aureobasidium pullulans, Bacillus amyloliquefaciens (two products), B. subtilis, Pythium oligandrum and Trichoderma atroviride (l. 17; Table 1).
- Design: 15, 20, 25 and 30 °C; relative humidity 60, 80, 90 and 100%; colonization period 1 to 13 days; berries then held at 25 °C and 100% RH for 7 days (l. 19-24).
- Variation among BCAs in control was large, with coefficients of variation from 44.7% (Polyversum) to 72.4% (l. 27; l. 239).
- Equation [1] fitted every BCA with R² > 0.94, RMSE ≤ 0.08, CRM ≤ 0.01 and CCC > 0.97 (l. 256-258).

## Dependence

- Starts from the general model of Fedele et al. 2020a, which the paper cites as finding temperature and moisture more important than a BCA's mode of action or timing (l. 117).
- The fitting data are the authors' own berry trials, from vineyards in Emilia-Romagna in 2018 and 2019.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t4 (grey mould) for choosing a BCA by weather, and t1 for the window between application and infection. Equation [1] takes temperature and humidity at spraying and in the days after, so a growers' engine could rank products by weather.
- The data are detached berries in growth chambers. No field test of the equation is shown. Not read in full.
