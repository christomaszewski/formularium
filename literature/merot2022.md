# Merot et al. 2022: a regional agronomic diagnosis of vine yield in two French networks

The regional agronomic diagnosis ranks the factors that limit yield. Here it is adapted to the two-year yield formation of vines and tested on the Alsace and PACA vineyard networks.

## What it holds

- **Networks (Figure 1, l. 110):** Alsace with 7 fields, PACA with 44 fields.
- **PACA yield (l. 382-385):** 42 plots, with yield achievement from 0.17 to 2.55 of the objective and a mean of 1.02. Fourteen plots missed the objective (ratio 0.9 or less), 18 beat it (1.1 or more) and 10 reached it.
- **Disease correlations (l. 393-395):** at harvest, −0.49 for powdery mildew and −0.26 for downy mildew, against the yield ratio. Water stress at flowering gives −0.16. These are correlations, not effects.
- **Main factor (l. 406-408):** in PACA, downy mildew at harvest was the most important factor, through its effect on berries per cluster.

## Dependence

The method is the regional agronomic diagnosis of Doré et al. (1997), adapted here (l. 25-26). The pest and disease indicators follow Merot and Smits (2020) (l. 405-406).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- **Strand t5 (damage and economics).** A yield-loss term could use the two-year component chain (clusters per vine, berries per cluster, berry mass) and the harvest-time disease ranking.
