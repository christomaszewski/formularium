# Molitor et al. 2022: partial double-pruning and bunch rot timing (supplement)

The supplementary tables of a 2016 to 2020 trial on Riesling and Pinot gris. Partial
double-pruning after bloom (PDP) delayed the date of 5 % bunch rot severity by 8 to 10 days.
The supplement does not name the pathogen, and the article itself is not in the drop.

## What it holds

- **Material:** Tables S1 to S3 and figure captions only. Two cultivars, 2016 to 2020 (l. 46-48).
- **Disease curves:** sigmoid fits, R2 from 0.95 to 1 in all years and both treatments
  (l. 36-37; Table S1, l. 13-33).
- **Delay:** the 5 % date was later under PDP in every year for both cultivars (l. 60). The
  average delay was 10.3 days in Pinot gris (P 0.003) and 8.3 days in Riesling (P 0.019)
  (l. 53, 56).
- **Yield:** rotten yield, normalised to control, 0.53 in Pinot gris and 0.41 in Riesling
  (l. 98, 107). Total yield normalised 0.90 and 0.81 (l. 121, 130).

## Dependence

None recorded. The supplement says the 5 % dates are compared by Welch t-test (l. 60-62).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Serves t4 (other grape diseases): a canopy timing treatment that slows bunch rot. A simulator
  could use the 8 to 10 day delay at 5 % severity as a check on a canopy term.
