# Steinkellner 1998: no flag shoots in Austria; cleistothecia on canes, few on bark

Buds from heavily infected canes collected over three winters burst clean, so powdery
mildew does not overwinter as mycelium in Austrian buds; cleistothecia on canes do, at
densities set by the previous season's fungicide, while bark holds almost none.

## What it holds

- **Flag-shoot test** (l. 14-15, 26-38, 56): canes from highly infected vines collected
  January to April 1994-1996; 84-98 % of buds burst, one shoot showed a spot, no perennating
  bud infection and none in the laboratory.
- **Cleistothecia per cane** (l. 55-82, Table 1): follow the previous season's fungicide,
  triadimefon 469.2 against pyrifenox 54.0 in 1994; treatment means fall from 261.6 (1994)
  to 0.2 (1998); F 160.71, the test not named.
- **By vineyard** (l. 79-98, Table 2): Türkenschanze 948 per cane in 1994 and 1 in 1997;
  Holzgasse 254 in 1996 and 40 in 1997, the highest of the sites; Mitterberg 2 then 33
  (table) where the text says it rose only slightly.
- **Per area** (l. 105-111): 4692 per m² at Türkenschanze in 1994 from 10 canes per m²,
  against Gadoury & Pearson 1988's 113-608 per m² and up to 92 % winter mortality (cited);
  even 8 % viability would leave a source.
- **Bark** (l. 125-127): 0.3-10.6 cleistothecia per vine on cordons, 0.0-2.4 on trunks.
- **Not printed:** the sites' cultivars beyond Grüner Veltliner, the counting method's
  sample sizes, any viability measure of its own.

## Dependence

- Observed counts; no model, no fitted curve. The laboratory finding is shared with van der
  Spuy & Matthee 1977, Sall & Wrysinski 1982 and Hill 1997 (cited), a shared result, not a
  shared method.

## Bearing (2026-10-26)

- For Cooptera's powdery-mildew overwintering (`thiessen2018.ascospores`,
  `moyer2014.ascospore_release`; the 10-20 search): cleistothecia densities per cane and
  per m² in a cool climate, and the absence of flag shoots there. No record.
