# WMO 2018 (WMO-No. 8, the CIMO Guide), chapter 8: the 120 W/m² sunshine threshold

The WMO's definition of sunshine duration, which Agrarium's F3 had "as remembered".

## What it holds

- **Definition (§8.1.1, l. 39-40):** "sunshine duration during a given period is defined as the
  sum of the time for which the direct solar irradiance exceeds 120 W m–2", citing WMO (2010).
- **From a pyranometer (eq. 8.3, printed p. 303; l. 264):** (G − D)/cos ζ > 120 W m-2, from
  global (G) and diffuse (D) irradiance and the solar zenith angle ζ, since I · cos ζ = G − D (eq.
  8.2). The text layer drops the slash; the page image (read by the main session, after an
  independent review caught it) prints a fraction.
- **History (l. 27-31, 60-62):** the burning-card recorders' thresholds varied between 70 and
  280 W m-2; 120 W m-2 was their mean; a 20 % threshold accuracy is accepted in instrument
  specifications; recommended by CIMO at its eighth (1981) and tenth (1989) sessions.

## Dependence

- A guideline; the threshold is a convention fitted to Campbell-Stokes recorders, not to any
  biology.

## Bearing (2026-11-03)

- **Agrarium F3:** `f3.sun_beam_w_m2` = 120 W/m² is the WMO value. It is direct normal
  irradiance; from horizontal readings the test is (G − D)/cos ζ > 120. F3's "direct light on
  level ground" is the horizontal component, (G − D) = I · cos ζ, so its threshold should be
  120 · cos ζ, not 120, or the direct beam I compared with 120.
