# Kabela 2006: dew on corn leaves and the ALEX model (MS thesis)

An MS thesis at Iowa State University. Two copies were dropped, byte-identical.

## What it holds

- Master's thesis (Iowa State, 2006) that tests the ALEX energy-balance model against hand-measured dew and Campbell wetness sensors in corn.
- Field data: Walnut Creek Watershed near Ames, Iowa, 2005-06-16 to 2005-07-03 (l. 326). Wiping leaves with paper towels at 1/3 and 2/3 canopy height.
- Three nights only: light (0.01 to 0.07 mm), moderate (0.08 to 0.2 mm), heavy (0.21 mm and up) (l. 1286).
- Sensor wetness lasted about 2.5 h (light), 6 h (moderate), 8 h (heavy) (l. 1845, l. 1948, l. 2088).
- ALEX gave onset and dry-off within 0.5 h on light and heavy nights; dry-off was about 1 h late on the moderate night (l. 2425).
- Manual maxima 0.11 mm (moderate, l. 1946) and 0.62 mm (heavy, l. 2045); the heavy value is questioned (l. 2162).
- ALEX parameters were not tuned to these data (l. 1447). Dew is the condensation part of canopy latent heat flux, eq. 4.10 (l. 1546).
- Dependence: computes Anderson et al. (2000, 2001) ALEX; vapor pressure rule from Allen et al. (1994). Nothing fitted here.
- Use for Formularium: a physical (energy balance) leaf-wetness reference and a sensor-lag example, not a rule. No statistics, only three nights.

## Dependence

- Untuned ALEX (Anderson et al. 2001; Hornbuckle et al. 2006) against three nights of dew; nothing fitted. Dew is the negative part of canopy latent heat (eq. 4.10).
- No author of an engine model.
