# Sun et al. 2025: K-means-SMOTE oversampling of rare severity classes for wheat Fusarium head blight in Henan (analogue crop)

Predicts four severity levels of wheat Fusarium head blight for 17 cities from weather summaries, adds synthetic samples for the two rarest levels by K-means-SMOTE, and compares it with SMOTE, ADASYN and other oversamplers and classifiers. No record: a method with measured results.

**Read this first.** Wheat, not a vine, and the one measured case of oversampling rare outbreak classes found in this strand. There is no run without oversampling, so what oversampling gains over none is not shown. The windows and features were picked on all 17 cities and 8 years before any split, the test set and split are not described (every accuracy printed is a multiple of 1/47, so about 47 test cases: arithmetic, not printed), and the best figure was chosen by looking at the test results. The Europe PMC author list ends with Fasi, M., who may be the journal's academic editor; the role is not stated in the text.

## What it holds

- **Real data** (l. 22, 24): wheat Fusarium head blight in 17 cities of southern Henan, 2016 to 2023; one observed annual average diseased-ear rate per city-year, from the provincial plant protection station, binned to four severity levels. Labels are observed.
- **Class counts** (l. 26): 64 at Level 0, 58 at Level 1, six at Level 2 and eight at Level 3 (136 city-years); imbalance ratio over 10:1. "There is typically only one set of wheat FHB severity level data each year" (l. 17).
- **Inputs** (l. 24, 86, 95): seven station weather summaries from the Henan Meteorological Bureau, 1 January to 1 June (rain, sunshine hours, wind speed, mean, highest and lowest temperature, mean RH), with a derived eighth (maximum temperature difference). Rain sums use 15-day windows and the others 5-day windows, sliding daily from 1 March to 20 May (81 periods per variable); the best window per variable was picked by Kendall tau on the whole 2016 to 2023 set, "pre-split".
- **Synthetic data, how made** (l. 100, 118): K-means-SMOTE interpolates new minority samples within clusters, with K of 1 or 2 because only six real Level 2 cases exist; 8 Level 2 and 6 Level 3 samples were added (pairs 8/6, 9/7, 10/8 tried) to bring the imbalance ratio near 1:1.
- **Scores, accuracy** (l. 129, 138, 142, 133): XGBoost with K-means-SMOTE 0.8936 (precision 0.9249, recall 0.8936, F1 0.8851, eight weather factors); with SMOTE 0.8723; with ADASYN 0.7660; random forest with K-means-SMOTE 0.8298. The gap between K-means-SMOTE and SMOTE is one case at 47 test cases (arithmetic, not printed).
- **Reduced model** (l. 154, 170): four factors (wind speed 24 to 28 April, rain 5 to 19 April, relative humidity, lowest temperature) give accuracy 0.8936 and F1 0.8898; a variant with the top four by importance gave 0.9149, chosen on the same test results.
- **Variables** (l. 129): XGBoost importances 0.307662 for wind speed, 0.287486 for rain, 0.191471 for maximum temperature difference and 0.118757 for mean RH; wind and rain carry 0.59 of the total (arithmetic, not printed).
- **Validation and significance** (l. 185): t-tests over ten random seeds, which vary the oversampler or split, not the weather; neither years nor cities are held out as blocks, as far as the text states.
- **Baseline:** no run without oversampling is printed; the comparison is among oversamplers and classifiers.

## Dependence

- Labels observed; inputs are station summaries; no mechanistic model. Method base: SMOTE (Chawla et al. 2002, cited).

## Bearing (2026-10-27)

- **D60 (synthetic outcomes test robustness, never establish a feature):** the only oversampling example in the strand, and a caution: synthetic minority cases made by interpolating six and eight real ones, scored on real cities, with the window and feature choice leaking from the full data and no no-oversampling arm. It does not show that generated outbreak cases help on held-out seasons.
- **D58:** wind speed and rain led the importances, RH only 0.12, on daily summaries over a 17-city, 8-year set; no held-out years, so not a variable shown to hold. It is no ceiling for hourly T, RH and rain.
- **Not shown:** the gain over no oversampling, a held-out-year score, a vine.
