# Fabre, Dubuis and colleagues 2016-2026: Agrometeo notices on the first downy mildew spots at Changins

Fourteen short news notices by Agroscope staff on Agrometeo that report the first downy mildew spots in the Changins outdoor laboratory or in untreated controls, and in most years name the primary infection each spot matches.

**Read this first.**
- The raw pages (web pages, not papers) are in the 2026-11-04 drop; there is no file to name here. No reuse licence is stated on the notices.
- Every date below is marked as a lesion seen (the day a spot was found), an infection dated back (the infection the notice says the spot "corresponds to"), or a report date (the notice's own date). They are three different days.
- "Notre laboratoire extérieur" (our outdoor laboratory) is the stock of overwintered leaves and canes near the ground at Changins. In 2018 and 2019 the notices list it apart from "nos témoins non traités" (our untreated controls); in 2017 the notice calls it "(témoin non traité)" (l. 8 of 238). Notices 194, 278, 279 and 310 name controls or trials, not the laboratory.
- The infection dates are Agroscope's own matching of spot to infection. In 2023 the notice says its infection is not in the Agrometeo model output (see the table).

- **The 2023 notice on the model (l. 9):** the 1 May infection "n'est pas visible sur Agrometeo" (is not visible on Agrometeo), because in the model for Changins the 140 degree-day sum "n'était pas atteinte" (was not reached) and the vine, in its usual setting, "n'était pas au stade réceptif" (was not at a receptive stage). So the infection dated back is not an output of the Agrometeo model. The notice's gloss is part of the same sentence; the 140 degree-day unit is "°C jours" as printed.
- **Gaps from infection to spot** (arithmetic on the dates above, not printed except for 2024): 2020, 11 to 12 days; 2021, 10 days (26 May) from 16 May; 2023, 10 days; 2024, 10 days (16 May from 6 May; the notice speaks of a longer incubation than the model); 2025, 8 days.
- **Notice 575 (2025):** the spots were "toutes de petites tailles" (all small), on canes near the ground.
- **Notice 424 (2021):** the two 2021 notices give two infections for two groups of spots, 16 May for the first spot and 22 May for the later ten.
- **Notice 507 (2023):** a second spot, 1 on Chasselas at Aigle in a residential zone (11 May), is said to match the same 1 May infection (l. 10, found with grep).

## Dependence

- The infection dates are Agroscope's judgement of which infection a spot matches. The notices tie them to the Agrometeo model run on Changins (VitiMeteo-Plasmopara is the model behind Agrometeo; named in the group's papers, not in these notices). The spots are observations.

## Bearing (2026-11-05)

- **Agrarium D70, what Leoni 2026's "observed primary infection" is:** in 2020, 2021, 2023, 2024 (plot unnamed) and 2025 these notices give, in Agroscope's own words, the day a first spot was seen and the infection that spot matches. The infection day is an infection dated back, 8 to 12 days before the spot. This is the kind of date Leoni's column holds if it equals the 2025 value (12 May); compare with literature/requests/leoni-dates-d70-answer.md. Evidence: dated notices by the observers.
- **Years with no laboratory spot in these notices:** 2019 (none by 2019-05-29; the controls none by 2019-06-20), 2022 (none on 13 May) and 2026 (none by 26 May). The 2018 notices name controls, not the laboratory. For 2018 and 2019 the notices do not support a laboratory spot date.
- **Model-dated infections:** the 2023 notice shows an infection the Agrometeo model did not show; a column of dated-back infections is not the model's output.
