# Verdugo-Vasquez et al. 2015: stable phenology zones in two Chilean vineyard blocks

Weekly phenology scoring on a 25 m grid in two irrigated blocks of about 1.6 ha, showing two spatially stable zones that differ by a few days.

**Read this first.**
- No per-vine or block standard deviation in days is printed. Table 4 (basic statistics, SD, CV per stage) is in phenological-scale units, not days, and was not extracted.
- Any block SD built from the zone gaps is arithmetic by the reading agent, not the paper's (see Bearing).

## What it holds

- **Design (l. 319-320, per reading agent):** a grid of 18 sites (field 1, Cabernet Sauvignon, 1.56 ha) and 19 sites (field 2, Chardonnay, 1.66 ha), 25 x 25 m, each site four consecutive plants (l. 319). Maule Valley, seasons 2009 to 2013, weekly scoring (reading agent).
- **Stability (l. 64):** a significant spatial variability in development and maturation, organised in space and stable in time from post-budburst to harvest and across years.
- **Zone gap, abstract (l. 74):** the difference between zones varied from 4 to 9 days by stage, and from 5 to 43 days for maturation.
- **Field 1 (l. 1106):** the gap between zones was 4 to 7 days across stages, largest at flowering.
- **Field 2 (l. 1082):** differences from the field mean after budburst were 3 to 4 days, rising to 5 days at veraison.
- **Flowering (l. 1257):** mean gap between zones 7 days (field 1) and 6 days (field 2).
- **Cause (l. 922):** the authors "hypothesize" stable factors, soil type, slope, topography and vegetative expression through canopy microclimate. No depth, elevation difference or temperature is measured.
- **Cited (l. 1366):** regional differences of 6 to 8 days in budburst, flowering and veraison on Cabernet Franc in the Loire (Barbeau et al. 1998).

## Dependence

- Own observations, 2009 to 2013. Field 1 is the same Cabernet Sauvignon block used in verdugo2025. Authors overlap with verdugo2025 (a flag only).

## Bearing (2026-11-03)

- **host.vine_sd_days (Agrarium, registry value 1.5 days, no source):** no per-vine SD is printed. Two zones 3 to 9 days apart, most at flowering, are the nearest evidence. The reading agent's arithmetic, not the paper's: a 78/22 area split (the split is from verdugo2025 l. 224, same field 1) with a gap of D days gives a block SD of about 0.41 D, i.e. 1.6 to 2.9 days for gaps of 4 to 7 days. On that reading 1.5 days is low for a block with such zones.
- **Kind of evidence:** field observation on 4-plant sites, two blocks, one valley. Spatially structured difference between zones, not random vine-to-vine scatter.
- **host.hollow_delay_days_per_m (0.5 day/m):** not supported. The late zone is explained only by a hypothesis of soil, slope and topography, with no depth.
