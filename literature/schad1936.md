# Schad 1936: a review's restatement of Capus's rule (summary, not data)

Three short passages of a French review of mildew control, which restate Capus's rule that rain
from November to April sets the severity of the next season's invasions. This is secondary text;
Schad gives no figures and no data of his own in what was seen. Line numbers are the excerpt
file's, section [B] (lines 48-85). The page labels (291, 293, 301) come from the archive agent's
notes; the excerpt itself prints no page numbers, so the page of each passage is not checked. The title is not printed in the excerpt text; the archive
agent's header names it as above, unchecked.

## What it holds

**Intensity** (p. 291, l. 51-56). Intensity of invasions depends mainly on the weather during the
contamination period. "D'après Capus (6)", it also depends on the abundance of rain from
November to April, "ce qui permet un plus fort pourcentage de germination des oeufs": "si elles
sont abondantes, la première invasion sera grave" (l. 52-55). The clause about egg germination
reads as Schad's gloss on the rule. A first invasion then is more general over a region of one
weather (l. 55-56). Summary of an earlier author; not a measurement.

**Secondary invasions in the south-west** (p. 293, l. 58-63). Their number follows May-July rain
(l. 58-59). But in the south-west, "d'après Capus (12)", November-April rain also bears on
secondary invasions' severity: plentiful rain gives strong invasions; little rain gives weak
ones, with a rainy May and June (l. 60-63). Note that reference (6) and (12) differ in the
text; the two statements differ too (first invasion against secondary invasions). The
reference list, as far as seen, gives Capus 1916 (Revue de viticulture, p. 157) and Capus 1918
(l. 80-85); which number is which was not seen.

**Cultivar and vigour** (l. 65-66): variety, growth vigour and vine age affect intensity; stated
as certain ("pas douteux"), no source.

**Summary line** (p. 301, l. 73-76). The earliness and intensity of summer invasions are "en
partie déterminées" by November-April rain; this would allow, in the south-west, the importance of summer
invasions to be foreseen, which also depend on May-June rain (signed G. Schad, l. 77).

## Dependence

- Capus 1918 (Rapport sur les avertissements ..., 1919) and Capus 1916 (Revue de viticulture,
  p. 157), both only as cited; neither seen here (see capus1918).
- Baldacci 1947 cites Schad 1943 (a different paper) for inoculum; this is the 1936 review.
- Baldacci 1947 tested the rule at Voghera and found it failed in 2 of 6 years.

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

Does the autumn or previous year set next spring's primary infections? Schad passes on a rule
that the winter-spring rain (November-April, which includes autumn's tail, November) sets
the severity of the first invasion, through egg germination.
- **Opinion/summary:** the rule and its mechanism are Capus's, restated. No data, no
  oospore counts and no numbers appear in what was seen.
- It names rain from November, not autumn disease or previous-year inoculum. A weak or strong
  previous year is not mentioned in these passages.
- The germination link ("plus fort pourcentage de germination des oeufs") is the reviewer's
  reading, not shown here.
