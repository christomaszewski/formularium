# Gregory 1915: P. viticola morphology, oospores and infection

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- Studies conidial, oospore and infection biology of Plasmopara viticola in Ithaca, New York, 1912-1914 (l. 19-29, 503-511, 656-660).
- Conidia germinated about 95 per cent in two to six hours at 50 F, 40 to 50 per cent at 70 F, and not at 80 to 90 F (l. 656-660).
- Cited optima differ: Istvanffi 20 to 22 C (l. 645), Viala 28 to 30 C (l. 640-641), Melhus 10 C (l. 649).
- Summer 1913 incubation: 7 to 12 days on Delaware, Niagara and Catawba, 20 days on Clinton (l. 943-944); Istvanffi's monthly table May 15 to 18 days, July 6 to 7 days (l. 937-938).
- Conidia stay viable about a week in nature; oospores kept a year still germinated, optimum about 23 C (l. 506, 516, 739).
- Stomata only on the lower leaf surface; no upper-surface infection; a continuous water film gave 3 of 5 infections (l. 961, 1014).

## Dependence

- none. The incubation values are Istvanffi (1913)'s monthly periods (cited, l. 937-938), not Goidanich (1957)'s table, which the paper does not cite; the germination and vitality figures touch the sporangia-survival topic (Blaeser & Weltzien 1979) and the sporulation-temperature topic (Lalancette 1988, Caffi 2013) only as observed values, with no shared equation, code, fit or form.

## Bearing (2026-10-10)

- Offers the truth a set of observed 1913 incubation and germination values for American varieties at Ithaca, but no weather record, so it is a historical check at most, and it shares no model with the engine.
