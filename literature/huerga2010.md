# Huerga & Diez-Navajas n.d.: multiplex PCR for airborne vine spores

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- A multiplex PCR detects spores of Plasmopara viticola, Erysiphe necator and Botrytis cinerea in one airborne sample (l. 50-54, 196-198).
- Spores are washed off leaves; B. cinerea is grown on PDA for 14 days at 21 C (l. 58-63).
- Bead-beating and boiling release DNA from spores on petroleum-jelly slides (l. 81-90).
- Product sizes: E. necator 367 bp, P. viticola 208 bp, B. cinerea 533 bp; nested B. cinerea 235 bp (l. 92-114).
- The B. cinerea band is not visible in the multiplex; the nested PCR gives it (l. 177-181).
- BSA at 0.5 µg/µL raises sensitivity for B. cinerea and E. necator (l. 166-171).
- No vineyard air samples are reported; quantification is future work (l. 210-214).

## Dependence

- none. This is a molecular detection assay for spores in air samples. It computes, borrows and fits no engine model, and contains no incubation, sporangia survival, wetness or infection rule.

## Bearing (2026-10-10)

- None for the truth or the engine: a laboratory spore-identification method with no field, weather or model content; it bears only on how airborne spore counts might be observed.
