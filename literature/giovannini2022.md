# Giovannini et al. 2022: tagatose vs grapevine mildews in the field

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- Tagatose at 8 kg/ha reduced powdery and downy mildew severity and incidence in the field (l. 38).
- It slowed fermentation briefly and did not change final wine chemistry (l. 39).
- In 2015, tagatose cut downy mildew AUDPC on leaves to 86.11 from 346.40 for the control (l. 507-508).
- In 2014, the control AUDPC was 499.93 and tagatose gave 320.69; copper gave 186.44 (l. 507-509).
- Field sprays followed a forecast and the RIMpro-Plasmopara DSS, plus rain-triggered sprays in 2015 (l. 152-156).
- Greenhouse inoculum was 3 x 10^5 sporangia/mL (l. 143).
- Field weather came from a station near the vineyards (l. 179).

## Dependence

- none computed or fitted by this paper. Field spray timing followed a weather forecast and the RIMpro-Plasmopara DSS (l. 152-153), a commercial system whose model this text does not describe. Whether that DSS shares the form of an engine model (for example Rossi et al. 2008 or Brischetto et al. 2021) is not established from this paper and was not checked. Author flags were not checked against the engine models' author lists.

## Bearing (2026-10-10)

- Field severity and AUDPC for a rare-sugar fungicide, with the spray timing set by the RIMpro-Plasmopara DSS, which should be checked against the engine's models before any shared form is claimed; no weather series is given here.
