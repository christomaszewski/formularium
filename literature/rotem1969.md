# Rotem & Palti 1969: irrigation and plant diseases

A review from the Volcani Institute, Israel. Its grapevine facts are second-hand.

## What it holds

- **Irrigation methods and foliage wetting (Table I):** only overhead sprinkling wets the
  foliage; furrow, flood, sub-soil and trickle irrigation have a "negligible or none"
  effect on tissue temperature, and surface irrigation's microclimate effect is "too weak
  to be significant" in dry countries.
- **Downy mildews profit most from irrigation's growth flushes** (p. 272; for Plasmopara an
  unpublished 1953 study), and from a longer period of susceptible organs (Gaudineau 1953,
  France).
- **Greece:** sprinkling in April released zoospores from oospores, and recorded infections
  matched the irrigated vineyards (citing Zachos 1959).
- **Drying after sprinkling** (other crops): minutes to hours, up to 20 h of continuous
  wetness when drops last until dew.
