# Lau et al. 2000: wetness sensors' lag by coating, angle and orientation

From Iowa State University.

## What it holds

- One season: 24 Campbell Model 237 sensors over processing tomato near Gilbert, Iowa, planted 27 May 1997 (l. 52, 55); two replicates per cell (l. 72).
- Reference is the eye: 12 tagged leaflets checked every 20 to 30 min on clear, still July-August nights (l. 99, 107, 110). Events: 13 dew onsets and 11 dryoffs (l. 18).
- Onset response starts when resistance falls below the 6,999 kOhm logger reading (l. 215).
- Table 2 gives the absolute mean time difference in h, not a signed lag (l. 161). Onset at 0/30/45 deg: unpainted 1.55 / 2.11 / 4.00 (l. 170); 3 coats 0.86 / 0.85 / 0.58 (l. 171); 9 coats 1.15 / 1.06 / 1.09 (l. 172).
- Dryoff is faster and flatter: unpainted 0.30 / 0.37 / 1.03 (l. 174); 3 coats 0.35 / 0.29 / 0.60 (l. 175); 9 coats 0.44 / 0.27 / 0.27 (l. 176).
- Unpainted sensors missed 15.4% (30 deg) and 30.8% (45 deg) of onsets (l. 263, 264). Painted sensors never missed (l. 266). The rates are unpublished data of a co-author.
- Compass had no significant effect: onset P = 0.4369 (l. 209), dryoff P = 0.0622 (l. 214).
- No model dates the reference. The wetness threshold comes from earlier calibration trials (l. 57).

## Dependence

- Its own sensors against visually wet leaflets (13 dew onsets, 11 dry-offs); no model dates the data. The unpainted sensors' miss rates (15.4% and 30.8%) are Gleason's unpublished data, and the table footnotes imply other rates (11.5% and 34.6%).
