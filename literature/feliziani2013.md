# Feliziani et al. 2013: preharvest fungicide, potassium sorbate and chitosan against storage grey mould

One Thompson Seedless vineyard in Parlier, California, 2009 to 2011. A four-spray fungicide
program, potassium sorbate and chitosan products were tested on natural grey mould after six
weeks of cold storage.

## What it holds

- **Trial:** one vineyard, treatments at berry set, bunch closure, veraison and 2 or 3 weeks
  before harvest (l. 73-89). Chitosan products were tested in 2011 only (l. 26).
- **Natural grey mould after storage (Table 1):** control 15.1 % (2009) and 24.2 % (2010);
  fungicide program 1.1 % and 4.7 %; potassium sorbate 4.9 % and 6.9 % (l. 202-204).
- **Most effective:** the program, alone or with potassium sorbate, in 2009 (l. 238). In 2010
  the program and sorbate were similarly effective.
- **Berry chemistry:** chitosan-A cut berry hydrogen peroxide by 70 % (l. 307). Chitosan-B raised
  resveratrol and quercetin in the skin (l. 296-300).
- **Fenhexamid:** in a separate test, markedly better than other fungicides for decay (l. 26-27).

## Dependence

None recorded as a borrowed model or dataset. Decay severity uses the McKinney index, as the
paper says (its ref 25).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Serves t1 (fungicide protection): preharvest sprays cut storage grey mould here, so spray
  timing could feed a storage risk term. The sorbate and chitosan results are alternatives for
  organic programmes. Table grape, not wine.
