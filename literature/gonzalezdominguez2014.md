# González-Domínguez et al. 2014: loquat scab infection model

An hourly mechanistic model of loquat scab on fruit. It chains dispersal, germination, latency and infection, and predicts infection periods and seasonal incidence in Alicante orchards.

## What it holds

- Field data: fruits from four shoots on each of 46 trees. Incidence at harvest was 27.3% in 2011 and 97.6% in 2012 (l. 205-210).
- Agreement: concordance coefficients above 0.95 in the main run (abstract l. 17-18; Table 2, l. 325-326). With a 420-degree-day latency, R2 and CCC fell below 0.88 (l. 326-327).
- Dispersal follows rain of 0.2 mm or more in an hour, and a further dispersal event needs more than 0.5 h since the last (l. 78-79, 164-165).
- Expert assessment agreed with the model in seven loquat seasons (l. 193-194).

## Dependence

- Rate equations for germination, infection and survival from González-Domínguez et al. 2013 (l. 184), the same authors' earlier model.
- Vapour pressure deficit from Buck (l. 175).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strands t1 (fungicide timing) and t4 (scab, another disease). The chain of dispersal, latency and infection driven by hourly wetness is a structure a vineyard model could copy.
- The rates are fitted for loquat, so vine parameters would need their own fit. Not read in full.
