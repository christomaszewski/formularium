# Legler et al. 2010: model of the powdery mildew life cycle

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- E. necator is polycyclic; ascospores start primary infections in spring, conidia drive the secondary cycles, and all five stages depend on weather (l. 14-31, 50-55).
- Europe used about 70,000 t of fungicide on grapes in 2007, 53,000 t against E. necator (l. 60-61).
- Chasmothecia maturation is a temperature-driven model (Rossi et al. 2009a) (l. 110-115).
- The UC Davis (Gubler et al. 1999) rules: 12 to 15 h of leaf wetness at 10 to 15 °C for ascospore risk; a 0-100 index that rises 20 points a day with at least 6 h at 21-30 °C and falls 10 otherwise (l. 162-164, 200-202).
- Gadoury and Pearson 1990: 2.5 mm of rain and 10 °C for ascospore discharge (l. 168).
- The chapter proposes a full life-cycle model for E. necator, on the P. viticola model (l. 274-288).

## Dependence

- describes, does not compute: the UC Davis powdery mildew risk index (Gubler et al. 1999), the listed 'other disease' model, is described in detail (l. 153-164, 196-215): its leaf-wetness hour rule and its 0-100 index with +20 and -10 steps. The chapter runs none of them and fits none to data. The Gadoury and Pearson 1990 discharge rule and Caffi and Rossi 2009 model are described but are not on the engine list.

## Bearing (2026-10-10)

- A review of powdery mildew models that writes out the UC Davis index's rules (l. 196-215), which the truth must not borrow for downy mildew; it has no observed data, so it offers no dataset.
