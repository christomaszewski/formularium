# Duchon & Biddle 2010: undercatch of tipping-bucket gauges in high rain rates

Field comparison at Norman, Oklahoma, of tipping buckets against a Geonor weighing gauge in a pit, over seven events.

**Read this first.** The text layer is two-column and interleaved; quotes were taken from within one column of a line. One site, seven events, a 1 m orifice height with an Alter shield.

## What it holds

- **Set-up (l. 24):** the above-ground tipping bucket has its orifice 1 m above ground, with an Alter-type wind screen. Reference: a Geonor T-200B in a pit; MetOne buckets in the pit and 1 m up.
- **Tip size (l. 249):** 0.254 mm per tip; one tip per minute is 15.24 mm/h, the resolution of 1-min rates.
- **Rate loss (l. 28):** tipping buckets noticeably under-read event totals when 1-min rain rates exceeded about 50 mm/h.
- **Wind loss (l. 31):** observable wind-induced undercatch by the 1 m bucket begins when the 2 m wind exceeds about 5 m/s.
- **One event, 2008-08-31 (l. 235):** the high-rate undercatch was 1.49 mm, 4 % of the 38.57 mm reference total.
- **Same event, wind (l. 236):** the wind effect was 2.28 mm, about 1.5 times the rate undercatch, so about 6 % of the reference total.
- **Shield (cited from Duchon & Essenberg 2001, l. 189):** an Alter shield increases measured rain by usually less than 1 %.

## Dependence

- Own field data (seven events). Cites Duchon & Essenberg 2001 for the shield effect. None else recorded.

## Bearing (2026-11-03)

- **sense.station_rain_factor (assumed 1.0):** measured, one site. Tipping-bucket losses are real but small in ordinary rain: they start above about 50 mm/h and above 5 m/s of 2 m wind, and cost 4 % (rate) and about 6 % (wind) in the one event printed. This supports a factor at or below 1, never above. It does not give an overcatch.
- Open points: XEMA gauge height, screen and tip size are not recorded here; whether Penedès storms reach 50 mm/h in 1 min is for the rain data to say.
