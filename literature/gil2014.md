# Gil et al. 2014: coverage on a Merlot canopy at three leaf-wall-area volume rates

A multi-row sprayer on a Lleida Merlot vineyard at 250, 350 and 500 L per 10,000 m² of
leaf wall area, coverage read on water-sensitive paper at nine points of the canopy, and
the canopy measured by LiDAR: coverage rises with the rate, uniformity does not change.

## What it holds

- **Trial** (l. 28-31, 78-100): Castell del Remei, Lleida; Merlot, canopy about 0.8 m high,
  rows 3.2 m; IRIS multi-row sprayer at 4.8 km/h over 6.4 m, 16-24 nozzles at 7-13 bar;
  LWA doses 500, 350 and 250 L per 10,000 m² of wall, Table 1's application rates 250, 175
  and 125 L/ha (half the LWA figures, unexplained); coverage on water-sensitive paper at
  nine points, four replicates.
- **Coverage** (l. 155, 226-238): mean 34.0 % at 250, 41.5 % at 350, 46.5 % at 500 L; the
  spread (SD) 28.7, 28.9 and 25.2, not significantly different; most values 25-50 %; the
  350 L/ha recommendation rests on a reading of Fig. 3 (l. 214).
- **Canopy by LiDAR** (l. 276-278, 332, 291-312): LAI 1.88-2.05, height 0.77-0.80 m, LAI
  1.52-2.23 along the row; Fig. 5 lines width = 0.4592 height + 0.1139 (R² 0.68), height on
  LAI (R² 0.78), width on LAI (R² 0.008), n not printed.
- **Not printed:** the LiDAR model and LAI method, the spray product, how LWA is computed
  from the canopy, the n of the fits; Table 4's letters mix cases.

## Dependence

- Observed; the LiDAR procedure is the group's own earlier one (Llorens et al. 2011a, b,
  cited) and the LWA concept Walklate & Cross 2013's. Gil and Llorens again
  (`gilmoya2014`, `gil2015`, `gil2021`): flags.
