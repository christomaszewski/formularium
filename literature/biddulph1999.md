# Biddulph et al. 1999: temperature and wetness, phoma leaf spot

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- Ascospores of L. maculans infected oilseed rape leaves at 8-24 °C and 8-72 h of wetness; infection efficiency about 25.8 % at 20 °C and 48 h (l. 24-28, 297-314).
- Time to first lesions fell from 15 to 5 days between 8 and 20 °C; time to 50 % of lesions about 145 degree-days (l. 29-33).
- Lesion regressions: controlled environment Pl = 1.31 + 0.061 Pp (R2 0.93); field Pl = 0.81 + 0.051 Pp (R2 0.90) (l. 35-39).
- Field: first plants with lesions mid-October to mid-November; 20 % of plants by 17 Oct to 20 Nov; estimated infection dates 3-20 October (l. 432-511).
- Optimum for infection about 18 °C, needing the shortest wetness (l. 538).

## Dependence

- none. The paper fits its own regressions to its own controlled-environment and field data and computes incubation in degree-days from weather records with no base temperature stated (l. 247-248). The linear rate-on-temperature fit (1/t50 = 0.012 - 0.0063T, eq. 1, l. 334) is a generic form, not a formulation the engine runs; it is flagged for comparison with Rossi et al. 2008's incubation regressions, which are not in this paper.
