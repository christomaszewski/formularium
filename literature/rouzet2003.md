# Rouzet & Jacquin 2003: thirty years of French oospore and primary-foci observations

Two officers of the French Plant Protection Service (SRPV Languedoc and Bourgogne); EPPO
conference, York, October 2002. Franche 2012's "60 cold days" rule cites this paper.

## What it holds

- **Severity of primary foci (Table 1):** scored 0 (absent) to 6 (exceptional) before the end
  of the third generation (end of May): Bordeaux and Cognac 1976-1999, Beaune 1988-1998.
  Early massive years: 1977, 1988; late sparse: 1984, 1989.
- **Oospore maturity (Table 2):** the date from which oospores, from leaves collected at the
  end of October and stored 2 cm under sand at Balma (Midi-Pyrénées), germinate within 24 h
  at 20-22 °C and high humidity ('code mildiou'): 1969-1998, 7 March (1977) to 27 April
  (1970); 1971 had two series (13 April, and 25-h germination on 15 March). Bordeaux,
  Cognac, Reims and Beaune also observed, with different storage.
- **Method:** daily Tmin, Tmax, rain; hourly temperatures by the Hallaire formulae;
  PCA, then "correlation by the window method": counts of days meeting a criterion in a
  30-60-day window, slid by 15 days, correlated with the biological variable.
- **Primary foci and climate (Tables 3-6):** autumn rain correlates with severity the next
  spring: Bordeaux r = 0.79 for days with rain ≥ 5 mm and Tmax ≥ 13 °C in the 60-day window
  centred on 30 October (20 years); Cognac 0.76 (23 years). Spring (March-April) rain and
  warmth correlate too; December-February rain does not; temperature alone never does. At
  Beaune (11 years) autumn rain does not correlate (autumns are always wet: 27 rain days in
  the 60 days, against 17 and 16), but April rain does (r up to 0.88).
- **Oospore maturity and climate (Table 7, Balma, 23 years):** cold days (Tmax 7-15 °C)
  around 29 October correlate with an earlier maturity (r = -0.80); no significant
  correlation after 15 December. "If the calculation was started after the oospores had
  undergone 60 'cold' days, the correlation coefficients increased, to stabilize by
  February." Then (p. 440): "the calculation was started when 60 'cold' days had passed
  (with maximum temperature between 7 and 15 °C) and then cumulated days when maximum
  temperature was over 10 °C": r = -0.72 to -0.87 from late January to late May.
- **Models (p. 441):** primary-foci severity from an autumn oospore stock, winter and spring
  rain with Tmin ≥ 10 °C: D² up to 0.80 at Bordeaux, unstable at the other sites. Maturation
  models (entry into dormancy October-December on temperature thresholds; maturation
  January-April above a threshold; also Q10) give only the divergence from the mean date.
- **Discussion:** less than 5-10 mm of rain in 3 weeks stops maturation, which recovers when
  rain returns; cold early spring delays it; days with Tmax over 15 °C often come just before
  germination begins. Citing Marc Weber: the date seems programmed long beforehand. "There
  is little prospect that oospore maturation can be modelled in the near future."

## Garbled or inconsistent

- The criteria list prints the cold band as "between 4 and 15 °C" and "Rainfall was less than
  1 mm (Rd ≥ 1 mm)"; Table 7 prints "Tm ≥ 7 and ≤ 15" and "Rd ≥ 1". Not resolved.
- "Rheinland area in 1994 (Hill & Breth, 1977)": a date misprint in the paper.

## Dependence

- Their own data (the SRPV's observations). The models they discuss (Milvit, Milstop,
  EPI-89, POM) are not computed here. No link to the engine's models.

## Bearing (2026-10-09)

- **The "60 cold days" is the start of a correlation window, not a published start of
  maturation.** When the count starts and whether the days are consecutive are not printed.
  `rouzet2003.cold_days` is corrected to say so; it should not run as a maturation start.
- Balma's 23-30 maturity dates and the primary-foci scores are data a truth's oospore and
  season-severity patterns could be compared with (sent to Agrarium 2026-10-09).
