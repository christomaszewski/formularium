# Chabrolin 1931: mildew and rainfall in Tunisia (abstract only)

A reviewer's French abstract of a Tunisian study of 30 years of mildew against rainfall.

## What it holds

All as the reviewer reports Chabrolin's findings; the underlying series is not in the
abstract.
- **Vineyard and climate** (l. 10-20): about 40,000 ha, almost all at the base of Cap Bon
  between Tunis and Grombalia, with 400-500 mm of rain a year, mostly autumn and winter rain;
  most of the vines' active growth falls in a very dry time, little favourable to mildew.
- **Frequency** (l. 23-34): mildew appears nearly every year somewhere but mostly as a few
  scattered spots, of no practical importance in 20 years out of 30 between 1900 and 1930;
  locally damaging in 8 of 30; generalised attacks "exceptional (2 years out of 20: 1915 and
  1921)" with a marked fall in wine output. The abstract's "out of 20" does not fit the 30
  years it states; 20 + 8 + 2 = 30.
- **Severe years** (l. 37-44): "characterised by the multiplicity of rains in March, April,
  May and June, but other factors also intervene: the rarity of winter eggs ('oeufs d'hiver',
  oospores) or the absence of early rains suited to favour their germination can oppose the
  generalisation of later invasions; hot, dry periods, with or without sirocco, can stop the
  disease abruptly." Reviewer's report of Chabrolin's reasoning, an inference from the
  rainfall record, not a measurement of oospores.
- **Control** (l. 47-55): the Tunisian vineyard is all Vitis vinifera; control is by preventive
  copper sprays; most growers now accept that spraying is needed to guard against severe
  attacks, although it is "perfectly useless two years out of three".
- **Not in the abstract:** the winter-rain rule as such (Sarejanni 1951 credits Chabrolin with
  applying Capus's rule to Tunisia; see [sarejanni1951](sarejanni1951.md)), any oospore count,
  or autumn data.

## Dependence

- No author is named in the abstract. No formulation computed or tested.
- The rainfall-rule link to Capus comes from Sarejanni 1951 (via Arnaud & Arnaud 1931), not
  from this abstract.

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- **Data (as abstracted):** 30 years in a dry-summer region: generalised mildew in 2 years,
  both with many rains in March to June.
- **Opinion (the reviewer's report of Chabrolin's inference):** few winter oospores, or no
  early rain to germinate them, can hold down later invasions. This is the only place the
  abstract ties spring severity to what the previous season left; no autumn data are given.
