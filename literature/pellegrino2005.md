# Pellegrino et al. 2005: which parts of shoot growth respond first to water deficit

Greenhouse Shiraz at Montpellier in 2001, one shoot a plant topped at node 15, held at four
stable soil-water levels (FTSW) for 38 days; growth of first- and second-order laterals
followed. Read for laterals under water deficit.

## What it holds

- **Plants** (l. 100-118): two-year-old Shiraz on Fercal in soil columns (0.3 × 0.7 m), one
  shoot topped at the 15th node at "19 separated leaves", inflorescences removed; daily mean
  temperature 16.3 to 24.6 °C from 1 March to 2 July; thermal time above 10 °C (l. 130-135).
- **Laterals I** (l. 340-375): final leaf size and internode length unchanged by mild and
  medium deficits, cut only by severe deficit; leaf emergence 0.05 leaves/°Cd in controls,
  0.018 under severe deficit.
- **Laterals II** (abstract, l. 23-38): their branching frequency and leaf emergence fell even
  under mild deficit; the ratio of leaves on laterals II to laterals I was more sensitive to
  FTSW than predawn water potential.
- **Responses** (l. 548-552, 636-637; text layer): each component as % of control against
  FTSW, for example LER II = 108.7 + 63.8 ln(FTSW), r² 0.83.

## Dependence

- Thermal time and FTSW methods of the same group (Lebon et al. 2001, 2004). A water-limited
  growth form on thermal time; not recorded as a formulation here.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- **Why dry vines stop growing early:** second-order laterals stop first under mild deficit,
  so unirrigated Penedès vines should finish their leaf area near the main shoots' end, the
  short tau. Greenhouse plants; no field leaf area.
