# Duga et al. 2014: spray deposition in pome fruit trees

In-field on-target deposition from three air-assisted orchard sprayers in four apple and pear training systems. Deposition followed air speed and canopy leaf cover, porosity and volume, not ground area.

## What it holds

- Three sprayers (CondorV, Duoprop, Tango) on fully leafed and bare trees, sprayed at Sint-Truiden, Belgium, in October 2010 and March 2011 (l. 311-314).
- Higher air speed meant higher on-target deposition. Total leaf cover, leaf wall porosity and tree volume "strongly affected" it (l. 52-60).
- Apple, fully leafed: Tango put 67% of the sprayed amount on leaves, against 43% (CondorV) and 45% (Duoprop), under the prevailing wind (l. 659-662).
- Pear: Tango gave the highest total leaf and branch deposition (46%), and CondorV the lowest on-target share (27%) (l. 743-747).

## Dependence

- Cites earlier airflow and nozzle work by Dekeyser et al. 2013 (l. 84, 193).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t1 (fungicide protection), by analogy. A canopy dose term needs leaf area, porosity and volume, not ground area. A vineyard spray model could take that structure.
- The trees are pome fruit, not vines, so the numbers are not a vineyard dataset. Not read in full.
