# Mabrouk, Carbonneau & Sinoquet 1997: leaf area, inclination and azimuth in Open Lyre and GDC Merlot (part I)

Part I of the pair of which mabrouk1997 is part II. 3D discretisation of the foliage of one vine for each of three canopies, giving leaf area density and leaf angle distributions.

## What it holds

- **Site** (l. 49-54): Merlot on SO4, ENSA-Montpellier, rows 2.5 m, vines 1.2 m, ESE-WNW; measured mid-July 1995; Open Lyre low and moderate vigour (OLLV, OLMV), Geneva Double Curtain moderate vigour (GDCMV).
- **Method** (l. 105-111): cells 0.10 x 0.15 x 0.30 m; leaf inclination = angle of leaf normal to vertical, by custom compass-protractor at the petiole sinus; classes of 10 deg.
- **Table** (l. 125-127): canopy height 1.4, 1.6, 1.6 m; width 0.3, 0.4, 0.5 m; lateral leaf share 0.29, 0.34, 0.39; mean leaf inclination 57.78, 53.30, 54.34 deg; leaf area density 4.73, 5.31, 4.66 m2/m3.
- **Shape** (l. 290): resembles de Wit's plagiophile with more leaves at 80-90 deg; OLMV = GDCMV by chi-square, OLLV more vertical (l. 91).
- **Azimuth** (l. 361): leaves clump at two azimuths, facing the inter-row for the dense canopies, more random for OLLV.
- **Cited** (l. 87): Smart 1985 means Concord about 45, Cabernet Sauvignon 60-75, Gewurztraminer about 75 deg.

## Dependence

- Method after Schultz 1995; leaf area by Carbonneau 1976. Same plots and vines as mabrouk1997 (part II) and mabrouk1998: shared input data. No engine overlap.

## Bearing

- Measured leaf inclination for hedgerow Merlot near 53-58 deg, which with plagiophile-like shape implies a G near but not equal to the spherical 0.5. G not printed.
- Preferred azimuth perpendicular to the row (l. 340) lowers the gap frequency near noon: it is a clumping effect, not given as a factor.
