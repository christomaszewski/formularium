# Housmans & Bertrand 2017: tilted-to-horizontal irradiance by inverse transposition

Inversion of transposition models so that irradiance measured on up to three tilted pyranometers is converted to horizontal, with seven months of data at Uccle (l. 17-25). No record.

## What it holds

- **Data:** seven months of in-plane global irradiance on the roof of the Royal Meteorological Institute radiation tower, Uccle, 50.79 N, 4.35 E (l. 19-21).
- **Result 1:** using more than one tilted pyranometer improves the conversion (l. 22-24). Single-plane conversions give RMSE from 19.0 W m-2 (6.8 %) to 33.9 W m-2 (12.2 %) for Plane 1, against 80.2 to 85.8 W m-2 (28.7 to 30.8 %) for the worst planes (l. 240-242).
- **Result 2:** moving from an isotropic to an anisotropic model is not significant; for example RMSE 20.0 W m-2 (7.9 %) against 19.6 W m-2 (7.7 %) for the best anisotropic model (l. 322-326).

## Dependence

- Builds on Perez et al. 1987 transposition and the inverse methods of Yang et al. 2013, Marion 2015 and Killinger et al. 2016 (l. 31-50).

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- **Strand s5 (sensor error) and s3:** a measured size for how far a tilted sensor misleads about horizontal radiation, and how a second sensor cuts it. A simulator could take it as a tilt-error allowance for a sensor on a slope or a canopy; the sensors are rooftop pyranometers, not vineyard ones.
