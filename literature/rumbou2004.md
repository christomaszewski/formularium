# Rumbou & Gessler 2004: one Greek plot, two seasons, genotyped

Every oil spot in an untreated 100-vine plot, genotyped through two seasons. A dry,
coastal, Mediterranean case beside the central European surveys
([gobbin2005](gobbin2005.md)).

## What it holds

- **Plot:**
  - 5 rows × 20 vines (1 m by 1.5 m) of Roditis, at the corner of a treated 1 ha vineyard
    in N. Aghialos, Thessaly, 0.5 km from the sea.
  - Untreated both years. Rain there is "random and short", so secondary cycles are few
    and separate.
- **Sampling:**
  - All oil spots, a third of each lesion cut and the rest left on the leaf.
  - 2001: five samplings, 28 May-10 July. 2002: four, 16 May-2 July.
  - Four microsatellites; lesions mapped to the vine.
- **Results** (Table 1):
  - **2001:** 327 lesions, 23 genotypes. The predominant clone made 72-92 % of each sample.
    New genotypes were 41-71 % of genotypes per sample after the first. 65 of 100 vines
    were infested.
  - **2002:** 426 lesions, 54 genotypes. The predominant clone made 90 % of the second
    sample, then 58 % and 38 %. New genotypes were 73-82 %. 95 vines were infested.
  - **"Oosporic infections occurred throughout the season."** The pattern is the
    "predominance of one clone".
- **Spread of the predominant clone:**
  - 2001: 10 vines around a focus at first, median 1 m. A week later it reached 15.6 m
    (the plot's maximum from that focus was 17.1 m), median 3.2 m.
  - 2002: 12.6 m from its focus within 27-29 days, then over the whole plot.
- **Weather** (Table 4, a station 500 m away):

  | | Jan-Apr rain | May-1 Jul rain | mean T, May / June | mean RH, May / June |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 2001 | 167.2 mm | 60.2 mm | 17.8 / 22.7 °C | 80.2 / 59.8 % |
  | 2002 | 34 mm | 15 mm | 17.9 / 23.8 °C | 74.6 / 61.4 % |

  May 2001 had 22 nights of leaf wetness (124 h) against 16 (74 h) in May 2002.
- **The authors' reading:**
  - 2002 was drier in winter and in season, yet more severe and more diverse: "not in
    accordance with the climatic conditions".
  - They point to the previous year's epidemic and the soil's inoculum: low disease in
    2000 may explain few oosporic infections in 2001.
  - They cite Zachos 1959 for January-April rain bearing on oospore maturation.

## Bearing (2026-10-09)

- **Genetic support for Sarejanni's "preparatory year"** ([sarejanni1951](sarejanni1951.md)):
  a heavier 2001 epidemic, then more oosporic infections and a worse 2002, despite a dry
  winter and spring.
  - It cuts against Capus's rule taken alone. The Benaki reports' 1952 case cuts the other
    way: abundant oospores, a dry late winter, no mildew
    ([sarejanni1950reports](sarejanni1950reports.md)).
  - Both say a season's inoculum depends on the previous one, which Agrarium's truth (one
    assumed dose for every season) does not represent.
- **History-matching numbers for a dry, coastal plot:**
  - one clone at 72-92 % early;
  - new oosporic genotypes 40-80 % of each later sample;
  - spread to about 15 m within a week or two.
