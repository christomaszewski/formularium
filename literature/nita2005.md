# Nita 2005: Phomopsis cane and leaf spot of grape in Ohio

A dissertation on Phomopsis in Ohio vineyards. It tests a weather-based warning system against a 7-day calendar, dormant sprays, splash-spore counts, spatial pattern and a state-wide grower survey.

## What it holds

- Warning system: fungicides sprayed on predicted infection periods gave control "often equal" to a 7-day calendar program, with fewer applications (l. 69-71). In the 2001 trial the warning-system benomyl program made four light-period and three moderate-period sprays, against seven for the calendar program (l. 1373-1376).
- Dormant spray (Table 4.1): spring calcium polysulfide gave 84.0% control in 2003 and 50.6% in 2004 (l. 4373-4377).
- State-wide survey (Table 5.2, 2002-2004): per-vineyard incidence ran from 4% to 87%, with a median of 38% on leaves and 57% on internodes (l. 5161-5163).

## Dependence

- The journal articles in its reference list are a strawberry Phomopsis note (Ellis, Nita and Madden 2000, l. 167-168), a mixed-model paper (Madden, Turechek and Nita 2002, l. 169-170) and a strawberry infection study (Nita, Ellis and Madden 2003, l. 172-173).
