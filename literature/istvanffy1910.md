# Istvánffi 1910 (Spanish): a grower's pamphlet on controlling mildew

A short practical guide by the Hungarian ampelological institute's director, translated for
the Mexican Ministry of Fomento. No data. The one sentence on overwintering is advice.

## What it holds

- **Winter sporangia and leaf burning** (l. 131-135, opinion and advice): "In autumn the
  peronospora forms in the leaves the winter sporangium ('esporangio del invierno'), which
  germinates only in spring and infects newly opened leaves. In autumn all dry leaves must be
  burned, so that next year the appearance of peronospora is partly avoided." Said without
  evidence or citation. (In the 1913 monograph the authors write that winter spores are found
  rarely and that removing infected material cannot be counted on: see
  [istvanffipalinkas1913](istvanffipalinkas1913.md), l. 4874-4886 of its OCR.)
- **Season** (l. 53-56): the disease attacks the green parts "from the second half of May
  until the beginning of autumn".
- **Wet weather** (l. 60-61, 151-153, 258-261): warm, humid, long-lasting weather and
  mist favour attack; in wet years, especially after hail or heavy fog, damage can be huge.
- **Spray timing** (l. 195-215, 278-282): start at the opening of spring, first spray with
  0.5 % Bordeaux mixture when shoots are a span long (about "una cuarta"); a "blind" spray
  with 2 % mixture three weeks before shoots break, useful if all vineyards of a district do
  it; flowering, post-flowering and veraison sprays follow.
- **Autumn:** nothing else; no autumn disease record, no oospore counts.

## Dependence

- Cites nobody. No formulation computed or tested.

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

- **Opinion only:** burning fallen leaves in autumn is said to reduce next year's mildew
  "in part"; no data, and the author's later monograph is more doubtful about such measures.
- Nothing here measures whether autumn disease or winter weather sets next spring's primary
  infections.
