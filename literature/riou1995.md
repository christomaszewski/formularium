# Riou & Pieri 1995: row-crop microclimate, a transpiration share k, and a water balance tied to wine quality

A review in French: how the Bordeaux bioclimatology group handles row crops, with the vine's share k of Penman ETP, a simple split vine and bare-soil water balance, and its use for Bordeaux vintages and for a European ripening map.

**Read this first.** A review. The data behind it are in riou1989 (held) and riou1994; no new measurement table is printed. Figures 1, 3, 4 and 5 are plots whose points are not readable in the OCR; their captions are used.

## What it holds

- **Interception model (l. 288-315):** the vine is a porous box (solid top and bottom), shortwave direct, diffuse and soil-reflected, with transmission through porous rows counted up to the third row crossed. Inputs: latitude, declination, equation of time, hour; foliage height and width, row spacing, orientation; soil and vine albedo; global and diffuse radiation. Output: shares absorbed by vine, soil, and reflected. Extended to thermal radiation in riou1994.
- **Leaf and air temperature (Fig. 2):** air temperature at row height and mean leaf surface temperature on east and west faces of the rows on 11 July 1990, mean plus or minus one standard deviation (l. 431-441; values only in the figure).
- **Three stages of vineyard water use (l. 338-368):** (a) wet soil and enough vine water: ETPv = ETv + Ep (vine plus inter-row soil); (b) dry soil surface, vine still supplied: ETRv = ETv (+ a little extra from sensible heat released by the soil); (c) vine short of water, regulation sets in. Work to date covers (a): vine and soil evaporation are practically equal in summer for N-S rows (l. 372).
- **Eq. 4-7 (page image):** Rg,v + Rg,s = Rg (1 - a); ETPv / (Rg (1 - a)) = ETPp / (0.8 Rg), where ETPp is Penman ETP with albedo 0.2 for the reference surface; so ETv / ETPp = Rg,v / (0.8 Rg) = k.
- **k for Bordeaux:** practically 0.5, so the vine gives half of the vineyard's water use after rain (l. 490).
- **Eq. 8-11:** ETRv = k ETP + E; ETv = k ETP; Ep = (1 - k) ETP; sum ETRv = sum k ETP + sum n (1 - k) ETP, where n depends on the number of rain episodes and the sum cannot exceed available soil water (l. 509-536). The vine is assumed to stay at k ETP while soil water lasts; the inter-row soil is assumed bare.
- **Bordeaux vintages (Fig. 4):** simplified balance (one rain gauge, initial reserve 200 mm on 1 April) over 1924-1989 (l. 545, 633): all great vintages occurred when the reserve was nil on 30 September (l. 546). The figure's points are not readable.
- **European sugar zoning (l. 560-673):** after regression and a cultivar correction, four variables are kept: soil water reserve on 30 September (corrected by that of 31 August), days from veraison to harvest, flowering date, and mean temperature from veraison to harvest weighted in dry conditions (l. 577-586). Low water reserve and earliness help; warm maturation helps unless drought lasts the whole summer 1 July to 30 September. Ripening index IMV, 222 plots (5-year means), 25 sites, R2 0.61 (l. 670-673).
- **Terroir (l. 680-785):** two approaches, global (Seguin 1986; Morlat 1989 in the Loire) and analytic; the argument ends with the idea that terroir is a buffer that narrows the spread of quality across years.

## Dependence

- Reviews riou1989, riou1994 and the European zoning of Riou 1994 (not held). k and eq. 7 are the same as in riou1994 (l. 427-438 there). No dependence on the engine.
- Shares authors with riou1994, riou1989 and pieri2003.

## Bearing (2026-10-30)

- **Vine water use in Agrarium:** a ready, light formulation of daily potential transpiration, k ETP, with k = Rg,v / (0.8 Rg) from the row geometry, and bare-soil evaporation as the remainder; this is the form Agrarium's vine water status can take before a stomatal or soil water model. Not shown: any dependence of k on the season or leaf area; the value 0.5 is for one traditional Bordeaux trellis.
- **Not shown:** in-canopy humidity, wind or leaf wetness; the leaf temperature values of Fig. 2.
