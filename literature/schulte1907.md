# Schulte 1907: the leaf-fall disease, false mildew of the vine (German book)

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- Preface dated October 1906 by Aug. Schulte, the Kreuznach viticulture adviser; it is written for vintners after the 1905 and 1906 epidemics (l. 134-211).
- The fungus, named Peronospora, came from America and spread in Germany from the late 1880s (l. 243-245).
- Spores germinate only with enough warmth and free water (rain, dew or fog); dry weather stops it (l. 415-417, 514-518, 932).
- The author gives no fixed date; in normal springs the first spray 8-14 days before flowering worked, and in warm, wet springs by the end of May (l. 933-954).
- Spraying in the morning, and a quick second spray in warm, wet weather (l. 968-975).
- Copper mixtures: concentrations fell to 1-2 %; the copper-lime mixture was most used (l. 591, 644).
