# Pérez-Rodríguez et al. 2015: a raindrop-by-raindrop model of copper wash-off

Laboratory wash-off of three copper fungicides under a single-raindrop simulator, with a
stochastic model of cumulative detachment by randomly placed drop impacts. Only the
abstract was read.

## What it holds

- Losses measured as total, dissolved and particulate copper; most copper left as
  particles of 0.3-1 µm (abstract).
- Best-fitting parameters (abstract): the area one impact clears, 1.7 cm² (large drops)
  and 0.38 cm² (small); the impacts on one area that exhaust the non-rainfast fungicide,
  2.5 ± 0.5 large or 4.2 ± 3.0 small drops at the high detachment level and 30 ± 10 large
  or 40 ± 88 small at the low; the mass one impact removes, from 1.27 ± 0.2 µg to 3 ± 1 ng
  of copper.
- In a factorial test of drop size, fall height and dose, dose mattered most for all three
  formulations (abstract).

## Dependence

- None recorded from the abstract; its own laboratory data.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- For Agrarium's grower and Cooptera's E6: the only mechanistic wash-off model found for
  copper, with a non-rainfast fraction exhausted by a few drop impacts; turning it into
  loss per mm of rain on a vine canopy needs the paper's equations and the canopy's drop
  interception, which the abstract does not give. The full text is closed (on the unread
  list).

## Read in full (2026-10-22)

The text's line numbers below refer to the 2026-10-22 copy.
- **The surface is not a leaf:** the inner wall of a polypropylene beaker, hit at 45° (l. 160-161).
  - One rain intensity, 14 mm/h (l. 155).
  - Drops of 2.63 and 3.79 mm from 1 and 2.5 m (l. 143-146, 308).
  - A factorial run is 10 min, printed as 3.3 mm of rain, though 14 mm/h for 10 min is 2.3 mm
    (l. 315).
- **Formulations** (Table 1, l. 128-137): Bordeaux mixture, 20 % Cu; copper oxychloride, 50 %;
  oxychloride with propanediol, 65.4 %. Air-dried 48 h at 25 °C before rain (l. 193).
- **Loss is counted in drop impacts, not mm.** After Calder 1986, the surface is L elements
  struck at random, each exhausted after q_j impacts at two "performance levels" (l. 214,
  268, 298).
  - Fitted for large drops: L 50, q1 2.5, q2 30, M1 0.98 µg Cu per element for oxychloride.
  - For small drops: L 223, q1 4.2, q2 40, M1 0.23 µg (l. 371-435, Tables 3-4).
  - Fall height did not matter (l. 432).
- **Per run:** oxychloride lost 87.5 mg Cu m⁻², 35 % of the sprayed Cu, nearly all as particles
  (l. 538-540, 554). Bordeaux mixture lost 7.3 mg m⁻² (l. 490).
  - The text's "approximately 10 % ... as CuL and 50 % as CuP" for Bordeaux mixture (l. 493) does
    not square with that.
  - The factor-analysis sentence at l. 471 contradicts Table 6B.
- **Not validated on leaves or in the field** (l. 590, 627).

## Bearing (2026-10-22)

- For Cooptera E6 and Agrarium's protection model, it gives no wash-off per mm on vine leaves.
  - Taken loosely: an oxychloride deposit loses about a third of its copper in the first few
    millimetres of rain on a smooth surface, after which loss slows by an order of magnitude.
  - Whether a leaf's wax and hairs hold copper better is not tested.
- Cabras et al. 2001 (vine leaves) is still the missing source; see the 2026-10-22 unread list.
