# Dey et al. 2020: a chipless RFID leaf wetness sensor

Research article, Laboratory, Monash University, Clayton, Victoria, Australia (no field site).

## What it holds

- Designs a paper-based passive chipless RFID resonator that measures leaf wetness as a transmitted-power change near 3-3.3 GHz and tests it in the laboratory on camellia, blueberry and passionfruit leaves, with wetness set by drying the leaves to 60-100% of fresh weight.
- It is a lab prototype with no field validation, response time or vineyard data in the lines read, so it is of indirect use for wetness-sensor error.

## Dependence

- No formulation of its own that a tool could run.
- No author of a model the engine runs.

## Bearing (2026-10-08)

- A laboratory prototype with no field error; nothing for a wetness-logger operator yet.
