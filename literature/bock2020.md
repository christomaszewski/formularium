# Bock et al. 2020: visual and sensor measures of disease severity

A review of how visual severity estimates compare with image and hyperspectral sensors. It asks where accuracy is still short.

## What it holds

- **Visual limit:** accuracy of visual estimates is near its apogee, with only small gains left (abstract, l. 25-27).
- **Sensors:** accurate under controlled conditions, but not yet shown in the field (abstract, l. 21-23).
- **Error:** accuracy means closeness to the true value. Systematic bias and constant bias are the two kinds of error (l. 123-138).
- **Scales:** nominal, ordinal and ratio scales are compared for disease severity (l. 131-143). The Horsfall-Barratt scale is one of the widely used class scales (l. 199-201).

## Dependence

- Earlier reviews of visual accuracy (Bock et al. 2010a, 2016a) and of standard area diagrams (Del Ponte et al. 2017) (l. 118-122).
- Horsfall and Barratt 1945 scale (l. 199-201).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Strand t2 (observation). Growers' or simulator observation layers need stated rater and sensor error. The review quotes figures from other studies and adds no new data, so it frames the question rather than giving a parameter.
