# Keller, Tarara & Mills 2010: spring temperatures and reproductive development

A field experiment, no model: buds and emerging shoots of own-rooted Cabernet Sauvignon (planted
1983, 1.8 × 3.1 m) at Prosser, Washington (46.30 N, 119.75 W, 270 m) were heated or cooled
with forced air from first sap flow (2002-2003; about 3 weeks earlier in 2005) until flowers
were visible. Read in full, for what spring bud temperature does to budburst and to fruit set.

## What it holds

- **Design** (l. 71-130): cool (air as close to 10 °C as possible), ambient, blower, warm (half
  the difference to hot, 2002-2003) and hot (continuous heated air, 40 °C ceiling); five pairs
  of buds a regime in 2002-2003, eight in 2005 with hot 7.5 °C above and cool 7.5 °C below
  ambient bud temperature, no warm. 2004 not repeated: cold injury (-8.7 °C on 31 October
  2003, -21.7 °C on 5 January 2004). Degree-days above 10 °C, no upper limit, from the start
  of control. Control start DOY 94 (2002), 92 (2003), 69 in the text for 2005 against 64 in the
  Table 1 caption (not reconciled).
- **Budburst** (l. 170-186; Table 1): heating advanced and cooling delayed budburst in all
  years, by up to 21 days between hot and cool buds; the blower did not differ from ambient.
  Budburst DOY, cool / ambient / blower / warm / hot: 2002 125, 118, 119, 115, 109; 2003 125,
  115, 116, 110, 104; 2005 123, 117, 116, hot 106. Degree-days from control start to budburst,
  2002: 91, 84, 89, 97, 95; they did not differ among regimes except hot in 2005 (cool 102,
  ambient 137, blower 135, hot 207).
- **Flower number** (l. 249): flowers per inflorescence fell as mean pre-budburst bud
  temperature rose (R = -0.41, P < 0.001); thermal time before budburst was not correlated.
  Fit coefficients not printed. Inflorescences per shoot did not differ (2002: 2.0, 1.9, 2.0,
  2.0, 2.0).
- **Fruit set** (Table 2; l. 294-302, 351-364, 478): inflorescence-basis fruit set 2002: 26, 33,
  30, 40, 45 %; 2003: 21, 34, 34, 44, 56 %. Leaf area per flower at anthesis 2002: 1.3, 2.9, 2.4,
  3.6, 4.9 cm²; 2003: 1.3, 3.3, 2.5, 8.7, 19.2 cm²; 2005: 1.2, 1.4, 1.3, hot 5.6 cm². Fruit mass
  per shoot 2002: 152, 230, 254, 318, 377 g. Pooled fruit set against leaf area per flower at
  full bloom: r = 0.68, P < 0.001; fruit set fell and varied widely below about 4 cm² per
  flower (the text calls about 4 cm² necessary for fruit set of 35 % or more, as printed). Flower
  number, leaf area per flower and flower size together explained 66 % of the variance in
  fruit set (multiple R = 0.81); coefficients not printed.
- **Leaf area** (l. 244-245, 355): shoots from hot buds had nine (2002) and 19 (2003) times the
  leaf area of cool-bud shoots at the final measurement, and more lateral shoots by anthesis.
  Averaged over three years, fruit mass per shoot rose threefold from the coolest to the
  hottest regime (l. 399).
- **Persistence** (l. 202-204, 458-460): phenological differences shrank through the season
  while vegetative differences grew; in 2005, with soil water held constant, fruit set was
  lower but the direction and size of the temperature effect persisted.
- **Not printed:** regression coefficients, days from budburst to flowering, leaf appearance and
  shoot elongation rates as numbers, leaf area per vine.

## Dependence

None. No equation of another model is computed; the 10 °C base is given without a source
(l. 74-75). The shoot and leaf results are in Keller & Tarara 2010 (Ann Bot 106:131-141),
the same vines and treatments; shared authors Keller and Tarara are a flag only.
