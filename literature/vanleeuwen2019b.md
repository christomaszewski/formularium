# van Leeuwen et al. 2019: a review of climate change impacts on viticulture and adaptations

A review with a few worked examples (Bordeaux, Alsace, Saint-Émilion). Read lightly.

**Read this first.** Pests and diseases get one sentence and two citations (l. 103). The numeric content is the GSR example and one trunk-height temperature trial.

## What it holds

- **GSR ripeness (l. 183-207):** 200 g/L sugar when the daily mean temperature sum reaches F*, base 0 C, from day of year 91 (1 April); F* by cultivar in Figure 3 (a plot, not read). Run on Bordeaux Mérignac: 1951-1980 harvest Sauvignon blanc 22 September, Cabernet franc 4 October, Merlot 7 October, Cabernet-Sauvignon 14 October (l. 195); 1981-2010 Sauvignon blanc 7 September, Merlot 18 September, Cabernet franc 21 September, Cabernet-Sauvignon 28 September (l. 207). Harvest adds 5 days (Sauvignon blanc, 210 g/L, l. 190) or 15 days (red, 230 g/L, l. 192) to the date for 200 g/L.
- **Observed trends (l. 134, 162):** Bordeaux growing-season temperature up about 2 C in 70 years; Riesling in Alsace over 70 years: budbreak 10 days, flowering 23, veraison 39, harvest 25 days earlier.
- **Trunk height trial (l. 373):** sensors at 30, 60, 90 and 120 cm on vine posts, four blocks, three replicates (l. 372), Saint-Émilion: Winkler index 60 degree-days lower at 120 cm than at 30 cm; about 7 days of ripening delay (l. 375, on a 19 C mean, base 10 C).
- **Leaf area to fruit weight (l. 386):** at least 1 m2 per kg for optimum ripening.
- **Row spacing (l. 549):** water balance modelled for 2, 3 and 4 m rows (the OENO One paper, vanleeuwen2019).

## Bearing (2026-10-30)

- **For Agrarium:** the Saint-Émilion trial is a measured vertical gradient in canopy-zone temperature (sensors 30 to 120 cm) summarised as one number; the raw series is not here. Nothing on leaf wetness or disease.
