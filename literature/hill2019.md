# Hill, Beresford & Evans 2019: weather predictors of Botrytis bunch rot

From Plant & Food Research (Auckland) and the Tasmanian Institute of Agriculture. It screens
weather variables as predictors of bunch rot at harvest; it fits no process model.

## What it holds

- **Data:** 101 site-years of untreated plots in five New Zealand and two south-east
  Australian regions, nine seasons from 2000/01 to 2012/13 (l. 25, 188): Hawke's Bay 46,
  Marlborough 17, Gisborne 13, Yarra Valley 9, southern Tasmania 8, Auckland 7, Wellington
  1 (l. 188). Gathered earlier in other studies (l. 95, 636); severity scored by eye at
  harvest; stations within 10 km, median 0.97 km (l. 147). A case is 3% bunch rot or more
  at harvest: 56 cases, 45 controls (l. 132).
- **Bacchus** as printed: x = 1/(84.37 - 7.238T + 0.156T²) per wet hour, wet meaning a
  sensor response above 50% (l. 141, 313), "a correction to the model published by Kim et
  al. (2007)" (l. 199), the correction not described.
- **Broome** with W as wet hours (l. 203); its "modified" form, 0.00616T - 0.00015T² (l. 214),
  has terms ten times smaller than eq. 2 gives for one hour, unexplained.
- **Results:** humidity hours above 63% a day, AUC 0.729, threshold 16.0 h, kappa 0.45 in
  Table 6 (0.46 in the text; l. 328, 461, 509); wet hours above a 1% wetness threshold, AUC
  0.752 (l. 462); Bacchus AUC 0.647 and Broome 0.648, kappa 0.18 and 0.17 (l. 468-469, 516,
  519). Neither model beats simpler humidity or wetness variables (l. 518).
- Thresholds were tuned on the same 101 site-years and checked by random splits of them,
  with no outside data (l. 268, 273). Season length confounds time-based variables
  (P = 0.001, l. 366).
- **Garbled in the text copy:** minus signs print as "_", eq. 1's decimals as colons (clean
  in the Fig. 3 caption, l. 313); displaced values in Tables 3, 4 and 6.

## Dependence

- No model produced or dated its data: visual severity and sensor weather.
- It computes Broome 1995 (an engine model) and Bacchus as predictors, without refitting
  them; using a model as a predictor makes nobody kin.
- Beresford is an author of Kim et al. 2007 (Bacchus): a flag within Bacchus's own lineage.
  No author of an engine model.

## Bearing (2026-10-08)

- **Bacchus is the Botrytis candidate**, recorded in Formularium as `kim2007.bacchus` with
  these coefficients; whether it is clear of the engine's Broome depends on D34 (a tag of
  its own). Kim et al. 2007 itself is not held.
- The 101 site-years are the pattern a Botrytis truth should reproduce (M7): which weather
  separates bad seasons from good. They are not in the text; the supplements S1-S5 hold
  them and are not held.
