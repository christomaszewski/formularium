# Claverie et al. n.d.: foliage-adjusted pesticide doses in France

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- Optidose is a method to reduce pesticide doses to the foliage, using leaf area, vine sensitivity and pathogen virulence; it depends on high spray quality (l. 16-30).
- Measurements (2007-2009, Grenache N) show LAI from 0.25 in May to over 3 in August, and a deposit per cm2 of foliage that falls about threefold as the canopy fills (l. 88-99).
- At bloom the deposit per cm2 was about twice the full-development value; no difference between training systems (l. 130-132).
- Adjusted doses were 22-65% below the full reference dose, median 43%, with trials on downy mildew (8) and powdery mildew (10) (l. 164, 172).
- Adjusted and full doses showed no harvest loss or maturity difference, the result not shown (l. 182).
- The open question is spray uniformity from atomisers in wide-row vineyards (l. 216-219).

## Dependence

- none of the engine models. The paper runs no weather, infection, incubation, sporulation, wetness or phenology model. Its ADS-LAI relation is a log trendline fitted to its own measurements (Figure 1), patterned after Viret and Siegfried 2007 and Viret et al. 2008 (Agroscope ACW) for spray deposit per unit of foliage (l. 47-50); that is a spray-deposit relation, not a listed engine model. The dose rule (dose in proportion to the ADS rise, l. 139-142) is the authors' own.

## Bearing (2026-10-10)

- Little for the truth or the engine: a spray-deposit and dose-reduction method for Mediterranean vines with no weather or epidemic model; the foliage-adjusted dose could be a management option in a scenario, not an epidemic input.
