# Rashad et al. 2021: silica nanoparticles against downy mildew

A field trial in Egypt of silica nanoparticles sprayed on Thompson Seedless vines infected with downy mildew. It adds molecular, ultrastructural and toxicity tests on the spray.

## What it holds

- Sprays at 50, 100 and 150 ppm (l. 163). The field trial ran on about 60 six-year-old vines at El-Baramon farm (l. 145–148).
- At 150 ppm, disease severity fell by up to 81.5%. Shoot length rose by up to 26.3% and yield per vine by up to 23.7% (l. 44, 47–48).
- Defence genes rose: β-1,3-glucanase up 11-fold, peroxidase 10.7-fold, PR-1 10.6-fold, chitinase 6.5-fold (l. 41–43).
- In the toxicity tests the spray had cytotoxic and genotoxic effects at that dose. The authors call for residue and pre-harvest data before any use (l. 70–74).

## Dependence

- None recorded.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Serves t4 (downy mildew) and t5 (yield). A disease model could take the severity reduction as a treatment effect, but for one cultivar and one site.
- The toxicity and residue gaps stop it from being a control option. Not read in full.
