# Atallah et al. 2013: leafroll spread between two vineyards and a side payment

A cellular-automata model of leafroll spread between two neighbouring vineyards, with the two
managers in a bargaining game. Without cooperation neither manager controls the disease. A
side payment from the high-value vineyard makes cooperation pay and can settle who stays in
production (l. 60-70). Model only: the numbers are simulation outputs, not field data.

## What it holds

- **Model** (l. 60-62, 249-258): cellular automata of disease spread in two ecologically
  connected vineyards, one high-value and one low-value. The spread is a plant-level, spatial
  dispersal function, not a farm-level one.
- **No cooperation** (l. 62, 877): no control is the best strategy for both managers. Expected
  payoffs are $214,000 per acre for the high-value manager and $37,000 for the low-value one
  (l. 926-928).
- **Cooperation** (l. 881-883, 938-942): cooperation gives a 25 % overall gain over no control.
  Two cooperative cases: $297,000 per acre (18 % above the non-cooperative total) and $313,000
  (25 %) (l. 938-942).
- **Side payments** (l. 883, 995, 1045-1047): a Pareto-efficient lump-sum payment of $36,000 at the
  start for one strategy (l. 995). A fixed transfer of $74,000 to the low-value manager to exit
  production is the paper's conclusion (l. 1045-1047).

## Dependence

- Economic impact of leafroll, $25,000-40,000 per hectare over 25 years in New York, from
  Atallah et al. 2012 (l. 98-100; reference l. 1113).
- Says it extends "Atallah et al. 2013" on disease management without inter-vineyard transmission
  (l. 252-253). The reference list has no 2013 entry for Atallah, so this citation is unresolved.
- Cites Fuller et al. 2011 on Pierce's disease cooperation (l. 241-243; reference l. 1183), and
  contrasts a farm-level function (Keeling et al. 2001) and a fixed diffusion (Fuller et al.)
  (l. 255-257).
