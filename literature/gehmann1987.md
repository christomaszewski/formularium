# Gehmann 1987: oospores and primary infection in Baden

Only the contents pages are held.

## What it holds (the contents)

- **Oospore formation (pp. 7-10, 22-56):**
  - artificial infection by leaf age, cultivar (field and potted vines), N and K
    fertilising, light, leaf zone and sporangial concentration;
  - natural infection in the field: season, leaf age and leaf zone.
- **Oospore germination and the date of primary infection (pp. 57-77):**
  - germination in a climate cabinet, 1983/84, 1984/85 and 1985/86;
  - primary infection in the field, 1984-1986: its extent, and its timing against the
    course of temperature (p. 70);
  - germination in the second year after formation (p. 73), and of isolated oospores
    (p. 75).
- **Reduced spray programmes (pp. 78-127):**
  - 1984-1986 at Freiburg, Blankenhornsberg, Hecklingen and Müllheim;
  - a Lufft wetness recorder tested in 1984.
- Discussion p. 128, summary p. 152, references pp. 155-169.

## Dependence

- **Already in Formularium:**
  - Gessler et al. 2011 (p. 7, [gessler2011](gessler2011.md)) give "Gehmann et al. 1987's" German
    rule: first oospore maturity at 160 °C·days above 8 °C from 1 January, at 2 m. That is
    the form of the engine's `vitimeteo.oospores` (`thermal-time-oospore-threshold`), and its
    flags record the variant.
  - Hoppmann & Wittich 1997 ([hoppmann1997](hoppmann1997.md)) name Gehmann 1987's field
    data among the Geisenheim model's bases.
  - Puelles et al. 2024 ([puelles2024](puelles2024.md)) credit the engine's UR oospore rule,
    140-160 °C·days above 8 °C from 1 January, to "Gehmann et al., 1987" (read
    2026-10-09).
- Which pages hold the 160 °C·day rule is not shown by the contents; probably 3.2.2.2
  (p. 70), primary infection against the course of temperature (inferred).
