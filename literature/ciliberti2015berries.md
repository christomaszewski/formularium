# Ciliberti et al. 2015: Botrytis infection of mature berries

From the Università Cattolica (Piacenza) and INRA Bordeaux.

## What it holds

- Eight B. cinerea strains (four transposa, four vacuma; the same eight as the inflorescence paper) were inoculated on ripe berries (l. 61, 63).
- Experiments 1 and 2 used Sauvignon blanc berries from Villenave d'Ornon, 2012, once each; experiment 3 used Barbera berries from Ziano Piacentino, 2011, with strains 18.13T and 18.21V only, twice (l. 95, 97, 98, 135).
- Eq. 1, mycelium plugs: y = [a Teq^b (1 - Teq)]^c / [1 + exp(d - e RH/100)] (l. 145). Tmin 0, Tmax 30 C, a 7.75, b 2.14, c 0.47, d 35.36, e 40.26, R2 0.99 (l. 197, 198, 199). No disease at 65% RH (l. 173).
- Eq. 2, conidia: y = [a Teq^b (1 - Teq)]^c exp[-d exp(-e WD)] (l. 146). Tmin 5, Tmax 35 C, a 6.416, b 1.292, c 1.42, d 2.30, e 0.05, R2 0.96 (l. 221, 222, 223).
- Incidence is rescaled by each strain's own maximum before fitting (l. 180); parameters are for the eight strains pooled (l. 160).
- At 36 h wetness, raw incidence was 80, 76, 57 and 35% at 20, 25, 15 and 30 C (l. 211); the abstract gives different round numbers (l. 30).
- Wounding raised incidence 1.5 to 5 times under intermediate RH or wetness (l. 21).
- Numerator is the Analytis beta equation (l. 157); data are the authors' own counts, not model-derived.
