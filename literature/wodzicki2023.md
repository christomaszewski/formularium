# Wodzicki et al. 2023: a laser-guided sprayer against grape diseases and beetles

A LiDAR-guided sprayer at two base rates, against a conventional constant-rate sprayer, over three Ohio seasons. Spray volume fell by 29 to 83%, with no change in deposit uniformity. The low rate gave more foliar disease.

## What it holds

- **Volume:** the intelligent sprayer cut spray volume by 29 to 83% against the conventional 935.4 L/ha (l. 27; abstract l. 53-55).
- **Savings:** US$469 per ha a year at the high rate, and US$712 at the low rate (l. 55-56).
- **Disease:** mean severity was 53% at the low rate, 40% at the high rate and 33% for conventional (Table 1, l. 285-296).
- **Yield:** low-rate marketable yield was 1.7 times that of the nontreated plots and not different from conventional (l. 40-43).
- **Deposit:** conventional gave more coverage, and uniformity did not differ (l. 33-38).

## Dependence

- LiDAR and radar sensing system of Shen et al. 2017 (l. 96-97).

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- **Strands t1 (spray volume and coverage), t5 (cost and yield) and t7 (Japanese beetles):** the volume and cost ratios, and the disease penalty at the low rate, give a base-rate floor for a spray engine.
