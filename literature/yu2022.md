# Yu et al. 2022: how many independent oospore infections a susceptible table-grape plot had, and what a rain shelter did to them (Shenyang, 2016-2017)

Two seasons of an open-field plot, a fungicide plot and a rain-shelter plot in one Chinese vineyard, with lesions genotyped at seven microsatellite loci so that single-genotype lesions can stand for new oospore infections.

**Read this first.** Three points.
- This is the paper already held as `yu2022shelter`, a triage note of 2026-10-08 that read only the first pages and the abstract. This note is the fuller reading, with the counts. The two should be merged or one retired; both carry the same DOI.
- "2110 genotypes" (l. 99) means 2,110 genotyped lesions, not 2,110 distinct genotypes. Table 3's Total row prints lesion counts by class that add up to 2,110: G1 501, G2 222, G3 132, single genotypes 838, other genotypes 417 (l. 176-180). G1, G2 and G3 are three genotypes holding 855 lesions. The same holds for 1,369 in 2017 and 423 in the fungicide plot ("329 single genotypes (77.8%)" is 329 of 423 lesions). So 2,110 divided by the plot area or the vine count is not a count of infections (it would give 5.3 per vine and 3.5 per m2).
- Independent oospore infections are the distinct genotypes: under the paper's rule, one pattern is clones of one oospore and different patterns are independent oospores (l. 841). Single genotypes appear once, "other" genotypes fewer than 50 times and predominant ones at least 50 (l. 841), so for 2016 there are 838 + 3 + 9 to 208 = 850 to 1,049 distinct genotypes (arithmetic, not printed; the 9 to 208 is 417 lesions in genotypes of 2 to 49 lesions each).

## What it holds

- **Site and design:** one naturally infected vineyard in Shenyang, Liaoning; Centennial Seedless, highly susceptible; fungicide, open-field and rain-shelter plots side by side from west to east; each plot 8 rows of 50 vines, 1.0 m between vines and 1.5 m between rows, vertical trellis, cordon pruned, 12 buds a vine; 2016 and 2017 (l. 828). That is 400 vines and 600 m2 a plot (arithmetic, not printed).
- **Onset:** open field first visible on 1 July 2016 and 7 July 2017 (l. 28-29); fungicide plot 15 and 21 July (l. 46-47); rain shelter 29 and 28 July (l. 37-38). The shelter delayed the onset by 28 and 21 days, cut the final disease index by 82 and 83 %, the canopy relative humidity by 11 and 8 % and the leaf wetness by 85 and 76 % (l. 3). The open-field disease index was 92.22 in 2016 and 64.89 in 2017 (l. 32-33); July to September rain was 428.6 mm in 2016 and 290.3 mm in 2017 (l. 12).
- **Sampling:** lesions were collected at nearly every two-week interval (l. 838); total sampling (every lesion) when incidence was under five lesions a vine, partial sampling (three single lesions at random per vine) above five (l. 838). Seven SSR markers (l. 3, 840). Genotyping took about 90 % of collected lesions (2,110 of 2,314; arithmetic, not printed).
- **Open field 2016:** 2,314 lesions, 2,110 genotyped; lesion shares G1 23.7 %, G2 10.5 %, G3 6.3 %, single genotypes 39.7 % and other genotypes 19.8 % (l. 99, 176-180). The paper takes the single-genotype share as primary infection by oospores and the rest, 60.3 %, as secondary (l. 99, 841). 2017: 1,547 lesions, 1,369 genotyped, singles 562 lesions or 41.1 % (l. 354-359, 483).
- **By date, open field 2016 (Table 3, l. 113-170; lesions collected, genotyped, singles):** 1 July 1, 1, none; 15 July 316, 281, 144 (total sampling); 29 July 554, 504, 213; 12 August 1,102, 1,019, 387; 26 August 225, 204, 61; 9 September 116, 101, 33 (partial sampling from 29 July). The singles are 51, 42, 38, 30 and 33 % of the genotyped lesions of the five dates (arithmetic, not printed).
- **Other plots 2016:** fungicide plot 476 lesions, 423 genotyped, 329 singles or 77.8 % (l. 99, 233); rain shelter 769 lesions, 695 genotyped, 55 singles, "only 7.9 %" primary (l. 99, 293). The plots shared the predominant genotypes; the shelter's lesions passed the fungicide plot's by early September owing to "surging lesions on both sides of the shelter" (l. 484). No genotype was found in both years (l. 484).

## Dependence

- Own field and SSR survey. The sampling design (total and partial sampling) and the primary-infection proxy follow earlier work the paper cites (its refs 16 and 27), which the reading agent identifies with Gobbin et al. 2005 (`gobbin2005`); DNA extraction is after Gobbin et al. 2003 (l. 839). A shared source, not a shared model.

## Bearing (2026-10-25)

- **dm.primary_dose_scale (oospores per g of dry litter, now 15,000):** nothing. The paper counts no oospores, litter or soil, so it cannot set a dose. It gives outcomes only.
- **Primary infections per vine and per m2 in an untreated vineyard:** open field, a highly susceptible table grape, 2016: 850 to 1,049 distinct genotypes, 2.1 to 2.6 per vine and 1.4 to 1.7 per m2; 2017: 572 to 720, 1.4 to 1.8 per vine and 0.95 to 1.2 per m2 (arithmetic, not printed; 562 singles, 3 predominant, 7 to 155 others). They are floors: from 29 July at most three lesions per vine were taken, and the plots neighbour each other. At the 15 July total sampling alone, 144 singles is 0.24 per m2 (arithmetic). For scale, Fedele's fit at 15,000 oospores per g gives 8.8 lesions per plant (arithmetic on the fit as recorded in the inoculum reading of `fedele2025`, not rechecked here), above this plot's floor; the two count different things. A measurement with an inference (SSR patterns), one vineyard in China.
- **Cooptera decision 17 (when the primary season ends):** singles, the paper's proxy for new oospore infections, were present at every date to the last, 9 September 2016 (33 of 101 genotyped lesions). That does not show late oospore infection: with partial sampling a secondary lesion whose clone mates were not sampled also reads as single, and only 1 and 15 July were totally sampled (my reading). Nothing here dates the end.
- **What the shelter shows:** under rain exclusion the first lesions came 21 to 28 days later and singles fell from 838 to 55 lesions (l. 179, 293; a factor of 15, arithmetic). That fits rain at the vine setting when infection starts, but sporangia came from neighbouring plots, so primary and secondary spread are not separated, and no oospore germination was tested.
