# Kusaba & Kawamura 2019: survival of Peronospora destructor oospores in soil

Oospores from diseased onion leaves (cultivar Tarzan, collected 18-19 May 2017 at the Saga
University field), mixed into sieved paddy soil, kept for 30 weeks at 10 °C, at 20 °C and
outdoors, with viability counted every 5 weeks. Onion, not grapevine. No record: the paper
fits no decay curve.

## What it holds

- **Method:** viability is the fraction of 500 oospores that plasmolyse in 4 M NaCl (l. 79,
  135-136). Soil from a paddy where onion had not grown for at least 19 years, moisture held
  at 20 % (w/w) by weighing every 2 weeks (l. 104). Indoor trials in the dark from 1 June 2017;
  outdoor planters on a campus road from 6 June 2017 to 2 January 2018, air temperature from
  JMA Saga (l. 173-174).
- **Initial viability:** 56.6 % (l. 17, 121).
- **Viability by week, read from Fig. 3 labels:**
  - 10 °C: 43.0 (5), 40.2 (10), 49.6 (15), 41.2 (20), 30.0 (25), 38.8 (30). It stayed at 30.0 %
    or above through the 30 weeks.
  - 20 °C: 32.4 (5), 8.8 (10), 8.4 (15), 4.8 (20), 4.8 (25), 4.0 (30).
  - Outdoors: 72.6 (5), 19.4 (10), 11.6 (15), 4.4 (20), 3.8 (25), 3.8 (30). The rise above the
    start at 5 weeks is unexplained (l. 221-222). The abstract gives 19.5 % at 10 weeks; the
    body and the figure give 19.4 %.
- **Outdoor fall:** a sharp fall between 12 July and 15 August 2017, when air temperature
  reached 36.9 °C (minimum 23.9, mean 29.5 °C), then a gradual one.
- **Not printed:** any decay model, rate or half-life; replication per point; variances;
  outdoor soil temperature or moisture; DOI; an English abstract.

## Dependence

- None computed. The paper cites McKay (1957, oospores surviving 25 years) and Van der Gaag &
  Frinking (1997) without using an equation of theirs. Air temperature is JMA data.

## Bearing (2026-10-18)

For strand 1 (survival of the overwintering inoculum):

- **What it shows:** in this soil, warm storage kills oospores within weeks (4.0 % at
  20 °C and 3.8 % outdoors at 30 weeks) and cool storage keeps them (30 % or more at 10 °C).
  Heat in July and August is the likeliest cause of the outdoor fall, as the authors place it.
- **Limits:** one onion pathogen, one soil, one year, a Japanese summer. It covers summer
  survival, not winter, and says nothing about grapevine oospores.
- **Take for the sweep:** a qualitative check that a survival rule for oospores should fall
  with warm soil and hold at cool temperatures. The numbers are too few (one series per
  condition, no replicates printed) to fit a rate; none was fitted.
