# Štambuk et al. 2021: Croatian grapevine varieties screened for P. viticola

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- 25 Croatian native grapevine varieties, plus 5 controls, were scored in a leaf-disc bioassay (OIV 452-1) (l. 26, 1380).
- Discs were inoculated with 2 x 10^5 sporangia per mL and held at 20 C and 80% air moisture for seven days (l. 1406, 1415).
- Sporulation was visible on day 6 or 7 on most V. vinifera varieties, and the test usually took no more than seven days (l. 182, 1213).
- Fluorescence and imaging parameters at seven times separated infected from healthy discs (l. 176-190).
- A logistic model fitted to 19 genotypes gave Cox and Snell R2 = 0.92 and classified all training observations; for the 10 prediction varieties it was right for class 5 and half of class 1 (l. 1168, 1176, 1181-1186).
- The sulphur spray on the greenhouse plants targeted powdery mildew only (l. 1391).

## Dependence

- none. The paper uses a logistic regression of OIV classes on fluorescence and imaging parameters, fitted to its own greenhouse leaf-disc data. It computes no incubation, infection, sporulation-temperature, wetness or phenology model. A grep for Magarey, Blaeser, Rossi, Goidanich, Kennelly, Lalancette, Caffi and Weltzien finds only a reference-list entry (ref. 28, Bove et al., with a co-author Caffi) and the word 'incubation' in the method (l. 1409, 1416).

## Bearing (2026-10-10)

- Little for the truth or the engine: a leaf-disc susceptibility ranking of Croatian cultivars that could set a varietal susceptibility parameter in a scenario, with no weather or field epidemic.
