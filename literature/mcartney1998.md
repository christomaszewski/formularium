# McArtney 1998: leaf area per shoot of Seyval and DeChaunac on days after bud break

An American doctoral thesis on the reserves (carbohydrate and nitrogen) a vine spends on its
first shoots. A scanned typescript: the text layer is partly garbled, so the figure equations
were read from page images. The parts that bear on the truth are the shoot leaf area curves of
Chapter 4 (Fig. 4.4, p. 47) and the field Seyval regressions of Chapter 9 (Fig. 9.1, p. 143).
The thesis prints no DOI.

## What it holds

- **Shading** (Ch. 4, l. 1949): 80 % shade cloth from flowering, on 'DeChaunac' and 'Seyval'
  (French-American hybrids). Table 4.1 and the abstract speak of its effects in the year after
  (l. 1957, 2366); eight replicate vines a treatment (Fig. 4.4 caption).
- **Sampling and fit** (l. 2201-2215): lamina length and width on three shoots a plant from 10
  to 50 days after bud break; a sigmoid fitted by nonlinear regression in SAS.
- **Shoot leaf area** (Fig. 4.4, p. 47; area = a / (1 + exp(b - c t)), t in days after bud
  break, area in cm² a shoot; read in the page image): Seyval unshaded a 299, b 4.0, c 0.17;
  Seyval shaded 197, 4.8, 0.22; DeChaunac unshaded 619, 4.3, 0.16; DeChaunac shaded 252, 5.0,
  0.25. Half the final area at b/c, my own calculation, not printed: 23.5, 21.8, 26.9 and
  20.0 days; time scale 1/c: 5.9, 4.5, 6.3 and 4.0 days.
- **Single leaf** (l. 2442-2444): area = k x length x width, DeChaunac k 0.83 (R² 0.98),
  Seyval k 0.86 (R² 0.99).
- **By position on the cane** (l. 2428, 2432): total shoot leaf area, basal, mid and upper,
  Seyval 44, 162 and 276 cm²; DeChaunac 176, 298 and 446 cm².
- **Final leaves a shoot** (l. 2474): shaded Seyval 7 and DeChaunac 9; unshaded 10 and 15.
- **Root dry mass** (l. 1953): fell by more than 50 % in the first three weeks after bud
  break.
- **Chapter 9, field Seyval** (l. 6464-6466, Fig. 9.1 B read in the image): leaf area on shoot
  length, Y = -5.73 + 10.9 X (cm², cm), R² 0.84, n 45 shoots; shoot diameter and leaf number
  each R² 0.71 (slopes not legible). The year is not confirmed in the lines read.
- **Shoot elongation** (Ch. 9 table, l. 6481; 15 clusters on 15 shoots; OCR values, not
  checked on the image): 13.98 mm/d from bud break to bloom, 17.34 mm/d from bloom to two
  weeks after, 5.61 mm/d from two to six weeks after.
- **Chapter 8** (potted 'Chambourcin', l. 5568-5578): elongation falls for 25 days after bud
  break, rises to about 35 days (bloom), then falls; qualitative.
- **Model prediction** (l. 2892): under a leaf transition size of 30 or 50 % of final area,
  half the shoot leaf area exports carbon 12 days after bud break. A model output, not a fit.
- **Not printed in what was read:** budburst dates, E-L stages, bunches per vine at a stage
  date, canopy height, width or porosity, any infection threshold by leaf age. Table 6.3's
  body was not extracted.

## Dependence

- None recorded. The Fig. 4.4 fits are the author's own SAS regressions; no model shaped the
  data. Elongation is said to resemble Miller, Howell & Flore 1996 (l. 5556): a lead only.
- Shared authors or data with the Pallas theses: none found.

## Bearing (2026-10-14)

- **The one growth curve in days after bud break found**, independent of the engine (no heat
  sum), and the form the truth now uses. Comparable numbers: a midpoint of 20 to 27 days and
  a time scale of 4 to 6 days.
- **For a shoot, not a vine, and for hybrids in Ohio:** not Vitis vinifera in a Mediterranean
  vineyard. Shoot leaf area halves its final value about three to four weeks after bud break.
- **Exponents read from a scanned figure** by the main session in the page image.
