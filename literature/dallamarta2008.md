# Dalla Marta et al. 2008: solar radiation and grapevine susceptibility to P. viticola

A light read (the deep-research B list).

## What it holds

- A 2006 Chianti vineyard with six plots, three under shading nets that cut radiation to about 35% of full sun (l. 25, 76, 88, 277).
- Epidermal polyphenols, measured with a Dualex, were higher in sun leaves from the start: 2.44 against 1.37 Dualex units before inoculation (l. 286).
- The gap grew from 0.79 units on 23 May to 1.25 on 1 August, about 70% higher in sun (l. 280-282).
- 72 leaves were inoculated on 20 July; severity was scored 18 days later (l. 194, 247).
- Infected leaf area was 15.7 +/- 8.4% in shade against 5.0 +/- 3.6% in sun (p < 0.0005) (l. 293).
- The inoculum is printed as 50,000 per mL in the abstract and 1.6 x 10^5 in the methods (l. 30, 208).

## Bearing (2026-10-10)

- A single-season field observation that more sunlight means more leaf polyphenols and less downy mildew, with a measured infection-area response; the truth could use it as observed light-to-severity data, but not for infection timing, wetness or incubation.
