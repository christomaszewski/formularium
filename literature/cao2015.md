# Cao et al. 2015: wheat powdery mildew models from airborne conidia and weather

Three seasons, 2009 to 2012, of weekly wheat powdery mildew ratings in unsprayed plots at
Langfang, Hebei. Airborne conidia were the strongest variable; weather added little.

## What it holds

- **Field:** unsprayed plots, three seasons from 2009 (l. 21-22). Weekly ratings on a 0 to 9
  scale (l. 138-139).
- **Models (generalized R2):** inoculum only 82.6 % (l. 165); weather only, six variables,
  84.1 % (l. 154); temperature and humidity variables, 72.4 % (l. 24, l. 159); inoculum plus
  weather, 86.2 % (l. 210).
- **Inoculum:** airborne conidia the most important variate (l. 21). Weather variables were
  kept only if they raised R2 by more than 0.3 % (l. 155-156).
- **Method:** cumulative logit (proportional odds) model, with a 7-day volumetric spore trap and
  an hourly weather station (l. 98-118).

## Dependence

The cumulative logit model and its generalized R2 from the likelihood, as the paper cites them
(its ref 30). Earlier inoculum-based models for strawberry, apple and other crops appear as
context only. No borrowed fit is recorded.

## Bearing (2026-10-20)

- Serves t3 only as a method. It is wheat, not grapevine. A grape powdery mildew forecast could
  copy the form: spore-trap counts and weather in a cumulative logit.
